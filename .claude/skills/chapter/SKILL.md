# /chapter: plan the next chapter

Nothing here needs the climax in memory: the brief carries what the plan must honour, so this runs as well in a fresh session as at the end of the checkpoint.

1. `git pull --rebase origin main`, then `python3 engine/saga.py now`. The previous chapter's climax must be recorded (`saga.py record` with its choice) and its week frozen (`darrow.py chapter-close`). If that climax was the Book's last (the KNOT line said the transition was due and `route decide --book N` ran at the checkpoint): `python3 engine/saga.py plan book open N+1` first (it seeds the new Book's beats and quests from the arc and folds the old Book's unwritten beats); the crossing's `### Choice` block is written when he answers, or by its default at the new chapter's first close. A new Book's register is `python3 engine/saga.py arc bookN.tone` (its baseline, a tone map by beat, and the lessons the planner carries); a Book that has none gets one written into `saga/bible/_gm/arc.md` before its first chapter is planned, from the Book's question, its beats and where the company is. A new Book also sets the Knot's window from the State's next gate (`python3 engine/darrow.py doc state 3`): `python3 engine/saga.py plan pace --earliest D --likely D --why "…"`, updated whenever the PT's outlook moves; the pace line reads it.
2. `python3 engine/saga.py brief --chapter`: the Book's beats left and the compress rule when a Knot nears (at most two core beats a chapter: one in its week as a beat choice on a spine slot, never Saturday's, one as the climax), the shape rules, the device kinds that rest this chapter, the quests that may run (gates met, required first, never a third live), a Tether stage waiting for a float slot, the open climax choice (it carries two scenes), any approval rule the road would cross before its moment at the measured income (`saga.py approval forecast`: raise the threshold, or let the moment come sooner), the threads owed a touch with their plant counts, the arc's next beat, and the template with the device-allowed slots marked. On demand: `python3 engine/saga.py arc <id>` for a beat or a quest, `threads --id N`, `history --thread N`, `lore` (the index, once per chapter).
3. Fill the template in a scratch file. **What the week is for:** the Book's next core beat moves on the page every day, so the climax answers a question the week has really built (never filler, then a climax). The template is spine every day; a quest slot appears only for an owed Tether stage, or when the pace line says the story is ahead of the Knot (then a Book quest, or a floating one, spends the spare week). Fill:
   - a one-line `question` (the beat's question for this week);
   - for each slot, a one-line `plan` (what happens: an event, an arrival, a discovery, a decision; never a mood), a one-line `turn` (who can, must, believes, risks or chooses what by the end that they could not at the start, and what Darrow attempts) and a `tone` (the register its events call for; the Book's tone map is in the brief);
   - the `climax`: its plan, its `tone`, `checks` (the climax brief sets the DCs from the tier), 2–3 `options`, each right for someone and costly for someone, and a `default` (the option the highest-approval companion present would pick; never one tagged `*[Inspiration]*`);
   - small choices where a real decision falls (two or three a week at most, never on neighbouring days, each with its `default`); a device (a challenge, an invitation, a project stage) only where the story wants one;
   - `world_moves`: three things the world did, for a day he misses.

   **Hard, because canon or the real world depends on them:** a truth is revealed only when its thread shows two plants on the page (otherwise the slot plants); a Tether stage only in order and once its stage gate is met; a challenge only on an Art the page has named; an invitation only from someone on the page, alive and where Darrow is, with a `why_now`. Everything else (`plan chapter open`'s notes on tones and devices) is advice. A slot's `plan` may name Annals entries as `lore:<id>`. Aim for a week a stranger would read at a sitting, with most chapters paying or closing something old as they plant something new.
4. `python3 engine/saga.py plan chapter open --number N '<json>'` (it refuses a third live quest, an open micro and a locked gate; it prints notes, never refusals, on empty turns, the week's tones and its devices). Then the chapter file `saga/chronicle/NN-<slug>.md`: `# Chapter N — Title`, the epigraph (1–3 italic lines from an in-world document, a `— source` line), `---`; a new Book opens with `# BOOK N — TITLE` above it. `python3 engine/saga.py check`, then `python3 engine/darrow.py ship -m "chapter N planned: <title>"`.
5. Afterwards a planned slot is edited with `python3 engine/saga.py plan slot N key=value …` (`--force` for a written or skipped one); a Knot tied mid-week re-plans the rest of the week toward the crossing only when the KNOT line says the transition is due at this chapter's climax.
