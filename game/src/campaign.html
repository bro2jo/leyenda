The Unkneeling · a side game · the campaign, tenth build

The Line and the Hand

The Chronicle, played. Thirteen missions, from the morning at Harrow Ford to the night on the mill bridge. The people, the places and the things are the page's own. Most missions are a scene as the page tells it; three are fights told larger than the page tells them, and each of those says so. He starts with the Grace in him and loses it; after that his reach on the board follows the Knots, and his cards carry the rank each Art had in that chapter.

How it differs from a run

  1. A main menu. Go on with the next mission, or go to the journey (the missions as a road, with a spur for each fight told larger than the page), the road, the fire, the cards, the company, or settings.
  2. The road. Once he is on the road in Book II, the menu opens a run of the game itself: three crossings and three captains, fought with the deck he has in the campaign and the pack from the fire, with the game's own camp between battles. His best Renown is kept. Nothing on the road is the Chronicle's.
  3. Everyone's deck has two layers. Hollis, Ash and Wren each fight from nine cards with a hand of three: the Strike and Guard everyone carries, under the cards that are their own (Hew, Stand Fast, Bellow; Take the Legs, Worry, Slip Away; Thrown Stone, The Count, Duck). They no longer see every card every hand, so their draw is luck too.
  4. Combos. Some cards want something first and land harder when they have it; the card says what it wants, and reads Combo in the hand when it is there. Stand Fast, then Hew. Worry, then Take the Legs. A blow, then the second stone. A blow, then Slip Away for a card. And across the company: Ash pins a man, or Wren or the Warden's Eye lays him Open, and Darrow's stick lands 4 harder.
  5. Growing is done with cards. Hollis, Ash and Wren earn a mark for every fight they take and another for a foe sent off, in missions and on the road. Marks buy ranks, and each rank is yours to place: choose one card of that fighter's own hand, and every copy of it rises, to a third rank at most. Darrow's Arts rise only when the page says so.
  6. Words between friends. Three fights with someone beside him make a bond: that friend's lent card comes already risen from then on, and a line of theirs from the page is kept on the company screen. Eight make a second: he starts with block when they stand with him.
  7. Something to reach, and a gamble. A lit diamond on the board is worth walking to, and what it holds is drawn from a small pool when someone gets there: one of several cards to the top of his pile, a moment's rest, or an Inspiration. Each one found is a star for the fire, once.
  8. A deeper deck, so the draw matters. From the Wicket on, his deck carries three more of his plain cards. A hand with an Art in it is now luck worth having, and so is a card from a find or the fire. Measured with luck held to one side: with good draws hand after hand the test player won 68 of 75; with bad ones, 51.
  9. A map longer than the board. The Thousand Steps are three boards long. Bring the whole company down and the view follows; what lies ahead comes onto the board as it comes into sight.
  10. Set it down, pick it up. The board is kept whenever it is your move. Close the page mid-mission or mid-road and the menu offers to pick it up again.
  11. How it went. The telling ends with the fight's own account: who fought, what each dealt, who was sent off, what was found. Tap one foe on the board to see where he alone can strike.
  12. A pass for plain sense. Played through as a first visit on a phone, and what tripped it up is fixed. The notes that pop up over the board sat on the heading or on the card being read; each now has one place (over the mission's box on the map, over the hint in a clash). A foe tapped said "Next answer", which meant nothing yet; it now says what he does on his turn and what he does if fought now. Who or what was tapped is told above what has happened, so a short screen shows it whole. On the phone the three buttons at the top, the switches in Settings and every underlined link are a fingertip tall; the foes' reach is a button with an eye, lit when it is on; held sideways, it asks to be turned upright. A mission lost without a blow no longer shows an empty box.
  13. Ground you can read. One look now means one thing on every map. A wall stands up: a dark top, a lit face on its south side, a shadow on the ground below it. Deep ground (the river, the drop off the Steps) falls away from its banks. Cover he can stand in (hay, drifted snow, the fold wall, trees) is drawn low on open ground and wears a small shield; slow ground wears a 2. The wicket, the stair and the tower door used to be the same plank of wood: each has its own look now, and its name written on it. And a tap on bare ground says what it is and what it does.
  14. Three weak missions, dealt with. A careless player used to win the Binding and the Wicket as often as a careful one. The Binding is now six weeks on a knee that floods: every Step swells it, a week or the Mender's hand brings it down, and past three he loses a week (careful play wins 97 in 100, careless 7). The Wicket is now won at the wicket itself: he shuts it with his hand of cards while another grey cloak each turn puts down one of those lending him a shoulder, and Wren runs for the bell (93 against 9; with the worst draws 75). Your Road, a puzzle with no cards in it that played the same every time, is gone: its line opens the next mission's card. Thirteen missions now.
  15. Played through and rebuilt. Three test players went through every mission eighty times each. Three missions could be won by ending every turn (now none can); the bell tower's clock ran by itself (now she climbs faster the better the chairs hold); on the bridge a blow that lands moves him back a pace, and four is the end of the bridge; more grey cloaks come up to the fold; the cold in the painted hall is worth fearing by the third round. And no two plays open alike: each foe begins somewhere else in his pattern, a point heavier or lighter.
  16. Every mission has its own aim, written under the board: reach a tile, hold for so many turns, walk a stretch, stay on a stone. There is no captain to bring down.
  17. He fights from where he is put. In the House of Menders he moves one step or none. A lit ring marks where someone has to get to; a green one, what must be kept.
  18. Not every opponent is a man. A river, a stair or the cold is fought as a clash: what you deal is ground covered, and its answer is what it costs him.
  19. The deck is the chapter's. Whoever stands beside him lends a card. In the page's own scenes nothing else goes in.
  20. The fire. Every star is a star to spend. At the fire it buys camp cards, up to three, and that pack goes with him into the three fights told larger than the page. A card can be put back for its stars at any time.
  21. Cards are found as he goes. Each mission told leaves one card at the fire, shown when the mission ends; stars open the last two. The cards screen keeps everything he has held, the Arts sealed since the ford, and a place for each card still to find.
  22. He is told what is happening. A mission's card lists what is new: who is beside him, an Art learned or risen, what his body can do now, what he meets and its habit. Over the board, who is with him and the story's own beats are said as they happen, and a rule is explained the first time it matters, once. A tap sends any of it on.
  23. Three stars a mission: one for finishing it, two for doing it the way the page did.

What the icons mean

Guard, Block
Soaks damage. Guard that holds a blow whole strikes back.
Open
The next hit lands for half again.
Broken
His answer this hand is halved. Start the fight against the tree yours beats and land one hit.
Pinned, Marked
He will not move next turn. The next friend has one more breath.
Tough
Each hit on him deals less. Big blows beat small ones.
Barbed
His blow, if it lands, leaves a Wound: a dead card until the battle ends.
Banner, Rallied
While the bannerman stands, the others strike harder.
Will enrage
At half health the captain strikes harder.
Flurry
His answer is several small blows.
Stance
A card that stays in force for the rest of the battle.
A throw in hand
Darrow can still open one fight from two tiles this battle.
Tends
Rae mends a friend beside her instead of fighting.
