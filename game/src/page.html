The Unkneeling · a side game · playtest build two

The Line and the Hand

Move the company on the map, pick a fight, and play it as a hand of cards. Three grounds, seven kinds of foe, Wounds, stances, spoils, and a camp between battles. Rae is with the company now: she does not fight, she tends. The game lives in the repo under game/, and the first of your own pictures and sounds are in.

How to play

  1. On the map, tap one of the company, then a lit tile to move. Tap anyone to see what is in force on them; tap an effect to read it.
  2. To fight, tap a foe ringed in red, read the forecast, and engage.
  3. Rae does not fight. Select her and tap a hurt friend beside her, ringed in green, to mend them and set aside a Wound. A fighter with Rae beside them is lent her hand.
  4. At the table, tap a card to read it and tap again to play, or drag it up onto the field. His answer and its number sit above his head.
  5. End the hand and he answers once. Block you kept becomes guard; breath you kept is held.
  6. Bring down the captain to win the battle. Every man felled pays spoils.
  7. At camp, spend spoils on cards, mending, honing or leaving a card behind. What you do not spend counts toward your Renown.

What the icons mean

Guard, Block
Soaks damage. Guard that holds a blow whole strikes back.
Open
The next hit lands for half again.
Broken
He cannot answer this hand.
Pinned, Marked
He will not move next turn. The next friend has one more breath.
Tough
Each hit on him deals less. Big blows beat small ones.
Barbed
His blow, if it lands, leaves a Wound: a dead card until the battle ends.
Banner, Rallied
While the bannerman stands, the others strike harder.
Will enrage
At half health the captain strikes harder.
Flurry
His answer is several small blows.
Stance
A card that stays in force for the rest of the battle.
Tends
Rae mends a friend beside her instead of fighting.

What is new, and where it came from

  1. A camp between battles. Five cards on offer, one of each, bought with spoils. From Dominion, where a shared supply of limited piles is bought from each turn and the cards join your deck.
  2. Leaving a card behind. Dominion calls it trashing and Slay the Spire sells it at the merchant: a smaller deck draws its best cards more often.
  3. Spoils against Renown. In Dominion the cards that score do nothing in play, so every purchase is a choice between a stronger deck and a higher score. Here, spoils you keep are your score.
  4. Wounds. Dominion's Curses and the Spire's status cards: dead cards an enemy puts in your deck. The crossbowman's bolt does it.
  5. Stances. The Spire's Power cards: played once, in force until the fight ends.
  6. Foes with one habit each. Tough is Runeterra's keyword for taking less from every blow. The banner, the flurry and the captain's temper each ask for a different answer.
  7. Three grounds. Two fords, a road through a wood, and a bridge. Fire Emblem's lesson: the same units play differently when the ground changes.
  8. Leftover damage and friends who strike. Follow Through is Runeterra's Overwhelm turned sideways. Call the Company pays you for standing the company around one man.

Foe pictures to make

Two pictures for each foe: a full figure for the clash, cut out the same way as your Wardrobe looks, and a square portrait for the map token. Attach one of Darrow's looks as the style reference so they match him. The style and background lines are taken from your Wardrobe prompt. These are a hired road company in faded red, not anyone from the Chronicle.

Shared: put this after every figure prompt

Style: painterly fantasy concept art in the hand-painted style of the attached reference image: visible, elegant brushwork; believable weathered medieval wool, linen, leather and steel with detailed stitching; soft, warm, natural light; grounded historical fantasy. Not a photograph, not a 3D render, not a cartoon.

Composition and output: vertical 2:3 portrait (1024 x 1536). One man only, his whole figure from the top of his head to his feet with a comfortable margin on every side. He is turned three-quarters toward the left edge of the picture, as if facing an opponent who stands just out of frame on the left. Background: one solid, flat chroma blue (#0000FF) filling the whole frame edge to edge: no gradient, no texture, no glow, no vignette. No floor, no ground shadow, no frame, no text, no watermark. Nothing he wears or holds is blue, and the blue casts no light or reflection on him.

What to watch for while you play

  1. Do the foes change what you do? You should want Overwall or one big blow for the shieldbearer, block for the skirmisher's flurry, and the bannerman dead first.
  2. Is the camp a real choice? If you always buy the same card, or never mend, or never leave a card behind, tell me which.
  3. Do spoils pull against safety? Going for the captain early wins faster and pays less.
  4. Can you read the board at a glance? Three icons a token is the limit; say if you wanted one that was not shown.
  5. Is Rae worth guarding? She mends and lends, and she is the easiest of you to hurt. If you leave her at the back every time, she needs more reason to come forward.
  6. Is one hand still enough? Try a battle with three rounds.

How it was tested

The rules, apart from the screen

571 rule checks, including one for every card that it does what its text says. 4,000 random games with every card, health total and effect accounted for after each move. None stalled.

The screen

107 browser checks at phone, small-phone and desktop sizes: tap, drag, keyboard, the camp, effect icons, Rae's tending, your pikeman and your sound, and whole runs played through the real buttons.

Balance

A bot that plays sensibly wins about four runs in five with one hand, now that Rae tends. One that only chases the captain wins a little over half. Careful play beating a rush is what I wanted, but it may now be too gentle: say if it is.

Not tested

A real finger on a real phone, and whether it is fun. That part is yours.

Others who mixed a map with cards

Fights in Tight Spaces

One fighter on a small grid against several. The cards are the moves themselves, including stepping and shoving, and enemies show what they will do next so you can push one into another's line of fire.

Card Hunter

A party of three on a board. Each character's deck of 36 comes from their class and what they carry, and you play one card at a time to move or strike.

Metal Gear Acid

Any card can be spent for its effect or simply to move. A card's cost is time: the more you spend, the longer until that character acts again.

Gloomhaven

Two cards a turn, the top half of one and the bottom of the other. No dice; a small deck of modifiers does that job.

All four make the cards the moves. This design keeps movement free, the Fire Emblem way, and uses cards only for the blow, which is why a clash can be a single hand. Card Hunter's idea that the deck is what the character carries is the same as the deck being the sheet.

The deck as it stands

Darrow's deck is the sheet: one card for each Art, its rank on the card, plus plain Strikes and Guards. The last row is what the company lends, plays, and offers between battles. Ten of his thirteen cards already have a picture.

The rest of the design

The company

Each companion has a small deck of their own and plays a hand of three on two breath. Hollis hits hard and moves little. Ash is fast and pins a man in place. Wren reaches two tiles and marks a foe so the next friend has more breath against him.

A run

Three battles, each a little harder. Darrow's health carries from one to the next; the company returns whole. After a win, take one of three cards for the rest of the run, mend, or take nothing so the deck stays lean. Inspiration is three for the whole run.

What the sheet does

Arts
The cards. A new rank is a better card.
Knots
How far he moves, and which cards are still sealed.
HP, Might
How long he stands and how hard the plain cards hit.
Ember
His breath each clash.
Inspiration
A new hand.
Regard
Works as a support rank: how good a companion's lent card is.

Nothing is kept

A run lends cards and ends. An Art, a rank or a point of Might only ever comes from the Ledger. The figure in the clash is a look from the Wardrobe, so what he wears there could be what stands on the field.
