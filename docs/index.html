Season
first snow of the year on the Steps

The night of the gate, after the night bell. Darrow sat up and played Hollis at knucklebones so his shaking hands had work ("Can't sleep either."); the shake left the hand the moment it had something to hold. Darrow took the stone by a hair, and Hollis chanted the loser's line to the roof, "and the bones go to him who can stand," and paid a story told true: at the gatehouse window, for as long as it takes to say it, he had thought it good that the Captain should go down and kneel for the rest; he would have gone down the Steps on Darrow's back sooner than kneel on the beech leg. Darrow let two jokes go by: "I had three heartbeats in that river. I know what a man can think in one." Hollis's fist shut on the bones and did not shake. "Go to sleep, Captain."

The quest

The List

The Confessors have come for the Faithless at Saint Ysolde's with a list: Darrow first, Hollis second. The named are to be taken down the Steps to the Oathspire and made to kneel where everyone can watch, which would say the Ninth drowned for its own bad faith, and would tear the thread in Darrow's knee. What stands between them and the hall is Old Mercy, the sanctuary bell: while it rings at dawn and dusk, no blade passes the gate and no name under it is taken down against its will. Miss one ringing and the law lapses till the next, and Sister Pell, who rings it, is very old. At the gate, Confessor Ivo Marrant read his names at the bars, and two of his men came through the wicket before the bell rang, late. Now five grey cloaks are camped on the lower terrace below the gate, and the sixth holds the winch-house, the cage and Rae's cart, so the road is theirs. Marrant's price: the Captain of the Ninth comes down and kneels, and the rest of the paper goes back in his cloak. Darrow went to the bars and gave him his name and his face. Marrant says he will wait, and that night a grey cloak at the bars sent the Captain of the Ninth the Confessor's good night, by his rank.

What he is fighting

The soft season. Maelis read the knee after the wicket and found it held, and said it is still soft and will be at the thaw; the quiet is a liar. He cannot kneel, and has asked fifteen times to go down the Steps and been told no fifteen times. Below the gate the Confessors sit up in turns round their fire, and their good night now comes to him by his rank.
