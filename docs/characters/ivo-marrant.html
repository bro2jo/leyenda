Confessor Ivo Marrant

Confessor, under the Knight-Confessor's seal

The Confessors

Appearance

in brief

A Confessor in his forties in the grey cloak of his order, who puts his hood back in the cold so that his bald head is bare to the snow: a pleasant, well-fed face and a smile he gives to a whole court at once. He carries the iron censer on its chain in his own hand, so that he stands in his own grey weather, and the smell of hot iron reaches a gate ahead of his voice. At the bars he held nothing but the censer and the writ.

Now

as far as the reader knows

Camped on the lower terrace below the gate of Saint Ysolde's, the law's ground and nobody's, with four grey cloaks; a fifth keeps the winch-house at the foot of the Steps. He has read his list at the bars under the Knight-Confessor's seal, Darrow first and Hollis second, been answered by Old Mercy and the law of names, and has said he will wait.

Story so far

spoiler-free

He led the grey cloaks up the Holloway and the Thousand Steps and came to the gate of Saint Ysolde's in the hour before dusk, with the censer in his hand. He had been told at the winch-house fire that the Faithless had gone down the far side a week since, and read his list to the House anyway: he has read to empty beds before, he said, and finds it steadies the hand. When the Prior had the wicket opened a hand's width for his paper, two of his men came through it and were put back out by the House's shoulders as Old Mercy rang, and Mother Ione gave him the law's other half: no name under the bell is taken down against its will. He thanked her for her candour, said bells grow tired, and made his camp on the lower terrace, calling up for the Captain of the Ninth to come down and kneel.
