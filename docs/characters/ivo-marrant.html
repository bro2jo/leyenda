Appearance

in brief

A Confessor in his forties in the grey cloak of his order, who puts his hood back in the cold so that his bald head is bare to the snow: a pleasant, well-fed face, round and clean-shaven, the cheeks pink with cold, pale brows that hardly show, small light-grey eyes that hold very still, a small mark on the chin, and a smile of neat, even teeth that he gives to a whole court at once. He carries the iron censer on its chain in his own hand, so that he stands in his own grey weather, and the smell of hot iron reaches a gate ahead of his voice. At the bars he held nothing but the censer and the writ. His grey cloak fastens at the throat with a plain iron toggle.

Story so far

spoiler-free

He led the grey cloaks up the Holloway and the Thousand Steps and came to the gate of Saint Ysolde's in the hour before dusk, five at the bars and the sixth at the winch-house, with the censer in his own hand. He had been told at the winch-house fire that the Faithless had gone down the far side a week since, and read his list anyway: he has read to empty beds before, he said, and finds it steadies the hand. He held his paper out for the wicket under the law of guests until the Prior could not refuse it without breaking that law; when the wicket opened a hand's width, two of his men came through, and Old Mercy rang late and stopped them a pace from the wounded. Mother Ione gave him the law's other half: no name under the bell is taken down against its will. He called her bell fine and a little slow on the rope, made his camp on the lower terrace, and called for the Captain of the Ninth to come down and kneel. The Captain came to the bars instead and gave his name; Marrant called him Captain and thanked the Prior for her candour.
