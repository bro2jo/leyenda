Ser Aldric Vane

Of the Second Lance

The Second Lancealive
The Second Lance

Appearance

in brief

Thirty-four, beautiful in a severe way and disciplined to the bone. Of the two boys who once held other men's horses together, he is the one with the better seat, the better manners and the better luck with the kind of women whose fathers mattered. He never raises his voice and spends few words; at Harrow Ford he sat his horse at Darrow's left hand, close enough to touch, and left the one word that mattered unsaid.

Now

as far as the reader knows

Offstage since Harrow Ford, where Darrow last saw him on the west bank as the river closed over him. Seven weeks after the Binding, with six grey cloaks and a censer on the Holloway, Darrow believes that the hand that wrote his name first on their list was that of the man who has ridden knee to knee with him since they were boys; the page has only Wren's silence and her look to say who was first.

Story so far

spoiler-free

He and Darrow were squires together, holding other men's horses, and were knighted on the same morning in the same cold chapel with their palms on the same warming stone. At Harrow Ford he had the Second Lance, held in reserve by the writ from command, and sat at Darrow's left hand on the west bank watching the far shore, telling him to ride wide, and then wider, with some other word left unsaid in his open mouth when the chaplain's bell cut him off. When the Grace went out of the Ninth midriver and Darrow went under, the last thing Darrow saw through the spray was Aldric's horse on the west bank, already turned, already climbing out, Aldric bent low over its neck; when the red-handed woman turned his head back to the water, the Second sat its horses in perfect order, untouched, watching. Seven weeks later, with grey cloaks on the Holloway and a list of the Faithless, Darrow thought of a man in a warm room in Calden writing a name, and heard again: Ride wide today.
