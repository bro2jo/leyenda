Maelis Vorne

Mender of Saint Ysolde's

The House of Mendersalive
The House of Menders

Appearance

in brief

Small and somewhere in her fifties, grey hair in a braid, scarred forearms, ink on her fingers. A clay pipe of bitterleaf stays clamped in her teeth, unlit; it shifted from one side of her mouth to the other when Darrow asked how many Bindings she had done. She was first seen standing in the gate at the top of the Steps with her arms folded, looking at a knight's leg as if it had said something rude. She speaks in short sentences and offers no comfort she can't back up; sitting on the end of a cot with two fingers on a joint and her eyes shut, she can stay still for a long time, and when she opens them and looks at a man properly, it is worse than the Reading.

Now

as far as the reader knows

In her cell off the infirmary hall of Saint Ysolde's, where she took Darrow after the script of light showed itself at the window. She has told him what the script counts and about the order the Crown burned for living by the built fire, forbidden him to speak of it to anyone, Hollis most of all, and answered the question of how she knows with 'I'm a Mender. We remember things.' She has just lifted the latch. Below the mountain, six grey cloaks and a cart with a smoking censer are on the Holloway, expected at the foot of the Steps by tomorrow night if the snow holds off.

Story so far

spoiler-free

She met Darrow at the gate of Saint Ysolde's when the lay brothers carried him up the Steps, read his knee with two fingers and her eyes shut, and named it not broken but undone. She would not touch it for six weeks, then sat on the end of his cot with a wooden box on her knees and offered him two ways off the mountain: the Crown's, which is fast, or hers, which is made of him and can't be taken back. When he chose hers, she bound the knee with emberthread, an old art she asked him never to mention she practices. Seven weeks on she still reads the Knots, has said no fourteen times when he asked to go down the Steps, and has warned him that the soft season is now and that the quiet in the knee is a liar. Then Wren brought word of six riders in grey cloaks on the Holloway and of a list of the Faithless at Saint Ysolde's read out at the inn in Coldmere; asked who was first on it, Wren only looked at Darrow.
