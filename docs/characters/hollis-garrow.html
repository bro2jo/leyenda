Ser Hollis Garrow

Of the Sixth Lance

The Sixth Lance

Only what the story has shown so far.

Appearance

Sixty-one, broad through the chest and thick through the neck, with a bald crown, close-cropped white hair at the sides, a full white beard and a face gone red and broken-veined from wind and drink. Pale grey-blue eyes sit in a nest of creases, an old nick of scar cuts through one white eyebrow, and his voice carries over the Wend in flood. His right leg is gone above the knee since Harrow Ford; under his cot lies the leg the lay brothers made him, beech and a leather cup, which fitted the way a boot fits someone else until the farrier remade the cup to the stump. On the road he rides the carter’s tailboard, or a mule he curses. He talks in a sergeant’s rhythm, profane and warm, with insults doing the work of affection, and he calls Darrow “Captain” and lets the word carry whatever he needs it to.

Now

Last seen Chapter 5 · Scene 4 — Since the Fair · Coldmere

Story so far

He had himself carried to the head of the stair both nights, the lad’s blanket over his knees, and told the Captain to look at the wall. He saw the hand before the man when they came up, and called for fire in the voice that carries over rivers. He wants to know who is told.
