Wren Ashdown

Ledger-keeper of Saint Ysolde's

The House of Mendersalive
The House of Menders

Appearance

in brief

About twenty, wiry and sharp-faced, with ink on her hands and a pencil behind her ear. She walked beside Darrow's litter up the Thousand Steps in the cliff wind, counting aloud, and did not stop until the top; on the first snow of the year she stood in the infirmary doorway out of breath, snow on the shoulders of her cloak, her cheeks red from the wind and the rest of her face white, her ledger clutched to her chest like a shield. She talks fast and irreverently, with a number in every third sentence and a 'technically' ready whenever she is about to disagree with you.

Now

as far as the reader knows

She has gone to the bell tower, from which, by Sister Pell's glass, she brought word of six grey-cloaked riders and a cart with a smoking censer on the Holloway, expected at the foot of the Steps by tomorrow night if the snow holds off. Mother Ione has been told. The whole House knows there is a list of the Faithless at Saint Ysolde's; Wren knows that Hollis is second on it, and when he asked who was first she only looked at Darrow.

Story so far

spoiler-free

She counted the Thousand Steps aloud beside Darrow's litter on the day the lay brothers carried him up to Saint Ysolde's, insisting there are eleven hundred and seventeen and that everyone who says a thousand is wrong. Her ledgers keep the House's tallies: the Steps, the cots, the bells, and how many times the captain of the Ninth has asked to go down the mountain and how many times Mender Vorne has said no. Seven weeks after the Binding, on the first snow of the year, she came down from the bell tower to the infirmary hall, out of breath, with word of six grey cloaks and a censer on the Holloway road, and of a list read out at the inn in Coldmere naming the Faithless they had come to bring down to kneel. Hollis is second on it. When he asked who was first, she looked at Darrow.
