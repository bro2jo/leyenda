Appearance

in brief

Tall and rangy, thirty-two years old, with dark hair kept short, a nose that was broken and reset, and a laugh that carries. At Harrow Ford he wore mail over a gambeson and sat a grey mare at the point of sixty knights; at Saint Ysolde's he has lain for weeks on a cot with his heel on a rolled blanket, in a long hall whose window at the far end goes from black to the grey of old pewter. He gets up the way he does everything now: weight on the good leg first, then the bad one, then the slow transfer, as if crossing a frozen pond. This week he walks the length of the hall without the stick, and he does not limp.

Now

as far as the reader knows

Seven weeks past the Binding, in the season Maelis says is the softest and will feel like the best weeks he has had since the river; he has asked fourteen times to go down the Steps and been told no fourteen times. Six grey cloaks, a cart and a smoking censer are on the Holloway below Saint Ysolde's, riding toward the foot of the Steps with a list of the Faithless that was read out at the inn in Coldmere, his own name first on it by Wren's look. Behind a cell door Maelis has told him of two fires, one poured in and one built, and that the script he saw counts only what is his; she has forbidden him to speak of it to anyone, Hollis most of all, and has just lifted the latch.

Story so far

spoiler-free

With the Grace of the field-stone warm in his chest and sixty knights behind him, Darrow led the Ninth Lance into the Wend at Harrow Ford, laughing, until halfway across the warmth went out of him, his horse went down, and his knee gave with nothing touching it as he planted and turned toward the boy who had gone under his horse. As he went under he saw Aldric Vane's horse on the west bank, already turned and climbing out, one heartbeat early. A red-handed clanswoman hauled him onto the east bank by his collar and made him look: the Wend carrying knights downstream, the Second Lance sitting untouched on the far shore, and a line of grey armored figures standing very still under the trees. Nineteen of the Ninth came home, and the Crown's heralds were already on the bell-house steps naming them the Faithless and bidding them kneel anew at the Oathspire. Carried up the Thousand Steps to the House of Menders, he chose Maelis Vorne's old art over the Crown's fast way, a strand of his own sinew bound in with emberthread, nothing given and nothing anyone can take back, and heard that he may never kneel again. Seven weeks on, walking without the stick, he learns that six grey cloaks with a cart and a censer are riding the Holloway toward the foot of the Steps with a list of the Faithless, and that when Wren was asked who was first on it, she looked at him.
