Story so far

At Harrow Ford the Grace went out of him midriver, his knee gave, and he saw Aldric Vane's horse on the west bank already turned; nineteen of the Ninth came home to be named the Faithless, and at the House of Menders he chose Maelis Vorne's old art for the knee. When the Confessor came to the gate with a list of the Faithless, Darrow crossed the court to the bars and gave him his name. On the feast morning after, Ser Benedek Orrin was found Hollowed at the dawn bell with frost on his mouth, and the Confessor's word for it, Ember witchcraft, came up from the lower terrace and turned the House's eyes toward the Mender. From his cot, with Wren for his legs, Darrow showed the hall that nobody had passed in the night; he burned a letter in Aldric's hand that bade him come home and kneel, kept his word to Maelis about the black glass, and followed the cold along the east wall to a door nobody had opened. After the night bell he laid the whole night out before the House, and the House walked the length of its hall behind Wren's candle and believed him; now he stands at the undercroft door, above a stair he cannot come back up alone.

Appearance

Thirty-two, tall and rangy, with short dark hair that won't lie flat, a few days of dark stubble and grey-green eyes in a lean, weathered face. His nose was broken once and set a little crooked, with a faint scar across the bridge; his smile comes out dry and lopsided, and his laugh carries the length of a hall. At Saint Ysolde's he wears a plain linen infirmary shirt, often with a grey wool blanket round his shoulders, and gets up the way he does everything now: the good leg first, then the bad one, then the slow transfer, as if crossing a frozen pond. This week he walks the length of the hall without the stick, and he does not limp.
