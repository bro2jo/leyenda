# Ultimate Frisbee Offseason Development Guide
### October 2026 → Late Spring 2027

## The Goal

Use this offseason to return not simply as the same player with a healthy knee, but as a more complete elite handler.

By late spring, the goal is to have:

- better speed and touch control across the entire throwing arsenal
- more reliable inside and around breaks
- better ability to manipulate marks with shimmies, fakes, release points, and rhythm
- a highly usable hammer and scoober package
- legitimate off-hand overhead options
- deeper, more consistent, more catchable hucks
- better defensive positioning and footwork
- greater confidence making all of these throws under pressure

This skill plan sits **alongside rehab and physical training**. It is not another conditioning program.

Any pivoting, lateral movement, reaction work, jumping, cutting, or game-speed defensive movement should progress according to rehab clearance rather than this calendar.

---

# 1. Weekly Commitment

## Standard Week

Aim for approximately:

**3 skill sessions**

**90–110 total minutes**

plus optionally:

**10–15 minutes of film/study**

A normal week might look like:

| Session | Focus | Time |
|---|---|---:|
| A | Throwing control + breaks | 25–35 min |
| B | Overheads + mark manipulation | 25–35 min |
| C | Hucks + full throwing | 30–40 min |
| Optional | Film / defensive study | 10–15 min |

The sessions do not need to happen on specific days.

Fit them wherever they make sense around rehab and training.

---

## Minimum-Viable Week

When life, rehab, fatigue, or motivation is difficult:

**2 × 20-minute throwing sessions + 1 × 25-minute huck session**

That is enough.

Do not compensate for a missed week by doubling your throwing the following week.

Consistency over several months matters much more than perfect adherence.

---

# 2. The Five Development Priorities

Everything in the offseason fits into five buckets.

## 1. Throwing Control

Become exceptional at intentionally controlling:

- speed
- spin
- height
- edge
- shape
- distance
- arrival angle

The objective is not simply to hit a target.

It is to decide **how the disc arrives there**.

---

## 2. Break-Mark Dominance

Develop:

- inside breaks
- around breaks
- compact pivots
- high and low releases
- quick-release throws
- shoulder and head manipulation
- convincing fakes
- rhythm changes
- shimmies
- same-look/different-throw releases

Eventually, the mark should have difficulty determining what is real.

---

## 3. Overhead Expansion

Build confidence with:

- dominant-hand scoober
- dominant-hand hammer
- off-hand scoober
- off-hand hammer

These should gradually become actual tactical tools rather than practice throws.

---

## 4. Elite Huck Development

Improve:

- maximum usable distance
- consistency
- shape
- hang time
- width
- receiver-friendly placement
- flick and backhand balance
- IO / flat / OI control

The primary metric is:

**How deep can I throw while still consistently giving my receiver an excellent chance to make the catch?**

---

## 5. Defensive Development

Progress from:

**understanding → controlled footwork → reactive footwork → game-speed defense**

Early in rehab, defensive improvement will come largely through:

- film
- positioning
- anticipation
- understanding handler tendencies
- stance awareness
- reading leverage

Movement gets added as rehab permits.

---

# 3. The Three Core Sessions

These are the basic building blocks.

You can modify the drills without changing the purpose of the session.

---

## Session A — Control + Breaks

**25–35 minutes**

Primary goals:

- touch
- speed control
- inside breaks
- around breaks
- release variety

Typical structure:

### Warm-up
5–8 minutes of comfortable throwing.

### Speed-Control Work
Throw the same basic throw at three intentional speeds:

**soft → medium → fast**

Work both forehand and backhand.

Change distances periodically.

Eventually repeat this with:

- inside throws
- around throws
- uplines
- leading throws
- hucks

### Break Work
Choose one or two break families.

Examples:

- around backhand
- around forehand
- inside forehand
- inside backhand
- high-release
- low-release

Quality over volume.

---

# Session B — Deception + Overheads

**25–35 minutes**

Primary goals:

- manipulate a mark
- expand specialty throws
- improve unusual release positions

Typical structure:

### Deception
Practice things such as:

- one convincing fake → opposite throw
- shoulder fake → same-side throw
- inside threat → around
- around threat → inside
- release-height changes
- changing rhythm
- shimmy without throwing
- shimmy → release

Early in ACL recovery, most deception work should come from the upper body and whatever lower-body movement your rehab team permits.

### Overheads
Choose one specialty throw each session.

Examples:

- dominant scoober
- dominant hammer
- off-hand scoober
- off-hand hammer

Work several distances rather than simply accumulating throws from one spot.

---

# Session C — Huck Lab

**30–40 minutes**

Primary goals:

- repeatable depth
- shape
- width
- catchability
- confidence

Work both:

### Backhand
- IO
- flat
- OI

### Forehand
- IO
- flat
- OI

You do not need to throw maximum-distance bombs every session.

Think of three huck gears:

**Controlled**

Comfortable distance with extremely high accuracy.

**Game Range**

The distance you would confidently use in competition.

**Stretch Range**

Longer throws used to expand your ceiling.

Most hucks should live in the first two categories.

---

# 4. Defensive Footwork Progression

This track runs alongside the throwing plan.

It advances according to rehab clearance.

## Level 1 — Study

Use this while meaningful lateral or reactive movement is inappropriate.

Study:

- defensive stance
- leverage
- positioning
- handler tendencies
- first-step decisions
- body orientation
- force
- upline positioning
- breakside positioning

Watch possessions and pause immediately before the offensive player moves.

Predict what happens next.

---

## Level 2 — Controlled Movement

Once cleared:

Add predictable, low-intensity movement.

Examples:

- controlled stance changes
- planned lateral steps
- mark positioning
- approaching a thrower
- predetermined handler-defense patterns

No racing.

No winning.

No reaction battles.

---

## Level 3 — Reactive Movement

Once cleared for reaction and change of direction:

Add:

- mirror work
- partner cues
- live marking
- controlled handler defense
- unpredictable direction changes
- reactions to fakes

---

## Level 4 — Ultimate Movement

Later:

- live handler defense
- give-go defense
- transition defense
- marking after movement
- double moves
- small-sided play
- chaotic game situations

Do not use calendar dates to force advancement through these levels.

---

# 5. The First 12 Weeks

## Week 1 — Establish the Baseline

### Main objective
Get comfortable having structured disc work again.

### Session A
**Control**

Focus on:

- comfortable forehands
- comfortable backhands
- three speeds
- clean mechanics

Notice what currently feels different post-surgery.

Do not try to correct everything.

### Session B
**Break foundations**

Work:

- compact inside releases
- compact around releases
- comfortable dominant scoobers

Think about efficiency.

### Session C
**Huck baseline**

Throw comfortable backhand and forehand hucks.

Explore:

- current comfortable range
- preferred shape
- what feels effortless
- what currently feels inconsistent

Do not chase maximum distance yet.

### Defensive development
10 minutes of film.

Watch elite handler defense.

Focus only on starting position.

### Weekly question

**What currently feels easiest, and what feels least natural?**

---

# Week 2 — Speed Control

### Main objective

Start separating throwing power from throwing intention.

### Session A

Choose several throws and deliberately produce:

**soft / normal / fast**

Use both forehand and backhand.

### Session B

Continue dominant scoober.

Add:

- faster scoober
- softer scoober
- different heights

Work basic upper-body fakes before break throws.

### Session C

Huck at approximately 70–85% effort.

Prioritize clean shape and spin.

### Defensive development

Watch handler defenders.

Focus on:

**Where are their hips positioned relative to the disc and offensive player?**

### Weekly question

**Can I intentionally change disc speed without losing shape?**

---

# Week 3 — Inside Break Week

### Main objective

Improve inside throwing confidence.

### Session A

Emphasize:

- inside forehand
- inside backhand

Work multiple speeds.

### Session B

Use an imaginary or passive mark.

Practice:

inside threat → throw

around threat → inside

small fake → inside

Continue dominant scoober.

### Session C

Add several IO hucks.

Compare them to your flatter hucks.

### Defensive development

Study how defenders attempt to remove inside lanes.

### Weekly question

**Which inside release feels most dangerous right now?**

---

# Week 4 — Around Break Week

### Main objective

Develop cleaner around throws and faster access to them.

### Session A

Emphasize:

- around backhand
- around forehand
- high/low release variation

### Session B

Practice:

inside fake → around

around fake → around

change of release height

Introduce or increase dominant-hand hammer work.

### Session C

Explore OI hucks.

Do not force extreme edge.

### Defensive development

Watch elite marks.

Pay attention to how they recover after being moved.

### End-of-month check

Rate 1–5:

- speed control
- inside breaks
- around breaks
- dominant scoober
- hammer
- backhand huck
- forehand huck

---

# Week 5 — Same Throw, Different Arrival

### Main objective

Become more sophisticated with touch.

### Session A

Choose one target.

Hit it with:

- floating throw
- normal throw
- fast throw

Then change the shape without changing the destination.

### Session B

Dominant hammer focus.

Experiment with:

- height
- edge
- distance
- pace

Add simple shoulder manipulation before breaks.

### Session C

Hucks with different arrival shapes.

Ask:

Can this huck:

- sit?
- carry?
- bend?
- finish flat?

### Defensive development

Study the first two steps of elite defenders.

### Weekly question

**How many different ways can I deliver the disc to the same space?**

---

# Week 6 — Deception Week

### Main objective

Make your body tell convincing lies.

### Session A

Normal speed-control work.

Add one believable fake before some throws.

### Session B

Primary session.

Work:

- shoulder fake
- head fake
- pivot suggestion
- rhythm hesitation
- shimmy
- fake → opposite release

Do not add movement your knee is not cleared to handle.

### Session C

Normal huck work.

Add several throws after a small fake or setup motion if appropriate.

### Defensive development

Watch elite throwers manipulate marks.

Study the defender as much as the thrower.

### Weekly question

**Which fake actually creates a reaction rather than simply creating movement?**

---

# Week 7 — Off-Hand Scoober Introduction

### Main objective

Make the off-hand scoober feel normal.

### Session A

Keep normal control work.

Do not neglect core forehand/backhand development.

### Session B

Off-hand scoober emphasis.

Begin close.

Develop:

- comfortable release
- consistent spin
- predictable edge

Distance is secondary.

### Session C

Backhand huck emphasis.

Aim for repeatable game-range depth.

### Defensive development

Study handler upline defense.

### Weekly question

**Does my off-hand scoober have a clear tactical purpose?**

---

# Week 8 — Huck Consistency

### Main objective

Reduce the gap between your best hucks and average hucks.

### Session A

Shorter session.

Touch + break maintenance.

### Session B

Scoober review.

Mix dominant and off-hand.

### Session C

Primary session.

Create a repeatable huck test.

For example:

Throw 10 backhands and 10 forehands at game range.

Track:

- usable?
- correct lane?
- correct shape?

Do not obsess over exact percentages.

Look for patterns.

### Defensive development

If cleared for controlled footwork, begin integrating simple predetermined defensive movements.

Otherwise continue film.

### End-of-month check

Rate the same categories again.

Also ask:

**Is my average huck clearly better than it was four weeks ago?**

---

# Week 9 — Quick Release Week

### Main objective

Reduce unnecessary movement.

### Session A

Experiment with:

- shorter windups
- quicker release
- same touch with less setup

### Session B

Break throws after minimal fake movement.

Practice:

see window → release

rather than:

see window → huge pivot → wind up → throw

Continue overhead maintenance.

### Session C

Throw several hucks from compact setups.

Do not compromise mechanics just to release faster.

### Defensive development

Study how handlers attack defenders who overcommit.

### Weekly question

**Where am I using movement that doesn't actually help the throw?**

---

# Week 10 — Off-Hand Hammer Introduction

### Main objective

Begin making the off-hand hammer understandable and repeatable.

### Session A

Normal control and break maintenance.

### Session B

Primary session.

Off-hand hammer.

Start conservatively.

Focus on:

- clean spin
- predictable edge
- release comfort

Do not worry about making it spectacular.

### Session C

Forehand huck emphasis.

Work comfortable depth and placement.

### Defensive development

If cleared, add brief controlled marking footwork.

Keep it technical rather than competitive.

### Weekly question

**Can I predict what my off-hand hammer will do before I throw it?**

---

# Week 11 — Combination Week

### Main objective

Stop training every skill in isolation.

### Session A

Mix:

- soft breaks
- fast breaks
- inside
- around
- different releases

### Session B

Create small combinations:

inside look → scoober

around look → inside

hammer look → normal throw

shimmy → break

fake scoober → backhand

Do not choreograph giant sequences.

Keep them efficient.

### Session C

Mixed huck session.

Backhand and forehand.

IO, flat, OI.

Different speeds and trajectories.

### Defensive development

Study possessions rather than individual movements.

Ask:

**What problem is the defender trying to solve?**

---

# Week 12 — Test + Consolidate

### Main objective

Measure progress without turning the week into an exam.

Reduce volume slightly.

### Session A

Revisit your original control work.

Compare:

- soft throws
- medium throws
- fast throws
- inside breaks
- around breaks

### Session B

Review all four overheads:

- dominant scoober
- off-hand scoober
- dominant hammer
- off-hand hammer

Identify:

**weapon / usable / developmental**

for each.

### Session C

Repeat your huck consistency test from Week 8.

Compare:

- comfortable range
- game range
- consistency
- shape
- confidence

### Defensive development

Review film from the previous three months if available.

### 12-week review

Rate 1–5:

| Skill | Rating |
|---|---:|
| Speed control | |
| Inside breaks | |
| Around breaks | |
| Mark manipulation | |
| Dominant scoober | |
| Off-hand scoober | |
| Dominant hammer | |
| Off-hand hammer | |
| Backhand huck | |
| Forehand huck | |
| Defensive understanding | |
| Defensive movement* | |

\*Only judge physical execution if your rehab progression makes that appropriate.

Then identify:

**Two biggest improvements**

**Two biggest remaining opportunities**

Those become the priorities for the next block.

---

# 6. January → Late Spring Roadmap

The exact timing should stay flexible.

Your rehab status matters more than hitting these phases perfectly.

---

## January–February
### Expansion

Continue developing the throwing toolbox.

Priorities:

- speed control at longer distances
- deception
- faster releases
- specialty-throw consistency
- off-hand usefulness
- deeper huck range
- controlled defensive movement as cleared

Start increasing variability.

Do fewer identical reps.

---

## February–March
### Integration

Move from:

**Can I execute the skill?**

to:

**Can I choose it correctly?**

Introduce more:

- live marks
- moving receivers
- target windows
- time pressure
- decision-making
- throwing after movement
- reactive defensive work

Only as physically cleared.

---

## March–April
### Pressure

Begin making skill work resemble elite ultimate.

Examples:

- active mark
- short stall count
- live reset situations
- handler movement
- break-side continuations
- hucks to real cuts
- give-go situations
- reactive defense
- transition from defense to marking

The goal is not enormous volume.

It is maintaining throwing quality as complexity increases.

---

## April–May
### Game Transfer

Move toward:

- small-sided play
- possession games
- live handler reps
- full-speed throwing situations
- hucking to game-speed cutters
- actual mark manipulation
- defensive matchup work

As cleared.

At this point, technical work should still exist, but more skill practice should happen inside game-like situations.

---

## Late Spring
### Sharpening

Reduce experimentation.

Emphasize your strongest weapons.

You should know:

- your best break
- your best bailout
- your preferred huck shapes
- your best attacking release
- which specialty throws are truly game-ready
- where your defensive advantages are

The objective becomes reliability.

---

# 7. What Progress Should Look Like

You do not need every skill to improve at the same rate.

A good progression might look like:

### October
“This feels technically cleaner.”

### November
“I have more control.”

### December
“I have more options.”

### January
“I can intentionally manipulate the disc and the mark.”

### February
“I can execute these skills with more variability.”

### March
“I can use them while moving.”

### April
“I can use them against defenders.”

### May
“I trust them in games.”

---

# 8. Monthly Review

Once every four weeks, spend 10 minutes reviewing the plan.

Ask:

1. What has improved the most?
2. What is currently limiting me?
3. What skill am I excited to practice?
4. What has become boring or stale?
5. What does my rehab now allow that it did not allow last month?
6. What should receive less attention?
7. What should receive more?

Then adjust.

You are not obligated to follow the original plan simply because it was written down.

The plan should evolve with you.

---

# 9. Rules for Keeping This Sustainable

### Rule 1
Never feel obligated to make up missed throwing sessions.

### Rule 2
Stop a session while you still want a few more throws.

### Rule 3
If rehab or training load is unusually high, reduce skill volume.

### Rule 4
Every session should have one primary purpose.

### Rule 5
Keep at least some throwing playful.

### Rule 6
Do not turn specialty throws into a distraction from elite fundamentals.

### Rule 7
Whenever movement and ACL recovery conflict, rehab clearance wins.

### Rule 8
Video occasionally.

You do not need to record everything.

A few clips every several weeks can reveal meaningful changes.

---

# 10. The Big Picture

The offseason is not about accumulating thousands of random throws.

It is about gradually making your throwing game more intentional.

By late spring, the ideal result is:

You can throw the same window at multiple speeds.

You can manipulate a mark without wasting motion.

Your inside and around breaks both demand respect.

Your scoober is routine.

Your hammer is dangerous.

Your off-hand overheads are legitimate options.

Your hucks are deeper, but more importantly, more repeatable and more catchable.

Your defensive footwork becomes increasingly efficient as your knee allows you to train it.

And your overall game has more solutions than it did before surgery.

That is the standard:

**not merely returning to elite ultimate, but returning with a larger and more reliable toolkit.**
