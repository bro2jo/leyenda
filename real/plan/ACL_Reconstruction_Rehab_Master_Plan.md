# ACL Reconstruction Rehabilitation Master Plan
 
## Criteria-Based Rehabilitation for Isolated ACL Reconstruction With Patellar Tendon Autograft
 
> **Important:** This plan is a general rehabilitation framework for an **isolated ACL reconstruction using a patellar tendon autograft**. Surgeon- and physical-therapist-specific instructions always take priority, especially for brace use, weight bearing, wound care, range of motion, exercise restrictions, and return-to-sport clearance.  
>
> This plan is **not appropriate without modification** if there was a meniscus repair, cartilage procedure, additional ligament surgery, fracture, complication, or another reason for a more conservative protocol.

# How to Use This Plan
 
ACL rehabilitation should be **time-aware but criteria-driven**.
 
The guiding rule is:
 
> **Time makes progression possible. Criteria make progression permissible.**
 
Reaching a certain week does **not** automatically mean the next activity is appropriate.
 
Before progressing to a new phase:
 
1. Meet the previous phase's objective criteria.
2. Maintain a quiet knee with no meaningful adverse response to the current workload.
3. Respect minimum biological healing time.
4. Maintain good movement quality.
5. Obtain surgeon or physical-therapy clearance when required.
The operating system for the entire rehabilitation process is:
 
> **Measure → Train → Monitor Response → Retest → Pass Criteria → Progress**

# Core Rehabilitation Principles
 
## 1. Restore full extension early
 
Early knee extension is one of the highest priorities after ACL reconstruction.
 
The long-term goal is not merely "0°" if the uninjured knee naturally hyperextends. The goal is to restore extension as close as reasonably possible to the opposite knee unless the surgical team instructs otherwise.
 
Loss of extension can interfere with:
 
- normal gait
- quadriceps activation
- stair mechanics
- patellofemoral function
- later strength development
---
 
## 2. Swelling is a training metric
 
Swelling is not just a symptom. It is feedback.
 
Increasing effusion can inhibit the quadriceps and often means that the knee is not tolerating the current workload.
 
Track swelling consistently.
 
A useful scale is:
 
- **0 / none**
- **trace**
- **1+**
- **2+**
- **3+**
As rehabilitation becomes more advanced, the target becomes **none or trace effusion**.
 
---
 
## 3. The next morning matters
 
Do not judge a session only by how it felt while exercising.
 
The more useful question is:
 
> **How did the knee respond over the next 12–24 hours?**
 
Track:
 
- pain
- stiffness
- swelling
- extension
- flexion
- quadriceps activation
- gait
- donor-site irritation
If the knee is clearly worse the next morning, the previous session likely exceeded current capacity.
 
---
 
## 4. Strength must be measured, not guessed
 
Visual appearance is not enough.
 
Preferred strength-testing methods include:
 
1. isokinetic dynamometry
2. fixed handheld dynamometry
3. standardized knee-extension-machine testing
Track:
 
- surgical-leg absolute strength
- uninvolved-leg absolute strength
- limb symmetry
- previous best values
- body-weight-normalized strength when available
### Limb Symmetry Index
 
```text
LSI = Surgical Leg Performance / Uninjured Leg Performance × 100
```
 
Example:
 
```text
Surgical leg quad strength = 80 lb
Uninjured leg quad strength = 100 lb
 
LSI = 80%
```
 
Do not rely on LSI alone. If the uninjured leg becomes weaker, symmetry can improve even while absolute strength remains inadequate.
 
---
 
## 5. Exercises are tools; progression criteria are the program
 
A good ACL plan does not need hundreds of exercises.
 
A relatively small group of movements can take rehabilitation very far:
 
- knee extension
- leg press
- squat
- split squat
- step-up
- step-down
- RDL
- hamstring curl
- calf raise
- running
- jumping
- hopping
- sprinting
- deceleration
- cutting
The critical questions are:
 
- What load?
- What range of motion?
- What volume?
- What speed?
- What impact level?
- What complexity?
- What criteria must be met before progression?

# Master Timeline
 
| Phase | Approximate Time | Primary Objective | Main Progression Gate |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **1** | 0–2 weeks | Protect knee, restore extension, control swelling, restore quad activation | 0° extension + SLR without lag |
| **2** | 3–5 weeks | Restore full ROM, normalize gait, begin early strength | No meaningful swelling + symmetric extension + flexion within ~10° |
| **3** | 6–8 weeks | Build strength foundation and single-leg control | Full ROM/gait + quiet knee + ~70% quad symmetry |
| **4** | 9–12 weeks | Serious strength + early impact preparation | ≥80% quad/hamstring/glute measures + movement criteria |
| **5** | 13–16+ weeks | Earn return to running | ≥80% quad + full extension + ≥95% flexion + trace/no effusion |
| **6** | 4–6 months | Running → jumping → sprinting → agility | ~85–90% strength/hop capacity depending on activity |
| **7** | 6–9 months | Cutting, reactive movement, sport-specific loading | ≥90% strength/hop before hard cutting/pivoting |
| **8** | 9–12+ months | Practice → competition | Comprehensive ≥90–95%+ RTS battery |
 
These timeframes are approximate. **Criteria always matter more than the calendar.**

# Pain, Swelling, and Load-Management Rules
 
## Green Light
 
Continue or gradually progress when:
 
- pain is about **0–2/10**
- no meaningful increase in swelling
- ROM is stable or improving
- full extension is maintained
- gait remains normal
- quadriceps activation remains strong
- the knee returns to baseline by the following morning
---
 
## Yellow Light
 
Hold the current level or reduce workload when:
 
- pain is around **3–4/10**
- mild swelling appears
- the knee is noticeably stiffer the next morning
- extension temporarily worsens
- quadriceps activation is poorer
- movement quality deteriorates
A practical reduction order is:
 
> **Reduce volume first → then load → then range/impact/complexity if needed**
 
---
 
## Red Light
 
Stop the progression and contact the rehabilitation team when appropriate if there is:
 
- significant new swelling
- meaningful loss of extension
- substantial worsening of pain
- recurrent giving-way or instability
- acute traumatic event
- sudden inability to walk normally
- major functional deterioration
---
 
## Pain Guide
 
| Pain During Exercise | Response |
|---|---|
| **0–2/10** | Usually acceptable |
| **3–4/10** | Monitor closely; do not progress |
| **≥5/10** | Reduce load/range or stop |
 
Persistent pain at the front of the knee deserves particular attention after patellar tendon graft harvest.

# Patellar Tendon Graft Considerations
 
The reconstructed ACL and the patellar tendon donor site are both recovering.
 
Common graft-harvest issues include:
 
- anterior knee soreness
- patellar tendon sensitivity
- discomfort with kneeling
- discomfort during knee-extension loading
- temporary quadriceps inhibition
- tenderness around the graft-harvest area
This does **not** mean quadriceps training should be avoided.
 
It means quadriceps training should be:
 
- progressive
- measurable
- appropriately loaded
- adjusted to donor-site tolerance
---
 
## Patellar Mobility
 
If the incision is sufficiently healed and the rehabilitation team has instructed you how:
 
- superior/inferior patellar glides
- medial/lateral patellar glides
### Typical Programming
 
```text
10–20 gentle glides each direction
1–3 times/day
```
 
Do not force movement through wound pain.
 
---
 
## NMES
 
High-intensity neuromuscular electrical stimulation can be useful early after surgery when quadriceps activation is poor.
 
This is best set up by a physical therapist because intensity, electrode placement, and device parameters matter.
 
A common supervised pattern is:
 
```text
10-second contraction
50-second rest
10 contractions
```
 
NMES should supplement active quadriceps work, not replace it.

# Phase 1 — Immediate Post-Op: Weeks 0–2
 
## Goals
 
- protect graft
- control pain and swelling
- restore patellar mobility
- restore full extension
- progressively restore flexion
- reduce quadriceps inhibition
- regain full active extension
- normalize basic transfers and early walking mechanics
---
 
## Mobility
 
### Heel Prop
 
```text
5–10 minutes
3–5 times/day
```
 
Setup:
 
- support heel or ankle
- leave the knee unsupported
- allow gravity to straighten the knee
---
 
### Prone Hang
 
Use if extension remains limited and the rehabilitation team approves.
 
```text
2–5 minutes
1–3 times/day
```
 
Start gently.
 
---
 
### Heel Slides
 
```text
2–3 sets × 10–15 reps
3–5 second hold near end range
2–4 times/day
```
 
Move smoothly. Do not aggressively force flexion into sharp pain.
 
---
 
### Patellar Mobilization
 
If cleared:
 
```text
10–20 gentle glides each direction
1–3 times/day
```
 
---
 
### Calf and Hamstring Mobility
 
Gentle stretching:
 
```text
2–3 × 20–30 seconds
```
 
---
 
## Quadriceps
 
### Quad Sets
 
```text
10 reps
5–10 second contractions
3–6 small sessions/day
```
 
Cue:
 
> Fully straighten the knee → contract the quad hard → watch the kneecap move upward → hold.
 
---
 
### Straight-Leg Raise
 
Perform only if the knee can remain fully straight.
 
```text
3 × 10
```
 
Sequence:
 
1. straighten knee
2. contract quad
3. lift
4. hold full extension
5. lower under control
If the knee bends during the lift, quadriceps control is not yet sufficient.
 
---
 
### Multi-Angle Quadriceps Isometrics
 
If instructed by the rehabilitation team:
 
```text
3 sets × 5 contractions
5–10 seconds/contraction
```
 
Common positions include approximately 60° and 90° knee flexion.
 
Adjust angle if donor-site pain increases.
 
---
 
## Other Strength
 
### Double-Leg Calf Raise
 
```text
3 × 12–20
```
 
### Hip Abduction
 
```text
3 × 12–15
```
 
### Ankle Pumps
 
Perform frequently throughout the day.
 
---
 
## Swelling Management
 
Common strategies include:
 
- elevation
- compression
- cold therapy if permitted
- ankle pumps
- gentle soft-tissue/retrograde techniques when instructed
---
 
## Walking
 
Use crutches and brace according to the surgical team's instructions.
 
Do not abandon an assistive device if doing so causes a limp.
 
Walking quality should progress toward:
 
```text
Heel strike
→ controlled weight acceptance
→ knee extension during stance
→ normal push-off
```
 
---
 
## Phase 1 Exit Criteria
 
Do **not** progress simply because two weeks have passed.
 
Aim for:
 
```text
[ ] Knee extension = at least 0°
[ ] Full active extension
[ ] Strong visible quadriceps contraction
[ ] Patella glides superiorly during quad contraction
[ ] Straight-leg raise without lag
[ ] Swelling controlled
```
 
If these are not present, continue Phase 1 priorities.

## Early Plyometric Preparation
 
Begin only when basic strength, control, and knee response permit.
 
Possible sequence:
 
```text
leg-press/shuttle jump
→ small double-leg pogo
→ snap-down
→ low vertical jump and landing
```
 
### Initial Volume
 
```text
2 exercises
2–3 sets
5–8 contacts/set
```
 
Stop while mechanics are still excellent.
 
---
 
## Single-Leg Squat Test
 
Target:
 
```text
10 controlled single-leg squats
to at least ~60° knee flexion
```
 
Look for:
 
- knee tracks over foot
- pelvis remains controlled
- no major trunk compensation
- no obvious unloading of the surgical leg
- smooth depth and tempo
---
 
## Drop Vertical Jump
 
Use only when appropriately cleared.
 
Look for:
 
- symmetrical loading
- controlled knee and hip flexion
- no major valgus collapse
- stable trunk
- quiet landing
- no pain or swelling afterward
---
 
## Phase 4 Exit Criteria / Return-to-Run Preparation
 
Before beginning normal running, aim for:
 
```text
[ ] Full extension
[ ] Flexion ≥95% of opposite side
[ ] No or trace effusion
[ ] Normal gait
[ ] Quad LSI >80%
[ ] Hamstring LSI ≥80%
[ ] Glute med/max strength ≥80% symmetry
[ ] 10 controlled single-leg squats to ~60°
[ ] Pain-free low-level impact preparation
[ ] Good landing mechanics
```
 
Do **not** start running just because Week 12 has arrived.

# Return-to-Running Progression
 
Use a walking warm-up and cool-down.
 
A practical progression:
 
| Session | Main Work |
|---|---|
| **1** | Walk 5 min / Jog 1 min × 5 |
| **2** | Walk 5 / Jog 1 × 5 |
| **3** | Walk 4 / Jog 2 × 5 |
| **4** | Walk 4 / Jog 2 × 5 |
| **5** | Walk 3 / Jog 3 × 5 |
| **6** | Walk 3 / Jog 3 × 5 |
| **7** | Walk 2 / Jog 4 × 5 |
| **8** | Walk 2 / Jog 4 × 5 |
| **9** | Walk 1 / Jog 5 × 5 |
| **10** | Walk 1 / Jog 5 × 5 |
| **Then** | Progress to continuous easy running |
 
Do not necessarily run on consecutive days.
 
---
 
## Running Rules
 
Initially:
 
- use forgiving surfaces when practical
- use non-impact conditioning on off days
- increase duration/mileage before pace
- avoid increasing two variables at once
- progress weekly running volume gradually
- do not progress after a pain/swelling flare
A useful conservative rule is:
 
```text
≤10% weekly increase in running volume
```
 
Progress only when there is:
 
```text
No increased swelling
No meaningful pain increase
No loss of extension
No next-day deterioration
Normal running mechanics
```

- effusion is none/trace
- quad strength is adequate
- impact tolerance is good


```text
~70–80% speed
6–8 reps
20–60 yd

```

```text

4–8 reps
```

# Phase 8 — Return to Sport and Competition: Months 9–12+
 
Return to sport should be based on a **test battery and graded exposure**, not a date alone.
 
For cutting and pivoting sports, nine months should be treated as a minimum reference point rather than automatic clearance.
 
---
 
# Final Return-to-Sport Battery
 
## 1. Knee Status
 
Target:
 
```text
[ ] Full extension
[ ] Full/symmetric flexion
[ ] No meaningful effusion
[ ] No meaningful pain
[ ] Stable knee
[ ] No giving-way
```
 
---
 
## 2. Quadriceps Strength
 
Practical final target:
 
```text
≥95% symmetry
```
 
For high-demand pivoting sport:
 
```text
Aim toward 100% symmetry
```
 
Also evaluate absolute strength, not only symmetry.
 
---
 
## 3. Hamstring Strength
 
Target:
 
```text
≥95% symmetry when possible
```
 
Also consider:
 
```text
Hamstring : Quadriceps ratio ≥66%
```
 
---
 
## 4. Glute Strength
 
Track:
 
- glute medius
- glute maximus
Target:
 
```text
≥90–95% symmetry
```
 
---
 
## 5. Hop Battery
 
Use a standardized battery:
 
### Single Hop for Distance
 
One maximal forward hop.
 
### Triple Hop for Distance
 
Three consecutive forward hops.
 
### Crossover Triple Hop
 
Three consecutive hops crossing a center line.
 
### 6-Meter Timed Hop
 
Hop as quickly as possible over six meters.
 
Final target:
 
```text
≈95% symmetry
```
 
with good landing mechanics.
 
---
 
## 6. Jump Testing
 
If force plates are available, assess:
 
- countermovement jump
- single-leg countermovement jump
- drop jump
- eccentric impulse
- concentric impulse
- peak force
- reactive strength
- force-time characteristics
Outcome symmetry is not enough. Evaluate the movement strategy as well.
 
---
 
## 7. Running and Change-of-Direction Mechanics
 
For high-demand athletes, evaluate:
 
- high-speed running
- acceleration
- deceleration
- change of direction
- vertical-force symmetry
- knee-loading strategy
- movement quality under fatigue
---
 
## 8. Patient-Reported Function
 
Useful measures include:
 
- **KOOS-Sports**
- **IKDC**
- **ACL-RSI**
Practical late-stage targets can include:
 
```text
KOOS-Sports >90
IKDC >93
ACL-RSI ≥80
```
 
These are not substitutes for strength and performance testing.
 
---
 
## 9. Psychological Readiness
 
Do not ignore:
 
- fear of cutting
- hesitation
- avoidance
- lack of trust in the leg
- protective movement patterns
A strong leg that is not trusted under sport conditions is not fully ready.
 
---

# Progressive Overload Rules
 
## Most Main Strength Exercises
 
As rehabilitation matures, many primary lifts should eventually reach approximately:
 
```text
RPE 7–9
~1–3 reps in reserve
```
 
Do not make every set maximal.
 
---
 
## Increasing Load
 
When all target reps are completed with excellent technique and the knee remains quiet:
 
```text
Increase load by approximately 2.5–10%
```
 
depending on the exercise.
 
Small isolation movements usually need smaller jumps than large compound exercises.
 
---
 
## Progress One Major Variable at a Time
 
Variables include:
 
- load
- reps
- sets
- range
- speed
- impact
- complexity
- uncertainty
Example:
 
```text
Week 1
Split squat
3 × 8 @ 20 lb
 
Week 2
3 × 10 @ 20 lb
 
Week 3
3 × 8 @ 25 lb
```
 
Avoid simultaneously increasing:
 
```text
load
+ reps
+ sets
+ depth
+ speed
+ impact
```

# Red Flags
 
Contact the surgical team promptly for concerning postoperative symptoms such as:
 
- fever
- significant new calf pain or calf swelling
- excessive wound drainage
- worsening redness around the incision
- uncontrolled or rapidly escalating pain
- chest pain
- shortness of breath
- sudden major loss of motion
- sudden inability to bear weight
- new instability after trauma
- rapidly increasing swelling
