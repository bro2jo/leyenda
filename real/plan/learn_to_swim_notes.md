## The four techniques I want you to prioritize

### 1. Back floating

This may be the single most beneficial skill for your deep-water fear.

- Relax your neck and shoulders.
- Look toward the ceiling.
- Allow your ears to enter the water.
- Keep your arms comfortably spread.
- Breathe normally.
- Have someone support you until you're comfortable.

Your legs may naturally sink somewhat depending on your body composition. Gentle kicking or sculling can help you maintain a comfortable position.

### 2. Treading water

Treading water gives you the ability to remain upright without needing to touch the bottom.

Start learning with support in shallow water. Eventually, under instructor supervision, work toward staying afloat for:

- 10 seconds
- 30 seconds
- 1 minute
- 2 minutes

These are surface flotation targets, not underwater breath-holding targets.

### 3. Underwater nasal breathing

Since you can already fully submerge your head, focus on breaking your dependence on holding your nose.

Practice gentle exhalation rather than forceful humming. You want to develop a relaxed, controlled release of air. U.S. Masters Swimming recommends this technique for developing underwater breathing control. (U.S. Masters Swimming)

### 4. Rolling onto your back

Learning to transition from swimming on your front to floating on your back gives you another way to rest and breathe if you become tired or anxious.

Practice this with assistance until you can do it reliably.
