# Open Every Page on the Story

Across games, LitRPG, webtoons, web serials, interactive fiction and visual novels, the deeper evidence keeps the first report's rule and sharpens it: visuals further a reader's understanding when they **carry what the prose has already established** (a face, a place, the road walked, what has been learned), stay consistent with the text and with each other, and sit beside or after the words; they hurt when they contradict the text, when extra words or sound interrupt the reading, or when progress turns into counting. Three corrections change how that applies: a purely pictorial decorative detail did **not** reliably harm learning in the largest meta-analysis (the harm came from extra text and audio), moving distracting material to the end **reduced** its cost rather than removing it, and pictures that contradict the text did **worse than no picture at all**. The designers read in full this time converge on one interface rule, **show the pressure and hide the consequence table**, and on proving quickly, and every time, that the story noticed. Measured live, the Chronicle's weekly pages open on words and game state: the Now page's first screen holds no picture and leads with Wardrobe news, mechanics fill about two of its five screens, the map sits below them, and no chapter or tale has a picture, while most fold headings are 18 to 23 px tall, map links 9 to 22 px, the Codex downloads about 1.65 MB of song audio before anyone presses play, and six 640-pixel portraits fill circles 48 to 86 pixels across. In priority order, the fixes are to open the Now page on the story, fold the countdowns and fix the thumb targets, small print and page weight (builder work that costs him no time), then to write a one-page style guide, paint the place he stands in, make the map the journey's record, give each Book a colour, choice marks and one closing picture, and tie every visible change to its scene. Treat it as well argued rather than proven: bot checks kept most academic papers to their abstracts, and no controlled study tests illustrations, maps or hidden mechanics in serial fiction read over months.

## Designers were read in full, papers mostly through abstracts

This report draws on six sets of notes compiled on 17 November 2026, each a verification pass that re-checked the first report's evidence against full primary pages: cognition and motivation; narrative game interfaces; LitRPG, progression fantasy, webtoons and light novels; web fiction, interactive fiction and visual novels with Nielsen Norman Group's usability research; art direction and AI image tools; and a live audit of the public site. The audit loaded every page type in headless Chromium at 390×844 and at 1280 px wide, measured the rendered page, logged every network response, tapped, opened and swiped, and read the raw HTML of 17 pages, without opening `docs/`, `real/` or any GM path ([live site](https://bro2jo.github.io/leyenda/), measured). Its limits are plain: the loads were cold, in desktop Chromium emulating a phone rather than on a real one; the desktop hover path, a level-up banner, an open choice and reduced motion were not exercised; and the 24 px and 44 px tap thresholds were applied from the brief, because the WCAG text on w3.org was outside the session's network.

## Pictures help when they match the page; mismatch and extra words do the damage

### Decoration is a small risk; contradiction is a large one

The largest meta-analysis of "seductive details", interesting material irrelevant to the point, pooled **58 papers, 68 effect sizes and 7,521 participants** and found harm to retention (g = −0.37) and transfer (g = −0.41) ([ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/339511797_Keep_it_Coherent_A_Meta-Analysis_of_the_Seductive_Details_Effect), META, abstract). A moderator the first pass missed changes how this applies: according to a later paper that read it in full, the harm held only when the detail came as audio or as text with a picture, and **"there was no overall significant effect from the mere presence of a pictorial seductive detail"** ([Frontiers 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1393113/full), META via citing full text). Newer pooled estimates are smaller, at g = −0.16 across 177 effect sizes ([AERA 2026](https://convention2.allacademic.com/one/aera/aera26/online_program_direct_link/view_paper/2273554/), META, abstract). Harp and Mayer's four experiments (not six, as Wikipedia's summary says) give the placement rule: highlighting, objectives and signalling did not remove the harm, **details placed at the start did the most damage, and details moved to the end reduced it**, because early material activates the wrong frame of prior knowledge ([scilit record](https://scilit.com/publications/162a7b78ef56a04497da909fb84d0592), CTRL ×4, abstract).

Pictures with a job (showing, organising or interpreting the text) aid learning while decorative ones do not ([Carney & Levin](https://www.doi.org/10.1023/A:1013176309260), META, abstract), and attractive colour and faces in *relevant* graphics help retention a little without much changing mood ([Lenski & Großschedl](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.966287/full), META via citing full text). The sharpest finding is about mismatch. In an adult study, a comic beat the same content as text, but **text alone beat the comic with its pictures swapped for incongruous ones** ([Sheffield Hallam](https://shura.shu.ac.uk/12833), CTRL n=90, abstract). Children's story apps show the same pattern at meta-analytic scale: pictures, music and sound that match the text help comprehension about as much as an adult reading along, while in one included study children who could play games "hardly spent time listening to the story" ([Takacs, Swart & Bus 2014](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.01366/full), META k=29, full), and interactive hotspots distracted ([2015 follow-up](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4647204), META k=43, abstract). Signalling with arrows and highlights helps readers new to the material most and may hurt experts ([Frontiers in Education 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2022.916105/full), META via citing full text), which matters for an author who knows his own world better than anyone. All of this measures learning from lessons, not pleasure in a story; read as risks on a story page, it ranks **a picture that contradicts the prose first, extra interesting words or sound inside the reading second, and a quiet picture that matches the page a distant third**.

### Adult readers imagine more easily with pictures, but prose-first readers feel more

The adult fiction evidence is thin and now mixed. The study the first report leaned on is a narrative-persuasion experiment in which **212 online adults** read a story about people with schizophrenia as a graphic novel or as text; the images made it easier to imagine, which raised engagement and lowered counter-arguing ([RTI](https://www.rti.org/publication/seeing-believing), CTRL, abstract). Two small 2025 studies cut the other way. A short literary story turned into a graphic novel with GPT-4 and Midjourney produced **no difference in comprehension or engagement** for 76 readers of English as a second language, and in a thesis version with 80 readers **the text-only group reported more emotional engagement**, while readers spotted the AI through its texture and inconsistencies ([TU Delft](https://repository.tudelft.nl/record/uuid:2ef58fcd-4119-4584-b42f-bb7f4ce56c69), CTRL, abstract; [Utrecht](https://studenttheses.uu.nl/handle/20.500.12932/48521), CTRL, abstract). People who read a novel before seeing its film were more transported by the film ([Buffalo record](https://researchconnect.buffalo.edu/en/publications/transportation-across-media-repeated-exposure-to-print-and-film/), CTRL and FIELD, abstract). Readers with no visual imagery felt less absorption and less sympathy for characters but appreciated the story as much ([Tilburg](https://repository.tilburguniversity.edu/items/80824de1-1604-439a-8276-3a651c3f2058), CTRL n=98, abstract), and qualitative work with young readers finds illustrations can either scaffold a reader's own pictures or override them ([Open University](https://oro.open.ac.uk/83783), qualitative, abstract). Failbetter met the override at the scale of a world: painting a sea first known in words always causes "some dissonance… 'So wait, the Iron Republic is *there*?'" ([Failbetter](https://www.failbettergames.com/news/mapping-the-sunless-sea-geography-spoilers), D, full).

What readers look at confirms the job pictures should do. Eye-tracking shows people scrutinise images that carry information and ignore "fluffy pictures used to 'jazz up' web pages", and one user spent 10% more time on a page's portraits than on biographies that took 316% more space ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/photos-as-web-content/), Study, full). A meta-analysis of 132 effect sizes found people most transported by stories with **identifiable characters and plots that readily call up images** ([EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/611188), META, abstract), and readers judge an illustrated character's moral nature from appearance alone with notable agreement ([Barcelona](https://diposit.ub.edu/dspace/bitstream/2445/206409/1/240624.pdf), CTRL, abstract), so a face says things the prose may never have meant. The rule therefore gains a clause: **prose first, picture after, and every picture consistent with the prose and with the pictures before it**.

### Readers do not build the map in their heads

The map stands on firmer ground than the first report claimed, for an unexpected reason. In ordinary reading, **people do not build precise spatial models of a story**; told to, they do, at a cost in reading speed ([Zwaan & van Oostendorp](https://www.bohrium.com/paper-details/do-readers-construct-spatial-representations-in-naturalistic-story-comprehension/811822596671668225-5684), CTRL, abstract). The protagonist-centred spatial gradient appears when readers have learned a map first and are tracking the hero ([Memory & Cognition](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13421-013-0329-2), CTRL, abstract). A map therefore supplies a frame readers otherwise skip, and it belongs beside the reading rather than inside it. Why it helps is contested: Griffin and Robinson found no recall advantage for a map over a list of its features ([Georgia Southern](https://scholars.georgiasouthern.edu/en/publications/does-spatial-or-visual-information-in-maps-facilitate-text-recall/), CTRL, abstract), which points to recognisable landmarks rather than exact layout. Failbetter found the same in play, when Sunless Skies captains flew "through featureless fog in the general direction of an icon" because "one group of rocks or bronzewood looks much like another", until the studio gave them distinct paths ([Failbetter](https://www.failbettergames.com/redesigning-the-reach), D, full). On a phone the thumb is the constraint: NN/g reports that **"maps on touchscreens often cause touch-target errors… users immediately make a 'focus face'"**, and that a list view should ideally be the default ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/touch-target-size/), Study-based guideline, full).

### Songs belong beside their lines, one at a time

Audio keeps its split. Lyrics heard while reading impair comprehension more than instrumental music ([PubMed](https://www.pubmed.gov/29958067/), META, abstract, not re-read this pass), while music and sound that match a story helped children's comprehension (Takacs, above), and browsers settle the autoplay question anyway, since Chrome has blocked sound without a user tap since version 66 ([Chrome](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/autoplay), Docs, summary). The composers behind inkle's and Failbetter's games describe **one recurring motif carried across a game's music**, Laurence Chapman's major sixth and Meghann O'Neill's pitch motif, and music matched to the painted look rather than to single scenes ([Failbetter, Chapman](https://www.failbettergames.com/news/coming-soon-the-mask-of-the-rose-soundtrack), D, full; [Failbetter, O'Neill](https://www.failbettergames.com/news/pitching-potential-dynamic-combat-music-in-sunless-sea), D, full). No study tests hearing the very words one is reading sung.

## Designers show the pressure, hide the table and prove they noticed

### Show the meter, keep the consequences for later

Red Hook first tried to keep Darkest Dungeon's stress opaque, then reversed: "we needed to completely expose the Stress meter and display stress increases and decreases directly as numbers", because it "provides clear feedback and consequences". What stayed hidden was the table of outcomes: **"we purposefully don't list all the behaviors that any given Affliction may have… In this way your time and veteranship are rewarded"** ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-i-darkest-dungeon-s-i-affliction-system), D, full). Jon Ingold puts the case against visible scoring more bluntly: "If you have a morality system just based on +3 nice points, it sort of undermines you from the start. It encourages the player to min-max", and "Interactive fiction is a magic trick" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/-t-m-i-preserving-the-suspension-of-disbelief-through-ambiguity), D via C, full). Failbetter's writer guidelines ask for "as few unique qualities as possible", since extra ones "clutter up the UI", and warn that "a string of quality changes in a result makes each one less important" ([Failbetter](https://www.failbettergames.com/news/fallen-london-writer-guidelines-part-ii), D, full).

Displayed odds are the clearest case. Jake Solomon says players see 85% and think "That's close to a hundred; that basically should not miss", so XCOM quietly tilted the number toward the player's feeling ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/jake-solomon-explains-the-careful-use-of-randomness-in-i-xcom-2-i-), D via C, full), and Sid Meier observed that "Whenever something random happens to the player, paranoia sets in" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/pc/gdc-sid-meier-s-lessons-on-gamer-psychology), D via C, full). Citizen Sleeper's designer found that "abstracted, thematic mechanics like dice rolls and clocks solved way more problems than it created", in a style "where you are asked to fill in the details" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/how-citizen-sleeper-was-inspired-by-tabletop-rpgs-and-gig-work), D, full). The case for visibility is real too. Meier also said "You cannot reward and reflect progress too much in a game", and Failbetter tells players how long a hardship lasts, because "you want to know how long you're going to be up on that cross" ([Failbetter](https://www.failbettergames.com/dont-poke-that-narrative-engineering-principles-part-1), D, full). The synthesis is the split the first report proposed, now with primary support: **show that the meter moved and the shape of what lies ahead, and keep how much, how close and what it opens one tap away**.

### Prove you noticed, quickly and every time

Ingold's case for 80 Days' map is that callbacks prove attention too late: "If you want to prove to players that you are paying attention you have to do it really really fast. The way we did that is with the map." A single trait word at the end of a passage, such as "suave", was "just enough to make it feel significant" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/-t-m-i-preserving-the-suspension-of-disbelief-through-ambiguity), D via C, full). In the same article Sean Vanaman names the other side of any such notice: once players learn that "Clementine will remember that", **every moment without the notice reads as "Oh, I'm on a linear path, nothing has changed"**. Supergiant built Hades around "moments where you feel the game is paying attention" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-supergiant-weaves-narrative-rewards-into-i-hades-i-cycle-of-perpetual-death), D via C, full), and Hades II keeps playing new scenes after a bond is maxed, so the top state never goes silent ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/roguelike/supergiant-drops-a-hades-2-patch-with-a-bunch-of-new-dialogue-better-romances-and-buffs-to-the-games-weakest-god/), C, full). For the Chronicle, **any mark that says "the story noticed" must appear every time, or not at all**.

### The record is a book the reader fills in, tagged by what he knows

Lucas Pope added Return of the Obra Dinn's book late, because simple logbook pages "wasn't nearly enough"; a book let him use "chapters, bookmark system, glossary, table of contents… without detailed instructions", and "just facing that empty book is enough to pressure me into filling it out" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/road-to-the-igf-lucas-pope-s-i-return-of-the-obra-dinn-i-), D, full). Heaven's Vault's timeline entries "are, of course, tagged with the states in the knowledge model that unlock them" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/ideas-and-dynamic-conversation-in-heaven-s-vault), D, full), and its critic loved the timeline but called it "a horrible way to store your translations" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/boldly-she-rode-how-i-heaven-s-vault-i-reinvents-adventure-games), C, full), a reminder to keep the chronological record apart from the reference. 80 Days originally showed nearly every route on its globe; halfway through development the team found players "were skipping through city content without reading it", so they **locked the routes and handed them out as rewards** ([postmortem](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/postmortem-inkle-s-i-80-days-i-), D, full). Fallen London's sea map "will fill in slowly as they learn about each location" ([Failbetter](https://www.failbettergames.com/news/map-for-the-unterzee), D, full), and Ingold refuses to write about heather the player has not seen ([inkle](https://www.inklestudios.com/highland/2022/05/03/letters.html), D, full).

Fans of the biggest LitRPG serial build the same thing by hand. The Wandering Inn wiki's Leveling History links every row to its chapter, records **when the page revealed a fact rather than when the character gained it**, and marks unknowns openly with "Lv. ?" and "+?" ([TWI wiki](https://wiki.wanderinginn.com/Yvlon_Byres/Leveling_History), Community, full), and its chapter pages carry a three-to-five-sentence summary and first-appearance markers ([TWI wiki](https://wiki.wanderinginn.com/Help:Policies/New_Chapter_Guide), Docs, full). Failbetter's reason for such reminders fits a serial read on a phone in short sittings: "people play FL in spurts… Provide reminders in content so players can reorient themselves" ([Failbetter](https://www.failbettergames.com/news/fallen-london-writer-guidelines-part-ii), D, full).

### Genre norms put art after the chapter, colour on kind and metal on rank

Web-serial authors place art after the reading. On Royal Road they keep chapter art in the top or bottom author's note, often behind a spoiler tag, because images mid-text "tend to feel disruptive", and one author called AI art in every chapter "a magnet for bad reviews" ([Royal Road forum](https://www.royalroad.com/forums/thread/133241?page=1), Community, summary; [thread 161872](https://www.royalroad.com/forums/thread/161872), Community, summary). The Wandering Inn wiki gives each chapter page one image, with the rule "No two chapters should have the same image", and bans AI images outright ([TWI wiki](https://wiki.wanderinginn.com/The_Wandering_Inn_Wiki:Chapter_Images), Docs, full; [policies](https://wiki.wanderinginn.com/Help:Policies), Docs, full). Light-novel inserts range "from inconsequential fan-service to a spoiler-filled climactic event", which is why their placement worries readers ([English Light Novels](https://www.englishlightnovels.com/2021/03/16/a-writers-reflections-on-light-novel-inserts/), poll, summary).

The genre also keeps two visual codes apart. The Wandering Inn colours Skills by *meaning* (green for new, red for forced or harmful, blue for restoration) and keeps rank in levels and metal adventurer ranks ([TWI wiki](https://wiki.wanderinginn.com/Skills), Community, full). Borderlands copied World of Warcraft's rarity colours on purpose, since "Why reinvent the colors when you're just going to confuse people?" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/analysis-i-borderlands-i-the-anatomy-of-a-gun), C, full), and ladders that break the intuitive precious-metal order confuse readers, as Goblin Slayer's six gems between steel and bronze do ([Goblin Slayer wiki](https://goblin-slayer.fandom.com/wiki/Adventurer), Community, summary). Colour alone fails many readers. Mark Brown's advice is to "avoid relying on color alone" and use "shapes, symbols, shading" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-maker-s-toolkit-explores-how-devs-can-design-for-colorblindness), C, full); She Remembered Caterpillars paired every colour with a symbol and tested it with Color Oracle ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/hand-drawn-colour-blind-friendliness-she-remembered-caterpillars), D, full); and NN/g finds most icons need visible text labels, with hearts and stars especially "hard to memorize and interpret precisely" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/icon-usability/), Study, full).

Typography follows the same restraint. PC Gamer's description of Pentiment's period fonts as "only ever offering redundant information", so the easy-reading mode loses no story, is the journalist's line rather than the designer's ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/how-pentiments-hand-crafted-fonts-give-pen-and-ink-a-voice/), C, full); Josh Sawyer's own words are that authentic fonts "were often quite difficult to read… the difficult-to-read part is not good for a game full of text" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/pentiment-director-explains-how-going-all-in-on-fonts-helped-elevate-the-medieval-detective-rpg-), D via C, full), and NN/g finds gothic-style fonts reduce legibility ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/legibility-readability-comprehension/), Study, full). Failbetter stripped gradients from its story page because the game "takes place largely in the imagination of the player, so we don't want anything interrupting that line of communication" ([Failbetter](https://www.failbettergames.com/news/redesigning-the-story-page), D, full).

### Change belongs on the same figure, with its history visible

Wildermyth's transformations "replace body parts over time, piece by piece", each a "clearly telegraphed choice", with comic panels between chapters ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/great-moments-in-pc-gaming-transforming-a-hero-into-something-bizarre-in-wildermyth/), C, full). For Darkest Dungeon II, Chris Bourassa wanted to "grow the characters up a little bit" while maintaining "the distinctive style" ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/interview-darkest-dungeon-2s-new-3d-look-monsters-the-narrator-and-its-surprising-aspirational-spirit/), D via C, full), and Victoria Tran treats costume as history: "What happened in their past that manifests itself today?" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/fashion-in-games-why-it-matters), D via C, full). Mask of the Rose dropped "+1 Coquettish" for a wardrobe that "talks back to you instead: your own player character telling you, in English, how you're likely to come across" ([Failbetter](https://www.failbettergames.com/news/dressing-outrageously), D, full). One design runs against the Chronicle's rule: Failbetter's Mandrake portraits work "a bit like tarot cards", with details that "seem like decoration at first" but later turn out to be clues ([Failbetter](https://www.failbettergames.com/news/character-illustrations-in-mandrake), D, full). A page-first rule forbids that direction, and the safe form is the reverse, where **details in a later portrait call back to what the prose has already shown**.

## Counting raises effort; a self he designs raises identification more than effort

### Visible counts move behaviour, sometimes the wrong way

Measurement raises output and can lower enjoyment. Across six experiments, people who tracked their output walked or read more but enjoyed it less; those who watched their page count read more in eight minutes but were less likely to keep reading, and framing reading as useful removed the penalty ([Duke Fuqua](https://www.fuqua.duke.edu/duke-fuqua-insights/etkin-counting-steps), CTRL ×6, abstract), though the reading study's sample size could not be confirmed and its enjoyment ratings clustered near the midpoint. Across 128 experiments, tangible rewards tied to engagement (d = −0.40), completion (d = −0.36) or performance (d = −0.28) each undermined motivation, more for children than college students, while **positive feedback enhanced it (d = 0.33)** ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10589297/), META, abstract). Points, levels and leaderboards raised quantity without changing intrinsic motivation ([bibbase](https://bibbase.org/network/publication/mekler-brhlmann-tuch-opwis-towardsunderstandingtheeffectsofindividualgamificationelementsonintrinsicmotivationandperformance-2017), CTRL, abstract). In randomised trials of gamified physical activity, the effect was g = 0.42, fell to **g = 0.23 against an active programme without game elements**, and to g = 0.15 about fourteen weeks after the programmes ended ([JMIR](https://www.jmir.org/2022/1/e26779), META of 16 RCTs, abstract); the first report's claim that a story wrapper moderated behaviour could not be verified ([ERIC](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1245270), META, abstract). Monitoring progress does help: across 138 studies it raised goal attainment (d = 0.40), more when progress was recorded and reported ([White Rose](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/91437/), META, abstract).

How progress is shown matters. Across 32 experiments, progress bars did not reliably reduce quitting, and **bars that seemed slow at first increased it** ([Google Research](https://research.google/pubs/where-am-i-a-meta-analysis-of-experiments-on-the-effects-of-progress-indicators-for-web-surveys/), META, abstract). A natural experiment on **433,138 developers**, read in full, shows what a visible counter does: when GitHub silently removed its streak counters, long streaks were abandoned, weekend activity dropped, and **"single-contribution days", the minimum needed to keep a streak alive, fell from 36% to 32% of days** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.02371), FIELD, full). A broken streak demotivates even when the behaviour behind it was identical, less so when it can be repaired, and the authors advise against announcing breaks at all ([JCR](https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article/49/6/1095/6623414), CTRL ×7, abstract). Where real effort feeds a count, a visible tally of days invites doing *something* every day to keep it moving, which is not the same as doing what the plan asks, rest days included. The engine already refuses to pay for doing more and excuses planned rest ([CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md)); the display should not ask for it either.

### Curiosity works when the gap closes

Curiosity peaks at middling confidence, predicted better recall of surprising answers one to two weeks later, and led people to spend scarce tokens and time to learn answers ([Berkeley](https://neuroecon.berkeley.edu/papers/papers_files/Kang_PsychSci_2009.html), CTRL n=19 plus follow-up, abstract). Seven studies found that creating an uncertainty and then resolving it, "teasing", is a net positive experience ([Iowa](https://iro.uiowa.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/The-Teasing-Effect-An-Underappreciated-Benefit/9984380448802771), CTRL ×7, abstract), while four found that curiosity drives people to resolve uncertainty even at a cost to themselves ([Iowa](https://iro.uiowa.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/The-Pandora-Effect-The-Power-and/9984380412002771), CTRL ×4, abstract), a warning that a hidden mechanic must never make *more effort* the way to find out what happens. A 2025 meta-analysis found **no memory advantage for unfinished tasks but a general urge to resume them** ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/pal/palcom/v12y2025i1d10.1057_s41599-025-05000-w.html), META, abstract), and people who knew they had won *a* prize, but not which, stayed happy longer ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/psychology-and-destiny-s-loot-system), C citing CTRL, full). **No controlled study compares hidden with visible mechanics.** Removing a game's on-screen data raised immersion only in expert players ([White Rose](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/130581/), CTRL, abstract), and NN/g's guidance on revealing detail in stages is to show the few most important things first, because "the very fact that something appears on the initial display tells users that it's important", and to stop at two levels, since beyond that "users often get lost" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/), Study, full). Greyed pieces that name what opens them, and an open choice, are resumption devices; each needs a resolution he can see coming.

### The Wardrobe's evidence is strong for identification and mixed for effort

People act like their avatars: across 46 of 83 candidate studies the effect sat between **r = .22 and .26**, with customisation only qualitatively linked to stronger effects ([Taylor & Francis](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/pdf/10.1080/15213269.2019.1623698), META, abstract). Studies read in full now show **customisation reliably raising identification but not fun or performance**. Choosing a League of Legends skin raised identification (d = 0.69) with no effect on fun or performance ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8765231/), CTRL n=24, full); customising a runner raised identification strongly (ηp² = .69), but identification did not carry through to a real treadmill run ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/virtual-reality/articles/10.3389/frvir.2022.873689/full), CTRL n=44, full); and in a preregistered online study, choosing one's look raised identification, autonomy and time spent with small effects ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2202.05315v1), CTRL, planned N=1,500, full). The study the first report credited with lower drop-out is in fact a crowdsourced mood experiment in which customisation raised identification and engagement but, without the meaningful training task, went with *less* resilience to a low mood ([JMIR](https://jmir.org/2019/1/e10133/PDF), CTRL quasi n=317, abstract), so customisation belongs inside a purposeful loop.

On bodies the evidence is conditional. In VR cycling, idealised avatars raised wishful identification but lowered performance, while **realistic avatars given a few idealised touches raised wishful identification without that cost** ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3313831.3376661), CTRL, summary, n not seen), and embodying a six-pack avatar produced *fewer* body movements ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.693543/full), CTRL n=96, full). The most relevant new study, a 2026 trial, found that an avatar whose muscle grew **gradually, in real time, with the user's own exercise** produced larger acute strength gains and a stronger sense of agency than one that switched to a muscular body at the end ([Frontiers in VR](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/virtual-reality/articles/10.3389/frvir.2026.1869292/full), CTRL n=32, full), with uncorrected comparisons and acute effects only. Such effects are often temporary ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.693545/full), CTRL n=104, full). For the Wardrobe, **his authorship of the look is the active ingredient for identification**; gear can be aspirational while the body tracks what the story has allowed; changes should arrive piece by piece as consequences, with their causes visible; and the pull will need renewing each Book. A fantasy knight on a phone is a weaker stimulus than a self-resembling avatar in VR.

### 1. Open the Now page on the story

The most-visited page should open on the story. Today the order runs Wardrobe news, plate, quest, companions, Reckoning card, tales, map, last answered choice, dice. Put the story plate first (with the open choice above it on days one is open), then the companions and the quest, **then the map directly under them**, then the Reckoning card with its arithmetic folded (change 2), then the dice, and shrink the Wardrobe news to one line inside the Reckoning card or at the page foot. NN/g's scroll data say what sits in the first two screens is what gets seen ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolling-and-attention/), Study, full), its disclosure guidance says placement itself signals importance ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/), Study, full), and Failbetter moved Sunless Skies' reading panel to the centre and put inventories in a separate panel that can be toggled ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-to-remove-the-frustration-of-reading-in-games), D via C, full). The change is a reorder in the builder, works without JavaScript and costs him nothing.

### 2. Fold the countdowns, keep the metal

Four places show by default how far there is to go or what opens next: each attribute tile and Art row on Darrow's page; the Collection, which opens on the full ladder with every rank's threshold; the Knot list, where untied Knots name what they will let the leg do; and the Now page's Reckoning card, with its figures and next-Knot line. Move everything that says *how far* or *what next* into one closed fold per surface, the pattern the dice already use for their arithmetic, so that **opening the fold becomes the act of asking**, the line CLAUDE.md draws when it says `/sheet` is asking. Keep open the metal of crests and frames, the medallion arcs against his former self, the rope's shape (including how many Knots it holds, Failbetter's "how long… up on that cross"), the bars as shapes without figures, and the words. The Collection keeps his rank and the next one in view, as CLAUDE.md allows, and folds the rest of the ladder; the 6 to 8 px medallion numbers go into the fold too, which also solves their legibility.

The evidence is Darkest Dungeon's split, in which "your time and veteranship are rewarded" by not listing outcomes (D, full); Ingold's "+3 nice points… encourages the player to min-max" (D via C, full); the GitHub finding that a visible counter drives minimal keep-alive actions (FIELD, full), which is exactly what an Art row's "so far" figure is; Etkin's enjoyment penalty (CTRL, abstract); and the finding that slow-looking bars raise quitting (META, abstract). Positive, informational feedback still enhances motivation (META, abstract) and monitoring helps attainment (META, abstract), so the fold moves the arithmetic without hiding that progress happened. Nothing here touches real data, and everything stays one tap away.

### 3. Fix the thumb, the small print and the weight

This is one builder pass. Pad every one-line `summary` and list link to at least 24 px tall, and toward 44 px where space allows: Codex rows, the roll folds, scene-list folds, chapter links, appearance links, the preview's "Open →", chips and the portrait tabs. The padding changes the tap area, not the look. Drop the word "LEVEL" from crests and let the numeral stand. Give the two metal ladders, character rank and Art rank, a second channel of a distinct crest shape plus a pip count or numeral, so they never read as one scale, any step outside the familiar metal order stays legible, and both pass a Color Oracle check ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-maker-s-toolkit-explores-how-devs-can-design-for-colorblindness), C, full). Lift the hidden 3.0:1 result label above 4.5:1.

For weight, serve zoomed round portraits from a small pre-cropped copy rather than a 640 px image clipped to a circle, which saves about 250 KB on the Now page and the roster and on each person's preview card. Set the Codex players to `preload="none"`, which removes about 1.6 MB and brings the Codex in line with every other page. Lazy-load every portrait slide after the first while keeping the first visible image eager, give the Wardrobe stage a phone-sized copy, and check that every requested font weight is actually used. Make the Codex enlarge fill the screen and allow pinch-zoom, so an enlargement is at least twice the inline size ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/photos-as-web-content/), Study, full). None of this changes what the site shows; it changes whether a thumb can reach it and what a phone pays for it.

### 4. Write a one-page style guide and a drift check

This comes before the next painting because **inconsistency is what readers notice, and a picture that contradicts the page is worse than none**: AI-illustrated readers spotted the tool through inconsistencies, and incongruous pictures did worse than plain text (CTRL, abstract). The primary sources agree on what a short art guide holds. It opens with a vision line used as a razor, Bourassa's "clearly articulated, externalized creative core" that "serves as a razor to make the hard cuts" ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1023424/A-Torch-in-the-Dark), D, session abstract) and Cammarano's rule that "anything contrary gets thrown away" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/effective-art-directors-gaming-s-something-something), practitioner, full). It keeps one approved target image per picture kind (portrait, 16:9 place, square card, map, full-body look), so a new picture is judged against its target rather than the last picture. It sets a palette by role with one symbolic colour per recurring person ([Game Developer, HoloVista](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/postmortem-aconites-holovista), D, full), a short list of motifs drawn only from what the page has shown, and a few lines per character (age, build, relations, temperament, marks) before any image, the way Pentiment's artists worked from "a short bio explaining their age and build" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/art/deep-dive-the-art-of-pentiment), D, full). It fixes framing (front or three-quarter view, one eye height, one light direction, one compass for places and the map), adds a distance test, since Baldur's Gate 3 found "three patterns which, while distinct up close, looked the same at distance" ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/larians-art-director-on-the-outfits-of-baldurs-gate-3-and-why-making-them-fit-matters-but-the-main-thing-is-looking-cool/), D via C, full), states a change rule that each new Book grows the figure up but never changes the medium, and ends with a near-miss page of his own real failures.

The tools have moved on. As of 17 November 2026 OpenAI recommends GPT-Image-2.6 for new work; it accepts up to 16 input images and has no fidelity dial, because it reads every input at high fidelity, and its guide says to "identify each input by number and purpose", to "say 'change only X' and list the details to preserve", and to chain edits by passing the last output back in ([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/image-prompting), Official, full). The 2026 research shows why chaining needs a limit. One vendor-written paper measured identity similarity falling from about 0.71 to 0.53 after four chained edits, with **full-body framing, where the face is small, the hardest case** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.04151v2), Paper, full); a benchmark found drift at every turn "even when instructed to reproduce the input image without modification" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.35611), Paper, full); and models place the first-mentioned thing on the left and apply vague edits to a default side, the mechanism behind a scar on the wrong side ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.03714), Paper, full). The guide's drift check therefore keeps a master pair (a full-body figure and a face close-up, each with its role stated), starts every new look in a fresh conversation from it, allows two or three narrow refinements at most, names every asymmetric mark with both the character's side and the viewer's side and lists those marks first, keeps hands simple and lettering out of pictures, and compares each new picture with the master, and with a flipped master, at thumbnail size before filing. Midjourney's Edit Model, which replaced Omni Reference in V8 and takes four references ([Midjourney docs](https://docs.midjourney.com/hc/en-us/articles/48495453462797-Edit-Model), Official, summary), is the better tool for a shared painterly style, while ChatGPT stays simpler for Wardrobe looks that need more references. The guide lives in `saga/art/` and describes only what the page has shown, and because **the build checks the file and its gating, never what the picture depicts**, his eye remains the spoiler check.

### 5. Paint the place he stands in

The current place has no picture, which is why the Now page's first screen holds none. One place painting does three jobs, because the builder reuses the file: it is the Now plate's backdrop, the 16:9 card in name previews inside chapters (the main way pictures meet the prose), and the Codex entry. Readers do not build space unprompted (CTRL, abstract), their spatial memory centres on the hero (CTRL, abstract), and attractive images help when they carry what the text says (META via citing full text), so **the current place outranks every other unpainted subject**. Paint a place when he arrives somewhere he will stay, and only once the prose has fixed its look, Failbetter's answer to the "So wait, it's *there*?" problem (D, full). Skip places he only passes through. The 75 Codex "things" can stay text, because recognisable landmarks matter more than paintings of objects (CTRL, abstract) and the drawn sigils already carry identity.

### 6. Make the map the journey's record

The map's structure is right: its route is drawn only as far as he has gone, the lesson of 80 Days' locked routes (D, full) and Fallen London's sea map that "will fill in slowly" (D, full). What fails is the thumb, and three builder changes fix it. **Give each marker an invisible hit area at least 44 px across**, so the 9 to 22 px labels are no longer the only targets. **Put a plain list of visited places under the map**, following NN/g's advice that a list should be the default where maps cause touch errors (Study-based guideline, full), which also works without JavaScript. And **give each visited place one short line quoted from the page**, linked to the scene where it happened, with a recognisable landmark drawn where the route bends, the way Firewatch's protagonist "circles areas of the map that are of importance" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-firewatch-s-ui-enhances-player-immersion), B, full) and the way landmarks solved Failbetter's "featureless fog" (D, full). The build can check each quote word for word against its scene, as it already checks Wardrobe quotes.

### 7. Draw the story's arc: Book colours, choice marks, a closing picture

Books appear only as text lines. **Give each Book one accent colour** when it opens, written as a CSS custom property that tints the chapter-head rule, that Book's segment of the Timeline and its leg of the map route. Failbetter gave each Sunless Skies region "its own distinctive colour palette" for navigation ([Failbetter](https://www.failbettergames.com/news/sunless-skies-pre-production-looking-to-the-skies), D, full), and Pope gave each Obra Dinn chapter its own musical theme as "subtle landmarks to help the player orient themselves" (D, full). Keep the accent off crests and card frames, so colour marks kind and metal marks rank, avoid incidental purple and orange, which gaming readers read as rarity, and check contrast against the night background. **Mark every answered choice on the Timeline** with a fork linked to it, on every one, because once the mark is learned its absence reads as "nothing changed" (Vanaman, D via C, full); only answers the page has shown appear. Give climax rolls a distinct rim, so a once-only roll looks different from an ordinary check without any odds. Last, **paint one closing picture per Book**, once the Book's final climax is on the page, shown at the end of that chapter and as the Book's emblem on the Timeline, with the build refusing it until that scene exists. Readers of the biggest serial maintain one image per chapter page by hand (Docs, full); this is the highest-value picture the site can add, for one painting per Book.

### 8. Tie every visible change to its scene

**Make every visible change a link to the scene that caused it**: each piece's reason on the Wardrobe, each Collection tile, each keepsake, each per-Book portrait tab, and each regard word where the page has shown the change. The 2026 avatar trial found change that arrives gradually, caused by the user's own effort, beat an instant switch on agency (CTRL n=32, full); Failbetter's writers say "when you can see consequence from a character… it immediately makes them more tangible" (D, full); and Mask of the Rose's wardrobe answers in words rather than numbers (D, full), which the site's reasons already do, so the scene link supplies the cause. Add a **"New in the Codex" row at each chapter's foot**, built from the per-chapter records, which is Heaven's Vault's knowledge-tagged timeline made static and the Wandering Inn wiki's "revealed versus obtained" and first-appearance markers (D, full; Docs, full). It sits after the chapter, where extra material does least harm (CTRL, abstract), and lists names rather than trivia.

### 9. Keep the cast pages the same figure, labelled by chapter

Add the chapter number to the fine print, so it reads "Only what the story has shown so far, as of Chapter N", and show a status only once the prose has stated it, a field even the Wandering Inn wiki hides ([TWI wiki](https://wiki.wanderinginn.com/Help:Policies), Docs, full). Show the current Book's portrait large with a row of labelled thumbnails ("Book I", "Book II") for direct access and the next one peeking at the edge, switch to a list beyond five frames, and never auto-advance, since NN/g finds most people stop after three or four carousel frames and that dots go unnoticed ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-carousels/), Study, full). Fold Appearances by chapter as accordions, so a long page shows its structure first ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-accordions/), Study, full). Each new Book portrait changes only what the prose has changed, with the same crop, light and eye-line, the figure grown up rather than redesigned, and its details call back to what the page has shown rather than seeding what it has not.

### 10. At most one optional climax picture, after the scene

If he wants to paint a week's turning point, give it **one optional slot at the end of the climax block, inside its fold**, gated on that scene. The corrected evidence lowers the harm a quiet, matching picture does but keeps the order rule, since material at the start does the most damage (CTRL ×4, abstract) and genre practice puts art after the chapter (Community, summary). It also lowers the expected gain: an illustrated version of a story earned no engagement advantage, and text-only readers felt more (CTRL, abstract), so this stays optional and scarce. Caption each picture with the line it shows, and show no empty frame in weeks without one, because a visible blank reads as a broken streak, which demotivates (CTRL ×7, abstract), and would turn a gift into a debt on his time.

### 11. Disclose plainly, caption with the page's words, add share cards

Add one footer line saying the pictures and songs are made by the author with AI tools from his own designs, and key captions to the prose. In three 2026 experiments the "AI" label lowered ratings of identical paintings **far more under reflective viewing (a mean difference of 0.45) than at a glance (0.11)**, and **brief interpretive text that helps viewers make meaning reduced the penalty** ([Frontiers 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1827956/full), CTRL, Study 3 N=127, full). A caption that quotes or points to the line a picture shows is that cue, and because it repeats the page, it is not the kind of extra text that harms reading. The keying step strips embedded provenance, since "editing, converting, or sharing a file can remove its metadata" ([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/content-provenance), Official, full), so the page has to be the disclosure; a widely cited 2023 preprint claiming disclosure had no effect on short stories has been withdrawn ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06217), Paper, full). Add `og:title`, `og:description` and an `og:image` (the current Book's picture or the painted map) to each page, since none of 17 has them today. The footer wording is his call.

### 12. Narrow the desktop measure; consider a light reading theme

On a phone the prose sits at about 50 characters a line; on a desktop it runs 88 to 93, past the 45 to 75 range typographers usually recommend (general guidance, not re-checked this pass), and a maximum width on the prose column fixes that without touching the phone. The dark-only theme is a deliberate identity, "a forge at night", and the evidence leans the other way only modestly. Light mode won for acuity and proofreading at all ages, and in one study at night as well; NN/g recommends not making dark the default for a general audience but letting users switch ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/dark-mode/), Study review, full); and users think about the setting "at the system level, not the application level", splitting about a third each between dark, light and both ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/dark-mode-users-issues/), Study, full). A light reading theme for chapter and tale text that follows the phone's own setting needs only CSS. Whether the forge identity is worth more than that modest edge is his call. Either way, keep the blackletter for titles, where it is now.

### What to keep, and what the evidence says not to add

Several things are already right. The tap-to-play songs beside the lines that sing them, and the build's lyric check, match the evidence. So do the gating of pictures on the page, other people's sheets that stay unread until the story lets him read them, the dice that never show odds before a roll and keep their arithmetic in folds, and the Wardrobe's greyed pieces that name what opens them, which are resumption devices with a resolution in sight. The route drawn only as far as he has gone, the blackletter kept to titles, folds that work without JavaScript, and phone prose at a comfortable size and measure all belong on that list too.

| Do not add | Why |
|---|---|
| Autoplaying songs under the text | Lyrics cost comprehension; browsers block it anyway |
| A day streak or "days so far" counter | Visible counters drive minimal keep-alive actions |
| Odds before a roll | Players read 85% as certain |
| Empty frames or locked picture slots | Blanks read as broken streaks |
| Lettering inside pictures | Models still misplace text; HTML stays readable |
| More stat blocks inside chapters | Each change in a string matters less |
| Games, hotspots or timed reveals in the text | Interactive extras pulled readers off the story |
| Roads ahead drawn on the map | Showing every route made players stop reading |
| An idealised body, or a pose ahead of the Knots | Idealised avatars lowered effort in some studies |
| Clue-carrying details in portraits | Pictures follow the page; use call-backs |

## Conclusion

The live audit moves the problem from *what* is drawn to *where it sits in the scroll*. The Chronicle already has the devices the evidence favours (a route that follows the hero, portraits that change by Book, previews beside the prose, songs beside their lines), but on the most-visited page game news and arithmetic come first and the map comes third. The deeper evidence moves the risk the same way: it lies less in quiet pictures than in pictures that disagree with the page, in extra words inside the reading, and in counters that reward keeping a number moving. That makes the cheapest changes the most valuable. Reordering, folding, padding and trimming bytes cost him nothing and change what every visit shows, and the next most valuable thing is a page of art rules, because consistency, not quantity, is what turns a picture from a mismatch into a memory aid.

For a reader who is also the author and the hero's real-life counterpart, the research cannot say whether folding the arithmetic will deepen the story or simply hide the sheet, and the site cannot measure it either, because it is static and has no analytics. The test is his own. If, after a Book with the countdowns folded and the place painted, he opens the folds out of curiosity rather than duty and remembers chapters by their places and choices rather than their numbers, the change worked; if he never opens them, the numbers were noise; and if he opens them every day, they were the game he wanted, and the fold costs him one tap.
