# Make Every Picture Confirm the Page

Across games, LitRPG, webtoons, web serials, interactive fiction and visual novels, and in the cognition research behind them, one result keeps returning: visuals deepen a reader's grasp of a story when they **carry something the words have already put on the page** (a face, a place, the road walked, what has been learned) and sit next to the words they belong to, and they add little or do harm when they decorate a key beat, get ahead of the prose, or turn reading into bookkeeping. The best controlled evidence comes from learning research rather than fiction, where pictures placed beside their text help (**g = 0.63**) and interesting-but-irrelevant ones cost a little (**g = −0.16**); the one adult experiment with an illustrated story found that pictures made it easier to imagine and more engaging. The strongest game and serial examples, from Heaven's Vault's timeline to 80 Days' lit routes and Crusader Kings' ageing faces, all record what the player has already seen, which is the rule the Chronicle's site already enforces. Motivation research adds a warning this site needs more than it needs more art: counting visible output raises effort but lowers enjoyment, and an avatar a person designs himself raises identification while an idealised one lowers effort. The site today has these priorities backwards, with its richest visuals encoding the mechanics while chapters, Books, choices and 12 of 17 places carry no picture. In priority order, the fix is to fold the sheet's countdowns behind a tap; draw the story's arc with a colour for each Book, its choices marked on the Timeline and one closing picture per Book; paint the place he is standing in, from a written style guide; make the map a record of the journey that works under a thumb; and spend any further paintings on at most one optional picture per climax, placed after the scene. Most of this is builder work and costs him no time. Treat it as well argued rather than proven: almost every source was read through search summaries because full-page fetches were blocked, and no controlled study tests illustrations, maps or hidden mechanics in long serial fiction.

## Six sets of notes, mostly read through search summaries

This report draws on six sets of research notes compiled on 17 November 2026. The first is an inventory of the Chronicle's public site, built by reading its builder, stylesheet, scripts, art folder and reader-safe registries. The other five review research and practice: cognition and motivation, narrative game interfaces, LitRPG and webtoon conventions, web fiction with interactive fiction and visual novels, and art direction with AI image tools. **Nearly every outside source was read only through search-engine summaries, abstracts or press releases, because the session's network blocked full-page fetches** from academic hosts, the games press, Royal Road, Goodreads, the Wandering Inn wiki and the image vendors' documentation. Quoted wording is therefore as those summaries gave it. Several effect sizes could not be checked against the papers, including the pooled seductive-details effect and the sample sizes in the two avatar-exercise studies, and a few designer quotes reached the researchers only through mirror sites. The overall shape of the evidence matters more than any single grade. Controlled research is plentiful on pictures in teaching material, on avatars and on progress displays, and almost absent on fiction: **the researchers found no controlled study of illustrated long-form adult fiction read over weeks, of fantasy maps, of timelines or relationship diagrams for fiction readers, of hidden versus visible game mechanics, or of readers who are also a story's author and its hero's real-life counterpart**. The game, serial and visual-novel evidence is mostly what designers intended and how critics received it. The sections below take positions anyway, because these separate bodies of evidence point the same way, and they flag where a recommendation rests on analogy alone.

## Pictures help when they carry what the page has already said

### Placement matters more than polish

Learning research sorts pictures by what they do. Levin, Anglin and Carney classed text illustrations as decorative, representational, organisational, interpretational or transformational, and every kind except the decorative one went with better learning from the text ([Carney & Levin 2002](https://www.doi.org/10.1023/A:1013176309260), META and REVIEW, mostly school-age readers in 1970s and 1980s studies). The opposite case, decoration that distracts, is better measured. **A meta-analysis of 58 studies and more than 7,500 students found that interesting but irrelevant "seductive details" lowered learning**, most when the detail sat beside relevant diagrams and stayed static on the page ([WSU](https://news.wsu.edu/press-release/2020/03/19/seductive-details-inhibit-learning), META). A newer analysis of 50 studies puts the overall effect at **g = −0.16** ([AERA 2026](https://convention2.allacademic.com/one/aera/aera26/online_program_direct_link/view_paper/2273554/), META, conference abstract), and in Harp and Mayer's experiment **moving the details to the end of the lesson removed the harm** ([summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seductive_details), CTRL). Placement is the strongest single lever. Putting words next to the picture they describe yields **g = 0.63 across 58 comparisons** ([Schroeder & Cenkci, TUM summary](https://www.clearinghouse.edu.tum.de/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/CHU_KR-23_Schroeder-2018_Lernmaterial.pdf), META), signalling what matters raises retention at g = 0.53 ([Schneider et al., lecture summary](https://ksvi.mff.cuni.cz/amulab/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/MultimLn3_BromHannemann_181117.pdf), META), and pictures are simply remembered, with people recognising about 83% of 10,000 pictures in a two-choice test ([Standing 1973](https://www.sas.upenn.edu/psych/rust-lab/publications/standing%5F73.pdf), CTRL). All of this measures learning from lessons, not pleasure in a story. Decoration on a story page is therefore low in benefit and small in risk rather than a proven harm, and the configuration with the clearest harm signal is **a striking still image beside the text of a key beat**.

### Adult readers imagine more easily with pictures, and order matters

Direct evidence on adult fiction is thin, and it runs against the folk worry that pictures crowd out imagination. **The only adult experiment with an illustrated story found that images made the story easier to picture, which raised engagement and reduced counterarguing**, with ease of imagining the largest path ([RTI](https://www.rti.org/publication/seeing-believing), CTRL n=212). A study of 52 adults found no comprehension gain from visual support, but readers preferred the illustrated versions ([Cambridge Core](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/3100975690CF0E5579299B5CCFA11716/core-reader), CTRL n=52). The crowding-out worry comes from writing about children and from the humanities, not from adult experiments. What the adult evidence does show is that the order of exposure matters. People who read a novel before seeing its film were most absorbed by the film, and a tendency toward vivid imagery predicted absorption for readers but not for viewers ([Green et al. 2008](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/abs/10.1080/15213260802492000), CTRL). Readers with no visual imagery report less absorption and emotional involvement but equal enjoyment ([Radboud](https://www.ru.nl/en/research/research-news/lack-of-visual-imagery-does-not-lead-to-less-pleasure-in-reading), CTRL n=98), and once casting fixes a face, readers report it is hard to un-see ([Dalhousie](https://ojs.library.dal.ca/djim/article/view/2010vol6Martin/53), OPINION). The defensible rule is **text first, picture after**: let the prose establish a person or place, let the picture confirm it, and keep pictures one tap away rather than forced into the reading.

### Genre practice spends pictures on peaks, after the scene

Genre practice reaches the same rule from the other direction. Light novels open on full-colour plates, and the complaint most often raised about them is that they spoil; in an informal poll a minority of readers wanted the plates moved to the back, and spoilers were named as the main risk of front placement ([English Light Novels](https://www.englishlightnovels.com/2021/03/16/a-writers-reflections-on-light-novel-inserts/), poll; [J-Novel Club forum](https://forums.j-novel.club/topic/2817/coloured-illustrations), reader anecdote). Visual novels reserve the expensive full-scene "event CG" for dramatic peaks, and the last CG of a route "often becomes the reader's lasting memory of it" ([Fuwanovel](https://forums.fuwanovel.moe/blogs/entry/4209-event-cgs-%E2%80%93-an-anatomy-of-visual-novels), practitioner essay), while developers warn that writers forget to count CG moments in a script "and end up with 30 of them" ([LemmaSoft](https://lemmasoft.renai.us/forums/viewtopic.php?p=434061), Community). Painted games ration art the same way. One wide travel vista in The Banner Saga could take "a couple weeks, easily" ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/the-banner-saga-background-artist-interview), D), and Failbetter kept Sunless Sea's art "swiftly rendered in a naturalist's notebook", with a camera far enough away to leave room for the prose ([Failbetter](https://www.failbettergames.com/caverns-measureless-to-man-or-sunless-sea-art-direction/), Designer). Webtoon adaptations show the price of a fixed image, since a drawn character "sticks in a reader's mind far longer than prose descriptions alone" ([Tabstory](https://www.tabstory.net/blog/how-to-turn-a-novel-into-a-webtoon), practitioner opinion); when one adaptation's live-action cast appeared, a fan wrote, "That is not the Yoo Joong Hyuk I imagined" ([allkpop](https://www.allkpop.com/article/2025/02/netizens-compare-the-synchronization-of-the-actors-to-the-web-novel-webtoon-characters-of-the-omniscient-reader-after-its-teaser-release), fan reaction). The strongest counterexample is Dickens, whose monthly parts carried **two etchings tipped in ahead of the text** ([Victorian Web](https://victorianweb.org/victorian/art/illustration/phiz/2cities/pva104.html), scholarly). But readers bought each part whole, and a serial read block by block as it is written is closer to the light-novel case, where a plate at the head shows what the reader has not yet reached.

### Maps help when they follow the hero

Readers build "situation models" of a story, indexed on time, space, characters, causes and goals ([Erasmus summary](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/120435/Repub_120435_O-A.pdf), THEORY and REVIEW). Their spatial memory centres on the protagonist: objects are most accessible in his current room, and the effect appears only when readers are tracking him ([Memory & Cognition 2013](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13421-013-0329-2), CTRL, multiple studies). Studying a map alongside a text improves recall of the text ([Arizona Regents record](https://experts.azregents.edu/en/publications/differing-map-construction-and-text-organization-and-their-effect/), CTRL), though Griffin and Robinson argue that **recognisable pictures of the features, not the spatial layout, do the work** ([Georgia Southern](https://scholars.georgiasouthern.edu/en/publications/does-spatial-or-visual-information-in-maps-facilitate-text-recall/), CTRL). Maps are also less universal than assumed, appearing in **only 34% of the 200 fantasy books in Ekman's sample** ([Map Room review](https://www.maproomblog.com/2013/06/review-here-be-dragons/), Study). A map therefore earns its place by following the hero's journey, with icons that look like the places, rather than by surveying the world. inkle reached the same conclusion when it moved away from an early version of 80 Days that showed almost every route on its globe at once ([Game Developer postmortem](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/postmortem-inkle-s-i-80-days-i-), Designer).

### Songs belong beside the lines they sing, never under the reading

Audio splits along the same line. **Background music with lyrics measurably, if mildly, impairs reading comprehension**: a Bayesian meta-analysis found lyrical music far more distracting than instrumental music ([PubMed](https://www.pubmed.gov/29958067/), Study), and a self-paced reading study found that instrumental music did not distract at all ([Journal of Cognition](https://journalofcognition.org/articles/273), Study). Sound effects and spatial cues raise mental imagery and absorption when listening is itself the activity ([Rodero & Romero 2022](https://doi.org/10.1080/15213269.2021.1880439), Study). By contrast, the soundtracked-ebook gains Booktrack reported came from a study the company commissioned ([EdWeek](https://www.edweek.org/teaching-learning/app-aims-to-boost-comprehension-by-putting-e-books-to-music/2014/07), Press). Visual novels keep their songs in an opt-in "Music Room" beside the picture gallery ([Ren'Py docs](https://doc-renpy-ru.vercel.app/rooms.html), Docs). The Chronicle already follows this pattern. A tap-to-play button sits beside the line that sings a song, the Codex works as the music room, and the build refuses any MP3 whose embedded lyrics hold a line the page has not sung ([build_site.py](../engine/build_site.py)). One question is untested, because every distraction study used unrelated lyrics and none measured reading words while hearing those same words sung.

## The best game and serial visuals record what the reader has already learned

### Knowledge screens write only what has been witnessed

The lore screens players praise share one habit: **they write only what the player has witnessed, at the moment of witnessing**. For each object found, Heaven's Vault "drops a short text box onto a timeline", announced by "an unobtrusive pop-up" ([GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/au/heavens-vault-review), C), and Kotaku said the history would be "tough to keep straight in your head without this tool" ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/heavens-vault-the-kotaku-review-1834082676), C). Outer Wilds has no upgrades, so knowledge is the only progression ([AV Club](https://www.avclub.com/outer-wilds-and-inner-depth-making-something-new-o), D), and its ship log draws "a visual graph of partial knowledge" ([Birdor](https://birdor-blog.pages.dev/outer-wilds-knowledge-progression-product-case-study/), W). Return of the Obra Dinn keeps faces blurred in its logbook until there is enough evidence to name them ([Archania](https://archania.org/p/the-symbolic-world/symbolic-works/video-games/return-of-the-obra-dinn), W). In Pentiment a character's speech can change font as the hero learns who they are, and the designers kept the fonts "only ever offering redundant information", so an easy-reading mode loses no story ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/how-pentiments-hand-crafted-fonts-give-pen-and-ink-a-voice), D). The Chronicle's rule that "a picture may show only what the page has already put on the page" ([saga/art/README.md](../saga/art/README.md)) is the same principle. The site already makes the Obra Dinn move: another person's Reckoning reads "⟦ unread ⟧" until the Art that lets him read people has reached it.

### Growth reads best as a change on the same figure

Growth reads most strongly as **a change on the same figure, caused by an event the player remembers**. Crusader Kings III ages one face across decades, adding lines and grey through a character's thirties and forties ([Paradox dev diary](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/threads/ck3-dev-diary-34-its-all-about-appearances.1406933/page-4), D). Mass Effect 2 worsens or heals Shepard's scars with the morality meters ([Fextralife](https://masseffect2.wiki.fextralife.com/Character+Creation), W), and players built mods to choose or remove them ([Nexus Mods](https://www.nexusmods.com/masseffectlegendaryedition/mods/210), F), which suggests a visible change driven by a hidden meter feels imposed rather than earned. Wildermyth pairs each permanent transformation with a comic-panel scene of the event that caused it, and those comics carry most of its story ([Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/wildermyth-review/), C). Costume designers say the same of clothes. "Costume Design is more than storytelling, it is the story," in Deborah Nadoolman Landis's words ([Daily Bruin](https://dailybruin.com/2023/03/12/oscar-nominees-express-importance-of-costume-sketch-to-screen-costume-design-panel), Primary), and Michele Clapton gave the reunited Starks "something they have taken from their parents in the shape of a dress, the cut of a cape or the quilting details" ([Watchers on the Wall](https://watchersonthewall.com/michele-clapton-talks-dressing-the-reunited-starks-and-indian-company-discusses-replicating-westeros), Primary via press).

### The best maps are records of the journey

The narrative maps that work are records of the journey. Players of 80 Days "uncover a map that lights up as you explore it" ([Cubed3](https://www.cubed3.com/games/reviews/pc/80-days), C), though they also complained that city names were unreadable unless you zoomed right in ([Quarter to Three](https://forum.quartertothree.com/t/80-days-thread/76165), F). Hollow Knight's maps update only when the player rests at a bench ([Hollow Knight wiki](https://hollowknight.wiki/w/Map), W), and Firewatch's paper map collects notes in the hero's own hand, in a tone that follows how the player plays him ([First Person Scholar](https://www.firstpersonscholar.com/environment-as-narratives/), C/D). Readers of long series value journey lines they can follow through time, and the fan map Quartermaester serves that with a slider that animates characters' paths and an option to show current locations only ([Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/this-spoiler-filled-map-tracks-the-travels-of-every-maj-5917835), Community/Press). Even the palette has precedent. Tolkien wanted four colours for his Hobbit maps and his publisher judged it too costly, so **a deliberately small palette is a fantasy-map convention** ([Frumious Consortium](https://thefrumiousconsortium.net/2018/09/04/tolkien-maker-of-middle-earth), Secondary).

### Reference stays spoiler-safe by filtering, or by staying small

Reference material sits on a spectrum from warning to filtering. Fan wikis mostly warn. The Wandering Inn wiki hides character status in its infoboxes yet "cannot guarantee a reader will not be spoiled" ([TWI wiki policies](https://wiki.wanderinginn.com/Help:Policies), Community/Docs), the Red vs. Blue wiki holds back a status field until an episode is public ([RvB wiki](https://rvb.fandom.com/wiki/Red_vs._Blue_Wiki:_Spoiler_Policy), wiki policy), and the Malazan wiki separates "spoiler-free" from "spoiler-friendly" ([Malazan wiki](https://malazan.fandom.com/f/p/2607692262670399645), wiki policy). A few tools truly filter by the reader's position, such as Coppermind's Time Machine, which shows a page as it stood on a chosen date ([17th Shard](https://www.17thshard.com/news/sitenews/you-can-now-visit-previous-versions-of-coppermind-pages-with-time-machine-r665/), Docs), and Kindle's "Story So Far", which summarises only up to the point the reader has reached ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/06/11/amazons-story-so-far-feature-rolling-out-to-us-kindles-and-iphone-app/), Press). Previews win when they stay small. Wikipedia's hover previews were turned off by **about 0.01% of readers**, and readers reached more distinct pages with them ([MediaWiki](https://www.mediawiki.org/wiki/Page_Previews), Data), while popups big enough to "completely block whatever it is I'm reading" pushed LessWrong toward smaller ones ([LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/tmdRw8h6hm7rCsfiu/new-feature-link-previews), Community). The Chronicle's name previews (a face crop or a 16:9 picture, a title and one line) are already that size. Its character and Codex pages show the current state, "spoiler-friendly" in the Malazan sense, which suits a reader who is always caught up.

### Colour can carry an arc

Colour can carry an arc across a whole work. Journey's palette shifts along "a curve established by the artists that is reflective of the narrative" ([CGW](https://www.cgw.com/Publications/CGW/2012/Volume-35-Issue-4-June-July-2012/Mysterious-Journey.aspx), D). Gris restores its colours stage by stage as its heroine recovers ([Gamereactor](https://www.gamereactor.eu/what-does-gris-mean-we-talk-to-nomada-studio/), D), and a Pixar colour script "covers the emotions of the film in color and light" ([AWN, Ralph Eggleston](https://www.awn.com/animationworld/ralph-eggleston-talks-inside-out), Primary). No source covers colour scripts across a multi-volume series, so carrying the idea to the level of a Book is inference.

## Visible progress motivates, but counting turns play into work

### LitRPG readers want the history, not the exchange rates

The genre's own craft writing says stat blocks should be rare, short and kept for the moments that matter. The typical block "is a receipt for something the prose has already proved", and dense ones "have trained readers to skip" ([Iloris](https://www.ilorisnovel.com/articles/stat-blocks-in-litrpg-when-they-work), craft opinion). Readers skim routine notices and slow down only for level-ups and new skills ([Iloris](https://www.ilorisnovel.com/articles/how-to-write-litrpg-system-messages), craft opinion), and the status window's real appeal is fairness, the reader watching effort turn into visible progress ([Stelland](https://stelland.medium.com/why-do-so-many-korean-webnovels-begin-with-status-windows-8e03f40dc5c0), craft opinion). One first-time reader gave up on a series that "leaned toward numbers over characters" ([Kessler](https://elliotkessler.substack.com/p/the-iron-tyrant-litrpg-review), reader anecdote). What readers build for themselves is the telling evidence: the Wandering Inn wiki keeps a chapter-by-chapter "Leveling History" for each major character ([TWI wiki](https://wiki.wanderinginn.com/Pisces_Jealnet/Leveling_History), fan wiki). **Readers want the history and the state, linked to scenes, not the exchange rates.**

### Progress displays raise effort and can drain the pleasure

Progress displays work. Café customers bought faster as they neared a loyalty reward ([Columbia Business School](https://business.columbia.edu/faculty/research/goal-gradient-hypothesis-resurrected-purchase-acceleration-illusionary-goal), CTRL, field). A card with two stamps already filled was completed 34% of the time, against 19% for a blank card needing the same purchases ([Coglode](https://www.coglode.com/nuggets/endowed-progress-effect), CTRL, field, secondary). An intact streak raises engagement, while **a broken streak demotivates most when people blame themselves for the break** ([University of Colorado](https://www.colorado.edu/business/faculty-research/2023/04/19/or-track-how-broken-streaks-affect-consumer-decisions), CTRL ×7). The costs are as well documented. Across 128 experiments, expected rewards that depended on performance undermined free-choice intrinsic motivation, while **positive, informational feedback enhanced it (d = 0.33)** ([Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999](https://depts.washington.edu/techdocs/papers/deciExtrinsicRewardsAndIntrinsicMotivation99.pdf), META k=128). Intrinsic motivation predicts the quality of work and incentives predict its quantity ([Cerasoli et al. 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24491020/), META k=183). Points and levels raised output without raising intrinsic motivation ([Mekler et al. 2017](https://glassmanlab.seas.harvard.edu/annotated_works/mekler17.pdf), CTRL), and a course gamified with a leaderboard and badges saw intrinsic motivation decline over the term ([Hanus & Fox 2015](https://www.smhp.psych.ucla.edu/pdfdocs/gamil.pdf), CTRL, quasi-experimental). The sharpest finding is that **measuring output made people do more but enjoy it less**. In one reading study, people who saw their progress read more pages but were less likely to keep reading afterwards, and framing the activity around learning rather than fun removed the penalty ([Duke Fuqua on Etkin 2016](https://www.fuqua.duke.edu/duke-fuqua-insights/etkin-counting-steps), CTRL ×6). In randomised trials of gamified physical activity, the effect was g = 0.42 overall, shrank to g = 0.23 against an active, non-gamified programme, and fell to g = 0.15 about fourteen weeks after the programmes ended ([Mazeas et al. 2022](https://jmir.org/2022/1/e26779), META of RCTs). **Game fiction, the story wrapper, was among the few elements that moderated behavioural outcomes** ([Sailer & Homner 2020](https://opus.bibliothek.uni-augsburg.de/opus4/frontdoor/index/index/docId/109056), META), while gamified rehabilitation has so far drawn only narrative and scoping reviews ([JMIR Serious Games](https://games.jmir.org/2020/3/e19914), REVIEW). The reading is plain: the story is the part with support, and the visible countdown is the part with risk. An "almost there" bar also speeds effort near its threshold, which matters when real effort drives it, and the engine's rule that doing more earns nothing ([CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md)) blunts that pull.

### No study settles hidden versus visible, so split the difference

**No controlled study compares hidden with visible mechanics.** The case for discovery rests on adjacent findings. Curiosity peaks at intermediate confidence and improves memory for the answer ([Kang et al. 2009](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/sds/docs/loewenstein/WickCandleLearning.pdf), CTRL n=19 plus a replication), and people work harder for an uncertain reward than for a certain, larger one when their attention is on the process ([Shen, Fishbach & Hsee](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:jconrs:doi:10.1086/679418), CTRL ×4). The case for visibility is real too. Interactive-fiction readers value stat sheets as a memory aid, "instead of trying to remember a choice you made (possibly days ago)" ([Choice of Games forum](https://forum.choiceofgames.com/t/what-do-you-like-to-see-on-a-stat-screen/13510), Community). Baldur's Gate 3's hidden approval had to be retuned after companions fell for the player too fast ([GosuGamers](https://gosugamers.net/news/69288-baldurs-gate-3s-thirsty-companions-were-a-bug-not-a-feature), D, reported), and Jon Ingold warns that players assume a game is not responding to them, so it must prove quickly that it noticed ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/-t-m-i-preserving-the-suspension-of-disbelief-through-ambiguity), D). The interface research is narrow. Removing on-screen data raised involvement only **in expert players** ([Open University](https://oro.open.ac.uk/46759), S), and VR players preferred an in-world interface without any measurable gain in presence ([Eurographics](https://diglib7.eg.org/bitstream/handle/10.2312/egve20211333/001-002.pdf), S). The synthesis is a split: **show that a deed counted and what changed, in words and metal, and keep how much and how close one tap away**. Dice follow the same logic. Odds shown before a roll get treated as promises: Jake Solomon found players read 85% as a sure hit ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/jake-solomon-explains-the-careful-use-of-randomness-in-xcom-2-i-), D), and Sid Meier found players felt cheated by losses at 3-to-1 ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1012186/The-Psychology-of-Game-Design), D). Disco Elysium marks stakes without numbers by separating retryable white checks from one-shot red ones ([RPG Codex](https://rpgcodex.net/article.php?id=11127), D/W), and Baldur's Gate 3's long, floaty dice animation drew criticism ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/baldurs-gate-3-larian-rpg-pc-steam-dice-rolls-d20-1850729004), C).

### The Wardrobe stands on the best evidence in the field

The avatar research is the best evidence behind any surface on the site. The Proteus effect, people acting like their avatar, holds across 46 experiments at about **r = .24** ([Ratan et al. 2020](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/full/10.1080/15213269.2019.1623698), META k=46). In three VR experiments, a self-resembling avatar whose body changed with the person's own exercise, or who was seen running, led to more exercise, in the lab and in self-reports over the following day ([Stanford VHIL](https://vhil.stanford.edu/publications/medical/virtual-self-modeling-effects-vicarious-reinforcement-and-identification), CTRL ×3). Designing one's own avatar raised identification and intrinsic motivation ([Birk et al. 2016](https://researchr.org/publication/BirkABM16), CTRL), and cut drop-out in a three-week field programme ([JMIR 2019](https://jmir.org/article/export/end/jmir_v21i1e10133), CTRL, field). The warning is idealisation. **In VR cycling against one's own avatar, idealised avatars raised wishful identification but lowered performance**, and riders went about 3% faster against a realistic self ([University of Bath](https://www.bath.ac.uk/announcements/buffed-up-avatars-deter-us-from-exercising-hard/), CTRL). Few studies compared an avatar with no avatar at all ([MSU review](https://innovationcenter.msu.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/Enhancing-Healthy-Behaviors-Through-Virtual-Self-A-Systematic-Review-of-Health-Interventions-Using-Avatars.pdf), REVIEW), and a fantasy knight on a phone is a weaker version of a photo-real self in VR, so the effect here will be smaller. The implication is still clear. **His authorship of the look is the active ingredient**, gear can be aspirational, and the body's capability must track reality. The Wardrobe already secures that last point by gating poses on the Knots, so that "a look never shows the leg doing what the story has not let it" ([wardrobe.md](../engine/wardrobe.md)).

### Rank ladders read best in two channels

Rank ladders have conventions of their own. The rarity code gaming readers carry runs grey, white, green, blue, purple, orange ([TV Tropes](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/ColorCodedItemTiers), fan wiki/reference). Metal and gem ladders such as Cradle's ([Cradle RPG wiki](https://cradle-rpg.wikidot.com/advancement), fan wiki) become legible once a story teaches them, and only the order "gold beats iron" needs no teaching. Accessible practice pairs colour with a second channel, a numeral or a star ([gacha-hub](https://github.com/adam-riffi/gacha-hub/pull/416), fan project). Persona 5 guards its signature red by allowing no other accent colours outside its health and magic bars, though its director admits the early menus were "impossible to read at first" ([Siliconera](https://www.siliconera.com/?p=668266), D; [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/rpg/persona-series-director-says-atluss-flashy-menus-are-actually-really-annoying-to-design), D). Heraldry's rule of tincture, never metal on metal, is a contrast rule first set down in 1568 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rule_of_tincture), Secondary).

## Ten changes for the Chronicle, ranked by value per hour of his time

The ranking weighs four things: how strong the evidence is, how large the gap is on today's site, how often a reader meets the surface, and how much of his time the change takes. Builder, stylesheet and script work is Claude's and costs him nothing, while every painting costs his own hours. Every item was checked against the site's hard limits ([build_site.py](../engine/build_site.py); [.claude/rules/chronicle.md](../.claude/rules/chronicle.md); [CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md)). The site stays a standard-library Python build of plain HTML, one stylesheet and a little script that works with JavaScript off and loads nothing external except Google Fonts. It is built from `saga/` alone, with no real-world terms and no GM text. Any new picture kind is gated on an on-page key, the way the Wardrobe's `page` unlocks are. Approval and Bearing never appear as numbers. And it respects his wish to discover the mechanics rather than study them.

### 1. Fold the countdowns and keep the metal

Darrow's page opens on the arithmetic: rank and XP as a pair of numbers, an Ember figure, a "temper … · next at …" line on each attribute tile, and inside the Arts panel a "next at … (… so far)" line on each row ([build_site.py](../engine/build_site.py)). The page is his sheet, but it is also the site's main picture of him, so **simply visiting it now means studying the exchange rates**, the opposite of the wish recorded in [CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md). The evidence favours a split over wholesale hiding. Informational feedback that affirms competence raises interest (Deci et al., META), while displays that count output raise effort and lower both enjoyment and the urge to come back (Etkin, CTRL ×6). Keep in the open everything that says a deed counted and what changed: the metal of crest and frames, the medallion arcs, the rope and its Knots, the Ember brazier and the words. Move every countdown, threshold and "so far" tally behind a closed `<details>` fold, the pattern the dice already use for their arithmetic. **Opening the fold then becomes the act of asking**, which squares the page with the rule that `/sheet` is asking. Keep the medallion arc measured against the Knight Who Fell especially prominent, because it is the Crusader Kings or Mass Effect change on the same figure, told in the story's own terms. The change costs him nothing, works without JavaScript, touches no real data, and takes the goal gradient's "almost there" pull out of the default view.

### 2. Draw the story's arc, not only the mechanics'

Books, the story's largest unit, appear only as text lines, and nothing marks his choices except a single "last answered choice" ([build_site.py](../engine/build_site.py)). Three moves fix this, and two of them cost nothing. The first is **a colour per Book**. When a Book opens, a reader-safe field gives it one accent, and the builder writes it as CSS custom properties on that Book's pages, tinting the chapter-head rule, the Book's segment of the Timeline rail and its leg of the route on the map. The colours are planned GM-side like a Pixar colour script and released one Book at a time, so no colour announces a Book before it opens; the Gris model of colour returning as the heroine recovers fits a story about getting back what was lost. The accent must stay a wash rather than become a new rank colour. Keep it out of crests and card frames, following Persona's lesson of one accent family for the game layer; avoid incidental purple and orange, which gaming readers read as rarity; and check its contrast against the night background. The second move is to **mark the choices on the Timeline** with a fork glyph on each chapter knot that ended in an answered choice, linked to it, so the site visibly shows that it noticed (Ingold, D). Only answers the page has shown appear, and consequences stay GM-side. The third is **one closing picture per Book**, painted once the Book's last climax is on the page and shown at the end of that chapter and as the Book's emblem on the rail. A visual novel's final CG becomes the reader's lasting memory of a route (practitioner essay), and one fantasy novel printed its map on the endpapers to be returned to "once finishing the book" ([Tor.com](https://tor.com/2022/08/25/fantasy-mapmaking-for-the-spear-cuts-through-water), Designer/author). **One painting per Book is the highest-value picture the site can add for the least of his time.** The build should refuse it until its climax scene is on the page. Because the rail already shows each closed chapter's tier seal, the emblem adds little spoiler risk for anyone reading from the beginning.

### 3. Paint the place he is standing in, from a one-page style guide

**Twelve of 17 places, the current one included, have no picture**, so the Now page usually opens with no sense of where he is ([places.json](../saga/state/places.json); [build_site.py](../engine/build_site.py)). A place picture is the hardest-working image the site has, because the builder uses the same file in three places. It is the Now plate's backdrop. It is the 16:9 card in a name preview inside chapters, which is the one way pictures currently meet the prose (and placement beside the text is the best-supported rule in this report). And it is the Codex entry. Readers' spatial memory centres on where the hero is (CTRL), so **the current place outranks every other unpainted subject**. Paint a place when he arrives somewhere he will stay and skip the places he only passes through; factions keep their drawn sigils, and Codex things stay text.

Before the next painting, write the style guide that only looks have today. The Forge carries a prompt template and two reference images, but portraits, places, Art cards and the map have no style anchor, so their consistency rests on his memory ([wardrobe.md](../engine/wardrobe.md); [saga/art/README.md](../saga/art/README.md)). The guide fits on a page. It names one painterly lineage, stated word for word in every prompt, the way Darkest Dungeon and The Banner Saga each held mixed art together by committing to one tradition ([GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/the-gothic-sensibilities-of-darkest-dungeon/1100-6424880/), Primary; [Pocket Gamer](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/feature/60429/the-making-of-the-banner-saga/), Primary). It sets rules per picture type: one light direction, crop line and eye-line for portraits; a horizon and time-of-day rule for places, with people small or absent so that no unrevealed figure slips in; a single action that reads in greyscale at thumbnail size for Art cards; and a fixed camera height for looks. It holds an identity block for each recurring face, pasted verbatim, naming every asymmetric mark with its side and only as the page has described it. And it keeps a "near-miss" page built from his own real failures ([Bugnet](https://bugnet.io/blog/how-to-write-an-art-style-guide), Weak). Pair the guide with the habits the vendors converge on. Repeat the appearance constraints in words every time ([OpenAI prompting guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/image-prompting), Official); go back to the last clean version rather than keep editing a drifted one ([aireiter](https://aireiter.com/blog/how-to-use-chatgpt-images-2-5-workflow), Weak); and start a fresh conversation when features drift ([Google Developers Blog](https://developers.googleblog.com/how-to-prompt-gemini-2-5-flash-image-generation-for-the-best-results/), Official). Keep chained edits short, since the Kontext paper's own failure case shows visible artefacts after six edits ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.15742), Paper), and colour-correct ChatGPT's warm cast afterwards rather than by re-prompting ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/ai-platforms-assistants/chatgpt/tired-of-that-yellow-tint-in-chatgpt-images-heres-how-to-fix-it-with-simple-color-correction), Secondary). Expect fine details to drift first: Midjourney's documentation concedes that freckles and logos "may not perfectly match" ([Midjourney docs](https://docs.midjourney.com/hc/en-us/articles/36285124473997-Omni-Reference), Official). The guide lives in `saga/art/`, so it may describe only what the page has shown. Every picture still needs his eye for spoilers and stray real-world objects, because **the build checks the file and its gating, never what the picture depicts**.

### 4. Make the map the journey's own record

The map already does the best-supported thing. Its ember route is drawn leg by leg only as far as he has gone, its markers stay grey until he visits and turn brass after, and earlier Books' legs are drawn fainter. It is the nearest web form of 80 Days' lit routes. Its weakness is the thumb: on a phone the place labels render at about 9 px, and **those labels are the only links to the places** ([site.css](../engine/site/site.css)). Three changes fix this at no cost to him. First, give each marker a transparent hit area of at least 44 px; WCAG sets 24 px as the minimum and 44 px as the comfortable target ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html), Spec). Second, put a plain list of visited places under the map. That is the text equivalent accessibility guidance asks of maps and other complex images ([W3C WAI](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/images/decision-tree/), Official; [Stanford](https://sitesuserguide.stanford.edu/build/media-library/alternative-text-alt-text-images-and-other-media), institutional), and it also works with JavaScript off. Third, give each visited place one short line from the page, opened in a small card rather than lettered onto the painting, and linked to the scene where it happened. That is Firewatch's map annotated in the hero's own hand, without 80 Days' unreadable labels. The build can check each quote word for word against its scene, as it already does for Wardrobe quotes. It already refuses a route that does not end where Darrow stands, so destinations never get drawn in.

### 5. Spend further paintings on climaxes, after the scene

Chapters, the pages he reads every week, have no image slot at all ([build_site.py](../engine/build_site.py)). If he wants to paint a week's turning point, add **at most one optional picture per chapter, placed at the end of the climax block inside its fold** and gated on that scene's key. Never put it at the chapter head, where it would come before what it shows. Four strands of evidence converge on that placement: the light-novel spoiler complaints (poll), the seductive-details finding that a striking still image beside key text does the most harm (META), Harp and Mayer's result that moving such material to the end removed the harm (CTRL), and the visual-novel habit of spending full illustrations on peaks (practitioner essay). Show no empty frame in weeks without a picture, because **a visible blank reads as a broken streak**, and broken streaks demotivate most when people blame themselves (CTRL ×7). A blank would also turn a gift into a debt on his time. Alt text should use only names the page has given, in a fragment of about fifteen words ([Cooper Hewitt](https://www.cooperhewitt.org/cooper-hewitt-guidelines-for-image-description/), institutional), and the build's existing wall already scans it.

### 6. Tie every visible change to the scene that caused it

Wildermyth's lesson, and the Mass Effect modders' complaint, is that a change lands when the reader can see the event that caused it. **Make every visible change a link to its scene**: the chapter on each Collection tile, each reason under "What he wears, and why" in the Wardrobe, each keepsake on the shelf and each per-Book portrait tab. The reader then discovers causes in the story instead of studying a table. Direct the Wardrobe as a costume arc. The silhouette and identity block stay fixed, and **one face master should serve both picture pipelines**: the round portrait and the full-length looks are made separately today, which gives Darrow two depictions that can drift apart. Earned pieces should look used, with scuffs, repairs and an inherited piece carrying a maker's touch, which is Clapton's device. The chroma screen should sit opposite the outfit's dominant hue, extending the existing switch from blue to green ([wardrobe.md](../engine/wardrobe.md)). And the body should never run ahead of the Knots, whatever the gear does, which puts the Koulouris finding into practice.

### 7–10. Thumb targets, the Codex, the cast pages and one line of disclosure

Four smaller changes finish the list. **Thumb targets and loading (7)** cost him nothing. Chips at about 23 px and portrait tabs at about 20 px fall below WCAG's 24 px minimum, and nav links and the song button pass it but sit well under 44 px. Every slide of a multi-portrait frame loads eagerly, when all but the first could load lazily, and the first visible image on a page should never be lazy ([web.dev](https://web.dev/lazy-loading/), Docs). In the same pass, give the two metal ladders, character rank and Art rank, which share metal names but not steps, a second channel of distinct shapes plus a numeral or pip, so they never read as one scale and stay legible to colour-blind readers. Give climax rolls a distinct rim, Disco Elysium's red check in this site's idiom, so the stakes show without odds.

**The Codex (8)** should gain a "New in the Codex" row at each chapter's foot, Heaven's Vault's unobtrusive pop-up made static. It should also stop treating its coverage (5 of 17 places, 1 of 8 factions, a single thing, 4 of 6 songs) as a debt, because paintings of things have the weakest payoff on the site and the drawn sigils already carry identity.

**The cast pages (9)** should keep per-Book portraits as changes on the same figure: the same crop, light and eye-line, with only what the prose has described changing. Wikis that stack many era images in one infobox find them hard to scroll on phones ([MSPA wiki](https://mspaintadventures.fandom.com/wiki/Forum:Infobox_Overhaul), Community), and the site's cap of one portrait per Book already avoids that. The pages should also add the chapter number to the existing fine print, "Only what the story has shown so far." A Coppermind-style reader-position filter could come later as a script-only extension for public readers catching up, but it matters little to an author who is always caught up.

**Disclosure (10)** is a single footer line saying the pictures and songs are made by the author with AI tools from his own designs. Undisclosed AI art is the recurring trigger of backlash in fantasy publishing ([Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/as-they-supposedly-battle-ai-slop-major-publishers-reportedly-cover-their-books-in-it-2000824603), Secondary), and stating the human direction softens the penalty that disclosure carries ([JGED](https://sp.ftn.uns.ac.rs/index.php/jged/article/download/2419/1596/5589), Paper, small sample). The keying step strips the images' C2PA metadata, so the page has to be the disclosure ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/advancing-content-provenance), Official). An `og:image` (the current Book's picture or the painted map) would also give shared links a face, which none has today. Both are his call, since the footer is otherwise pure story.

### What to keep, and what the evidence says not to add

Several things are already right. The tap-to-play songs beside the lines they sing, and the lyric check behind them, match the evidence. The one condition the research penalises, reading on while the dock plays a song with lyrics, is his own choice and a mild cost. The on-page gating of pictures, the hidden Wardrobe pieces that are only counted, the "⟦ unread ⟧" Reckonings and the dice folds all sit where the curiosity evidence wants them, with enough known to feel the gap. The dark theme is the one place where the evidence leans the other way. NN/g's review favours light mode for readers with normal vision but also tells designers to offer dark mode ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/dark-mode/), Study review). The palette passes contrast, and the forge-at-night identity is a deliberate commitment worth more than that modest edge. The table below lists what the evidence says not to add.

| Do not add | Why |
|---|---|
| Autoplaying or looping songs under the text | Lyrics while reading cost comprehension ([PubMed](https://www.pubmed.gov/29958067/), Study) |
| More stat blocks inside chapters | Receipts that readers learn to skip ([Iloris](https://www.ilorisnovel.com/articles/stat-blocks-in-litrpg-when-they-work), craft opinion) |
| Odds shown before a roll | Readers treat a high percentage as a promise (Solomon, D; Meier, D) |
| Empty frames, or locked slots hinting at future pictures | Locked gallery slots signal unseen content ([TV Tropes](https://TVTropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/VisualNovelTropes), Community); blanks read as broken streaks (CTRL ×7) |
| An idealised body, or a pose ahead of the Knots | Idealised avatars lower effort (CTRL); the site's own rule |
| The blue holographic "system window" | Coded as Korean system fiction, and pastiche on a knight's serial ([CBR](https://www.cbr.com/solo-leveling-system-explained/), secondary explainer) |
| Scroll-triggered effects, timed reveals, swipes inside the reading column | The Pudding advises against overriding scrolling ([The Pudding](https://pudding.cool/process/responsive-scrollytelling/), practitioner); readers call timed text a "pet peeve" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-to-remove-the-frustration-of-reading-in-games), Community) |
| A whole-world map with destinations drawn in | inkle moved away from showing every route at once ([postmortem](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/postmortem-inkle-s-i-80-days-i-), Designer); readers follow the protagonist's path (CTRL) |

## Conclusion

Across these six fields, the site's spoiler rule turns out not to be a constraint on good visual storytelling: it is the definition of it. What is off balance is where the drawing effort goes. It is lavish on the mechanics, which he wants to discover and which the evidence says can turn play into work once they are counted, and thin on the story's own units: chapters, Books, places and choices. Rebalancing needs surprisingly few paintings, because colour, placement and links in the drawn layer can carry most narrative signals for free. Only the scarce peaks (a Book's closing picture, the place he stands in, a climax he wants to keep) need his hand. Those are the same economics that led The Banner Saga and Wildermyth to treat expensive art as punctuation.

For a reader who is also the author and the hero's real-life counterpart, the pictures' job shifts. He already knows the story, so comprehension matters less than three other jobs: memory across months, a sense of place, and identification and reward. Those are exactly the functions with the best evidence behind them: memory for pictures, spatial memory centred on the hero, and identification raised by customising. The research cannot say whether hidden mechanics or illustrated serial fiction serve such a reader better, but he can find out cheaply. The top four changes are reversible builder work, so a Book read with the countdowns folded and its colour on the page is itself the experiment the literature has not yet run.
