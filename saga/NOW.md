# NOW — the Chronicle

*Narrative only. Nothing from the real logs appears on this page except through the Reckoning.*

**BOOK I — THE HOUSE OF MENDERS · Chapter 2 — The Grey Cot · the night of the gate**

- **Where:** Saint Ysolde's, the House of Menders, high in the Greywater; the infirmary hall at night, after the night bell, on his cot between Hollis's and the window. Snow is falling fine and dry. Below the gate the Confessors' one fire burns on the lower terrace.
- **The quest:** *The List.* Confessor Ivo Marrant read his writ at the bars: Darrow first, Hollis second, the far cots not read ("The Crown has no quarrel with men who can't answer"). Two grey cloaks came through the wicket before Old Mercy rang, late; the Prior spoke the law of names, and they went back out. Marrant camped on the lower terrace ("Bells grow tired") and called for the Captain of the Ninth to come down and kneel, and the rest of the paper would go back in his cloak. Darrow went to the bars and gave him his name and his face. Marrant says he will wait.
- **What Darrow is fighting right now:** the soft season, and the waiting. Maelis read the knee by lamplight after the wicket: *"Held."* Another man's weight on that door a moment longer and she would have had nothing left to tie it to; it is still soft, and will be at the thaw.
- **Just happened (Scene 1):** *Good Night, Captain.* The hall had his name before he reached his cot. Hollis, carried down from the gatehouse, gave the hall the names that were read, and Benedek's was not among them: *"It's mercy after all."* Darrow did not tell him why the Confessor's words about men who can't answer had followed him up from the gate. While Maelis read the knee, the word under his name changed on his palms. From the window the camp sits up in turns round its fire, where the whole House can see. A grey cloak at the bars sent the Captain of the Ninth the Confessor's good night, by his rank; Darrow had the gate-ward say he had given it. At the night bell his throat shut on the oath again, and he answered Benedek's good night aloud.
- **Earlier today, in order:** nothing yet.
- **With him:** Hollis Garrow (second on the list; dry, and has told Darrow so, nobody else; turned his face to the wall at the night bell) · Ser Benedek Orrin (on the next cot; not read at the gate, and takes it for mercy) · Maelis Vorne (read the knee: held, and still soft; the Confessor's eyes found her at the bars) · Wren Ashdown (ran for the bell on his word; has his promise to tell her first, in her ledger) · Mother Ione (would not reproach him; he is in the names she says every night) · Rae Thorne (her shoulder shut the wicket where his could not; her cart still under a grey cloak's boots).
- **Also in the House:** Sister Pell, keeper of the bells, whose hands were slow on the rope · the far cots, which were not read.
- **Against him:** Confessor Ivo Marrant and four grey cloaks sitting up in turns on the lower terrace, who have his name and his face; the sixth at the winch-house.
- **Next:** the morning after the gate, and the feast the kitchen has been counting loaves for.
- **Open choice:** none. The next comes at Chapter 2's climax.
