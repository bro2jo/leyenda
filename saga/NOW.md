# NOW — the Chronicle

*Narrative only. Nothing from the real logs appears on this page except through the Reckoning.*

**BOOK I — THE HOUSE OF MENDERS · Chapter 2 — The Grey Cot · the feast morning**

- **Where:** Saint Ysolde's, the House of Menders, high in the Greywater; the infirmary hall on the feast morning, on his cot by Hollis's. Hollis has eaten, and has Benedek's hand back in both of his. The kitchen is counting loaves. Below the gate the Confessors' fire has been let burn up.
- **The quest:** *The List.* Confessor Ivo Marrant read his writ at the bars: Darrow first, Hollis second, the far cots not read ("The Crown has no quarrel with men who can't answer"). Two grey cloaks came through the wicket before Old Mercy rang, late; the Prior spoke the law of names, and they went back out. Marrant camped on the lower terrace ("Bells grow tired") and called for the Captain of the Ninth to come down and kneel, and the rest of the paper would go back in his cloak. Darrow went to the bars and gave him his name and his face. Marrant says he will wait.
- **What Darrow is fighting right now:** the soft season, and the waiting. Maelis read the knee by lamplight after the wicket: *"Held."* It is still soft, and will be at the thaw. Now a lad of the Sixth lies Hollowed two cots away, and nobody came up the row in the night.
- **Just happened (Between):** *One Hand.* Darrow brought Hollis half a feast loaf. Hollis would not let go of Benedek's hand: *"I'll eat when he does."* *"He's not going to, Hollis."* Darrow saw that the thick hands are still only while they hold something: *"Give him to me. For the length of a loaf."* *"He's Sixth."* *"I know whose he is."* He put the bread into Hollis's hand and took the lad's out of the other; Hollis ate all of it the way men eat after a battle, and the hand never shook because it was never empty. Then he held out his palm and took the lad back. *"Tell her I ate. Don't tell her how."*
- **Earlier today, in order:** *Feast Bread* (Scene 2): the feast bread went down to the gate under the grey cloaks' eyes; at the dawn bell Benedek lay Hollowed, frost on his mouth; Darrow held the hall's eyes on the lad's mouth, not the door; Maelis found the black glass under his tongue, *"The far cots never had this"*; Hollis took the lad's hand and said it out loud, *"I told him to get some sleep"*; the kitchen began counting loaves; the oldest of his three: *"Nobody passed, Captain."* · *Wrong Question* (Between): he asked Maelis what the glass was and got "Wrong question"; she made him say what he feared: the lad can't answer now, so the bell's law can't keep him, and Marrant can carry him down the Steps; of the glass, only "not the far cots' sickness"; then the order, the slow way every time, and the job: make Hollis eat.
- **With him:** Hollis Garrow (second on the list; dry, and has told Darrow so, nobody else; sworn to say his thoughts aloud; ate only when Darrow held the lad's hand for him, and has it back; Darrow owes him silence about how) · Ser Benedek Orrin (Hollowed in the night: breathing, eyes open, frost on his mouth) · Maelis Vorne (read the knee: held, and still soft; found the black glass, and has it in her coat; will say only that it is not the far cots' sickness; has ordered him the slow way, every time, and sent him to make Hollis eat) · Wren Ashdown (ran for the bell on his word; has his promise to tell her first, in her ledger) · Mother Ione (would not reproach him; he is in the names she says every night) · Rae Thorne (her shoulder shut the wicket where his could not; her cart still under a grey cloak's boots) · three men of the Ninth, read with him at the gate, near the fire at the gate end (they will not go down the Steps on his back; he has promised they will be asked; the oldest was awake all night).
- **Also in the House:** Sister Pell, keeper of the bells, whose hands were slow on the rope · the far cots, which were not read.
- **Against him:** Confessor Ivo Marrant and four grey cloaks sitting up in turns on the lower terrace, who have his name and his face; the sixth at the winch-house.
- **Next:** the day after the feast, and the questions: who was awake, which doors were barred, and what the House will believe.
- **Open choice:** none. The next comes at Chapter 2's climax.
