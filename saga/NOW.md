# NOW — the Chronicle

*Narrative only. Nothing from the real logs appears on this page except through the Reckoning.*

**BOOK I — THE HOUSE OF MENDERS · Chapter 1 closed on *The Law of Names* and his answer; Chapter 2 — The Grey Cot opens on the night of the gate**

- **Where:** Saint Ysolde's, the House of Menders, high in the Greywater; right now at the far end of the low wall-walk beside the gate at nightfall, with Wren. Across the court the hall's lamps are lit; below the gate, on the lower terrace where the springs steam, the Confessors' fire burns.
- **The quest:** *The List.* In the hour before dusk five grey cloaks climbed to the gate (the sixth keeps the winch-house and Rae's cart). Confessor Ivo Marrant read the writ at the bars to the House standing in the court: Darrow first, Hollis second, the far cots not read ("The Crown has no quarrel with men who can't answer"). He held his paper out for the wicket under the law of guests until the Prior could not refuse it; two grey cloaks came through before Old Mercy rang, late, and stopped a pace from the wounded. Mother Ione spoke the law of names: while the bell rings, no name under it is taken down against its will. Marrant called it a fine bell, a little slow on the rope, made his camp on the lower terrace ("Bells grow tired"), and called for the Captain of the Ninth to come down and kneel. **Darrow gave him his name instead:** he crossed the court to the bars, called the Confessor back by both his names, and gave his own and his rank. Nobody went down the far side, and nobody is going down this one. Marrant looked at his face, called him Captain, and thanked the Prior for her candour. He says he will wait.
- **What Darrow is fighting right now:** the soft season, and now the waiting. He timed the bell for the Prior with one word to Wren, and the timing was right; the bell was late. He flinched at his own name where only Wren and Hollis saw. At the wicket his shoulder could not shut it, and the knee said one thing, quite clearly. Maelis looked at it before she looked at his face, and will read it tonight.
- **Just happened (Between):** *The Count.* He went after Wren on the wall-walk instead of the hall. She made him count the hall's lamps while she told him how stupid it was (*"I had a number for you, Captain. One in twelve. Now it's one in one."*), and he kept the count. *"Next time you mean to be brave, tell me first. So I don't run."* He promised; she wrote it in her ledger anyway.
- **Earlier today, in order:** Scene 4, *Boots on the Board* (the Prior hands him the bell's timing; the grey cloaks in at the winch-house three bells early; Rae lets the road shut) · *What an Ox Will Stand* (Between) · *The Grey* (Between) · the Climax, *The Law of Names* (the reading at the bars, the wicket, the late bell, the law of names) · the Choice, *Darrow of Edgemoor* (he gave his name at the bars; Marrant thanked the Prior for her candour; Maelis: *"Hidden, you were a lie they'd have found by the thaw. Named, you're a law they'll have to break where the valley can watch."*)
- **With him:** Mother Ione (held the gate and spoke the law of names; asked whether his shoulder was hurt) · Maelis Vorne (approved his name in her own coin, a law they must break being safer than a lie they'll find; will read the knee tonight; the Confessor's eyes found her) · Hollis Garrow (second on the list; saw the Captain move and told him nobody else had; called him Captain; dry, and has told Darrow so, nobody else) · Wren Ashdown (ran for the bell on his word; made him count lamps for it, then made him promise to tell her first) · Rae Thorne (her shoulder shut the wicket where his could not; her cart still under a grey cloak's boots).
- **Also in the House:** Sister Pell, keeper of the bells, whose hands were slow on the rope · Ser Benedek Orrin, in his cot in the hall; the far cots were not read.
- **Against him:** Confessor Ivo Marrant and four grey cloaks on the lower terrace below the gate, who now have his name and his face; the sixth at the winch-house.
- **Next:** the hall, at last: the names come back to the cots, and Maelis reads the knee.
- **Open choice:** none. The next comes at Chapter 2's climax.
