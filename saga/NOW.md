# NOW — the Chronicle

*Narrative only. Nothing from the real logs appears on this page except through the Reckoning.*

**BOOK I — THE HOUSE OF MENDERS · Chapter 2 — The Grey Cot · the night of the gate**

- **Where:** Saint Ysolde's, the House of Menders, high in the Greywater; the infirmary hall at night, after the night bell, back on his cot between Hollis's and the window after going down the hall to his three Ninth men. Hollis's knucklebones lie on the stool between their cots. Snow is falling fine and dry. Below the gate the Confessors' one fire burns on the lower terrace.
- **The quest:** *The List.* Confessor Ivo Marrant read his writ at the bars: Darrow first, Hollis second, the far cots not read ("The Crown has no quarrel with men who can't answer"). Two grey cloaks came through the wicket before Old Mercy rang, late; the Prior spoke the law of names, and they went back out. Marrant camped on the lower terrace ("Bells grow tired") and called for the Captain of the Ninth to come down and kneel, and the rest of the paper would go back in his cloak. Darrow went to the bars and gave him his name and his face. Marrant says he will wait.
- **What Darrow is fighting right now:** the soft season, and the waiting. Maelis read the knee by lamplight after the wicket: *"Held."* Another man's weight on that door a moment longer and she would have had nothing left to tie it to; it is still soft, and will be at the thaw.
- **Just happened (Between):** *Same Paper.* After the night bell Darrow went down the hall to the three Ninth men read with him at the gate, whom he had not looked at all day, and told them to go on not knowing him; he would tell the Confessor himself they were not his. "He read us, Captain. Same paper." When he said the Confessor's terms aloud, the oldest answered: *"We came up the Steps on other men's backs, Captain. We'll not go down them on yours."* He ordered them to sleep: "First one since the river." Back at his cot Hollis asked the wall "All three?", called them "Ninth" as if it were the worst word he knew, and his hand went on shaking under the blanket.
- **Earlier today, in order:** *Good Night, Captain* (Scene 1): the hall had his name; Hollis gave it the names that were read, and Benedek, not among them, took it for mercy; Maelis read the knee, *"Held,"* and the word under his name changed on his palms; the camp sits up in turns round one fire; the Confessor's good night came to him by his rank; at the night bell his throat shut on the oath, and he answered Benedek's good night aloud.
- **With him:** Hollis Garrow (second on the list; dry, and has told Darrow so, nobody else; turned his face to the wall at the night bell, and his hand has not stopped shaking under the blanket) · Ser Benedek Orrin (on the next cot; not read at the gate, and takes it for mercy) · Maelis Vorne (read the knee: held, and still soft; the Confessor's eyes found her at the bars) · Wren Ashdown (ran for the bell on his word; has his promise to tell her first, in her ledger) · Mother Ione (would not reproach him; he is in the names she says every night) · Rae Thorne (her shoulder shut the wicket where his could not; her cart still under a grey cloak's boots) · three men of the Ninth, read with him at the gate, near the banked fire down the hall (they will not go down the Steps on his back).
- **Also in the House:** Sister Pell, keeper of the bells, whose hands were slow on the rope · the far cots, which were not read.
- **Against him:** Confessor Ivo Marrant and four grey cloaks sitting up in turns on the lower terrace, who have his name and his face; the sixth at the winch-house.
- **Next:** the morning after the gate, and the feast the kitchen has been counting loaves for.
- **Open choice:** none. The next comes at Chapter 2's climax.
