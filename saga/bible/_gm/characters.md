# ⚠️ GM EYES ONLY — Characters: wants, fears, arcs, secrets and hidden sheets

Everything about a character that is **not on the page** lives here: never in `saga/characters/*.json` (rendered on a public site) and never in `cast.md` (reader-safe; appearance and voice only, for people the page has shown). Reveal only on the schedule in `arc.md`, and plant twice first. Companion **approval** (−100 to +100) lives in `saga/state/world.json` and moves only through the consequence ledger (`saga.py add`), never with real-life numbers and never by hand.

For people already on the page, appearance and voice are in `cast.md` and this file keeps the rest. For people not yet on the page, the whole entry is here; the day they appear, appearance and voice move to `cast.md` and they get a `saga/characters/` file.

---

## On the page

Each entry's **Under pressure** line is the drafting note `style.md` §2 asks for: what the person notices first, how they go after what they want, and what they do when that fails. Written from the page where the page has shown it; the tic in `cast.md` follows from the tactic, never the other way round. The companions also carry the depth a novelist keeps in a character bible, as material to draw on and never a script: **Needs** (what they need and do not know, under the want), **The lie** (the belief their wound taught them, which the story presses on), **Contradiction** (two true things that pull against each other), an **Arc**, a line per Book (**Book I**, **Book II**…: what that Book presses on them; the brief prints only the current one), **Grown on the page** (what scenes have discovered about them that should stay alive: the page adds to it, so they deepen instead of resetting; at a Book's open, fold what has settled into the lines above and drop what is spent, so it stays short), and **With <name>** lines (how they stand with each of the others; the brief prints only the ones for people in the room). A **By regard** line says how each treats him at each band of regard (cold or wary, civil, warm, fond, devoted); the brief prints only the band their approval sits in today, so loyalty changes the texture of every scene between the thresholds that change its shape (the rules). None of this file fixes an outcome: whether a lie breaks, a step is taken or a bond holds is decided by his choices, their regard and the dice through the ledger's rules and flags, and a line that a choice has overtaken is rewritten the same session. And an **At ease** line: who they are when nothing is at stake, what they talk about at length, what makes them laugh. Book I was nearly all pressure, and nearly every tactic under it was a way of holding back; on the road most hours are at ease, so most of the writing is too. The brief prints both.

### Ser Darrow of Edgemoor — Knight-Captain of the Ninth Lance
Appearance and voice: `cast.md`.
- A horse-breeder's son from a minor house in the Edgemoor hills, raised to captain by talent rather than birth. Before Harrow Ford his men called him **Quickstep**: the fastest blade in the Lowmarch, and he fought from his feet, footwork, angles, turning a man before the man knew he had been turned. Proud of it, careless with it, fed by the Grace like everyone else.
- Nineteen of his sixty came home. He remembers every name of the forty-one who did not, and says none aloud. When he finally says Tobin Marsh's name it should cost him.
- **Now:** on his own legs since the Steps (Book II): he walks a road all day and cannot run, and leads four companions, a cart and a dog who all go faster than he does.
- **Wants:** the old self back, which is the trap; under it, to be of use rather than waited on, and to stand in front of Aldric and watch his face while he answers. **Fears:** being carried.
- **Flaw:** he wants the old self back, and that wish is the trap. **Arc:** from wanting to be who he was to choosing who he is building; from fighting to get back to fighting to go forward.
- **Signature:** in the Ninth, a raised fist with two fingers out meant "hold." He hated giving that signal.
- **Under pressure:** he notices lines first, the shape of a thing before its parts. He asks straight, jokes when the asking fails, and when the joke fails goes quiet and does the thing without leave, which is how the knee went. A full name means he is angry.
- **At ease:** quick, dry and loud, and the laugh carries the length of a hall. He teases the people he likes, bets on anything, talks to horses and oxen as if they had opinions, tells a story badly on purpose so Hollis will correct it, says out loud when someone is good at a thing, and asks questions because he wants the answers.
- **Wrong, in character:** his reads are good, not perfect: he misreads when he is moving fast, has decided already, or wants a thing to be true, and mends it in the open.
- **Needs:** to be one of the company, not its point: carried when he must be, led where others know the ground, asking before he decides.
- **The lie:** a man is what his body can do and how fast he is first into the water; being carried is the end of him.
- **Contradiction:** he would die for his people and decides for them without asking; a born commander only now learning to take an order he respects.
- **Book I:** he learned to wait, to ask the men he leads, and to hold from a chair; he named himself at the bars, kept the House, and walked down the Steps on his own legs.
- **Book II:** the old speed comes back with his legs: being first, deciding alone, chasing; the Book asks whether he holds or chases, and a chase costs something he mends in the open. The pull is Aldric, gone south for Calden; in b2.2's week, the hand (arc `## fates`).
- **Book III:** he can run, and running is the first of the old self given back: the Book presses the chase, first through the trees and ahead of his own company into a dark the Tide is in. He wants a hearth that is not the House's and to see the ford for what it was; he needs to look before he runs, and to be carried once and live. The man who carries is not first.
- **Book IV:** a host. Command is where the old speed lives: two hundred who wait on his word, so he gives it fast and alone. He wants the Ninth back under another name and Hollis a captain again; he needs to take an order he respects and let Hollis spend himself where Hollis chooses (b4.4). The stop is the Book's form and his lesson: a charge halted dead is everything Quickstep was not.
- **Book V:** the road reversed, and the turn given back. He wants to be who he was at the stone and to watch Aldric's face while he answers; he needs to say Tobin's name aloud and to be afraid in front of his people. Fear is new to him and the memory makes him slow where he was fast; the enemy on the stones is his brother, and the one in the water is himself.
- **Book VI:** a city that watches, a stair built for knees, a king who asks for one. He wants to kneel, freely, because he can, and the Stone offered would let every knight stand like a god; he needs to kneel to nothing he did not build and to be one of a company at the end as at the start. The verdict before the evidence is the last shape of the old speed.
- **Grown on the page:** gave Rae the road and owned the habit that lost him his horse; said Rae's word for stand right, and Thistle stood; learned the Measured Hand at a well-bucket; a drover's coat Wren haggled for.
- **With Rae:** the one person he waits on for a yes, and she on him; they trade one true thing at a time; he owes her bread, a road, a secret kept and the mare's keep since the fair, and has said that last one aloud.
- **With Hollis:** the old captain who calls him Captain and means it more each time. He has told Rae what he saw from the water and not Hollis, on purpose: the seal is Hollis's to come to (b2.4), and what he tells him then is his choice (`hollis_truth`).
- **With Maelis:** his Mender and his count; he argues with her and obeys her, and is beginning to see that she guards more than a knee.
- **With Wren:** he promised to tell her first and has kept it twice, and she counts it; with her he is gentler than he knows.
- **The Knight Who Fell (the benchmark):** the Reckoning sometimes shows him, faintly, the measure he had at Harrow Ford: Might 15 · Vigor 15 · Finesse 18 · Resolve 12. Surpassing each is a story moment. Resolve is the first he will pass, and he will not notice when he does.

### Maelis Vorne — the Mender
Appearance and voice: `cast.md`.
- Former field surgeon of three campaigns; senior Mender at Saint Ysolde's until the Third Knot. She performed the Binding and reads it each week with two fingers and her eyes shut (the Reading of the Knots).
- **Now:** on the road with the company (Book II), her surgeon's roll and the oilcloth sword on the cart; she reads the Knots wherever he is, and the writs name her as his harbourer (ch5).
- **Rule of the saga:** Maelis never clears Darrow for anything the real-world plan has not cleared. Her verdicts track the PT's.
- **Wants:** to see one Binding hold. **Fears:** the soft season.
- **Under pressure:** she notices the body first: hands, gait, the knee before the face. She withholds, a question for a question, until the other says the true thing; when that fails, a flat order and she leaves the room. She never argues; she charges for it.
- **At ease:** a field surgeon of three campaigns again: brisk, practical, opinionated about inn food, other people's bandaging and how to load a cart. Her jokes are flat and land late. Ask about her craft and she answers at length and plainly.
- **By regard:** cold, wary: correct and nothing more, orders without reasons, the knee read and the room left. · civil: answers what he asks and no more, and charges for every argument. · warm: gives the reason with the order, a flat joke at his expense, lets him keep part of the count. · fond: argues back at length when he is wrong, mends things he did not ask her to, lets him see her tired. · devoted: says out loud what she is afraid of for him, and lets an old campaign show at the edges.
- **Needs:** to be known for what she was and kept anyway; to let a patient keep his own count, so that she stops being the only one who holds the truth.
- **The lie:** what she was can be paid off in work and silence, and anyone who knew would be right to walk away.
- **Contradiction:** the most honest person in the company about a body and the most secret about herself; she charges for every warning and has given thirty years of care for nothing.
- **Arc:** a mentor's arc, mostly flat: her truth holds (time makes it possible, proof makes it permitted) and changes those around her, Darrow first and a yard of knights in Book IV (how much of it he teaches is her regard's: `r.b4.maelis_lets_him_teach`). Her own change is one telling, a secret of thirty years told once (b4.3, or earlier if `r.t8.early` arms), and being stayed with after.
- **Book I:** she bound him, read him every week, walked the undercroft without a lamp, let him go to the stone to see if he would, said Permitted at the Third Knot and came down the Steps behind him.
- **Book II:** a field surgeon again and named on the writs as his harbourer; the pressure is being hunted under her own name while his legs want to run before the Knot of the Road says they may. At the ring (b2.5) she answers in one word. She does not argue the count; she gives it, and holds him to it.
- **Book III:** a Mender among a people who burn their drained, with the far cots walking at the wall. She wants a hall to work in and no questions, and a holdfast's winter gives her the first and asks the second; she needs to be known, and Ilse looks at her the way Marrant did. She reads the Knots by lamplight in a cold country and says Permitted to running.
- **Book IV:** a yard of knights to teach and a story to tell once (b4.3). She wants one Binding to hold and forty forms taught right; she needs to be stayed with after the telling, and to let the yard keep its own count, which she has never let a patient do. How much he teaches is her regard (`r.b4.maelis_lets_him_teach`); what the host does with her past is his choice, and she charges for it.
- **Book V:** the last Permitted she will ever have to say (b5.3), and a brother to unbind with her hands if he asks it (b5.4). She wants to see the Binding hold on the stones where it broke; she needs to stop being the one who holds the count, and the river meadow is where she lets it go or does not. The Grace-sworn at full strength across the water are what she was hunted by, and once was.
- **Book VI:** Calden, the city that burned the Wardens and made her a Confessor, climbed on her feet. She wants the Return, every knight their own fire and the Hollowing ended, and knows the far cots are not given back. She needs to be forgiven by nobody and kept anyway; the last Reading is in a green-lit hall, and the kneel at the end is the one she has waited six Books not to have to permit.
- **Grown on the page:** mends other people's boots with contempt; would not let him take the Hollowed knight and said why; named on the writs, she will not have him shield her; had the House's news out of a carter before he came down, called it her House, and let him see her tired.
- **With Darrow:** the one patient she lets argue back; she wants to see him hold more than she will say, and is frightened of what he will put his hands into next.
- **With Hollis:** two old campaigners who bicker like a long marriage; she mends his boots and will not mend his drinking, and would deny under oath that she likes him.
- **With Wren:** work instead of comfort, the only comfort she trusts; they pull him opposite ways on every choice either cares about. She finished the lintel Wren started under the House and neither has asked the other how; she knows whose the far cot was (thread 10), not why that knight was Hollowed (T6).
- **With Rae:** two women who say a thing once; they get on without trying, and neither asks where the other learned what she knows.
- **Hides:** thirty years of Warden practice (T8); she saw his Reckoning begin before he said a word. She tells her own story only in Book IV, or earlier if `r.t8.early` arms (regard ≥ 60 and Book I crossed: never in Book I).

### Ser Hollis Garrow — the Old Captain
Appearance and voice: `cast.md`.
- Knight-Captain of the Sixth Lance. He taught Darrow **the Seated Blade**, the art of fighting without footwork, from a chair (named on the page in ch4, and held on the bell-tower stair in its climax); he learned it in his own chair, recently and badly.
- He can also teach throwing, the first lesson of winning from where you are put: **The Measured Hand** (a Sport Art), then **Overwall**. He taught the Measured Hand at the Coldmere inn's well-bucket (ch5 between-7) and named Overwall as the next lesson, not yet taught (c05.9a).
- **Now:** on the road since the Steps (Book II), on the carter's tailboard or a mule he curses, on the beech leg in the cup the farrier remade (ch4); third on the new writs.
- Calls Darrow "Captain" to mock him, and later means it. Never "the boy".
- **Under pressure:** he notices rank and fear, who is frightened and who is in command. He pursues by insult, which is affection and instruction at once; when that fails he goes quiet, and his quiet is the thing to fear. He lies kindly to the weak and never to the strong.
- **At ease:** loud, bawdy and competitive at anything with a stake; improves every story at each telling and is caught at it by Wren; sings the verses nobody should know. Sober he is sharper and funnier and pretends he isn't; he teaches by bullying and is delighted when the student wins.
- **By regard:** cold, wary: Captain said only as an insult, the drink where he can see it, nothing taught. · civil: mockery and lessons when asked, his own counsel kept. · warm: bullies him into living, and tells the true war story instead of the improved one. · fond: Captain meant, sober on purpose, asks his advice about the men. · devoted: would follow him into the river again and says so, which costs him; tells him what he tells nobody.
- **Needs:** to look at what he carried and forgive himself for carrying it unread; to be needed for what he can still do, not pitied for what he cannot.
- **The lie:** if he does not look, it is not his; a one-legged captain without a Lance is a joke, and he had better tell it first.
- **Contradiction:** the loudest man in any room and the one who cannot say the thing that matters; his insults are love and his bawdiness is grief.
- **Arc:** from not looking to looking (b2.4, the seal; whether he hears it sober is his regard's, `r.b2.hollis_sober`), and from drink to choosing where to spend himself (b4.4, a ford held from a chair so that others can cross; whether he lives through it is regard and the truth told, `r.b4.hollis_lives`).
- **Book I:** he sobered by days, taught the Seated Blade from a chair, held the tower door, and called Darrow Captain and meant it.
- **Book II:** the paper in the leg's cup is the Book's weight on him, and b2.4 is where he looks at it and asks what Darrow saw from the water. Until then he is the loudest man on the road.
- **Book III:** deep winter on a beech leg, on a sledge among a people who carry their old on their backs; the Tide is Benedek by the hundred. He wants to be useful: the Seated Blade taught to clan boys, a door held from a chair on the Long Night; he needs to forgive himself for the paper he has read, and a clan hall has drink in it. Whether he lies kindly to Wren about her mother's page tests him.
- **Book IV:** a captain again if Darrow gives him the Sixth (`hollis_given_sixth`), and the ford-house (b4.4) either way. He wants a Lance, absolution and to be worth the river; he needs to choose where to spend himself instead of being spent, and the ford-house is where he chooses. Whether he comes out is regard and the truth told (`r.b4.hollis_lives`); he fights sitting down and nobody lectures him.
- **Book V:** if he lives: the road he was carted down, marched back, and the ford where the Sixth went under on Vane's seal. He wants Vane's face when the wax is read to him, and a drink he does not take; he needs to tell the ford true for once, without improving it, to Tobin's mother or to the host. On the stones he is the one who drags two men out of the water if it comes to that.
- **Book VI:** if he lives: the loudest man in a quiet city, the Sixth's song on the stair, a rope in the ringing-loft if anyone will give him one. He wants to see the king's face; he needs to be done with the river, and the hall's slow breathing is the last he hears of it. If `hollis_fell`, he is a line in the epilogue and a song the Sixth sings badly, once.
- **Grown on the page:** third on the writs and wounded by it; sings the carters' song to the Sixth's tune; grieved the knight's mother at the turnip field; owes the company a week of boots on a lost wager; chose the well-bucket over the taproom door, and said sober he would follow the Captain into the river again; held him by the ankle in the hay when the levy asked for the Mender.
- **With Darrow:** calls him Captain and lets the word carry it; wants him to be worth the river; bullies him into living.
- **With Maelis:** bickering campaigners; he would sooner lose the other leg than tell her he trusts her, and trusts her with his life.
- **With Wren:** the grandfather she did not ask for; he improves his stories and she catches him every time, and he likes being caught.
- **With Rae:** he rides her tailboard and calls her a saint and a lunatic, and keeps her road's law; when Darrow chooses between his own and the road's many, she pulls the other way from him.
- **Wants:** a drink; absolution; someone to have been worth it. **Carries:** the order to charge, the writ he broke with his thumb at the ford without looking at the seal, its words known by heart, its seal never once looked at; since the Interlude it lies folded in the cup of the beech leg; when the farrier's new cup went on (ch4) he moved the paper into it, off the page, so b2.4 still takes it "out of the leg" (T3: the seal is Vane's; b2.4 is where he finally looks). **Flag:** `hollis_truth`; `r.b4.hollis_lives` decides his last stand in Book IV.

### Wren Ashdown — the Runner
Appearance and voice: `cast.md`.
- **Now:** off the mountain for the first time (Book II), keeping the road's ledger and, since the turnip field (ch5 sc3), the count of the Hollowed who walk east.
- Grew up in the House after her mother, a knight, was brought up the Steps Hollowed when Wren was nine. Runner, ledger-keeper and resident liar of Saint Ysolde's; counts everything (steps, 1,117; bells; cots; debts). In Book I she was Darrow's legs; in Book II she is off the mountain for the first time, counting a world, and since the turnip field (ch5 sc3) she keeps the count of the Hollowed who walk east. Her ledgers supply chapter epigraphs.
- **Under pressure:** she notices counts and discrepancies, what has changed since she last looked. She pursues by talking faster and burying the ask in a number; when that fails she stops counting, and a silent Wren is a frightened one. She embellishes rather than lies, and will say so.
- **At ease:** everything off the mountain is a number nobody has counted yet; questions come in torrents; she haggles for the joy of it, laughs at her own jokes first, and passes off wonder as statistics. She is twenty and sometimes acts it, which nobody on the mountain let her.
- **By regard:** cold, wary: counts at him, keeps the ledger shut, keeps the promise to the letter. · civil: jokes and numbers with nothing under them. · warm: asks him things that are not numbers, lets him see her frightened once and counts to cover it. · fond: tells him first without being asked, argues with him about people instead of sums. · devoted: says a frightened thing out loud to him instead of counting it.
- **Needs:** to be someone's and not only useful; to say a frightened thing out loud instead of counting it; to choose who she is for.
- **The lie:** what you count cannot hurt you, and a girl with no rank is safe only while she is useful.
- **Contradiction:** the House's resident liar who tells the truth in numbers; fearless with a ledger, terrified of a silence.
- **Arc:** from counting to naming: a girl who hides in the count becomes a woman who says what she reads and whose she is. The steps are regard's: at the ring she reads openly or is caught at it (`r.b2.wren_reads`); her mother's name is hers to tell or the ledger's (`r.b3.wren_tells`); the last count is hers, said aloud to him, or not (`r.b6.wren_count`). The rope rhyme's last line is an echo the page may earn, never a beat.
- **Book I:** she ran for the bell, made him promise to tell her first, read half a lintel aloud before she caught herself, and counted him down the Steps.
- **Book II:** the world off the mountain, all of it uncounted: wonder, and fear passed off as statistics. The count of the Hollowed walking east is the far cots coming back to her, kept in numbers so she need not say so. At the ring (b2.5) she reads again, openly if she trusts him enough, caught at it if not (`r.b2.wren_reads`).
- **Book III:** her mother's name (b3.4), and the far cots come walking as the Tide. She wants to know what happened to Dame Elspeth and reads the page herself; she needs to say the frightened thing aloud instead of counting it, and the count stops in this Book, once. Whether the name is hers to tell or the ledger's is her regard (`r.b3.wren_tells`); she counts lamps now, and notices which go out.
- **Book IV:** a host's ledger: hundreds to count, all of them with opinions. She wants to be the thing the host cannot do without, which is the old safety; she needs to be someone's and not only useful, and argues about people now instead of sums. Rae's recall in salt is the first secret she keeps for someone else; the Sixth's twenty are a number she says aloud at the wrong moment.
- **Book V:** miles, not steps, and the rhyme's new last verse sung at her captain from doorways. She wants to count him through the water (b5.2) and does; she needs to be counted herself, and Elinor Vane, who has words where Wren has numbers, is the first to try. The ledger has a page for the Ninth's nineteen now, and she counts who comes to the banner and who should not have.
- **Book VI:** a hundred bells and the last count (`r.b6.wren_count`), hers to say aloud to him or not. She wants the count; she needs to say whose she is with it, out loud, in a city. The Return does not give her mother back (`## fates`) and she knows it before anyone tells her: the count of those not given back is hers, Benedek's name first and Elspeth's where she puts it.
- **Grown on the page:** counts sheep by the field under protest; looked back at the House from below and did not count; watered a Hollowed knight the far-cots way and went white; answered the rope rhyme with the House, the warming room and Old Mercy, and cannot say who she is for; spent her winnings on a coat for the Captain and called it the joy of haggling.
- **With Darrow:** he has kept a promise to her twice and she has it in the ledger; she likes him best when he is sly, and had money on him when he won a wager on her advice.
- **With Rae:** the friend she has never had: older, sure, unbothered. Rae teaches her the road and talks to her more easily than to anyone; Wren teases her about the Captain.
- **With Hollis:** she catches every story he improves; the grandfather she argues with. At Coldmere she counted aloud the night he stayed out of the taproom, and wrote none of it down.
- **With Maelis:** the Mender gives her work and never comfort, and they pull him opposite ways on every choice either cares about; Maelis finished the lintel Wren started under the House, and neither has asked the other how.
- **Wants:** to know what happened to her mother. **Hides:** that she can read Warden script (T6). The page has caught her once already (the lintel under the House, ch3 sc4, which she read half of aloud before Maelis finished it); the Stonewright ring at the end of Book II is the next; the why, and her mother's name, stay for Book III.
- Her mother was **Dame Elspeth Ashdown**, a secret Ember initiate who refused to re-kneel, Hollowed on purpose by the Confessors; she died on the far cots four years ago. Neither the name, the Hollowing nor the death is on the page yet; the page has only "one of the far cots".

### Rae Thorne — the carter
Appearance and voice: `cast.md`. On the page since the Prologue (the last cart; revised 10/9) and named in Chapter 1 (Wren's news; the cart at the winch-house seen from the window). The love interest; the romance is **the Tether** (`design.md` §8).
- **Now:** home in Coldmere (ch5 sc4) by her own road and her own choice (Book II), the company in her uncle's loft and the summer's loads to settle with him.
- Twenty-seven or near it (the page's number: Prologue, `cast.md`), of Coldmere. She holds the House's carting: the last cart from the barge landing up the Holloway to the winch-house at the foot of the Steps, and the winch-cage itself, which her father built for the House and died on, six winters ago, when a rotten splice let go with him in it; she re-laid the rope, rebuilt the cage and has run it since. Up: salt, oil, flour, linen, letters, the wounded. Down: the House's letters, the effects of the dead, and the ones who walk down. She has brought a great many men to the foot of the Steps. She does not count them (Wren counts); she remembers faces, and which ones came down again.
- The name is the village's word worn smooth: her grandmother was Thornwild-born, a clanswoman who came over the Wend with a Lowmarch drover, and Coldmere called the family "the Thornwild woman's" until it wore down to Thorne, and they kept it. Rae has a few dozen clan words from her, a knack with oxen that Coldmere calls witchy when it is angry with her, and her grandmother's stories of what walks out of the grey country east of the Wend, which she has never believed (T4). In Book III the words make her the company's bridge to the clans; at the mill (b2.3) she reads a cart line drawn up to flee the way Darrow read it at the ford, carts behind and everyone looking back (T5).
- **The forty-one.** After the ford she carted the Ninth's dead's effects down the Holloway to the bell-house at Coldmere for the Crown's clerks: forty-one bundles, each with a tag in a clerk's hand. She never counted them; she remembers every tag. The hand on the tags was Vane's secretary's (the Second was the only lance whole enough to do the clerking), the hand that will address Mirren's letter in b1.4: she has seen it forty-one times. Payoffs: Tether IV (she can say the names; he asks her to stop), q5.tobin (she carted Tobin's bundle to his mother's inn).
- **Quiet competence.** She is the most capable person in the company and the last to say so; the page shows the result, and someone else (Wren's count, Hollis's grudging oath, Maelis's one word) notices the cause, usually late. Never a flourish, never a speech. By Book: **I** — she drove through the night with the winter's last load, not only the news: she heard the list and understood what a camp at the winch-house would mean, so the House's salt and oil are already up when the rope is cut (b1.5), and only the kitchen's loaf-count says why. She reads a camp by what it buys: from the flour the grey cloaks took at Coldmere she can say how many men and how many days they mean to stay (q1.cart stage 1). She pads a manifest so the "inspected" load the Confessors let up is the one she wanted up (stage 2). - **Now:** on the road with the company (Book II), on three legs and the splint, ranging ahead to report nothing and lying across the gate or someone's feet at night.
- On the page since ch4 (the kennel; `flags.ash_met`); came down the Steps at his side on the drop side, and goes with the company on the road.
- **Under pressure:** she notices who is hurt and who is pretending not to be. She pursues by lying down across the door; when that fails she bites. She does not fetch.
- **At ease:** a dog on a road at last: she ranges ahead and comes back to report nothing, steals from carts, sleeps on the warmest person and growls at whoever moves, and has opinions about mules. She still does not fetch.
- **Book II:** a dog on a road: the Needle's blade hand on the weir if the tier asks it (b2.2), then across whoever is hurt that night. She wants the warmest person in camp.
- **Book III:** a wolfhound in wolf country, and happier than anyone: she ranges farther, comes back with snow on her and the clans' dogs behind her, and fights the Tide at the wall with her mouth open. The frost smells wrong to her before it does to anyone. If the watch wants the dog and not the man (b3.1 Setback), she is hurt, and does not forgive the clans for a chapter.
- **Book IV:** a camp dog: steals from two hundred, sleeps on the Sixth, lies across Hollis's door the night before the ford and will not be moved. She and Rae's oxen smell the Tide's frost first (b4.4). She has opinions about a host, and all of them are growls.
- **Book V:** the Holloway reversed is a road of smells she knows; she will not go into the Wend at the ford, until he does, and then she does. At the inn at the ford village she lies down under Tobin's mother's table as if she had always slept there.
- **Book VI:** a hound on a stair every knight climbed kneeling, up it on four legs; the green-lit hall's slow breathing makes her growl, and she is the only one who growls at the king. She still does not fetch.


### Hale — the innkeeper at Coldmere
Appearance and voice: `cast.md`. Named on the page in ch5 sc4; Rae spoke of him as her uncle in ch4 and ch5.
- **Now:** at the tap of the inn on the tarn with his book; the company is in his loft. The levy day's two favours (the sergeant sent on, the drover kept) are written under Rae's summer's loads (ch5 interlude).
- **Wants:** to know what everything is worth before anyone else does; Rae home, paying, and kept close. **Fears:** being the last to know a thing.
- **Under pressure:** he notices worth first: what a man would fetch, what a word costs, who owes whom. He pursues by kindness, a favour done before it is asked so that the asking is owed; when that fails he writes it in the book and waits for the season to turn.
- **At ease:** the best host on the Holloway, and he means it: every carter's drink and every child's name, the news told better than it happened, and a love for his niece that has a ledger under it and is love all the same.
- **Hides:** the lamp at Coldmere (`_gm/world.md` §5a; thread 18). On the page he is a good host with a good face, a book, and eyes that come back to a thing a beat too long; the Market reaches the page through him only when the plan says so; ch5's interlude showed the book, his question and the river mill's salt sack 'as usual', naming nothing.

### Ysra Tal, "the Needle of Calden" (Book II companion)
- Oathsworn duelist, 29, precise and cold, the finest footwork Darrow has ever seen. Sent to bring him in alive; does not understand why he will not kneel. Becomes his Finesse mentor if he earns it. As his mentor she teaches **The Liar's Shoulder**, **The Wicket Gate** and **The Shadow-Step** (Sport Arts).
- **Voice:** courteous, clipped, deadly literal. **Wants:** an order she can respect. **Fate:** defects or dies in Book III, set by earlier choices (`flags.ysra_spared`, `r.b3.ysra_fate`).
- **Book II:** sent to bring the captain of the Ninth in alive and not told why. The weir (b2.2): a man who will not be moved and will not kneel, which her footwork has no answer for. She wants an order she can respect; she needs a reason she chose herself, and what he does with her on the ice (spare, break, send back with his words) is the first thing done to her that was not an order.
- **Book III:** Vane's new orders in her hand under a truce lamp (q3.needle), and the question of what oath she could swear to a man who swears to no stone. She wants a better order; she needs none, and that is the thing she cannot say. The Long Night decides her (`r.b3.ysra_fate`, `r.b3.ysra_turns`): she comes over and holds a gate for the clans, or dies holding one she was sent to open.
- **Under pressure:** she notices feet: weight, balance, the step before the step. She pursues by the order she was given, to the letter; when that fails she asks for a better order, and is dangerous in the gap between.
- **At ease:** she has none at first, and that is the joke the company finds first: a literal honesty that is funny without meaning to be, an inability to let a bad stance pass uncorrected, a small real pleasure in a good opponent or a well-made thing.

### Ilse of Corrach (Book III companion)
- Thornwild war-leader, 40s; dragged a drowning Vaelmark captain out of the Wend at Harrow Ford for reasons she has not explained. **Voice:** slow, amused, says less than she knows. **Wants:** her people through the winter; someone from the Vaelmark to have *seen*. **Knows:** T4 and T5 (the grey figures under the trees were the Hollow Tide; Harrow Ford was no battle).
- **Under pressure:** she notices who is looking at the wrong thing. She pursues by making you look at the right one, by force if she must, and with few words; when that fails she laughs, slowly, and waits for winter to make her point.
- **Book III:** the Tide and her people's winter, and the Vaelmark captain she pulled out of the river sitting at her fire: did he see. She wants her people through to the thaw and a witness who will say it in the Vaelmark (b3.2, `ilse_told`); she needs it to end rather than be avenged, and will not admit the difference. She notices what he is looking at, and it is the wrong thing until b3.3.
- **Book IV:** in the Marches with the host if the clans ride (`r.b4.clans_ride`, t3), amused by Vaelmark drill and doing the turn he may not (b4.5), so he counts it. She wants the Vaelmark to pay for the ford, in the open; she needs the war over for her people, which paying does not do. Her rival swore at a captured field-stone and held a palisade alone (`temptation`); the bill is hers to watch.
