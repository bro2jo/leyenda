# ⚠️ GM EYES ONLY — Design: the systems behind the Chronicle

The final reference for how the story is run. `CLAUDE.md` has the per-session steps; `style.md` has the craft rules and the formats (scene kinds, interlude heading, micro-choice list, Reckoning box); `_gm/arc.md` has the plot. This file has the machinery. Everything here is executed by `python3 engine/saga.py`; the engine does the bookkeeping, you do the writing.

Four principles bind every piece:
1. **Real world outranks the realm.** Nothing here changes how deeds become XP or attributes (`darrow.py` owns that). Bearing, roads and consequences move only with story choices and chapter tiers, never with real numbers. The knee is cleared only by real gates and is never the price of anything.
2. **The wall.** Reader-safe surfaces (`saga/` outside any `_gm/` directory, `docs/`, `CLAUDE.md`, `README.md`, the skills) never carry a name, place, thing or plan the Chronicle has not put on the page. Everything else lives under `saga/bible/_gm/` or `saga/state/_gm/`. The build scans the GM `.md` files to block their sentences and names from the site; JSON under `_gm/` is never rendered.
3. **Context-light.** Per session read `CLAUDE.md`, `real/NOW.md`, `saga/NOW.md`, and `saga.py now` (≤ 30 lines). Consult the arc, ledger and plan through `saga.py` sections, never whole.
4. **No shame, no penalties, no recklessness rewarded.** Low roads cost Darrow ground, standing and company, never survivors and never the knee. Missed days are plot turns.

Decision of record: Arts and Knots are a skill tree, shown sealed on the site by design; they are not plot and need no planting.

## 1. Files

| File | Who | What |
|---|---|---|
| `saga/state/world.json` | reader-safe | book/chapter, `scenes[]`, location, season, `current_quest {name, on_the_page}`, struggle, last_beat, on-page `companions` (approval, present, note), `choices[]`, inventory, recaps |
| `saga/state/bearing.json` | reader-visible | the four axes, names, epithet, leans, history (§3) |
| `saga/state/_gm/plan.json` | GM | position, chapter plan (slots, world moves, climax), core beats, quests, route, flags, factions, companion preferences, companions to come, `quest_summary`, open micro, temptation (§5) |
| `saga/state/_gm/consequences.json` | GM | the consequence ledger: baseline approval, entries, rules (§2) |
| `saga/state/_gm/threads.md` | GM | open plants and payoffs, by hand |
| `saga/bible/_gm/arc.md` | GM | the plot, in id-headed sections (`saga.py arc <id>`) |
| `saga/bible/_gm/lore.md` | GM | the Annals: history and texture in id-headed entries with tiers (`saga.py lore`; §10) |

`saga.py fmt` keeps the four JSON files canonical (2-space indent, key order preserved, trailing newline); `check` fails when a hand Edit left one non-canonical. Approval and Bearing are never edited by hand: only `saga.py add` (and `bearing`) move them, so the ledger always reconciles (`baseline + Σ now.approval == world.json`).

`world.json → choices[]` entry (every key required): `{"chapter": 1, "scene": "climax", "kind": "climax", "option": 2, "text": "…", "date": "2026-10-11", "ledger": "c01.1", "by": "darrow"}`. A micro has `kind: "micro"`, `scene` = the key of the scene whose numbered list it answers (`"3"`, `"interlude"`), `by: "darrow" | "bearing"`. Chapters are ints.

## 2. The consequence ledger

Every choice gets **one terse entry**: what it changed now and what it will change later, with triggers the engine resolves. Never re-derive consequences from memory or the arc; `saga.py now` prints the ones that apply.

```json
{"id": "c01.1", "made": {"chapter": 1, "scene": "climax", "date": "2026-10-11"},
 "chose": "Stepped forward and answered to his name at the gate",
 "now": {"approval": {"maelis": 5, "wren": -5}, "bearing": {"candor": 2}, "flags": {"named_himself": true}, "factions": {"confessors": -5}},
 "due": [{"id": "c01.1a", "when": "ch02", "weight": "scene", "what": "The Confessor uses his rank in front of the House"},
         {"id": "c01.1b", "when": "next", "weight": "color", "what": "Wren will not meet his eye; she counts the gate's hinges aloud"}]}
```
- `id`: `c<chapter, two digits>.<n>`; due items append a letter. `made` is structured.
- `when` is what you wrote; `at` is the static target `add` computes (`next` → `ch<chapter>:s<next_scene>`, or `ch<chapter+1>:s1` while the stage is climax/choice; else `at = when`). Vocabulary: `next` · `later` (a callback: never due or overdue, listed by `due --later`, fired wherever it fits; §5.6) · `chNN` · `chNN:sN` · `chNN:climax` · `bookN` · `bookN:beatK` (while core beat `bN.K` is `in_progress`) · `bookN:climax` · `transitionN` · `finale` (= `book6:climax`) · `on:<flag>` (the flag must be one `plan.json` lists) · `any` (standing).
- `weight`: `color` (a line) · `scene` (a beat of a scene) · `route` (bends a road) · `fate` (a life or an ending). `status`: `pending` → `fired@ch03:s2` or `void:<reason>`.
- **Due now** = `at` matches the current position; **overdue** = `at` is behind the position and still pending; both print in `now` until fired or void. `saga.py due` with no arguments lists everything due or overdue now (owed world moves included); `due --at <target>` is an exact-target lookup that also shows what is due now; `due --all` is the whole ledger. Fire what you used (`saga.py fire ID --where ch03:s2`); void what the story made impossible, with a reason.
- `now.*` deltas are applied only by `saga.py add`: approval → `world.json` companions (clamped −100…100; a companion not yet in `world.json` needs `--pending`, which banks it in `companions_to_come.<id>.approval_pending`), bearing → `bearing.json`, flags/factions → `plan.json`. A flag must already be listed in `plan.json → flags` (`plan flag --new name=false` adds one; the arc's `## flags` names it) and be true, false or null. **Every flag an entry sets also gets a due item** for the arc's Downstream line (`(auto: the flag's Downstream line)`: a climax choice aims it at the next chapter, a micro at the next scene), so a consequence is owed and printed by `now`, never remembered; `fire` it where it is honoured, `void` it with a reason if the story made it moot. `plan next --full` on a spine slot also prints the previous beat's Downstream line. `--witnessed maelis,wren` adds ±2 approval per witness whose preferred pole (or its opposite) the entry's bearing move lands on, and writes it into the entry's `now.approval` so the record is complete. `--dry-run` prints every old → new and writes nothing.
- **Rules** (`rules[]`) are standing conditions the finale, roads and a few fates consult: `{"id": "r.finale.return", "if": "approval.maelis >= 50 && approval.wren >= 50 && (flags.anselm_spared || flags.vane_unbound)", "then": "…", "where": "book6:climax"}`. Grammar: `&& || !` (a rule may begin with `!`), parentheses, `>= <= > < == !=`, numbers, quoted strings, `true/false`, the beats as `beats.b1_1.status` (the id's dot as an underscore), and the terms `approval.<id>`, `bearing.<pole>` (axis value if the pole is the axis's right word, negated if left), `flags.<name>` (null → false), `route.bookN` (`""` while undecided), `factions.<id>`, `temptation.count`, `quests.<id>.status`, `tether.stage` (the engine's Tether stage, read from `saga/state/darrow.json`; §8). Evaluation is an `ast` walk over a whitelist; anything else is MALFORMED and reported by `check`. A bare name (`shard_found` without `flags.`) is an UNKNOWN TERM, never a silent 0. Missing dotted terms read 0/false; unlike types compare false. `now` shows rules ARMED, near (a numeric term that has moved off 0 and sits within 30% of a threshold under 15, or within 15 of a larger one) or UNKNOWN TERM; `check` fails on malformed rules and unknown identifiers. Fixed ids the arc writes to: `r.finale.shatter`, `r.finale.take.tempting`, `r.finale.return`, `r.t8.early`, `r.b4.hollis_lives`, `r.b3.ysra_fate`, `r.route.bookN.low|high`, `r.once.early_arrival`, `r.rae.form`, `r.rae.parts`.
- `saga.py archive` moves entries whose due items are all fired/void and older than two Books to `consequences_archive.json`, which `now` never reads.

## 3. The Bearing

Who his choices are making him: four axes, −10 … +10, story-only, reader-visible in words.

| Axis | Left | Right | Decides |
|---|---|---|---|
| mercy_flint | Mercy | Flint | the guilty and the beaten: pardon or punishment |
| candor_guile | Candor | Guile | the plain truth vs. the useful lie |
| hearth_banner | Hearth | Banner | whom he spends himself for: the few he knows vs. the many he does not |
| sworn_unsworn | Sworn | Unsworn | the old bonds (brother, rank, the Lances' forms, the law of bells) vs. owing nothing he did not build |

- No axis concerns the leg; no micro-option and no climax option in any Book is about testing the knee or defying the Mender on it.
- Moves: ±1 (micro), ±2/±3 (climax), ±4 only for a betrayal or a sacrifice. `saga.py bearing <pole> <n> --why <ledger id>` moves toward that pole, clamped, and appends to `history`; normally the ledger entry's `now.bearing` does it for you.
- **Leans** at |value| ≥ 4 ("leans Candor"); **named** at ≥ 8. The **epithet** is the named pole with the largest |value| (ties → the pole moved most recently): Mercy → *Open-hand* · Flint → *Flintheart* · Candor → *Plainspoken* · Guile → *the Fox of Edgemoor* · Hearth → *Hearthkeeper* · Banner → *Captain of Strangers* (Hollis's jibe, turned into a name) · Sworn → *the Faithful* · Unsworn → *the Masterless*.
- **An epithet must be spoken on the page** (a writ, an enemy's mouth, a companion's jibe) in the scene or climax whose choice earns it. The site shows pole words and the epithet; prose shows no numbers, only how people address him, which options exist, the epithet in writs and talk.
- **Companion preferences** (`plan.json → companions.<id>.prefers`): Maelis Candor, Sworn · Wren Guile, Unsworn · Hollis Mercy, Hearth · (to come) Rae Banner, Candor · Ysra Sworn, Candor · Ilse Flint, Banner. A choice on a companion's pole, witnessed or learned of, rides ±2 approval (`add --witnessed`).
- **Every climax option sets something downstream** (`arc.md`: the **Downstream** line under each beat's choice): a flag the next beat or a later Book reads, so a choice changes who is where and what a later scene can be, not only the Bearing. **Every companion has approval rules in every Book** (`_gm/characters.md`, "Approval with teeth"): thresholds that decide the shape of a later scene, never a death except where the arc already says so.
- **Gating:** an option may require a Bearing value: `*[Guile 1]*` for a first step, `*[Guile 4]*` for a lean; struck through when unmet, like a stat gate. Roads and endings consult Bearing only through ledger rules.

## 4. Roads: one per Book

`saga.py route decide --book N` scores the Book's closed chapters (`route.bookN.chapters`, Knot week included) from `chapters.csv`: Triumph +1, Setback −1, others 0. **Low** if score ≤ −2, or ≤ 2 chapters and the last climax was a Setback; **High** if ≥ +2, or ≤ 2 chapters and the last was a Triumph; **Main** otherwise. It refuses while a listed chapter has no `chapters.csv` row unless `--road … --why …` (recorded as `override`). The decision sets `route.bookN.transition_chapter` to the last listed chapter if unset.

Every transition chapter has three written shapes in the arc (`tN.high`, `tN.main`, `tN.low`). In all three the Knot ties, the next Book's first core beat happens, nobody is lectured or humiliated, no survivor count drops, the knee is never the price. What differs: the circumstances of the crossing, who is with him, what he carries, what the world believes of him, and **one carry-forward** per road as a ledger rule `r.route.bookN.<road>` (low: a cost on ground, standing or company; high: a boon; main: none). A road's text may key one line on a Bearing lean. "Arrives a chapter early" fires at most once per Book whichever source armed it (`r.once.early_arrival`).

A Knot tied mid-week: re-plan the rest of the week's slots toward the crossing, Sunday's climax becomes the transition, and `route decide` runs at that checkpoint after `darrow.py chapter-close`. From Book II on, one **temptation** beat per Book shows the fast, borrowed way working for someone (its bill due a Book later); `plan temptation add "…"` records each and `temptation.count` feeds `r.finale.take.tempting`.

## 5. Day → week → Book

### 5.1 Scene kinds (every daily scene is exactly one)
- **Spine:** advances the chapter's core beat. ≥ 2 per chapter; Saturday's slot is always spine; if Saturday is missed, `plan done N --skipped` and its spine content folds into the climax (Friday's slot is never promoted; nothing is re-planned).
- **Quest:** one stage of an active side quest. ≤ 2 stages of one quest per chapter.
- **Interlude:** another POV, ≤ 1 per chapter, heading `### Interlude — Title`, not tied to a day (a slot with `"day": null`, outside the 7-day count). It is written on any close-day of its chapter, after that day's own scene (never instead of it), or at the checkpoint before the climax if still unwritten; `plan done N --wrote chNN:interlude` closes it.
- **Cutaway:** a red-light day; the world moves without him. Never a setback frame.

**Days.** A day with a row in `daily_log.csv`, `nutrition_log.csv` or `food_entries.csv` is **open** until `closed=Y` (food alone opens a day); it gets its slot when closed, in any order (`plan next --date D`). Only a day with nothing logged at all is **missed**: no row is ever created for it, no scene; `plan done N --skipped` records it, pops one off-page **world move** from `chapter.world_moves[]` onto the slot, and `now` and `plan next` print that move as owed until the next scene or climax opens on it (`plan done --wrote` and `plan set stage=climax` mark it used). A skipped spine slot's content folds into the next spine slot or the climax. The two `float: true` quest slots are the first dropped. `plan done N --force` redoes a slot already written or skipped.

**Colour, never content.** The slot's content is fixed in advance; the day's real deeds set only the tone, and the tone is Darrow's own: warmth or cold in him, stamina, how he reads a room and hears a remark. Never how anyone treats him: that follows temperament, intent, what they witnessed and approval (`style.md` §1). Outcome is set only at the climax by the tier. `plan next --date D` prints the colour in one line, computed from the logs vs `real/config.json → nutrition_targets`: `rest` if light = red; `warm` if both floor rounds and (a session or both kcal and protein ≥ 90%); `cold` if neither floor round and no session; else `mild`. GM-only; it never appears anywhere reader-facing.

### 5.2 Side quests
Each quest in `arc.md` (`## q1.wager — Title`) has `priority` (`required | optional | floating`), `hook`, 2–4 `### stage N` sections (one daily scene each, ending on movement) plus `### compressed` (two stages in one scene: it keeps each stage's setup, its consequential choice and the reaction to it, and drops the texture), one `### micro` at its turn, `payoff`, `spine link`, `can run when`. Once Chapter 2 opens at least one quest is live, never more than two (a quest marked `standing: true`, the Tether, is not counted, nor is the Book's `project: true` quest: §8, §5.6); stages run in order and finish within two chapters (the Tether's finish when the Ledger says); `required` first; a quest dropped for plot reasons is `dropped` with one line of why; `floating` quests may run in any Book at the House or be re-skinned on the road. `plan done` on a quest slot advances `quests[q].stage` and sets `live`/`done` (done when the slot carries `"last": true` or the stage count in the arc is reached; quests whose arc stages are compressed into one line have no count, so close them with `plan quest ID status=done` by hand after their last stage). From Chapter 2 on `now` warns when no quest is live.

Book I: `q1.kennel` (required; Ash; sets `flags.ash_met`), `q1.letters` (required to its second letter; the third is a micro on sworn_unsworn), `q1.cart` (optional; Rae's thread: the winch-house, the mare's price, `plan companion arrive rae`), `q1.wager`, `q1.steam`, `q1.farcots`, `q1.ledger` (optional), `q1.snow`, `q1.hollownight` (pinned to the week of real Halloween if the Book is still open), `q1.door`, `q1.blade` (Hollis's lessons; the Seated Blade named on the page), `q1.hand` (the throwing games; the Measured Hand and Overwall named) (floating), `q1.rail` (the Book's project: a rail for the nine steps to the warming room; §5.6), and `rae.tether` (standing, every Book; §8). Core beats `b1.1` … `b1.5`; transition `t1`.

### 5.3 plan.json
`position {book, chapter, beat, next_scene, stage ∈ scene|climax|choice|transition, last_written}` is the cursor every `at` resolves against. `chapter {number, week_start, question, slots[], world_moves[], climax {plan, checks, options, default}, previous_devices[]}`; a slot: `{n, day, kind, beat | quest+stage (+ last: true on a quest's final stage) | pov, plan, turn, micro, challenge, invite, float, status ∈ planned|next|written|skipped, wrote, world_move}` (`turn`: one line, who can, must, believes, risks or chooses what by the end that they could not at the start, and what Darrow attempts; `plan next` prints it, `check` notes a planned slot without one, `plan chapter open` warns); a micro: `{ask, axis, options[2–3], bearing[per option, one axis, ±1 or null], default}`. `core_beats` (`planned|in_progress|done|folded`), `quests` (`available|live|done|dropped`, stage, priority, `project`, `gate`: a rule-grammar condition from the arc's *can run when*, §5.6), `route.bookN {chapters, road, override, transition_chapter}`, `flags`, `factions`, `companions.<id>.prefers`, `companions_to_come.<id> {note, bond, approval_pending}` (→ `plan companion arrive ID` when they step on the page), `quest_summary` (the GM's version of the quest line), `open_micro`, `temptation.planted[]`. New flags a Book needs are listed in the arc under `## flags`.

### 5.4 Chapter (week) and Book
A chapter has a **question** (a sub-question of the Book question) that the climax answers in the tier's shape; the choice bends the next chapter's slots. Planning a chapter (`plan chapter template --week_start <Sunday>` prints a canonical skeleton to fill; then `plan chapter open --number N '<json>'`, checkpoint step D; afterwards `plan slot N key=value …` edits one planned slot, `--force` for a written or skipped one): 7 slots Sun–Sat (≥ 2 spine, Saturday spine, ≤ 1 interlude, two `float` quest slots), micros in non-adjacent slots touching ≥ 2 axes, `world_moves`, the climax plan with checks, options and a `default`; required quests first; the chapter touches ≥ 1 open thread. Across a Book's climaxes all four axes must be offered at least once, and no axis in more than three of them; the planner enforces this, `check` cannot. `check` enforces the shape; `plan chapter open` refuses while a micro is open (resolve it first), and `plan climax` prints the current chapter's question, climax plan, checks, options, default and world moves left. `plan chapter open --number N` takes the next number only (one chapter per calendar week; `--force` to open out of sequence). A chapter with **no slots** is a **cutaway chapter**: a week with nothing logged, closed by the engine as it scores and written as one interlude with no climax (the checkpoint skill, section A); `check` asks nothing of its slots. A Book opens with `plan book open N` (sets `position.book`, seeds `route.bookN` and the Book's core beats from the arc, marks the old Book's unwritten beats `folded`). A Book: its question, core beats, quests, road, transition variants; the Knot ends it. Core beats unwritten when the Knot ties fold into the transition in order; `now` warns when more than two core beats are still `planned`.

### 5.5 Micro-choices
At most one open at a time, one per scene, in non-adjacent slots (a micro is a device: no device on a neighbouring day, §5.6); exactly one axis, ±1 per option, 2–3 options (format in `style.md`; the parser accepts "What does Darrow do?" and "say?"). Opened with `plan micro open N` when the scene is written; answered in any later message while open (a bare number is an answer only while a choice is open); `/choose` records it (ledger entry → `add` → `choices[]` → `plan micro close --option K`), and the consequence is folded into the opening of the next scene, no separate block. Unanswered, it carries across one scene; still open at the following close, it resolves to the slot's `default` (set at planning: the option the highest-approval companion present would pick, else the one that moves the quest), recorded `by: "bearing"` and closed with `--by bearing`; the Ledger reply says in one line that Darrow answered for himself. Every close-day Ledger reply ends with "Still waiting on Darrow: 1 … / 2 … / 3 …" while a choice is open. If a new micro is due while one is open, the open one resolves by default first (`plan micro close --by bearing` with no `--option` takes the planned default). The site labels a micro "A small choice" and a climax "The choice".

**The climax choice** carries across the first two scenes of the next chapter; if still open at the second close it resolves to `chapter.climax.default` (set at planning by the micro rule: the option the highest-approval companion present would pick), recorded `by: "bearing"`, and its `### Choice` block is written then. Slot 1 may be written while the choice is open: it covers the Sunday and ends before the choice is felt.

## 5.6 Devices: challenges, the project, invitations, callbacks

Beside its content, a slot may carry one **device**: a micro-choice (§5.5), a **challenge**, an **invitation**, or a **project stage** (a quest slot of the Book's `project: true` quest; its choice is that stage's micro). Devices give the days teeth without new systems: each is a field on a slot or a quest, each resolves through the ledger, and `check` holds the pacing. `plan next --full` (and so `darrow.py close`) prints the slot's device with its people's *Under pressure* lines; `now` lists the chapter's devices in one line.

- **Challenge** (`slot.challenge`): `{objective, approaches: [{name, stat, art?, dc}] (1–3), goal (1–4), limit (1–3), progress, strain, status: open|won|lost|void, result: {won, lost}}`. The objective is the scene's want; the approaches are what he can try, each a stat (`might|vigor|finesse|resolve`), an optional Art and a DC. Two counters: `progress` toward `goal`, `strain` toward `limit`. Dice come from `darrow.py roll chNN-sK-<slug> --stat … --dc … [--prof --art …]` as always; after each roll `saga.py challenge N success|fail` moves a counter and resolves the challenge when one fills. It runs inside its scene, or into the next one at most. The counters never appear in prose: the page shows a man trying things and what they cost. A lost challenge is a cost or a turn, never a wall. What it changed is recorded with `saga.py add` (a flag, approval, a due item), so the climax and the next chapter read it. Never an approach the knee could take.
- **The project** (`quests[id].project = true`, one per Book, `priority: floating`): something in a familiar place that Darrow gets built or mended, in three stages spaced across chapters (never two in a row), with a micro-choice at each stage (the arc writes them under each `### stage`). Its stages are quest slots like any other; the project is exempt from the two-live count and from "finish within two chapters". Between stages a Between may lay a hand on it: `saga.py project ID work --where chNN:between-K --what "…"` notes it GM-side and the next stage's scene shows it; a Between never advances a stage. When a stage's scene is written, the reader-safe trace goes to `world.json → projects[]`: `{id, name, place, stage, of, status: live|done, visible, decisions: [{stage, chose, scene}]}`, with `visible` one line in the page's words. The site shows it on the place's codex entry under *What Darrow changed here* and as *Work in hand* on the Now page; `places.json` gets the changed description and `codex.md` its line. The finished thing stays on the page in every later scene set there, without comment.
- **Invitation** (`slot.invite`): someone comes to Darrow with a want of their own: `{who, kind: favour|diversion|opinion|problem, want, why_now, when?, if_ignored, status: waiting|taken|passed}`, planned at chapter open from that person's *Under pressure* line. `why_now` is one line of why this person comes to him now, from their want and the moment; `when` is an optional condition in the rule grammar for the situation it needs. It is offered in one in-world sentence at the end of the close-day reply, never as a menu, and the plan does not wait for it. If he answers in voice, it is a Between that the other person opens (`/play`; `saga.py invite N take`), under the Between limits, and his answer goes through the ledger like any Between. If he has not engaged by the next close, `saga.py invite N pass` and the `if_ignored` line opens that scene in a clause: the person did what they would have done. A favour is not always a problem, an opinion wants nothing but to be heard, and a diversion may be the whole of it.
- **Callbacks** (memory): approval, due items and rules are already how people remember him. For what has no date, a shared joke, a kindness, a promise, a quarrel half-made-up, a due item with `when: "later"` is a **callback**: never due, never overdue, counted in `now`, listed by `saga.py due --later`, fired wherever it fits (`fire ID --where …`) at most once per scene, at most six alive (`check` notes more; the checkpoint fires the ones that fit the climax or voids the stale ones with a reason).

**Gates: the devices never run ahead of the page** (`check` refuses; `plan next` and `plan chapter open` say *locked*). A challenge approach may name only an Art the chronicle has already named, in prose or a Reckoning box: an Art on the sheet but not yet taught or earned on the page is locked, and `check` reads the chronicle to know. An invitation comes only from someone with a file in `saga/characters/` whose status is alive: the people of the GM file's not-yet section cannot invite, and neither can the fallen. A project lives only at a place that is on the page. Any quest may carry a `gate` in the rule grammar (`plan quest ID gate='beats.b1_1.status == "done"'`, a beat id with its dot written as an underscore; also `quests.ID.status`, `flags.x`, `approval.x`, `tether.stage`), mirrored from the arc's *can run when*: a planned slot of that quest is refused while the gate is not met. Device text (an objective, a want, a callback) that names what the registries do not know draws a note, because nothing the page has not spoken may reach a scene or a Between. **The narrative gates on an invitation** (`check` refuses, `plan next` says *locked*): the person must be where Darrow is, by the page's own bookkeeping, their file's `last_seen.place` against `world.json → location.place`, unless the slot's plan names them, in which case the scene must bring them to him first and `check` only notes it; a companion marked `present: false` cannot come; `why_now` must be there; the invitation's `when`, if any, must be met; and the person's standing rule in `plan.json → invite_rules` must be met (`plan invite_rule WHO EXPR`: Rae does not approach him before the Tether's first stage, `tether.stage >= 1`; Benedek not once `benedek_hollowed` is set). What the gates cannot read, the planner must: an invitation carries only what that person knows (the threads' *Who knows* column), fits the night the spine has made (nobody brings a diversion to a man the night the grey cloaks arrive unless the diversion is the point, and Hollis's would be), and never a quest's stage or a truth ahead of its schedule.

**The pacing rule** (`check` enforces it; `plan chapter open` warns): one device per slot; never two devices on neighbouring days; a chapter carries at most two of the three kinds (challenge, project stage, invitation), at most one challenge and at most two invitations; and no kind that the previous chapter carried (`chapter.previous_devices`, set at open). Micro-choices keep their own rules (§5.5) and count as devices for the neighbouring-day test. The rotation is the point: nothing recurs on a rhythm a reader could hear, and a chapter without devices is a chapter, not a gap.

## 6. The arc file
`saga.py arc <id>` prints one section (≤ 40 lines) of `_gm/arc.md` by heading id: `## bookN.question`, `## bN.K — Title` (core beats), `## qN.id — Title` (quests, §5.2 fields), `## tN.high|main|low` (transition shapes, ending `- **carry-forward:**` matching the ledger rule), `## temptation`, `## flags`, and the top sections `## pacing`, `## truths`, `## motifs`, `## beliefs`. `check` validates every plan id against these headings. Never reveal a truth ahead of its schedule; plant twice first; the schedule may add plants, never move a reveal earlier.

## 7. Procedures (where they live, and what the engine enforces)

The step-by-step procedures live in one place each, the skills, and nowhere else: closing a day in `.claude/skills/log/SKILL.md` §6 (it starts with `darrow.py close DATE`), a choice in `.claude/skills/choose/SKILL.md`, the Sunday checkpoint and a missed week in `.claude/skills/checkpoint/SKILL.md`, a Between in `.claude/skills/play/SKILL.md`, a Tether stage in the log skill. `CLAUDE.md` keeps the rules and points at them. This section lists what the engine enforces around the procedures, so a skipped step fails loudly:
- `darrow.py close DATE` refuses a missed day (nothing logged: no row, no scene), a closed day (it has its scene), a day that has not happened, and a new-week day while an earlier week's chapter is unclosed (the checkpoint first); then closed=Y → `sync` → `saga.py now` → `plan next --date DATE --full`.
- `darrow.py chapter-close` freezes the week's tier: the dice (`roll --chapter N`), the road and the XP bonus read `chapters.csv`; late logs move totals and the live score, and `sync` prints a note when the two differ.
- `saga.py add` refuses a flag the plan does not list, a non-boolean flag value and an `on:<flag>` target naming no flag; it adds the Downstream due item for every flag it sets.
- `saga.py plan done` refuses a slot already written or skipped (without `--force`), another chapter's key, a scene number ahead of `next_scene`, and `--wrote` together with `--skipped`; it warns when the chapter file has no heading for the key yet and reminds you of the slot's micro.
- `saga.py plan chapter open` takes the next chapter number only (`--force` otherwise) and runs only when no micro is open; `plan set` takes integers for `book`, `chapter`, `next_scene`; `plan flag` takes known names (`--new` to add one) and true/false/null.
- `saga.py check` reconciles the chapter file (`### Scene N`, `### Interlude`, `### Between`, `## Climax`, `### Choice`) with the written slots, `world.json → scenes[]` and their titles, `position.next_scene`, the stage (a Climax on the page needs `stage=climax`), the Between limit (at most two in a row), and the Ledger: a written slot's day is closed, a skipped slot's day has nothing logged, a closed day's slot is written.

**Every session:** `saga.py now` after `darrow.py sync`; `saga.py check` before every commit that touched `saga/`.

## 8. The Tether
The one line from the real world into the story that is not the knee and not the fire: the romance with **Rae Thorne**, the carter (`_gm/characters.md`).
- **What it is.** Engine-owned the way Ember is. `darrow.py` counts the days logged as time together on purpose (`daily_log.csv → together=Y`; nothing else about them is recorded, asked for or scored) and stages them by `rules.json → tether.stages` (1 / 4 / 10 / 20 / 35 days) under the names *a name · a hand · a road · a door · a home*. It only climbs. No XP, no temper, no tier: a date buys nothing in the week's score and a quiet month costs nothing; the thread rests, it never frays (no shame: principle 4).
- **What it moves.** One planned scene per stage (`arc.md → rae.tether`), written in the next free quest slot after `sync` reports the stage, in whichever Book it lands, re-skinned to the road; her approval sets her warmth and the slot's colour sets his. The sheet and the site show the Tether only from stage I (`THE TETHER · N of V · <name>`); the scene that pays a stage closes with `> The Tether: **<name>**.` in the Reckoning box, and the first of those is where the page explains it. `rae.tether` is a **standing** quest in `plan.json` (`standing: true`): `check` leaves it out of the two-live count and refuses a slot planned at a stage the engine has not reached; `tether.stage` in the rule grammar reads the engine's sheet.
- **What it never moves.** Her approval: story-only, through the ledger like everyone's (`plan companion arrive rae` when q1.cart stage 3 writes; `--pending` before). Rules read both tracks: `r.rae.form` (stage V and approval ≥ 50) decides the epilogue's last image; `r.rae.parts` (approval ≤ −25) sends her home for a Book and holds the scenes, never the stages.
- **The wall.** A logged evening is never Rae on the page; a scene is never written because one was logged; the Ledger never asks what the evening was; the glimpse never mirrors it. Her presence in a scene comes from the chapter's plan and her own road (`arc.md → rae.road`); only the Tether's stage, like a Knot, says which of her scenes may be written yet. Choices with her move the four axes as usual; no axis is about her, and no option tests the knee for her sake.

## 9. Betweens (role-play)
The third way the story moves, beside the Ledger's days and the plan's choices: **he tells Darrow what to do**, and the scene extends from it (`.claude/skills/play/SKILL.md`; the format in `style.md` §3).
- **What it is.** A `### Between — Title` block (120–300 words) appended to the chapter after the last written block, in the story's present: after the last scene, before the next slot. The site parses it (key and anchor `between-K`, label "Between", rendered smaller and listed with the scenes but never counted as one), `world.json → scenes[]` records it with `"after": "<scene key>"`, and `now` counts the Betweens written after the last scene (two at most; a third is two lines and the moment passes).
- **What it may do.** Honour his action within the world, the cast and the sheet; let people answer in voice, refuse, deflect or lie; play a small game on one die (`darrow.py roll chNN-between-K-<slug>`, never `--chapter` or `--tier`); win or lose a copper, a line, a chit in Wren's ledger; change regard (one ledger entry at most: `made.scene = "between-K"`, approval −3 … +3, one axis ±1 with the usual witnessed ±2, `due` items at `next` so the following scene honours what was said or done; `fired@chNN:between-K` is a valid position). A told secret, a promise, a quarrel is a `due` item, and the plan bends to it at the next `plan slot` edit or `plan chapter open`, never by rewriting a slot from inside a Between.
- **What it may never do.** The plan's work: nothing from the next slot, the quests, the world moves or the climax; no truth ahead of its schedule, no plant, no reveal, no name, place or thing the page has not spoken; nothing real; nothing with the knee the real plan has not cleared (the world refuses in voice, never a lecture; no reward for trying); no XP, Inspiration, Ember, Art, Knot or Tether stage; no slot closed, no quest stage advanced, no due item of the plan's paid; no choice list (choices belong to scenes and climaxes; a Between while a choice is open leaves it open).
- **Invitations and the project** (§5.6). An invitation is a Between the other person opens, with their want, when he answers its one-line offer in voice; `invite N take` records it, and his answer goes through the ledger as usual. A Between may lay a hand on the Book's project (`project ID work`) and never advances its stage. A thing said in a Between that has no date to be remembered on is a callback (`when: later`).
- **Why it fits.** The arc is a spine with planned shapes; a Between is the connective tissue between vertebrae. Because its consequences go through the same ledger as the choices, the climax and the next chapter's plan read them the same way, and nothing he plays is lost or contradicted.

## 10. The Annals (lore)
`saga/bible/_gm/lore.md`: the realm's history and texture in id-headed entries (`kind.name`; kinds: age, house, realm, folk, song, rhyme, saying, maxim, craft, calendar, beast), each with a tier (`common`: the kitchen knows it; `learned`: a Mender, a clerk or an elder), a `spoken:` mark, the unspoken `names:` it uses and a `use:` hint. Read through `saga.py lore` (the index, once per chapter), `lore <id>`, `lore grep <word>`, `lore pick saying|verse|maxim|rhyme` (the glimpse's line, so the realm's proverbs are not invented six times a week), and `lore spoke <id> --where …` when the page speaks one.
- **The doctrine** at the file's top binds the writing: the scene first; at most one lore touch per scene, none in a climax's action beats, never one that answers the scene's question; the tenth shown, through a person, an object or a habit, never a narrator's paragraph; double duty (a touch also characterises, raises stakes or sets the place); received, not revealed (the arc wins every conflict and the lore is corrected to it); no new plot; the fixed numbers hold; one new entry per chapter at most.
- **`/lore`** (`.claude/skills/lore/SKILL.md`) tells him `common` entries in a House voice, paraphrases around unspoken names, never tells a `learned` entry before the page has opened it, and writes any invention into the file as a `common` entry before telling it. A telling binds the world, never the story.
- **What the engine enforces:** `check` parses the Annals (unique ids, known kinds, tiers, a spoken field) and notes any capitalised name in the current chapter that no registry knows (codex, cast, places, factions, the Annals, the game's own words); `saga.py names --all` lists them for every chapter. Spoken entries reach the reader only through the page and `codex.md` (its `Songs and verses` and `Histories` sections), never from this file.
