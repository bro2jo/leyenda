# The tales — the past, a piece at a time (GM only)

*What this file is.* The tales are the saga's side narrative: the past of Darrow, of Rae, and of the people around them, told in pieces and paid for by the works (`real/projects.json`; `darrow.py project`). Each task he reports done owes one piece; the last piece of a tale leaves a keepsake on someone's page. The rules are `design.md` §12. This file holds the plans: one `## tale.<id> — Title` per tale, its fields on one line (`of`: whose past, a character id · `when`: the opening line, reader-safe, which goes on the page the day the tale is bound · `pieces`: how many · `fits`: the task counts it can be told in, as a range (`2–4`; default: its pieces only) · `flex`: how it re-cuts to another count (which piece splits, which two merge), printed in the brief when the count differs · `keepsake`: what the last piece leaves and to whom, when the project finished in its week · `keepsake_late`: the lesser keepsake, smaller and plainer, when the project finished after its due day (`real/projects.json → due`, the Saturday of the week it was first planned; the brief says which applies, the engine marks the record) · `plants`: what it may set down for the present, threads by number (`thread 4`) and the Annals as `lore:<id>` · `never`: what it must not say), a paragraph on what the tale is, then `### piece K — Title` with each piece's want, turn and last image. `saga.py tale bind <project>` takes the first unbound plan with a piece per task (or `--tale`); `saga.py brief --tale` prints the piece owed, and the plan may be edited until that piece is written. A plan's pieces may be re-cut to fit a project (a piece per task); a piece told stays told.

**Rules of the past.** A tale never reveals a truth (T1–T8, `arc.md`) ahead of its schedule; it may plant, and the plant counts like any other (two on the page before a reveal). It never contradicts a known fact on a person's page, and never makes the present cheaper: nothing of the House, no future, no foreshadowing said aloud; the reader may feel the echo, the prose never points at it. Close third on its own person, past tense, a whole small story in every piece. Close third on its own person: Darrow, Rae, or a companion whose past the plan opens (`of` names them; never Maelis's or Wren's before their truths are on the page). Names: the page's, the plan's, or none; a new person needs a base file first; a place not in `places.json` is described, not named. The tales should alternate, over the months, between Darrow's past and someone else's (Rae's most often): the plans say whose each is, and the order of the unbound plans below is the order a plain `tale bind` takes them. A resting tale is resumed before a new one is bound.

## tale.quickstep — Where Not to Stand
- **of:** darrow · **when:** Edgemoor and the Lowmarch, in the years before the Ninth was his · **pieces:** 3 · **fits:** 3–4 · **flex:** 4: piece 2 splits in two: the grey broken on the hill with the filly in the thistles, then the ride to the muster and the sergeant's question · **keepsake:** the name Quickstep, to darrow: the name the Ninth gave him for the feet, said down the whole line and answered to, spoken on the page for the first time in this tale and not heard since the river · **keepsake_late:** a name said once, to darrow: the word said by one man in a yard, which he pretended not to hear and which did not follow him out of it; what the Ninth came to call him stays untold
- **plants:** thread 15 (the grey: how a grey came to him, and her name for what she stood in; nothing of her after the Wend) · thread 11 (the Ninth sang on every march, and one sang worst) · lore:realm.edgemoor · lore:realm.lances
- **never:** what the Grace is or where it comes from (T1, T7); anything of Aldric's seal or foreknowledge (T2, T3); anything that explains Harrow Ford; the grey after the river

How a horse-breeder's son from the Edgemoor hills got the fastest feet in the Lowmarch and the name that went with them, given by the men he would later count back. The feet are the point: every piece shows him moving the way the present cannot, without once saying so. The tale ends on the name, and the reader hears the knee in it.

### piece 1 — The Breeder's Son
A boy of ten or twelve at his father's paddock, walking at a horse's head before he can argue (lore:realm.edgemoor); the greys that go better uphill; the one thing he can do that the older boys cannot: get out of the way of a horse that means him harm, on his feet, in the mud. Want: to be let on the horse nobody can sit. Turn: he gets on it by not being where it expects him. Last image: his father saying nothing and opening the gate for him. A grey mare is in the far field, the one he will ride off the hill.

### piece 2 — The Grey
The day he rides the grey mare off the hill to the Lowmarch muster, a boy of sixteen or so with his father's brand on her quarter. Want: to go. Turn: a lance's sergeant watches him lead the mare through a line of levy boys and billhooks without touching one, and asks whose son he is, and he says his own. Last image: the two of them on the Holloway at dusk, his hand at her head. Plant: a lance buys Edgemoor greys and brands its own (lore:realm.lances); a grey filly out of this mare, standing in the thistles at the edge of the far field to be left alone, whom he has already named for it (the page has her name; nothing of where she goes).

### piece 3 — The Name
Years on: a new knight of the Ninth, not yet its captain, in a yard at a fair or a practice ring, turning a bigger man before the man knows he has been turned; the Ninth watching; the youngest of them then (unnamed; Tobin is not yet in the Ninth) says the word first, and the sergeant makes it stick. Want: to be seen. Turn: the name is given by the men, never asked for; he pretends to hate it and does not. Last image: the name said down the line as they ride out, and him answering to it. Keepsake: *Quickstep*. The piece ends on the name. Late: the word is said once, by one man, in the yard, and nobody takes it up; he rides out with the Ninth and no name.

## tale.thorne_road — Road's the Road
- **of:** rae-thorne · **when:** The Holloway, when Rae Thorne was small enough to ride the box · **pieces:** 3 · **keepsake:** the ox-bell at her belt, to rae-thorne: her grandmother's bell, which the page has heard ring at the foot of the Steps without saying whose it was · **keepsake_late:** the word for stand, to rae-thorne: one clan word of her grandmother's, kept for the oxen; the bell's story stays untold
- **plants:** thread 4 (her grandmother's stories of what walks out of the grey country east of the Wend, told to frighten a child, and not believed) · thread 14 (Rae on the page: what the road made of her) · lore:craft.carting · lore:realm.holloway · lore:realm.coldmere
- **never:** her father and the winch-cage; the forty-one bundles; the grey mare and the fair; the Market and any crate; the debt on the cart; the truth of the clans or the grey country (T4, T5); her grandmother's clan beyond "Thornwild-born, come over the Wend with a drover"

Rae as a girl on the box of her grandmother's cart: how the road's law got into her, how the name wore smooth, where the bell came from. The grandmother is the voice: a few clan words for the oxen, a story at every milestone, and the rule for the Elbow. Nothing of the House, nothing of knights but one who tries to pass on the Elbow and learns. Close third on the girl; the grandmother stays unnamed on the page unless a piece needs her name (then a base file first).

### piece 1 — The Elbow
Want: to drive. The grandmother lets her hold the lines on the straight and takes them back at the Elbow, where nobody passes a cart (lore:craft.carting). A rider in a lance's colours tries; the grandmother does not move the cart an inch, and tells the girl why with the one saying: road's the road. Turn: the girl understands that the road has a law and the law is the bell. Last image: the rider waiting behind them all the way down, and the girl not looking back.

### piece 2 — The Bell-House
Coldmere's bell-house (lore:realm.coldmere); the manifest read right way up by the clerk and upside down by the grandmother, who cannot write and reads a manifest better than he does; the inn hearing everything; the girl learning the Thornwild word for stand, and that Coldmere calls the family "the Thornwild woman's" and the words are wearing down. Want: to be from somewhere. Turn: she decides the worn-down name is hers. Last image: the oxen standing to the clan word, and the clerk pretending not to have heard it. Plant: the grey country east of the Wend in one of the grandmother's stories, told to frighten, and not believed.

### piece 3 — A Carter Without a Bell
The grandmother's last run, or the day she hands over the lines for good; the bell comes off her belt and onto the girl's: a carter without a bell is a thief until proved otherwise (lore:realm.holloway). Want: to not take it yet. Turn: she takes it, because the road is the road. Last image: the bell ringing on the first jolt, and the girl not hearing it. Keepsake: the ox-bell. The piece ends on it. Late: the grandmother keeps the bell this once more, and gives the girl the word for stand instead; the bell's own day is not this one.

## tale.rae_shut — Carried Shut
- **of:** rae-thorne · **when:** The Holloway, when Rae Thorne was twelve and the cart was still her grandmother's · **pieces:** 3 · **fits:** 2–4 · **flex:** 2: pieces 2 and 3 merge (the seal broken on the road, and the next month's parcel going by another cart, told as one day and its sequel in a line); 4: piece 2 splits: the night she does not open it, then the morning she does · **keepsake:** the carter's rule, to rae-thorne: what is carried is carried shut, in her grandmother's words, said once on the box and never again because it never needed saying · **keepsake_late:** the broken seal, to rae-thorne: a thumb of red wax she kept, the one thing she ever opened; the rule stays unsaid
- **plants:** thread 14 (what the road made of her: the habit the page has seen, a load weighed and never opened, and where it came from) · lore:craft.carting · lore:realm.holloway · lore:realm.coldmere
- **never:** what she carries now or for whom; any crate, any market, any lamp; the cold in a cart-bed; her father and the winch-cage; the forty-one; the grey mare; the debt on the cart; her uncle as anything but a good host; the clans' truth or the grey country beyond a grandmother's story; reading or writing (whether she can read is not this tale's to say)

Rae at twelve, on the box beside her grandmother, asked by a woman at the barge landing to carry a small sealed parcel up the Holloway to a farm above the Elbow. She opens it. Nothing in it is worth the opening (bread, a twist of salt, a ribbon), and the seal cannot be mended. The woman at the farm says nothing; next month her parcel comes up the road on another cart. The tale is where Rae's rule came from, and the lie it grew into (what you never open cannot cost you): the reader who knows her now feels it, and the prose never points at it. Nothing of the present; the grandmother is the voice, as in Road's the Road, and stays unnamed.

### piece 1 — The Parcel
The landing, the barges, the woman with the parcel and its seal, and the grandmother taking it as she takes everything, by weight, without a question. Want: to know what is in it. Turn: the grandmother puts it in Rae's lap to hold, which is trust, and Rae knows it. Last image: the parcel on her knees, warm from the woman's hands, all the way to the seventh milestone.

### piece 2 — The Seal
Night at a carters' stop below the Elbow; the grandmother asleep under the cart; Rae with the parcel and a thumbnail. Want: to see. Turn: she opens it, and there is nothing in it worth a broken seal, and the wax will not go back. Last image: the bread, the salt and the ribbon wrapped up again as well as a child can, and the seal in two pieces in her fist.

### piece 3 — Next Month
The farm above the Elbow takes the parcel without a word and pays; the grandmother sees the seal and says nothing either. A month on, at the landing, the woman's next parcel goes up the road on another carter's cart, and she does not look at theirs. Want: to be forgiven. Turn: nobody says anything, and that is the cost, and the grandmother tells her the rule once, on the box, not as a scolding. Last image: the rule said, and Rae's hands on the lines. Keepsake: the carter's rule. The piece ends on it. Late: the grandmother says nothing at all, and Rae keeps the broken seal instead, and the rule is a thing she makes for herself and never hears said.

## tale.two_boys — Two Boys Holding Horses
- **of:** darrow · **when:** Calden, in the years when he and Aldric Vane held other men's horses · **pieces:** 3 · **fits:** 2–4 · **flex:** 2: pieces 1 and 2 merge into one summer (the muster and the race, the hand shown at the end of it); 4: piece 3 splits: the vigil the night before, then the chapel and the hand · **keepsake:** the open hand, to darrow: the lance signal for ride wide, which Aldric Vane showed him first as a boy and said to him last at the ford · **keepsake_late:** a wager unpaid, to darrow: the copper from the race Aldric Vane would not win, never paid and never asked for
- **plants:** thread 1 (Aldric's "ride wide" and the open hand, as a boy's lesson; nothing of a heartbeat early) · lore:realm.lances · lore:realm.calden · lore:realm.oath
- **never:** Aldric's foreknowledge or his seal (T2, T3); what the Oath takes (T1); Aldric's sister by name; any judgment of Aldric the page has not earned: he is loving here, and he believes

How Darrow of Edgemoor and Aldric Vane came to be knee to knee: two squires of different fathers holding horses in Calden's yards, one with the better seat and the better manners, one with the feet; what each could do that the other could not; the morning they were knighted side by side in a cold chapel with their palms on the same warming stone. The present stays in the reader's mind and out of the prose. Aldric must be likeable here: the tale is what Darrow lost.

### piece 1 — Other Men's Horses
Two boys of fourteen holding horses at a muster in Calden's yards, assigned to a knight apiece; a horse bolts, Darrow stops it on foot, Aldric stops the knight's temper with manners; each sees what the other has. Want: to be noticed by the knights. Turn: they notice each other instead. Last image: Aldric showing him the open hand swept outward, the signal he learned from his father's lance, and what it means: give the flank room (lore:realm.lances).

### piece 2 — The Better Seat
A ride or a race in the river country: Aldric's seat against Darrow's feet; a wager; a fall that could be either's and is Darrow's, and Aldric, who could have won, pulling up. Want: to beat him. Turn: Darrow learns Aldric will not win a thing that way, and trusts him for it. Last image: the two of them walking the horses back knee to knee, and one word between them that the page does not give.

### piece 3 — The Cold Chapel
The morning of the knighting: the stair climbed on the knees (lore:realm.calden), the cold chapel, the Oathstone warming under their palms, the short words (lore:realm.oath, received as a frightened boy receives them, never glossed), Aldric's shoulder against his. Want: to be sworn. Turn: Darrow feels the warmth come up through his hands and does not ask where it comes from; Aldric, beside him, kneels correctly and watches that Darrow does too. Last image: outside, Aldric's open hand swept outward across the morning: ride wide. Keepsake: the open hand. The piece ends on it. Late: Aldric reminds him of the copper from the race, laughing, and does not take it; the hand stays at his side.

## tale.hollis_table — Knucklebones
- **of:** hollis-garrow · **when:** An inn yard on the Holloway, the night the Sixth and the Ninth shared it · **pieces:** 2 · **fits:** 2–3 · **flex:** 3: piece 2 splits: the story Darrow pays, then the one Hollis pays back, which the table did not expect of him · **keepsake:** a story told true, to darrow: the stake the Sixth plays knucklebones for, which Hollis Garrow taught him across a table and the page has seen him pay since · **keepsake_late:** the stone on the back of the hand, to darrow: the catch Hollis Garrow showed him, a trick and not a stake
- **plants:** thread 13 (Hollis: the insult as affection; the kind lie to the weak, never to the strong) · lore:rhyme.knucklebones · lore:saying.soldiers
- **never:** Hollis's leg, or anything of what the Sixth will cost him; his drink as a wound (he drinks as soldiers drink, and the prose never leans on it); the writ, the chair, the ford; Tobin's fate

Hollis Garrow on two legs and a full voice, the evening a horse-breeder's son of the Ninth turned him in an inn yard in front of the Sixth: the old captain's side of the first game of knucklebones between them, close third on Hollis. He sits down meaning to take the boy down a peg and gets up having decided what kind of man he is, by the Sixth's own stake (no coin; a story told true, and the table decides whether it was). It is the same night as the Ninth on the Road's third piece, seen from the other side of the table: whichever is told second touches the other in a line and never retells it (`style.md` §2). The reader feels the echo; the prose never points at it.

### piece 1 — The Stake
Want: to win back at a table what he lost in the yard. Turn: he loses the game as well, finds he is glad of it, and names the stake to see what the boy will do with it. Last image: Hollis saying "pay, then," and the table going quiet.

### piece 2 — Paid
The young knight tells a story true (something from Edgemoor; never the grey's fate), plainly and without improving it; Hollis, who meant to pay back with a good lie, tells his own true instead, which the Sixth at the table has never heard him do. Want: to know whether the boy lied. Turn: Hollis applies his one rule (he lies kindly to the weak and never to the strong) and finds he has already applied it: he told the truth, so he has decided what the boy is. Last image: the stake named as the Sixth names it, and the boy saying it back. Keepsake: a story told true. The piece ends on it. Late: Hollis pays in a trick instead of a story, the stone on the back of the hand, and the stake's name waits for another table.

## tale.ninth_road — The Ninth on the Road
- **of:** darrow · **when:** The Holloway, the summer before the river · **pieces:** 4 · **fits:** 3–5 · **flex:** 3: pieces 2 and 3 merge (Tobin singing on the road the Sixth comes up behind); 5: piece 4 splits: the line already won, then the road home and Tobin's verse · **keepsake:** Tobin's verse, to darrow: the verse of the Grey Mare that nobody agrees on, as Tobin Marsh sang it, badly, the whole length of the Holloway · **keepsake_late:** two lines of Tobin's verse, to darrow: the two lines he can still hear; the rest went down the road with the singer
- **plants:** thread 11 (Tobin: nineteen, the gap in his teeth, the singing) · thread 13 (Hollis of the Sixth: the insult that is instruction; nothing of a chair) · lore:song.greymare · lore:realm.levies · lore:realm.holloway · lore:realm.lances
- **never:** the ford, the writ, the clans (T5); the Grace as anything but warmth; Hollis's leg; Tobin's death said or foreshadowed; Rae; Aldric's seal

The Ninth on a summer march up the Holloway to hold a line the Lances had already won: the levies' song coming up the road before the levy, Tobin singing the Grey Mare worse than anyone and more often, the Sixth sharing the road and Hollis Garrow sharing his opinion of the Ninth, a captain's habits (the signals; the fist with two fingers out he hated to give). Four small days of a lance that was whole. The reader knows what the prose never says.

### piece 1 — The Long Peal
The bell-house rings the long peal (lore:realm.levies); the Ninth rides out of the Lowmarch muster; Darrow's first morning as its captain in the field; the signals. Want: to not have to give the hold signal. Turn: he gives it, once, and the Ninth stops dead, and he hates how well it works. Last image: the levy singing up the road before anyone sees it.

### piece 2 — The Worst Singer in the Lowmarch
Tobin Marsh, newest and youngest, singing the Grey Mare off the key the whole road (lore:song.greymare); the Ninth trying to stop him and failing; the gap in his teeth; Darrow deciding not to stop him. Want: quiet. Turn: Darrow learns the lance sings better with the worst singer in it. Last image: sixty men on the verse nobody agrees on, every one of them wrong.

### piece 3 — The Sixth Shares the Road
The Sixth comes up behind them at a milestone (lore:realm.holloway); Hollis Garrow, two legs and a full voice, on the Ninth, on fast lances, on horse-breeders' sons; the first insult, which is the first lesson. Want: to answer back. Turn: Darrow answers with the feet, in a yard at an inn, and Hollis buys the drink and does not say he was wrong. Last image: Hollis's "Captain", said for the first time, to mock him.

### piece 4 — The Line Already Won
The line they were sent to hold, already won when they arrive; the levy boys with billhooks going home by harvest; the Ninth turning for home singing; Tobin's verse, the one he made up, the only verse the Ninth ever agreed on, because it was his. Want: to have done something. Turn: Darrow learns that a lance that comes home whole has done the thing. Last image: Tobin's verse, given in full on the page (original lines, in the Grey Mare's metre), and Darrow riding at the head not singing and knowing every word. Keepsake: Tobin's verse. The piece ends on it. Late: the wind takes most of it; two lines reach the head of the column, and those are the two the page gives.

## tale.rae_salt — Salt Goes Up
- **of:** rae-thorne · **when:** The barge landing below Coldmere, Rae Thorne's first winter driving alone · **pieces:** 3 · **fits:** 2–4 · **flex:** 2: pieces 2 and 3 merge (the fog on the road and the song at the gate, one evening); 4: piece 1 splits: the landing that will not make room, then the old carter who does · **keepsake:** the second verse of Salt Goes Up, to rae-thorne: hers to start, in any line of carts on the Holloway, since the winter the farms first heard it in her voice · **keepsake_late:** a place in the line, to rae-thorne: the old carter's cart moved a length to let hers in at the landing, every time after; the song stays the carters'
- **plants:** thread 14 (Rae starts the singing and denies it: where that came from) · lore:song.salt · lore:craft.carting · lore:beast.greywater · lore:realm.holloway
- **never:** what she carries now or for whom; any crate, market or lamp; the cold in a cart-bed; her father and the winch-cage; the forty-one; the grey mare; the debt on the cart; her uncle as anything but a good host; the grandmother's death (she is off the box, not gone, unless the page has said otherwise by then)

Rae's first winter on the box alone, a girl with her grandmother's bell among carters who have driven the Holloway for thirty years and do not make room. The road teaches her its law the way the Elbow did, and the carters teach her their song: the first verse is the road's, the second is the one the farms know. By the end she is the one who starts the second verse, and pretends she did not. Close third on the girl; one old carter (unnamed) is the voice the grandmother was.

### piece 1 — The Landing
The barge landing at first light, the carts in a line for the salt, and nobody making room for a girl's cart. Want: a place in the line. Turn: she gets one by being the cart nobody has to watch (her load stowed right, her oxen standing to her grandmother's word), and an old carter moves his cart a length without looking at her. Last image: her bell and his, one after the other, going up from the landing.

### piece 2 — The Fog
The line coming up the Holloway in a fog off the river so thick a cart is only its bell; the old carter's load shifts on the Elbow and his near wheel goes into the bank. Want: to be home by dark. Turn: she stands her oxen and reads his load by how it hangs, and says the one sentence that rights it, and loses the daylight doing it. Last image: the carts behind them in the fog, standing, every bell going.

### piece 3 — The Gate
Evening at a farm gate above the Elbow, the line stopped to water; the farm comes to the gate with its hands in its shawls at the sound of the bells; the carters sing the first verse and stop where the second should be, because the old carter's voice has gone with the cold. Want: to not be looked at. Turn: she starts the second verse, alone, badly, and the line takes it up. Last image: the farm going back in, satisfied: it was only salt. Keepsake: the second verse, hers to start. The piece ends on it. Late: nobody starts the verse; the old carter moves his cart a length for hers at the next landing, and every landing after, and that is what she keeps.
