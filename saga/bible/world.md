# THE UNKNEELING — World Bible

> *What is given can be taken. What is built is yours.*
> — the First Precept of the Emberwardens, carved over a door no one uses anymore

This file is canon. Anything invented during play that recurs goes into `saga/state/codex.md`, and wins over memory. Spoilers live in `_gm/arc.md`; nothing in this file gives the twists away.

---

## 1. The premise in one breath

For three hundred years every knight of the Vaelmark has knelt at an Oathstone, sworn to the Crown, and risen with **the Grace**: strength past mortal limits, granted on the spot. At the Battle of Harrow Ford the Grace went out of every sworn knight on the field at once, for three heartbeats. Two hundred knights died in those heartbeats. Ser Darrow, Knight-Captain of the Ninth Lance, was turning to cover his flank when his left knee gave way under him. No blade touched him.

The Crown says the Grace left because the knights were faithless. The survivors are being rounded up to kneel again. Darrow was carried up the Thousand Steps to the House of Menders, where a surgeon rebuilt his knee with a strand of his own sinew and a thread of something older than the Crown.

Now he cannot kneel. And a knight who cannot kneel cannot be sworn, which means, for the first time in his life, he cannot be bound.

## 2. The two powers

### The Grace (given)
- Received by kneeling at an **Oathstone** and swearing the Oath of the Bended Knee. Every Oathstone answers to **the First Stone** in the Oathspire at Calden.
- Grants the **Oathsworn** immediate, enormous strength, speed and endurance. A squire sworn this morning can fight like a veteran by nightfall.
- It feels like warmth poured in from outside. Knights speak of being "full of Grace", of "the Crown's hand on my back."
- It can be withdrawn. Every knight knows this in the way people know they will die: true, and not to be thought about.
- Grace-sworn knights cannot see the Reckoning (below). There is nothing of their own to count.

### The Ember (built)
- The old power, from before the Crown. It is kindled by the body's own work, fed by what sustains it, and kept by discipline. It grows slowly and is never granted. Once built, no one can take it back.
- Its practitioners were **the Emberwardens**, a small order of knights and healers. One Warden was worth ten men, but a Warden took ten years to make.
- Three centuries ago King Aurel raised the Oathstones, and a thousand Oathsworn outmatched a hundred Wardens. The Wardens were declared heretics in **the Quenching**: their halls were burned, their script outlawed, their name made a curse ("ember-mad" still means a fanatic in the lowlands).
- One who has kindled the Ember can see **the Reckoning**, a script of pale light that shows the measure of a person: their Might, Vigor, Finesse and Resolve, the state of their Ember, the Arts they have earned. The Reckoning cannot be flattered and does not lie. (In story terms this is the LitRPG status screen. It is diegetic.)
- The Wardens' maxim, still spoken by Menders who don't know where it comes from: ***Time makes it possible. Proof makes it permitted.***

The tension of the whole saga lives in the gap between the two powers. The Grace is fast and borrowed; the Ember is slow and owned. Every shortcut in this world is real and tempting, and each one has a price that comes due later.

## 3. The Binding

Darrow's knee was not healed by Grace. **Maelis Vorne** took a strand from the sinew below his kneecap and wove it into the ruined joint with **emberthread**, a Warden art outlawed for three hundred years.

- *"It is made of you now. It will be exactly as strong as you make it, and not one hair stronger."*
- The Binding passes through a **soft season**. The thread loosens before it sets, and it is weakest at the very time the knee begins to feel strong. Knights who trusted the quiet have torn their Bindings and never walked right again. Maelis has seen it happen.
- The Binding is secured by **Seven Knots**. A knot is not tied by time; it is tied when the knee proves it. Each tied knot opens a new part of the world to Darrow (see `mechanics.md`).

| Knot | Name | What it proves |
|---|---|---|
| I | The Knot of Straightening | the leg can be made straight |
| II | The Knot of Walking | the stride is true again |
| III | The Knot of Standing | the leg bears load; the descent of the Thousand Steps |
| IV | The Knot of the Road | it can run |
| V | The Knot of Lightning | it can leap, land, sprint and stop |
| VI | The Knot of Turning | it can turn at speed: the movement that broke it |
| VII | The Knot of the Field | it is whole for battle |

When the saga opens, Knots I and II are tied.

## 4. Geography of the Vaelmark

```
              GREYWATER PEAKS
         Saint Ysolde's (House of Menders)
               │  the Thousand Steps
   SALTREACH ── THE LOWMARCH ─────── the river Wend ──► HARROW FORD ──► THE THORNWILD
   (coast)      (farms, mills,          │                                (forest clans)
                 Holloway road)         │       THE ASHEN FIELDS
                                        │       (grey waste where
                                     CALDEN      the Hollowed drift)
                                 (the Oathspire)
```

- **Saint Ysolde's, the House of Menders.** A hospice cut into a mountain shoulder above the cloud line: bell towers, hot grey springs, a cloister gallery, an infirmary hall with forty cots, a winch-cage for supplies, and below it all an undercroft older than the House. Reached only by **the Thousand Steps**, a switchback stair cut into the cliff (there are actually 1,117; Wren has counted). By ancient right, **no blade may climb the Steps while the sanctuary bell is rung.**
- **The Lowmarch.** River farmland, mill towns, grey stone villages, the **Holloway** road to the capital. The heart of the realm's grain and its levies.
- **The river Wend.** Cold and fast, it runs east through the Lowmarch to the border.
- **Harrow Ford.** A broad shallow crossing of the Wend at the edge of the Thornwild. Standing stones on both banks. Where it broke.
- **The Thornwild.** Old forest beyond the Wend, home to the **clans** (Corrach, Haskel, the Nine Rivers and others). The Crown calls them raiders and witches.
- **The Ashen Fields.** A wide grey waste between the Wend and the hills, where nothing grows well and the **Hollowed** wander. The Crown says clan witchery made it so.
- **Calden.** The capital, white stone, a hundred bells. At its center the **Oathspire**, a tower of black glass and iron where the First Stone sits and King Aurel holds court.
- **Saltreach.** A coastal city of fog and smugglers, home to the **Lantern Market**, where anything can be bought, including names, writs and Warden script.

The realm's season follows the real one. The saga opens in autumn, with first snow on the Steps.

## 5. Peoples and factions

| Faction | What they want | Where they stand at the start |
|---|---|---|
| **The Crown** (King Aurel the Evergreen, the Oathspire) | order; continuity; that nothing change | distant, absolute; the king has not been seen in public for nine years |
| **The Confessors** (Knight-Confessor Ser Aldric Vane) | to bring the "Faithless of Harrow Ford" back to kneel | hunting survivors; sending writs up the Steps |
| **The Menders of Saint Ysolde** (Mother Ione, Maelis Vorne) | to heal anyone who climbs the Steps; to keep sanctuary | sheltering the wounded; quietly afraid |
| **The Faithless** (the Harrow survivors) | the truth about the Three Heartbeats; to not be taken | scattered, wounded, frightened, angry |
| **The Thornwild clans** (Corrach and others) | to survive something the Vaelmark won't name | blamed for Harrow Ford |
| **The Lantern Market** (Saltreach) | profit; information | sells to everyone |
| **The Stonewrights** | — | long gone; they made the Oathstones; their ruins remain |

## 6. Customs and texture

- **Kneeling is everything.** Knights are sworn kneeling, marry kneeling, are knighted kneeling, die (it is hoped) kneeling. "To bend the knee" is the common phrase for loyalty. "Stiff-kneed" is an insult meaning proud or traitorous. A knight who cannot kneel is a joke, a scandal, or a threat, depending on who is watching.
- **Lances.** Companies of sixty knights and their squires, numbered. The Ninth Lance was Darrow's. Nineteen of its sixty came back from the ford.
- **Bells.** The Vaelmark tells time, mourning and law by bells. The House of Menders rings the hours; the **sanctuary bell** (called *Old Mercy*) is rung only when the House claims protection.
- **The Hollowed.** Knights who have lost not just their Grace but their selves: breathing, walking, obeying simple commands, gone behind the eyes. Families are told that the Hollowed are victims of clan witchcraft. Many end their days in houses like Saint Ysolde's, on the far cots, where the novices are sent to feed them.
- **Warden script.** An angular script of strokes and dots, outlawed. Old doors and lintels in the House still carry it under the plaster.
- **Oaths and names.** Knights are "Ser" (all genders). Menders are "Sister/Brother"; the senior surgeon is "Mender". Clan people use given name plus clan ("Ilse of Corrach").
- **Swearing.** Common oaths: "Stone and Crown", "Bells take it", "Wend-cold" (meaning terrible), "kneel to that" (agreement, ironic among the Faithless).

## 8. Tone

Grounded, northern, cold-weather fantasy: weathered stone, wet wool, bells, hot springs steaming in snow. Somber with real warmth and real jokes; the people in it are funny the way soldiers and nurses are funny. Magic is rare, costly and physical. Violence is fast, frightening and has consequences. Think the companions and moral weight of *Baldur's Gate 3*, the grit of *The First Law*, the patience and intimacy of *The Curse of Chalion*, the visible stats and progression of LitRPG, and the hard-won body of a sports comeback story that never once says "sports."
