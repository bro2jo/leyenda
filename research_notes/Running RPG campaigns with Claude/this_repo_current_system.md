## 1. Arc and chapter planning: Books, chapters, slots, beats, roads, quests, devices, climaxes, Knots, pace

### Takeaway
The story is planned top-down in three layers — a hand-written GM arc (Books → core beats / quests / transitions, addressed by ids), a per-week chapter plan of seven dated "slots" plus a climax, and per-slot one-line `plan` / `turn` / `tone` fields — and it is clocked to the real world: one chapter per calendar week, one scene per logged day, and a Book can only end when a real rehab gate ("Knot") is recorded. A computed "Pace" line tells the planner whether to stretch (insert side-quest slots) or compress (two beats per chapter).

### Cited Findings
- **Layers and ids.** The GM arc is one Markdown file whose headings begin with ids the engine parses: core beats `bN.K`, quests `qN.<name>`, Book transitions `tN` with three variants `tN.high|main|low`, `bookN.question`, `bookN.tone`, plus `pacing`, `truths`, `motifs`, `beliefs`, `temptation`, `flags`, and a standing relationship quest `rae.<…>` — [engine/saga.py:961-992 `ID_RE`, `arc_sections`](/home/user/leyenda/engine/saga.py). Any section is printed on demand, capped at 40 lines — [engine/saga.py:1002 `print_arc`](/home/user/leyenda/engine/saga.py).
- **Plan file shape** (inferred from code that reads/writes it; file not opened): `position {book, chapter, beat, next_scene, stage ∈ scene|climax|choice|transition, last_written, rank_on_page}`, `chapter {number, week_start (a Sunday), question, slots[], world_moves[], climax{plan, tone, checks[], options[2–3], default}, previous_devices}`, `core_beats {id: {title, status ∈ planned|in_progress|done|folded}}`, `quests {id: {status ∈ available|live|done|dropped, stage, priority ∈ required|optional|floating, gate, stage_gates, standing, project, work[]}}`, `flags {name: true|false|null}`, `factions {id: −100..100}`, `route {bookN: {chapters[], road, override, transition_chapter, knot_window{earliest, likely, why}}}`, `companions {id: {prefers[poles]}}`, `companions_to_come {id: {approval_pending, prefers, note, bond}}`, `invite_rules`, `tallies`, `temptation`, `open_micro`, `open_choice`, `answered_choice`, `tales[]`, `saga {finale_book, chapters_per_book}` — [engine/saga.py:172-190 constants; 2703-3187 `cmd_plan`; 4177-4212 `cmd_check`](/home/user/leyenda/engine/saga.py).
- **Slot shape.** `{n, day, kind ∈ spine|quest|interlude|cutaway, beat | quest+stage | pov, plan, turn, tone, micro, challenge, invite, float, status ∈ planned|next|written|skipped, wrote, world_move, last}` — [engine/saga.py:174-175, 1121 `SLOT_FIELDS`, 2921-2928](/home/user/leyenda/engine/saga.py). `turn` is defined as "who can, must, believes, risks or chooses what by the end that they could not at the start, and what Darrow attempts"; `tone` is a one- or two-word register — [engine/saga.py:1122-1124 `TURN_HINT`, `TONE_HINT`](/home/user/leyenda/engine/saga.py).
- **Scene kinds.** spine (advances the chapter's core beat; the default; ≥2 per chapter; Saturday's slot always spine), quest (one stage of a side quest or of the Tether; ≤2 stages of one quest per chapter), interlude (another POV, ≤1 per chapter, not tied to a day), cutaway (a plan-directed rest day: the world moves without him, "never a setback frame") — [saga/bible/style.md §3 L129](/home/user/leyenda/saga/bible/style.md); enforced in [engine/saga.py:4316-4328, 4548-4554](/home/user/leyenda/engine/saga.py).
- **The chapter-planning procedure** (`/chapter`): (1) pull, `saga.py now`; if the last climax closed a Book, `plan book open N+1`, write/refresh the Book's tone section and each companion's GM "Now" line, set the Knot window with `plan pace`; (2) `saga.py brief --chapter`; (2b) prune the GM character notes (`saga.py notes`) and rewrite or void callbacks "offered six times and never fired"; (3) fill the template in a scratch file: a one-line `question`; per slot a one-line `plan` ("an event, an arrival, a discovery, a decision; never a mood"), `turn`, `tone`; the climax plan with `checks`, 2–3 `options` "each right for someone and costly for someone" and a `default` ("the option the highest-approval companion present would pick; never one tagged *[Inspiration]*"); small choices "two or three a week at most, never on neighbouring days"; devices only where the story wants one ("one or two invitations a week are wanted"); three `world_moves` for missed days; Book-level fixed elements the planner must place (an interlude from the antagonist side in a named beat's week; a planned loss that lands "in every tier, priced by the tier", with its plant a chapter earlier and the following chapter "warm by plan"); (4) `plan chapter open --number N '<json>'`, create the chapter file with title and in-world epigraph, `check`, `ship`; (5) later edits via `plan slot N key=value` — [.claude/skills/chapter/SKILL.md L12-27](/home/user/leyenda/.claude/skills/chapter/SKILL.md).
- **Hard planning rules named by the skill** ("because canon or the real world depends on them"): a truth is revealed only when its thread shows two plants on the page; a Tether stage only in order and once its gate is met; a challenge only on an Art the page has named; an invitation only from someone on the page, alive, where Darrow is, with a `why_now`. "Everything else … is advice." — [.claude/skills/chapter/SKILL.md L25](/home/user/leyenda/.claude/skills/chapter/SKILL.md).
- **What the planning brief provides** (`brief --chapter`, measured at ≈18,800 chars ≈ 4,700 tokens): beats current/left; the KNOT line if a gate was tied; the Pace line; the shape rules in one line; which device kinds ran last chapter ("those kinds rest this chapter"); last chapter's tones; live quests and up to 10 candidate quests with gate state; an owed Tether stage; the open climax choice; approval rules that would be "met for free"; the Book's register section (up to 30 lines); up to 6 open threads with plant counts; the next spine beat's arc section (≤40 lines); and a JSON template with `device_allowed` marked on slots 1/3/5/7 — [engine/saga.py:5360-5437 `brief_chapter`](/home/user/leyenda/engine/saga.py).
- **The template** is seven dated slots, all spine on the Book's next core beat, except: slot 3 becomes a Tether quest slot if the sheet's Tether stage is ahead of the page, and slot 5 becomes a filler quest slot when the story is "ahead" of the Knot — [engine/saga.py:1221-1265 `chapter_template`](/home/user/leyenda/engine/saga.py).
- **`plan chapter open` behaviour**: refuses out-of-order chapter numbers, an open small choice, an Inspiration-tagged default, and a plan that would make >2 non-standing quests live; prints *notes* (not refusals) on empty turns, tone variety and repeated device kinds; carries unpaid world moves forward; drops blank world moves; marks the chapter's first spine beat `in_progress`; lapses expired callbacks — [engine/saga.py:2900-2984](/home/user/leyenda/engine/saga.py).
- **Pace against the real world.** `pace_state` = (weekly chapters from this week through `route.bookN.knot_window.likely`) − (core beats still planned/in progress). Output line: "ahead by N" → spend N weeks on the Book's quests or a floating one; "behind by N" → "two beats in a chapter where they fit (one in its week, one as its climax), never rushed; the story may trail the Ledger"; "on pace" → one core beat a chapter. The window is set by hand from the real plan's next gate (`plan pace --earliest D --likely D --why …`) — [engine/saga.py:1268-1301 `pace_state`, `pace_line`; 3150-3168](/home/user/leyenda/engine/saga.py).
- **Knots → Books.** A Knot is recorded on the real side only with clearance/measured evidence (`darrow.py knot tie`); Book N ends at Knot N+2. When the sheet's knot count reaches that, `now`/brief print a KNOT line saying when the transition falls due ("at this chapter's climax" or "in N chapters … each chapter lands at most two [beats]") — [engine/saga.py:664-684 `knot_line`](/home/user/leyenda/engine/saga.py); [CLAUDE.md golden rule 7](/home/user/leyenda/CLAUDE.md); [saga/bible/mechanics.md L44-59](/home/user/leyenda/saga/bible/mechanics.md).
- **Compress rule / beat choice.** A folded core beat's own choice can ride a daily spine slot as a "beat choice" (keeps the arc's climax weights ±2/±3, one pole per option, never on Saturday) — [engine/saga.py:4272-4294](/home/user/leyenda/engine/saga.py); [saga/bible/style.md §3 L182](/home/user/leyenda/saga/bible/style.md).
- **Roads.** Each Book's transition has three variants. `route decide --book N` scores the Book's closed chapters (+1 per Triumph week, −1 per Setback week): ≥+2 → high, ≤−2 → low, else main (in a ≤2-chapter Book the last tier decides); a manual override needs `--why` — [engine/saga.py:921-941 `road_score`, `decide_road`; 2592-2630 `cmd_route`](/home/user/leyenda/engine/saga.py).
- **Opening a Book.** `plan book open N` seeds `bN.K`/`tN` beats and `qN.*` quests from the arc headings (quest priority read from a `priority:` line), folds the previous Book's unwritten beats, drops its unrun optional quests, warns about unrun required ones, lapses callbacks, and prints a to-do for refreshing GM notes — [engine/saga.py:2985-3054](/home/user/leyenda/engine/saga.py).
- **Devices** (at most one per slot): a *challenge* `{objective, approaches[1–3]{name, stat, dc 8–20, art?}, goal 1–4, limit 1–3, progress, strain, status, result{won, lost}}` advanced by `challenge N success|fail` after each roll (counters "never reach the page"); an *invitation* `{who, want, why_now, when?, kind ∈ favour|diversion|opinion|problem, if_ignored, status}`; a *project stage* (one "project" quest per Book with a visible trace in `world.json → projects[]`) — [engine/saga.py:180-183, 3796-3871, 4357-4442](/home/user/leyenda/engine/saga.py).
- **Missed days and world moves.** A day with nothing logged gets no scene: `plan done N --skipped` pops the first planned world move onto the slot; the next scene (or the climax) "opens on" it; a skipped slot's invitation and challenge are voided — [engine/saga.py:2788-2811, 1072-1084](/home/user/leyenda/engine/saga.py). A wholly unlogged week still becomes a chapter (one interlude, no climax, no choice) via `plan chapter cutaway` — [.claude/skills/checkpoint/SKILL.md L17](/home/user/leyenda/.claude/skills/checkpoint/SKILL.md); [engine/saga.py:2871-2890](/home/user/leyenda/engine/saga.py).
- **Quest and stage gates** are expressions in the same rule grammar as consequences (e.g. a placeholder `beats.b1_1.status == "done"`); `check` refuses a planned slot whose gate is not met — [engine/saga.py:1457-1466, 4414-4420](/home/user/leyenda/engine/saga.py).

### Inferences
- The plan is deliberately thin per scene (three one-line fields) and thick per Book (the arc): scene content is fixed a week ahead, the day's real-world performance is allowed to change only tone, and the week's performance only the climax's outcome shape. Player choices bend the *next* chapter's slots (`/choose` step 6) rather than the current scene.
- Pacing is the system's most unusual constraint: the plot's end-of-Book is hostage to an external, unpredictable date, so the planner has explicit stretch (quests) and compress (two beats per chapter, beat choices, folded beats) tools rather than a fixed outline length.

### Gaps
- The arc's actual structure (how many beats per Book, what a beat section contains beyond the per-tier sketch lines, `**Choice:**` line and `Downstream` line that the brief extracts) is GM-side and was not read; only the parser's expectations are known.
- `saga/bible/_gm/design.md` is cited throughout skills and code as the full specification (§2 ledger schema, §3 Bearing ladder, §5 quests/devices, §8 Tether, §9 Betweens, §11 records, §12 tales). It is GM-side and was not read, so any rule that lives only there is missing from this map.

## 2. The brief → write → record loop

### Takeaway
Every block of prose is bracketed by two engine calls: `saga.py brief` assembles a bounded, sectioned context packet (MUST / CONTINUITY / MAY / ARC / TONE / FORMAT / RECORD skeleton) from the plan, the ledger, the per-block records and trimmed GM notes; after writing, `saga.py record` takes the filled skeleton back, validates it against the page text and registries, appends one event + one GM note, advances the plan, rebuilds all derived state ("projections") and runs `check`. The model is told "nothing else needs opening."

"Records in, projections out." — [.claude/rules/chronicle.md L12](/home/user/leyenda/.claude/rules/chronicle.md).

### Inferences
- This is an event-sourcing design applied to fiction: prose is the source of truth, a structured event per block is the index, and every "current state" view is disposable. Hand-edited state is detected (stale projection, approval mismatch, Bearing replay mismatch) rather than prevented.
- The brief functions as a retrieval layer with hand-tuned caps instead of search: relevance is decided by string-matching the slot's plan/turn for names and `lore:<id>` tags, by the callback `fits` expressions, and by thread recency — not by embedding or by the model choosing what to read.
- The evidence rule converts two classes of hallucination into hard errors: (a) derived "facts" about what someone thought/knew that the prose does not support, (b) claiming a debt was paid when the page did not pay it. It checks presence of the quoted words, not that they actually discharge the item.

### Gaps
- The record's `gm.next_must_know` and `gm.kept_off` fields are stored in the GM notes but no brief mode prints them; `cmd_brief` never calls `load_notes` (they surface only via `history --gm` and `threads --id N`) — [engine/saga.py:5050, 5739, 5852, 5954](/home/user/leyenda/engine/saga.py). Whether this is intentional (the stated channel for "the next scene must honour X" is a ledger due item with `when: next`) could not be confirmed without the design doc.

## 3. Rules and mechanics on the story side

### Takeaway
Outcomes are governed by a small set of interlocking state machines: engine-only dice whose DCs are back-solved from the week's real-world tier; companion approval and a four-axis "Bearing" moved only by recorded choices; a consequence ledger of dated debts, undated "callbacks" and standing condition→effect rules written in a tiny expression language; plot threads with plant counts; and a few counters (tallies, temptation, Tether stage, Inspiration). Each exists to hand the writer an obligation or a constraint at the right moment, never a number to print in prose.

### Cited Findings
- **Dice.** Only `darrow.py roll` casts (Python `secrets`), writes every cast to `rolls.csv`, and refuses to re-roll a label ("ALREADY ROLLED (no takebacks)"). A check is d20 + attribute modifier (+ proficiency, + an Art's rank, + the chapter tier's modifier, ± Ember effect, + bonus) vs DC; results are success, *partial* ("success at a cost"), failure, with criticals — [engine/darrow.py:2347-2389 `roll_mods`; 2439-2474 `cast`, `cmd_roll`](/home/user/leyenda/engine/darrow.py); [saga/bible/mechanics.md L106-111](/home/user/leyenda/saga/bible/mechanics.md).
- **Week → tier.** A 0–100 week score from real adherence (weights: floor 20, sessions 25, fuel 30, checks 10, weigh-ins 5, logging 10; plan-directed rest days excused) maps to Triumph ≥85 / Hard-won ≥70 / Costly ≥50 / Setback; `chapter-close` freezes it in `chapters.csv`, after which "a late log still moves totals and the live score, never the tier" — [engine/darrow.py:665-753 `week_score`; 2520-2579 `cmd_chapter_close`](/home/user/leyenda/engine/darrow.py); [engine/rules.json `week_score`](/home/user/leyenda/engine/rules.json).
- **Tier → DCs and shape.** Each tier carries a `roll_mod` (+3/+1/0/−2) and a `target_odds` (0.80/0.65/0.50/0.35 in `rules.json`); the climax brief asks `darrow.py odds --target P` for each check kind and prints the DC at which a full success has that probability given his actual modifiers ("the planned DCs are advisory"). The tier also fixes the outcome *shape*: Triumph "the plan works and something extra"; Hard-won "he wins and it costs something real"; Costly "one thing won, one lost"; Setback "the enemy gains ground and the reversal sets up the next chapter (never humiliating, never a lecture)" — [engine/saga.py:4944-5005 `climax_odds_lines`; 5183-5185](/home/user/leyenda/engine/saga.py); [engine/darrow.py:2392-2430](/home/user/leyenda/engine/darrow.py); [engine/rules.json `week_score.tiers`](/home/user/leyenda/engine/rules.json).
- **Daily deeds → tone only.** "What happens in a scene is fixed when the chapter is planned; the day's real deeds set only its colour, and the colour is Darrow's alone … nobody is warmer or shorter with him because of a day they could not know about. … A warm day never buys a victory and a cold day never spends one." — [saga/bible/style.md §1 L32](/home/user/leyenda/saga/bible/style.md).
- **Inspiration** (cap 4; earned by real-world streaks, levels, a Triumph week, a finished real project): spent by choosing an option tagged `*[Inspiration]*` (never the default; `choose` validates the entry before spending) or a reroll; a climax check that fails outright is automatically cast again once, and "a second cast never comes clean" (a success is downgraded to partial); the prose must show the first die's mark — [engine/darrow.py:2495-2509](/home/user/leyenda/engine/darrow.py); [engine/saga.py:1635-1666, 6004-6039](/home/user/leyenda/engine/saga.py); [engine/rules.json `inspiration`](/home/user/leyenda/engine/rules.json).
- **Approval / regard.** Per companion −100..+100, stored in `world.json`, moved only by ledger entries (`saga.py add` / `choose`) and the engine's own "auto" entries. Guidance: ±1–3 for a small choice, ±5–15 for a climax. **An approval delta is refused unless the companion is listed as `--witnessed` or `--learned <id>:<how>`**. Witnesses also get an automatic ±2 if the choice's Bearing move matches/opposes their `prefers` poles. Companions not yet travelling bank `approval_pending`. The brief shows regard as a word band (cold < −25, wary < 0, civil < 15, warm < 30, fond < 50, devoted) and prints only the GM "By regard" behaviour line for that band — [engine/saga.py:2012-2017, 2048-2158 `add_entry`; 4640-4647; 4672-4710](/home/user/leyenda/engine/saga.py); [.claude/skills/choose/SKILL.md L12-14](/home/user/leyenda/.claude/skills/choose/SKILL.md).
- **Approval forecast.** For every rule that reads an approval threshold, the engine projects when the companion's regard would cross it at the measured per-chapter income (Betweens, choices and engine costs separately; Betweens capped by standing) against the rule's earliest/typical chapter, with verdicts "already met / met for free / in reach / late / not at this rate"; the chapter brief repeats the "met for free" lines with the advice "raise the threshold, or let the moment come sooner" — [engine/saga.py:2285-2438](/home/user/leyenda/engine/saga.py).
- **The Bearing.** Four axes (Mercy–Flint, Candor–Guile, Hearth–Banner, Sworn–Unsworn), each −10..+10; moves are an append-only history (`why` = a ledger id or `gm:<reason>`; ≤±4 per move); values, per-pole movement, and a title ladder with hysteresis (lean gained at 4/kept until 1; named at 8/kept until 5; "mixed" when ≥6 moved each way and within ±3) are *replayed* from the history. The realm's epithet changes only when a climax or Choice block's text actually contains the next name (`EPITHET PENDING` until then). Options can be gated on Bearing or stat values and shown struck through when unmet — [engine/saga.py:324-524](/home/user/leyenda/engine/saga.py); [saga/bible/mechanics.md L113](/home/user/leyenda/saga/bible/mechanics.md); [saga/bible/style.md §2 L88](/home/user/leyenda/saga/bible/style.md).
- **Consequence ledger — entries.** `{id cNN.n, made{chapter, scene, date}, chose, now{approval, bearing, flags, factions}, due[], declined[], learned{}, inspiration, auto}`. Each due item: `{id, when, at, weight ∈ color|scene|route|fate, what, status ∈ pending|fired@<where>|void:<why>, evidence}`. `when` grammar: `next`, `later`, `chNN`, `chNN:sN`, `chNN:climax`, `bookN`, `bookN:beatK`, `bookN:climax`, `transitionN`, `finale`, `on:<flag>`, `any`; `at_status` classifies each as due / future / overdue against the position. Every flag an entry sets automatically gets a due item pointing at the arc's "Downstream" line so it is "owed, not remembered" — [engine/saga.py:214-219, 535-630, 1866-1989](/home/user/leyenda/engine/saga.py).
- **Callbacks** (`when: later`): never due or overdue; optional `fits` (rule grammar incl. `present.<id>`, `place.<id>`), `priority` 1–3, `valid_until`, `if_void {approval: {...}}`. The brief lists ≤4 that fit; a record that could have paid one and did not increments `offered`; at 6 offers it is "set aside" for the next chapter plan to rewrite or void; >10 alive is noted; a callback past `valid_until` lapses automatically "at its cost" through an engine-written entry — [engine/saga.py:184, 2441-2528, 4895-4941, 5628-5637](/home/user/leyenda/engine/saga.py).
- **Rules** (`ledger.rules[]`: `{id r.…, if, then, where, fired[], evidence{}}`). `if` is parsed with Python's `ast` restricted to boolean/comparison nodes over the roots `approval. bearing. flags. route. factions. temptation. quests. tether. beats. present. place. tally.`; a bare or unknown name is an error, not a silent false. States: **ARMED** (condition true and its moment due/overdue/any), **armed, waits** (moment later this Book), **near** (a numeric term within 15, or within 30% for small thresholds, and non-zero), **quiet**. ARMED lines are printed before due items in `now` and marked honoured with `fire r.id --where …` (a standing `where: any` rule may re-arm once per Book). Placeholder example: "rule r.bN.example is ARMED when `approval.X >= N && flags.y`" — [engine/saga.py:694-913; 1683-1715 `rule_lines`; 2169-2191 `fire_item`](/home/user/leyenda/engine/saga.py).
- **Threads**: `{id, title, truth (T#), feeds, status open|paid, who_knows, note, on_page[]}`; `on_page` is rebuilt from events' `threads:[{id, how: plant|touch|payoff}]`; the brief derives "may reveal" from ≥2 plants — [engine/records.py:394-401, 906-925](/home/user/leyenda/engine/records.py); [engine/saga.py:4872-4875](/home/user/leyenda/engine/saga.py).
- **Invitations and promises.** An invitation is one in-world sentence at the end of a day-close reply; if the player answers in voice it becomes a Between that opens on the other person's line; if not, the next scene record passes it automatically at a regard cost by kind (favour/problem −2, opinion/diversion −1). A promise is a callback with `valid_until` and `if_void` — [engine/saga.py:2495-2511, 5689-5693](/home/user/leyenda/engine/saga.py); [.claude/skills/play/SKILL.md L34](/home/user/leyenda/.claude/skills/play/SKILL.md).
- **Tallies and temptation**: named counters the GM increments by hand (`plan tally NAME +1 --why …`, `plan temptation add`), readable by rules as `tally.<name>` / `temptation.count` — [engine/saga.py:3106-3131, 788-791](/home/user/leyenda/engine/saga.py).
- **Arts, Ember, rank, the Tether (real-side inputs the story reads).** Arts are techniques ranked by days of matching real practice and gated by Knots; an Art's rank adds to checks and "an Art at rank III should visibly change how a scene plays out". Ember is a rolling 7-day fuel score with tiers that add ±1 to some checks and colour warmth/cold in prose. A rank crossing owes one prose beat (`RANK OWED`). The Tether stages (driven only by days logged as time together) each buy exactly one planned scene, in order, gated by story position; it "never moves anyone's regard" — [saga/bible/mechanics.md L63-98](/home/user/leyenda/saga/bible/mechanics.md); [engine/saga.py:709-729](/home/user/leyenda/engine/saga.py); [engine/rules.json `arts`, `ember`, `tether`](/home/user/leyenda/engine/rules.json).
- **"The knee on the page."** Each Knot in `rules.json` has an `allows` line — what the story may let the leg do — printed in every brief; "the story can never get ahead of your knee" and "must never reward Darrow for recklessness" — [engine/rules.json `knots`, `knots_about`](/home/user/leyenda/engine/rules.json); [engine/saga.py:4838-4845](/home/user/leyenda/engine/saga.py); [.claude/rules/chronicle.md L31](/home/user/leyenda/.claude/rules/chronicle.md).

### Inferences
- The mechanics are designed around *obligation surfacing*: almost every structure (due items, Downstream items, armed rules, callbacks, RANK OWED, EPITHET PENDING, owed world moves, declined options) is a queue whose head is printed in the next brief and which is cleared only with quoted evidence.
- Calibrating DCs to a target success probability decouples character growth from difficulty: bonuses never trivialise climaxes; the real-world week decides the odds, the die decides the detail.
- Witness-gated approval encodes an information model (who saw or heard) into the relationship system; it is the only place where NPC knowledge is mechanically enforced rather than left to the "who knows" text field.

### Gaps
- No command adds a rule; rules appear to be hand-authored in the GM ledger file (the engine only reads them and appends `fired`). How many exist and how they are written could not be checked without opening GM files.
- The ledger schema's intended magnitudes (approval ±1–3 / ±5–15) are stated in the skill but `validate_entry` checks only Bearing magnitudes (≤4) and Between approval caps — approval size for choices is unchecked — [engine/saga.py:1886-1907](/home/user/leyenda/engine/saga.py).

## 4. Memory and state

### Takeaway
Canonical story state is split into (a) prose, (b) an append-only per-chapter event log that indexes the prose, (c) small hand-authored registries, and (d) GM-side plan/ledger/threads/notes; everything a reader or the next session sees is regenerated. Long-term recall is by narrow query commands rather than by rereading, and GM character notes are the one deliberately hand-grown, hand-pruned memory.

### Cited Findings
- **Ownership table** (who writes what) — [saga/bible/mechanics.md L117-135](/home/user/leyenda/saga/bible/mechanics.md). ### Inferences
- There is no summarisation hierarchy in the usual sense (no rolling chapter summaries fed back to the writer). Long-range continuity is carried instead by: per-character `story_so_far` (rewritten at each climax), `known_facts` tails, threads with `who_knows`, the ledger's pending items, and the hand-grown GM character notes. A one-line `recap` per chapter exists but is for the reader site.
- The design bets that "what must be remembered" can be enumerated as debts and facts at write time; anything not captured in a record, a thread, a codex item or a GM note is recoverable only by rereading prose (`history` shows last beats, not full text).
- Engine-side work grows linearly (every `record` reloads all events and rebuilds all projections; name checks read all GM text), but that cost is CPU, not model context.

### Gaps
- No evidence was found of retrieval over the prose itself (search/grep of chapters is not part of any procedure except "the last block or two" and `/saga read`). Whether older prose is ever consulted before, e.g., a callback to a chapter-1 moment, depends on the model choosing to open the file.
- The size of GM-side stores (number of ledger entries, rules, notes) was not measured, to respect the spoiler wall; the brief header reported 18 open threads.

## 5. Information separation: GM-only vs reader-visible, and the real-world/story "wall"

### Takeaway
Separation is enforced at three layers: directory convention (`_gm/` vs everything else), write-time validation in `record`/`glimpse` (unspoken names refused; facts need page evidence), and a publish-time build that fails on real-world vocabulary, any GM sentence, any unrevealed name, or an unsupported fact. The player-facing channels (site, replies, commit messages, the `/saga` view) are each given an explicit rule; the model itself always holds full GM knowledge.

### Cited Findings
- **The `_gm/` wall**: "Anything the page has not spoken (names, places, plans, truths, consequences) lives only under `saga/bible/_gm/` or `saga/state/_gm/` … Reader-safe surfaces (the rest of `saga/`, the events included, `docs/`, this file, `README.md`, everything in `.claude/`) never carry it" — [CLAUDE.md golden rule 4](/home/user/leyenda/CLAUDE.md). The README tells the human "Don't open them unless you want spoilers" — [README.md L88-90](/home/user/leyenda/README.md).
- **Publish-time checks** (`build_site.py run_checks`): (1) a regex list of real-world terms (e.g. calorie/protein/rehab/surgery/reps/squat/gym/sport words, plus case-sensitive ACL/PT/RPE) across every generated file; (2) a blocklist built from every sentence (≥28 normalised chars) of every GM `.md` file and every string in GM notes (`*.jsonl`) and `threads.json`, exempting only text the chronicle or the published game rules already contain — violations are reported by length and hash, not content; (3) "unrevealed names": capitalised words used in GM text that the chronicle has never used (and that GM text never uses in lowercase); also applied to roll labels; (4) broken links. The build writes to a temp dir and leaves `docs/` untouched on failure — [engine/build_site.py:3985-4209, 4286-4331](/home/user/leyenda/engine/build_site.py).
- **What the site may read**: chapter prose and tales, character files, `world/places/factions/darrow/bearing/codex_art/looks.json`, `codex.md`, `chapters.csv`, `rolls.csv`, `rules.json`, `wardrobe.json`, art. - **Write-time name gate**: `record` and `glimpse add` refuse text containing a capitalised word that GM files know and the page has not spoken; other unregistered capitals are noted ("a coinage gets a codex item or a character file"); `--allow-names` overrides for non-names — [engine/saga.py:3556-3593 `gm_unspoken_names`, `name_problems`; 5554-5561; 5975-5977](/home/user/leyenda/engine/saga.py).
- **Gradual disclosure on the site**: other characters' stat sheets are gated by the protagonist's "Warden's Eye" Art rank (I: level/rank; II: attributes; III: Arts; IV+: deeper lines), with an `unread` flag for sheets that would confirm a doubt; public NPC sheets "may be lower than the truth"; approval is shown "as words and a bar, never a number" — [saga/characters/README.md L62, L67, L70, L72](/home/user/leyenda/saga/characters/README.md).
- **Lore tiers**: a `common` entry may be told freely; a `learned` one only once `spoken:` is set; `/lore` must paraphrase around names the page has not spoken and never use arc, plan, ledger or mechanics — [.claude/skills/lore/SKILL.md L16-19](/home/user/leyenda/.claude/skills/lore/SKILL.md).
- **Player-facing view**: `/saga` must not open any `_gm/` directory nor run `saga.py now` ("it prints the plan"); it reads `NOW.md`, `world.json`, the chapter file and `bearing show`, and says approval, Bearing and tier in words only — [.claude/skills/saga/SKILL.md L10-22](/home/user/leyenda/.claude/skills/saga/SKILL.md).
- **Commit messages**: "reader-safe: he reads them. Never a GM name, flag, rule id, plan or anything the page has not shown"; GM changes are described by kind — [CLAUDE.md "Every session"](/home/user/leyenda/CLAUDE.md). Recent messages follow the form `close DATE: chNN scene K, <title>` / `play chNN between-K: <title>` / `GM: …` — `git log --format=%s | head`.
- **Real ↔ story wall**: a translation table maps each class of real deed to how it may appear (warmth/cold, "the forms", a weekly "Reading", etc.) and bans *mirroring* ("no scene exists because of a food log"); game numbers only inside Reckoning boxes and dice lines; real project titles "never reach the Chronicle, the site or a commit message"; on the real side saga.py reads only the day's log rows (to compute the colour and to reconcile closed days with slots), the nutrition targets and timezone from the config, and the real projects file (a tale's task counts and status) — [saga/bible/style.md §1 L7-34](/home/user/leyenda/saga/bible/style.md); [.claude/skills/project/SKILL.md L10](/home/user/leyenda/.claude/skills/project/SKILL.md); [engine/saga.py:1023-1061, 3695-3705](/home/user/leyenda/engine/saga.py).

### Inferences
- The separation protects *published and replied* text; it does not separate knowledge inside the model. One context holds secrets and writes reader prose, so paraphrased leaks are possible; the style guide acknowledges this ("A paraphrased secret is a spoiler the build cannot catch") and assigns it to the manual reread — [saga/bible/style.md §4 item 1](/home/user/leyenda/saga/bible/style.md).
- Tool output is itself a spoiler surface: `brief`, `now`, `due`, `threads` print plan, arc sections and rule effects into the session. The rules cover the reply, the site and commits, and `/saga` avoids `now`, but whether the player can see tool results in his client is outside the repo.

### Gaps
- The sentence blocklist scans GM `.md`, notes and threads; it does not scan strings in the plan or consequence ledger JSON (code comment: "every name it carries is also in a GM .md") — [engine/build_site.py:4065-4082](/home/user/leyenda/engine/build_site.py). Whether that leaves sentence-level leaks from slot plans or due-item texts unguarded in practice could not be assessed without reading GM files.
- "Story never appears in the Ledger" is an instruction with no automated check found.

## 6. Prose and scene quality controls

### Takeaway
Quality is controlled by a long style guide framed as "aims, not recipes", exact parseable formats, planned variety (tone per slot, scene kinds, device rotation), an author-run nine-point "reread", and one small automated linter (`saga.py reread`) that counts a handful of known tics and wall words. ## 7. Player agency and interaction

### Takeaway
The player has four story inputs — answers to offered choices (small and climax), free-form in-fiction actions ("Betweens"), answering or ignoring NPC invitations, and lore questions — each with its own procedure, budget and consequence channel; a fifth, indirect input is real-world behaviour (tone daily, tier weekly, Knots per Book). Agency is real but bounded: only choices move Bearing and the ledger; free play may never do "the plan's work".

### Cited Findings
- **Routing without commands**: "Anything that reads like a log is a log"; "A message that answers an open choice is handled before anything else in it"; a Darrow action with nothing open is `/play`; a question about the world is `/lore` — [CLAUDE.md routing table](/home/user/leyenda/CLAUDE.md); [.claude/skills/log/SKILL.md L14](/home/user/leyenda/.claude/skills/log/SKILL.md).
- **Small choices ("micro")**: at most one open, one per scene, never two scenes running; exactly one Bearing axis, ±1 per option; carried across one scene, then "Darrow answers for himself with the planned default" (recorded `by: bearing`, shown on the site as "answered for himself"); the consequence is folded into the next scene's opening — [saga/bible/style.md §3 L171-181](/home/user/leyenda/saga/bible/style.md).
- **Climax choices**: 2–3 options; carried across the first two scenes of the next chapter, then the planned default; a Book's last climax does not carry; consequence written as a `### Choice — Title` block from `brief --choice` — [saga/bible/style.md §3 L161-169](/home/user/leyenda/saga/bible/style.md); [.claude/skills/choose/SKILL.md L17](/home/user/leyenda/.claude/skills/choose/SKILL.md).

- **Invitations**: planned NPC-initiated hooks ("someone comes to him with a want of their own, and if he lets it pass the engine charges the pass, which is the point") — [.claude/skills/chapter/SKILL.md L19](/home/user/leyenda/.claude/skills/chapter/SKILL.md).

- **Real projects → tales**: each task of a real-world weekly project reported done "pays" one piece of a pre-planned backstory tale (past tense, another character's POV allowed); the last piece leaves a "keepsake" on a character's page (a lesser one if the project finished late); the tale "moves nothing in the present"; the player is never told which tale or keepsake a project pays for — [.claude/skills/project/SKILL.md](/home/user/leyenda/.claude/skills/project/SKILL.md); [.claude/rules/chronicle.md L14](/home/user/leyenda/.claude/rules/chronicle.md).
- **No shame**: "Missed days are plot turns, not lessons. Overshooting targets earns nothing extra"; a Setback is "never humiliating, never a lecture"; glimpses react "to what was done, never to what is missing" — [CLAUDE.md golden rule 6](/home/user/leyenda/CLAUDE.md); [.claude/skills/log/reply.md L34](/home/user/leyenda/.claude/skills/log/reply.md).
- **Safety override**: red-flag health symptoms stop the story for the day and are answered "in plain language, out of story voice" — [CLAUDE.md golden rule 2](/home/user/leyenda/CLAUDE.md).

### Inferences
- This is an asynchronous, low-frequency play loop (one planned scene a day, optional free play in between, one big decision a week), closer to play-by-post or a serial with reader votes than to live tabletop. Player agency is concentrated in *who the protagonist becomes* (Bearing, approval, flags that bend later beats and select callbacks) rather than in moment-to-moment plot direction.
- Defaults exist so the story never stalls waiting for input; in the data so far they have never fired (12/12 choices answered by the player).
- The Between budget and the "one lands badly" rule are counterweights to a known LLM-GM failure mode (endless agreeable side-chat that inflates relationships): approval gains from banter are capped and diminishing, and at least one exchange per chapter is supposed to go against him.

### Gaps
- How the system handles a player action that *does* try to do the plan's work (e.g. going straight to a location the plan reserves) is specified only as "the world refuses in voice"; no worked example or engine check exists for detecting it.
- The pricing of write-in answers, and which Between is the one that "lands badly", are left entirely to the model's judgement; only the record of the result is validated.

### Inferences
- The system has clearly evolved by accretion in response to concrete failures (dated notes such as "since 10/27", "since 11/5", "Book I's trouble was…", stale-callback handling, approval "met for free" forecasts, the witness rule). That gives it unusually specific guardrails, and also explains the drift between help text, skills and code.
- The highest-leverage unverified area is the scene itself: structure, canon and debts are checked, but nothing independent reads the prose for the things the style guide cares most about (the turn, living NPCs, variety, tone). A comparison with outside practice should focus there, and on whether curated GM notes + debt queues are sufficient long-term memory compared with summarisation or retrieval over prose.
- The amount of standing instruction is large relative to the brief; any outside best practice about instruction budget, progressive disclosure of rules, or splitting planner/writer/recorder roles is directly applicable.
