## 1. Royal Road primary sources: AI art and cover policy, status-window formatting on mobile and the new reader, reader threads on stat bloat, illustrations, chapter art

### Takeaway
None of Royal Road could be read in full (Cloudflare challenge), so this section is all "summary only". The summaries still add concrete, dated detail. The 2026 redesign drew complaints that chapter tables were broken, plus complaints about margins and the default dark mode. Royal Road strips text colours too close to black or white. Authors keep chapter art in the top or bottom author's note, often behind a spoiler tag, because inline images interrupt reading and load slowly. One author warns that AI art in every chapter is "a magnet for bad reviews."

### Cited Findings
**AI policy (text and art)**
- **[summary only, platform policy, mid-2023 with later clarifications]** The AI-text policy separates AI-Assisted from AI-Generated. A later update lets works with "a negligible amount of AI-generated snippets" carry the AI-Assisted tag "but add a warning in the author notes on the chapter with the AI-Generated content." Reviews, comments and forum posts "can not include AI-generated content unless it's a quote." — [Royal Road blog #57: AI-text policy](https://www.royalroad.com/blog/57/royal-road-ai-text-policy)
- **[summary only, staff forum reply, undated]** A staff reply says AI "enhancement, or general assistive technology, does not fall under this policy." This is a forum post, not a policy page. — [RR forum: Should I add the AI-assisted tag?](https://www.royalroad.com/forums/thread/140766)
- **[summary only, reader opinion, undated]** In a thread asking for an "AI-free" tag, one commenter argued that a "Readers thought this might be AI" flag "would just have 90% of the organic content flagged" (worry about false positives). — [RR forum: AI free tag?](https://www.royalroad.com/forums/thread/157365?page=1&pid=1669669)
- **[summary only]** Older forum threads on AI cover art exist ("AI Artwork for covers," #134803; "Regarding Cover creating Using Ai," #161531). User replies there say AI covers need no tag; these replies are not authoritative and may predate the current knowledge-base wording. — [RR forum #134803](https://www.royalroad.com/forums/thread/134803); [RR forum #161531](https://www.royalroad.com/forums/thread/161531)
- **[summary only, third-party blog, June 2026]** Royal Road "does not currently ban AI-generated covers outright." Moderators have required disclosure and pulled covers with visible artefacts or apparent unauthorised use of other artists' work. This contradicts a strict reading of KB #114; give the official page more weight. — [Iloris: Why AI book covers cost web fiction authors more than they save](https://www.ilorisnovel.com/articles/ai-book-covers-web-fiction)

**Status windows: tables, colour and mobile**
- **[summary only, community guides]** Game-style status panels are built by wrapping a table in a window, with a split tag so the table stays inside it. `[window]` and `[table]` can each be used once and nested in either order, but a tag cannot contain itself. — [RR forum: Ploopie's In-Depth Guide To Tables (Revamped)](https://www.royalroad.com/forums/thread/119743); [RR forum: Practical Coding Tags & Usage Guide](https://www.royalroad.com/forums/thread/55374)
- **[summary only, anecdote]** On phones, "the texts in and out of the tables will automatically try to fit the screen," so a desktop layout can wrap badly. The author dashboard's Preview button "isn't foolproof." One user suggested keeping separate phone and desktop versions inside spoiler tags. — [RR forum: Tables and system messages on royalroad](https://www.royalroad.com/forums/thread/120146); [RR forum: Help with formatting: LitRPG style system calls](https://www.royalroad.com/forums/thread/133773)
- **[summary only, anecdote, ~2025]** "RR removes colours too close to black or white," probably to support dark and light reading modes, so custom text colours can vanish or shift. — [RR forum: Formatting Question](https://www.royalroad.com/forums/thread/153970?page=1)
- **[summary only, author practice]** Some authors drop boxes entirely: "I use [brackets] to denote system messages and skills." — [RR forum: Status Screen Writing?](https://www.royalroad.com/forums/thread/104153)

**The 2026 redesign (new reader interface)**
- **[summary only, user feedback, about Mar–Jul 2026]** Users reported that "tables in chapters are broke" and that "stories on the new ui don't show tables." One noted this matters because the site hosts so much LitRPG and GameLit. Other complaints: side margins grew (one user: from about 0.5 cm to 2.5 cm on a vertical monitor); requests for a reader column 40% wide or less; a default dark mode with "really bright white text"; sluggish scrolling and longer loads. A staff-side post said the design had been tested in-house for months and needed real-user exposure. Users asked for an opt-out or legacy mode. Whether the table bug has been fixed was not found. — [RR forum: Thoughts on the new Royal Road site](https://www.royalroad.com/forums/thread/166689); [RR forum: feedback on the site redesign](https://www.royalroad.com/forums/thread/171034); [RR forum: Redesign UI – Not a fan](https://www.royalroad.com/forums/thread/171098?page=1&pid=1874272); [RR Ideas: opt out of the redesign](https://www.royalroad.com/ideas/2454)

**Illustrations and chapter art**
- **[summary only, community advice]** Royal Road does not host chapter images: authors upload them elsewhere (Imgur, ImgBB, catbox, DeviantArt, Discord) and paste a direct link ending in .jpg or .png. — [RR forum: How to upload images in chapters?](https://www.royalroad.com/forums/thread/117592); [RR forum: Add images to each chapter?](https://www.royalroad.com/forums/thread/161872)
- **[summary only, craft opinion]** Placement: one member "strongly recommends" putting images in the top or bottom author's note, because images in the middle of the text "tend to feel disruptive." Some authors wrap images in spoiler tags with a warning in the pre-chapter note. Another says the spoiler tags exist mainly because the images are "massive." Readers ask that images not be "too huge." — [RR forum: Inserting illustrations into chapters?](https://www.royalroad.com/forums/thread/133241?page=1); [RR forum: How well do illustrations do in stories?](https://www.royalroad.com/forums/thread/126423)
- **[summary only, anecdote]** On reception: one author said "people said that the images were the highlight of my fiction"; another doubted readers come to Royal Road for art. One author said they can't afford an image in every chapter without using AI, "and that's a magnet for bad reviews." — [RR forum: How well do illustrations do in stories?](https://www.royalroad.com/forums/thread/126423); [RR forum: Add images to each chapter?](https://www.royalroad.com/forums/thread/161872)
- **[summary only, author statement]** A current Royal Road serial's page says its cover "was commissioned from Chuhari" and that the author works with living artists. Crediting human artists is now something authors advertise. — [RR fiction: To Touch Infinity](https://www.royalroad.com/fiction/176444/to-touch-infinity-martial-arts-strategy)

**Stat-block bloat and skimming:** no Royal Road thread specifically about stat-block bloat or skimming surfaced in this pass's searches (the earlier pass's craft-opinion sources remain the best available).

### Inferences
- On a phone-first site the author controls, the Royal Road failures to avoid are clear: status panels that depend on host CSS; colours that a dark or light mode strips out; tables that shrink text to fit; images inline mid-paragraph. Real HTML that reflows, with the meaning carried by text and shape (not only colour), avoids all four.
- The community's habit of placing art *after* the chapter text (bottom author's note) or behind an opt-in spoiler matches the site's rule that a picture shows only what the prose has shown. Placement after the scene is the genre's own norm, not just a house rule.
- Readers notice and resent AI art used as per-chapter filler. An author who draws the art himself, and says so plainly, is on the side of the norm readers reward.

### Gaps
- Exact wording, dates and current status of the Royal Road knowledge base, the redesign table bug and every forum post: blocked by Cloudflare. Recheck in a browser.
- No Royal Road guidance on maps was found.

## 2. The Wandering Inn wiki and official serial: policies, Leveling History, [Skill]/[Class] formatting, infoboxes, galleries, art and spoilers

### Takeaway
The TWI wiki is the most developed reader-built progression record in the genre, and it was readable in full. Each character page has tabs (Info, Appearances, Leveling History, Relationships, Gallery, Quotes). Leveling History is a four-column table keyed to chapters that separates **when something was revealed** from when it was gained. The wiki bans AI-generated images and text, and it fights spoilers mainly by leaving a character's status out of the infobox. In the story's own rules the System announces levels while a person **sleeps**, and it colour-codes Skills by *meaning*, not by rarity. Fan art is filed by character and by chapter, with chapter numbers in the captions.

### Cited Findings
**Policies [full page read, wiki policy, retrieved Nov 17, 2026]**
- AI ban: "A.I. generated images and text are not to be used on the wiki. While a LLM can be useful they are prone to hallucinations about events or connections between them and as such are not allowed." — [TWI wiki: Help:Policies](https://wiki.wanderinginn.com/Help:Policies)
- Paywalled content: knowledge "from chapters locked behind pirateaba's Patreon subscription are not to be referenced until they are publicly posted on the TWI website." — [Help:Policies](https://wiki.wanderinginn.com/Help:Policies)
- Spoilers: "Spoilers on the wiki are unavoidable." The standards in place are "Hiding the status of a character at the top of their page within their information box, such as *Alive* or *Deceased*," and "Placing a general spoiler warning at the wiki's main landing page." — [Help:Policies](https://wiki.wanderinginn.com/Help:Policies). In practice the infobox template has an optional `status` field, and Yvlon Byres's infobox simply leaves it out. — [Template:Infobox character](https://wiki.wanderinginn.com/Template:Infobox_character); [Yvlon Byres](https://wiki.wanderinginn.com/Yvlon_Byres)
- Licence: CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. — [TWI wiki: Copyrights](https://wiki.wanderinginn.com/The_Wandering_Inn_Wiki:Copyrights)

**Character page layout and Leveling History [full page read, fan wiki]**
- Character pages use tabs: Info / Appearances / Leveling History / (Relationships) / Gallery / Quotes. — [Layout: Leveling History](https://wiki.wanderinginn.com/The_Wandering_Inn_Wiki:New_Character_Layout/Leveling_History); [Yvlon Byres/Leveling History](https://wiki.wanderinginn.com/Yvlon_Byres/Leveling_History)
- The table has four columns: **Chapters** (15%, linked to the chapter) · **Classes/Levels** · **Skills** · **Additional Information** (prose notes). Cells use status labels: **Revealed**, **Skill Revealed**, **Skills Obtained/Revealed**, **Learned** (in green), **Conditions Met** with an arrow for class change ("[Wounded Warrior] → [Silversteel Armsmistress]"), **Skill Change** ("[Crescent Cut] → [Sword Art: Curve of the Moon]"), **Skill Received**. — [Yvlon Byres/Leveling History](https://wiki.wanderinginn.com/Yvlon_Byres/Leveling_History)
- Uncertainty is shown openly: "[Warrior] Lv. ? (over 22)", "[Wounded Warrior] Lv. 27+ (+?)", "[Emperor] Lv. 25 (+?)". Rows can run out of chronological order because the table records when the *page* revealed a fact: chapter 4.24 reveals a level she held earlier, and its row sits above a 3.09 row. Notes explain such cases ("In 6.39 it was discovered that she gained that level by having trained..."). — [Yvlon Byres/Leveling History](https://wiki.wanderinginn.com/Yvlon_Byres/Leveling_History); [Layout: Leveling History](https://wiki.wanderinginn.com/The_Wandering_Inn_Wiki:New_Character_Layout/Leveling_History)

**How the story itself delivers and codes progression [full page read, fan wiki citing chapters]**
- Level-ups arrive in sleep: "the announcements can only be heard when asleep, unless the person is physically unable to sleep, like an undead, or they possess a <Faith> or <Miracle> class." "Leveling occurs the moment the person qualifies... and will immediately show up on [Appraisal]." — [TWI wiki: Classes](https://wiki.wanderinginn.com/Classes). "The announcement in people's heads is a universal language." — [TWI wiki: Grand Design of Isthekenous](https://wiki.wanderinginn.com/Grand_Design_of_Isthekenous)
- Level-ups and new Skills can be refused ("one can cancel the obtaining of Skills"), except red Skills, which "cannot be refused." — [TWI wiki: Skills](https://wiki.wanderinginn.com/Skills)
- **Semantic colour code (meaning, not rarity):** Green, newly created/"Original" Skills and Classes; Orange, fame/reputation and Titles; Golden, royal or holy; Red, "morally repugnant to the wielder, forced on them, or come with ill consequences" (Blood Skills); Blue, "restoration and resolution" (removal of Blood Skills or Conditions, a Vow); Purple, Authority Skills "granted... by virtue of their position, not their actions." Elsewhere, teal marks classes locked to a single personality, and a purple "Rank" mechanic belongs to old organisations. — [TWI wiki: Skills, "Colored Skills"](https://wiki.wanderinginn.com/Skills); [TWI wiki: Classes](https://wiki.wanderinginn.com/Classes)
- **A ladder told through social meaning:** "the majority of people reach Level 20... Fewer individuals manage to reach Level 30, with a handful in each town... Level 40 is regarded as a true mark of accomplishment. People who are Level 50 are world-famous national assets... Level 60 [are] considered capable of shaking the world." Adventurer teams use metal ranks ("adventurers are considered Gold-rank around Level 30"). — [TWI wiki: Classes](https://wiki.wanderinginn.com/Classes)

**Art: galleries, chapter images, fan works, official art [full page read, fan wiki]**
- Gallery tabs split **"Fan-Artworks By Portraits"**, **"By Chapters"** (each caption carries the chapter, e.g. "(6.66 H)", "(9.37 HO)") and "Other," and every image is credited to its artist. A fan-run tagged image board ("Booru") holds more. — [Layout: Gallery](https://wiki.wanderinginn.com/The_Wandering_Inn_Wiki:New_Character_Layout/Gallery); [Yvlon Byres/Gallery](https://wiki.wanderinginn.com/Yvlon_Byres/Gallery); [Booru:Booru](https://wiki.wanderinginn.com/Booru:Booru)
- The fan-works index warns "Note that Spoilers are present here." An archived "Chronology Fanworks" page listed art in chapter order, each image with the chapter link and a one-line description of the scene. — [TWI wiki: Fanworks](https://wiki.wanderinginn.com/Fanworks); [Chronology Fanworks (archived)](https://wiki.wanderinginn.com/Chronology_Fanworks)
- Chapter-page images: "it is preferred to use an existing image from the wiki than uploading a new one. No two chapters should have the same image." — [TWI wiki: Chapter Images](https://wiki.wanderinginn.com/The_Wandering_Inn_Wiki:Chapter_Images)
- **A commissioned-art incident in a LitRPG fandom:** the wiki keeps a "Cutlery Yvlon" gallery, "a community reaction to some scam artist mashing together the work of other artists with copy-paste, and selling it to the commissioner as original artwork of Yvlon." — [Yvlon Byres/Gallery](https://wiki.wanderinginn.com/Yvlon_Byres/Gallery)
- Official editions: ebook cover art is by John Anthony Di Giovanni; ebooks of Volume 7 started in April 2025. — [TWI wiki: Ebook](https://wiki.wanderinginn.com/Ebook). The audiobook is narrated by Andrea Parsneau, published by Podium Audio and "commissioned by Pirateaba." Book 1 came out Sep 10, 2019; Book 10, Sep 19, 2023 (the page's list stops there). — [TWI wiki: Audiobook](https://wiki.wanderinginn.com/Audiobook)
- pirateaba keeps their own Fanworks page on the official site (the wiki notes it is "possibly no longer as up-to-date"). wanderinginn.com itself could not be read. — [TWI wiki: Fanworks](https://wiki.wanderinginn.com/Fanworks)

### Inferences
- **Level-up at rest.** TWI's convention that the announcement comes in sleep (the level is earned at once but *heard* later) maps directly onto a day that closes with a scene: the level-up lands in the night's close, not mid-action, and the reader discovers it there.
- **Revealed vs gained.** The wiki records what the *page* has shown and when, marks unknowns honestly ("Lv. ?", "+?"), and links each row to its chapter. A Codex or sheet history for the site can take exactly this shape: one row per chapter, with cells labelled "first shown / changed / learned", and the rule "the picture shows only what the prose has shown" extended to numbers.
- **Colour for meaning, metal for rank.** TWI keeps rank (levels, metal adventurer ranks) separate from colour (the *kind* of a Skill). The site's metal crests already carry rank. If colour is ever used for technique cards, it should mean a kind (e.g. a hard-won Art versus a gift) and stay rare, or readers will try to read it as a second rank code.
- **Social benchmarks beat numbers.** "Level 40 is a true mark of accomplishment" teaches the ladder without a table. "Measured against his former self" works the same way: the meaning of a rank comes from who else stands there.
- **Spoiler hygiene for an archive.** Leave out "current status" fields; caption every picture with its chapter; warn on gallery pages. These are the wiki's own fixes and cost little.

### Gaps
- wanderinginn.com (the official serial) was not readable, so how pirateaba places art inside chapters, and their own spoiler practice, are unconfirmed.
- No TWI chapter text was read, so the exact typography of an in-chapter level-up (line breaks, bold, centring) is not quoted here.
- How Andrea Parsneau voices level-ups in the TWI audiobook was not found.

## 3. Academic and survey work on LitRPG and progression readership, gamified fiction, web novels and webtoon adaptation

### Takeaway
No peer-reviewed reader survey on stat screens exists in the indexes reachable here. arXiv returns **zero** results for "LitRPG," "web novel webtoon" and "web serial fiction." The scholarship that does exist is about genre and media industries (a Springer chapter on LitRPG/"RealRPG," Korean webtoon-to-drama adaptation, webtoon reading interviews), and those publishers' pages were blocked. The nearest rigorous evidence is adjacent: gamification-preference and illustration-comprehension studies, plus UX research on progressive disclosure and icons.

### Cited Findings
- **[full page read, negative result, Nov 17, 2026]** arXiv full-text search for "LitRPG," "web novel webtoon," "web serial fiction" and "gamification fiction reading" returned no relevant papers. — [arXiv search: LitRPG](https://arxiv.org/search/?query=LitRPG&searchtype=all)
- **[summary only, academic chapter; Springer, c. 2024–25]** "Multiplayer Online Games Become Reality: A New Literary Genre" treats the in-world System (skill assessments, notifications, quests) as becoming "not just a digital model of reality, but a global law of the universe." It names the genre's core trait as "the unity of the player in two worlds" and calls it "LitRPG and RealRPG." — [Springer chapter 10.1007/978-3-031-76797-5_9](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-76797-5_9)
- **[summary only, student journal, Spring 2025]** "Embracing AI in LitRPGs: Co-creativity and its discontents" (Scholarly Review Journal) draws on an interview with one author and a review of 100+ LitRPG novels. It notes the strong author–reader relationships in the genre. The focus is authors, not reader behaviour. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/390314752_Embracing_AI_in_LitRPGs_Co-creativity_and_its_discontents)
- **[summary only, interview study]** A RUSQ (American Library Association) article used semi-structured interviews with **29 webtoon readers** to compare webtoon reading with book reading. — [RUSQ article PDF](https://journals.ala.org/index.php/rusq/article/download/8427/11700)
- **[summary only, survey]** A Japanese Journal of Ergonomics questionnaire found vertical scrolling made up 58% of layouts in Korean-webtoon-style apps, yet more respondents rated single-page horizontal layouts as easiest to read. — [J-STAGE: Japanese Journal of Ergonomics 59 Supplement](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jje/59/Supplement/59_JS-12/_article)
- **[summary only, econometric study]** Of 232 Korean dramas on Netflix, about 12.5% came from "web originals" (webtoons and web novels), and those adaptations did better worldwide. — [Applied Economics Letters (T&F)](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/13504851.2025.2566889)
- **[summary only, academic chapter]** A case study of *Cheese in the Trap* follows how a slow webtoon is reshaped for film and TV and how fans argue online about fidelity and character choices. This supports the earlier pass's point that the first visual version becomes the reference. — [Springer chapter 10.1007/978-981-15-7857-1_3](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-15-7857-1_3)
- **[summary only, HCI paper, 2026]** "AI in Webtoon Creation: Challenges, Perceptions, and Design Implications" (ACM) exists; its contents could not be read (blocked). — [ACM DL 10.1145/3772318.3790343](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3772318.3790343)
- **[full page read (abstract), survey, Dec 9, 2025]** A best-worst-scaling survey (n = 125) of ten game-design elements found learners "consistently preferred GDEs that support learning processes directly—most notably progress bars, concept maps, immediate feedback, and achievements." It recommends tools that "visualize... progress" over "solely... extrinsic incentives." (Education context, not fiction.) — [arXiv 2512.08551, Marquardt, Schulz, Koziolek, Happe](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08551)
- **[full page read (abstract), experiment, Oct 24, 2016]** 150 fifth graders read a chapter in which pictures *replaced* some of the text. Children with stronger mental-imagery skills understood the story better; no such gap appeared with text-only or full-text-plus-pictures versions. — [Frontiers in Psychology 10.3389/fpsyg.2016.01630](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2016.01630/full)
- **[summary only, experiment]** For beginning readers, cluttered illustrations drew gaze away from the text and lowered comprehension compared with streamlined ones. — [PMC7522290, "Keep it simple"](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7522290/)
- **[full page read, psychology study cited by a practitioner, Oct 8, 2014]** Kurtz, Wilson & Gilbert (2007, *Journal of Experimental Social Psychology* 43(6)) found that subjects who knew they had won *a* prize but not *which* one stayed happier for longer than those told at once. Madigan links this to Destiny's unidentified "engrams": "The anticipation is sweet." — [Game Developer: Psychology and Destiny's Loot System](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/psychology-and-destiny-s-loot-system)
- **[full page read, UX guideline, Dec 3, 2006]** Progressive disclosure: "Initially, show users only a few of the most important options. Offer a larger set of specialized options upon request." "In practice, designs that go beyond 2 disclosure levels typically have low usability because users often get lost." The link to the next level needs "strong information scent." — [NN/g, Jakob Nielsen, "Progressive Disclosure"](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/)

### Inferences
- The "discover, don't study" stance has some empirical backing from outside fiction. Visible progress (a bar, a crest changing) is the element people value most. Revealing *that* something improved before revealing *what* prolongs the pleasure (the Kurtz et al. uncertainty effect). Keeping the full numbers one tap deeper (progressive disclosure, at most two levels) protects first-time readers without hiding anything from those who look.
- Pictures help when they illustrate what the text already says and hurt when they replace it or clutter it (child-reader evidence; extrapolating to adults is a weak inference). This supports the site's rule that a picture comes *after* the prose and shows only what the prose has shown.

### Gaps
- No reader survey, in any index reached, measures how many LitRPG readers skip stat blocks or prefer tables to inline text. The question remains open.
- Taylor & Francis, ACM and Springer full texts were blocked; Korean-language databases (RISS, DBpia: "웹소설 상태창") were not searched.

## 5. Tier and rarity visual languages: metal, gem and colour ladders, card frames, colour-blind-safe practice

### Takeaway
Metal ladders are a genre norm in progression fiction (HWFWM: iron→bronze→silver→gold→diamond; TWI: metal adventurer ranks). Metal and gem ladders become confusing once they stop following the intuitive precious-metal order: Goblin Slayer puts six gems *between* steel and bronze. Game designers keep familiar colour codes because "why reinvent the colors when you're just going to confuse people?" They also insist colour must never work alone: pair it with shape, symbol, shading or animation, test with colour-blindness simulators, and give icons visible text labels.

### Cited Findings
**Ladders in fiction**
- **[summary only, fan wiki]** *Goblin Slayer* adventurer ranks, lowest to highest: Porcelain (10), Obsidian (9), Steel (8), Sapphire (7), Emerald (6), Ruby (5), Bronze (4), Silver (3), Gold (2), Platinum (1). Gems sit between steel and bronze, against everyday intuition, and one blog lists them in a different order, a small sign that readers do get confused. — [Goblin Slayer Wiki: Adventurer](https://goblin-slayer.fandom.com/wiki/Adventurer); [myanimethoughts: ranks explained](https://myanimethoughts.com/goblin-slayer-adventurer-ranks-explained-porcelain-to-platinum-why-he-stays-silver)
- **[summary only, reader notes]** *He Who Fights With Monsters* uses iron, bronze, silver, gold, diamond, with each rank tied to monster-level bands. Readers note that silver is "the last time" one advances just by adventuring and that the will is explored at gold. — [Goodreads reader notes, HWFWM 7](https://www.goodreads.com/notes/61379442-he-who-fights-with-monsters-7/8375218-dayna/59e885ae-96a0-4f17-b641-df98b909191a); [Goodreads reader notes, HWFWM 10](https://www.goodreads.com/notes/131499749-he-who-fights-with-monsters-10/11584533-steve/e5ba4761-76b7-4c44-9259-d6e36d3511fb)
- **[full page read, fan wiki]** TWI ties metal ranks to levels ("Gold-rank around Level 30"; in an older era the Gold benchmark was Level 50), and keeps colour for Skill *kind* (§2). — [TWI wiki: Classes](https://wiki.wanderinginn.com/Classes); [TWI wiki: Skills](https://wiki.wanderinginn.com/Skills)

**Familiar codes and colour plus a second cue (games)**
- **[full page read, practitioner article, Nov 24, 2009]** Borderlands keeps WoW's colours for familiarity, and its small robot icons add weapon *type* as a second channel, though "it also may take a bit of time to recognize the symbolism." — [Game Developer: Borderlands & the anatomy of a gun](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/analysis-i-borderlands-i-the-anatomy-of-a-gun)
- **[full page read, practitioner report, Aug 23, 2018]** Mark Brown (Game Maker's Toolkit): run colour-blind filters and check whether "HUD elements, alerts, enemy differences, loot rarity or damage indicators are lost or difficult to distinguish"; "avoid relying on color alone"; "Designers should try to use shapes, symbols, shading, animation, and other visual tricks." Deuteranopia (green) is the most common type, and colour-blindness "does not mean seeing in black and white." Tools: Color Oracle (static), Sim Daltonism (live; the article spells it "Sim Dotulism"). — [Game Developer: GMTK explores designing for colorblindness](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-maker-s-toolkit-explores-how-devs-can-design-for-colorblindness)
- **[full page read, developer post-mortem, Feb 21, 2017]** *She Remembered Caterpillars* (eight colours) paired every colour with a symbol, borrowing Kandinsky's shape–colour associations. It kept "a visible contrast between the colour, the associated symbol, and the backgrounds," checked with Color Oracle, and ended up with darker, less saturated backgrounds as "a working trade-off somewhere between legibility and a good sense of place." The developer gives the prevalence as about 10% of people. — [Game Developer: Hand Drawn Colour-blind Friendliness](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/hand-drawn-colour-blind-friendliness-she-remembered-caterpillars)
- **[summary only, practitioner articles]** "Double coding": accompany colour used for game state with another cue. *Mini Metro*'s colours-plus-shapes on a neutral background worked for players with different colour-blindness types. — [Game Developer: Color in games](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/color-in-games-an-in-depth-look-at-one-of-game-design-s-most-useful-tools); [Game Developer: Mini Motorways](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/-i-mini-motorways-i-and-the-delicate-art-of-marrying-complexity-and-minimalism)

**Icons, labels and tables on phones (UX research)**
- **[full page read, UX guideline, Jul 27, 2014]** "Due to the absence of a standard usage for most icons, text labels are necessary." "Icon labels should be visible at all times... Don't rely on hover to reveal text labels... it also fails to translate well on touch devices." "Intricate details are difficult to distinguish at smaller sizes." 5-second rule: if a fitting icon takes more than five seconds to think of, an icon probably can't carry the meaning. — [NN/g, Aurora Harley, "Icon Usability"](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/icon-usability/)
- **[full page read, UX guideline, Sep 17, 2017]** On phones, "For complex or wordy entries... only 2 columns may fit legibly"; "Rotating the phone is a last resort"; use sticky column headers; make horizontal scrolling visibly obvious (cut-off elements or arrows beat dots); use accordions for groups of data. — [NN/g, Amy Schade, "Mobile Tables"](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-tables/)

### Inferences
- The site's iron→brass→steel→silver→ember→gold→white-gold ladder sits within genre norms (iron starts; gold and white-bright end), but two steps are non-intuitive, like Goblin Slayer's gem band: **brass above iron yet below steel**, and **ember** (not a metal) between silver and gold. A short text name on every crest (always visible, never hover-only) plus a numeral or pip count makes the order readable without explaining mechanics.
- Colour-vision risk: brass/gold and steel/silver are close in hue and differ mainly in value and texture. Encode rank a second way too: pips, numerals I–V on the technique cards, frame ornament that grows with rank, or a finish that moves from matte to bright. Test the actual crest and frame colours with Color Oracle or Sim Daltonism.
- A sheet comparing "now vs former self" should fit two legible columns on a phone, keep headers sticky if it scrolls, and fold groups into accordions instead of asking the reader to turn the phone.
- The genre's lesson about *meaning-colour* (TWI) versus *rank-colour* (WoW/Destiny) argues for keeping companion-regard glyphs and technique-card frames on different visual systems, so one never reads as the other.

### Gaps
- No contrast or colour-vision study specific to metal palettes was found; the actual crest colours need testing.
- Pokémon TCG shape-rarity marks (● ◆ ★) and gacha frame colours remain unsourced (no readable primary page).
- Xianxia realm ladders (Qi Condensation → Foundation Establishment → Core Formation...) and how Western readers handle them were not researched in this pass.
