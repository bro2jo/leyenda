## 1. Visual novels: sprites, event CGs, backgrounds and unlockable galleries as reward and memory aid

### Takeaway
VN practice keeps the expensive full-scene illustration (the "event CG") for dramatic or intimate peaks. The last CG on a route becomes the reader's lasting memory of it. Galleries fill only with CGs the reader has already seen, so a gallery works as a reward, a progress meter and a way to recall scenes, and locked slots hint at what is still unseen. I found no authoritative rule for how many CGs to use: practitioners say budget decides it and warn against scripting too many CG moments.

### Cited Findings
- **What a CG is for:** VNs use event CGs "to heighten drama at key story beats or intimate character moments." The final CG of a route "often becomes the reader's lasting memory of it." One well-timed CG can make a routine scene feel more dramatic and real. Super-deformed (comic) CGs mark shifts in tone. [Practitioner essay, July 2024] — [Fuwanovel: Event CGs – An Anatomy of Visual Novels](https://forums.fuwanovel.moe/blogs/entry/4209-event-cgs-%E2%80%93-an-anatomy-of-visual-novels); [Towards the End Sky (full series)](https://towardstheendsky.blogspot.com/2024/07/event-cgs-anatomy-of-visual-novels.html)
- **How many:** No standard count exists. Developers say budget decides it, and one commenter calls the event CG possibly "the single most expensive part" of a VN. A developer warns that writers forget to count the CG moments in a script "and end up with 30 of them." Others say "no such thing as too many." CGs should be placed so they "don't undermine a scene's mood." [Community] — [LemmaSoft: CG in visual novels – too many or too few?](https://lemmasoft.renai.us/forums/viewtopic.php?p=434061)
- **Gallery as progress and reward:** Galleries "fill up with event CGs as players view them," which also shows how far along a player is. Locked CGs in a gallery signal unseen content and encourage replays. [Community] — [TV Tropes: Visual Novel Tropes](https://TVTropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/VisualNovelTropes); [LemmaSoft thread](https://lemmasoft.renai.us/forums/viewtopic.php?p=434061)
- **How the dominant engine does it:** Ren'Py's Image Gallery is a screen of buttons, each with conditions that decide when it unlocks (`Gallery.unlock()`, `Gallery.unlock_image()`). "Seen" images are stored in a persistent record that survives across playthroughs. Ren'Py also has a **Music Room**, an out-of-play gallery of tracks. [Docs] — [Ren'Py docs, Image Gallery/Music Room (8.4.0 mirror)](https://doc-renpy-ru.vercel.app/rooms.html); [Ren'Py docs translation](https://translations.launchpad.net/renpy-doc/6.16/+pots/rooms/de); [Argent Games: layeredimage CG gallery unlock (2019)](https://blog.argentgames.co/post/2019-09-30-layeredimage-cg-gallery-unlock); [itch.io: music room thread](https://itch.io/t/5508392/creating-multiple-music-rooms-and-disabling-music-room-briefly-during-play). I could not retrieve how the Music Room marks a track as heard.
- **Sprites and expressions, and the problem of choice (Failbetter's *Mask of the Rose*, 2023):** Because players choose, Failbetter "often [doesn't] know which character will be appearing when," so placing characters on screen is hard. Their system uses a small set of full-body poses, each able to show several attitudes, plus a larger library of faces built from eye, brow and mouth parts on one tilting head. Authors can script poses to moments. Without a script, default rules apply, and in two-person scenes the fallback placement can show whether the characters get along (friendly characters stand closer). Sprites are lit to match each scene. [Designer] — [Failbetter: Character positioning in Mask of the Rose](https://www.failbettergames.com/news/character-positioning-in-mask-of-the-rose); [Failbetter: The art of Mask of the Rose](https://www.failbettergames.com/news/the-art-of-mask-of-the-rose-bring-the-characters-to-life); [PC Gamer forum dev thread](https://forums.pcgamer.com/threads/developing-a-dynamic-romantic-platonic-visual-novel.123510)
- **Wardrobe as self-presentation (*Mask of the Rose*):** Emily Short (Nov 2023) writes that different wardrobe items "advertise different social affiliations, or spin the PC towards a snobbish, flirty, or unfriendly self-presentation," and so change which dialogue options appear. [Designer] — [Emily Short: Dialogue Expressiveness in Mask of the Rose](https://emshort.blog/2023/11/22/dialogue-expressiveness-in-mask-of-the-rose/)

### Inferences
- For a text-first serial, the event-CG pattern maps onto **one illustration per climax or turning point, not per scene**. Scarcity is what makes the image memorable, and a "final image of a Book" can serve as that Book's memory anchor.
- A gallery that fills only with images whose scenes the reader has reached is the VN form of the "only what the prose has shown" rule. Showing locked slots is optional. In VNs they invite replay, but on a linear serial a locked slot could hint at future content, which may count as a spoiler. **Showing count only ("12 of ?") or showing nothing is the safer choice.**
- The Mask of the Rose pose and expression system shows that portraits can stay few while still varying with the scene. Per-Book portraits are the coarse form of the same idea.
- Mask of the Rose shows that clothing which changes how a character presents himself has a precedent in narrative games. The serial's Wardrobe sits close to this pattern, although there the clothing changes dialogue rather than appearing in a gallery.

### Gaps
- I found no design article or GDC talk giving a CG-per-word or CG-per-hour ratio. The figure is budget-driven folklore.
- I could not open Ren'Py's official page (blocked) to confirm the exact Music Room "heard" mechanics.
- The devlogs "CGs and visual beats" and "What I learned from trying to sell an 800-CG visual novel" appeared in results but could not be read.

## 2. Interactive fiction on web and mobile: inkle, Failbetter, Choice of Games, Twine/Ink. What the designers said and why

### Takeaway
The leading IF studios treat the **interface as part of the story's legibility**. inkle builds the screen around a map or globe so that the route is the story, keeps passages short so reading feels almost accidental, and treats UI as "a graphic design problem." Failbetter releases text in small portions so the text "itself was a reward," marks requirements with small icons, and keeps environment art distant so prose carries the imagination. Choice of Games lets authors show only the stats they choose. Readers are split on stat screens, but many value them as a memory aid.

### Cited Findings
- **80 Days (inkle, 2014): the globe as interface.** The design frames the game as "part interactive fiction, part globe-based board-game." The team wanted players to "visit any city on the globe, and build their own route." An early version showed almost all routes on the globe at once, and the team moved away from it. [Designer, postmortem] — [Game Developer: Postmortem, inkle's 80 Days](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/postmortem-inkle-s-i-80-days-i-); [inkle: 80 Days](https://www.inklestudios.com/80days/). The official site cites 150 bespoke cities, and one reviewer counted 144. [Data/Press] — [inkle 80 Days page](https://www.inklestudios.com/80days/); [Indie Game Reviewer](https://indiegamereviewer.com/?p=21109)
- **inkle on UI:** Jon Ingold calls UI design "a graphic design problem." The team asks why a player is looking at a given button right now, "and if you get that right, players will happily find their own way around." Good graphic design can make a cluttered interface look simple, and bad design can make the simplest layout unusable. Art director Joseph Humfrey says the spinning globe and suitcase browsing "has got to feel as nice as possible" and that he strives for simplicity. [Designer, 2014] — [Vice: The makers of 80 Days on the importance of amazing UI](https://vice.com/en/article/the-makers-of-mobile-hit-80-days-on-the-importance-of-amazing-ui-852)
- **Map-framed interface (Sorcery!, inkle):** Major choices were tied to which paths to take or places to visit on a map. [Press/historian] — [IF50: 2014: 80 Days](https://if50.substack.com/p/2014-80-days)
- **Heaven's Vault (inkle, 2019): a timeline that fills as you learn.** Each object the player finds adds a short text box to a history timeline that can be reviewed at any time. [Press] — [GamesRadar review](https://gamesradar.com/heavens-vault-review). Senior artist Laura Dilloway drew an *architectural* timeline that lets players place a site in history by its architecture. [Press, Edge making-of, Sept 2021] — [Edge: The making of Heaven's Vault](https://pocketmags.com/edge-magazine/september-2021/articles/the-making-of). Critic Katherine Cross called the timeline "gorgeous" but "a horrible way to store your translations." [Press; the search attributed this to a Game Developer column, most likely [Boldly she rode](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/boldly-she-rode-how-i-heaven-s-vault-i-reinvents-adventure-games), but I could not confirm the exact page.] inkle says it built "4,000 years of history," while Humfrey's GDC talk says 5,000. [Designer; sources conflict] — [inkle blog](https://inklestudios.com/blog/)
- **Text pacing (inkle and Failbetter):** Failbetter presents text "in smaller volumes to convince players to pay closer attention," aiming for players to feel "the text itself was a reward." In *Overboard!* (inkle, 2021), passages are short enough that "the player almost reads the text by accident, while in the process of planning, reflecting, or thinking about their next move." Failbetter reworked the reading panel in *Sunless Skies* after finding flaws in the first UI. [Designer, via press] — [Game Developer: How to remove the frustration of reading in games](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-to-remove-the-frustration-of-reading-in-games). Humfrey's GDC talk *Designing Text UX for Effortless Reading* organises text UX around two pillars, **focus and pacing**. [Designer] — [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1025104/Designing-Text-UX-for-Effortless). For *Overboard!*, recorded playtests "informed so much of the story and interface," and they produced a fast-replay mode. [Designer] — [PC Gamer: how inkle made Overboard! in 3 months](https://pcgamer.com/how-the-studio-behind-80-days-made-one-of-the-best-games-of-2021-in-just-3-months)
- **Players dislike timed text:** Commenters call timed or delayed text "infuriating" and a "pet peeve" when used more than sparingly. [Community] — same Game Developer piece and linked threads (e.g., [intfiction.org: passage length](https://www.intfiction.org/t/thoughts-on-individual-passage-length/10246))
- **Fallen London: storylets and quality icons.** In Failbetter's story-page redesign, "quality requirements on storylets and opportunities now have their own little icons," and storylet icons were made smaller so more fit on the page. [Designer] — [Failbetter: Redesigning the story page](https://www.failbettergames.com/news/redesigning-the-story-page). Failbetter's writer guidelines warn that a string of quality changes in one result makes each change matter less. [Designer] — [Fallen London writer guidelines, part II](https://www.failbettergames.com/fallen-london-writer-guidelines-part-ii). Emily Short argues storylets "foreground the stats" and push writers toward systems thinking. [Designer, Dec 2019] — [Emily Short: Storylets Play Together](https://emshort.blog/2019/12/03/storylets-play-together/)
- **Sunless Sea: sparse, stylised illustration so prose carries the imagination.** Failbetter's art direction aimed for illustrations that look "swiftly rendered in a naturalist's notebook." [Designer] — [Failbetter: Sunless Sea art direction](https://www.failbettergames.com/caverns-measureless-to-man-or-sunless-sea-art-direction/). A later Failbetter post says the camera in Sunless Sea and Skies sat "so far away, much was left to interpretation," which let writers invent freely without adding art cost. [Designer; the post came from a Failbetter news search result, possibly [Terrain, buildings and boats](https://www.failbettergames.com/news/terrain-buildings-and-boats), not confirmed.] A reviewer notes that a port arrival shows a description plus a few choices, and that you mostly look at "a ship, a lot of dark water." [Press] — [Hardcore Gaming 101: Sunless Sea](https://www.hardcoregaming101.net/sunless-sea-windows-macintosh-2015/)
- **Choice of Games stat screens:** The stat chart "only shows what you want it to show". Variables not listed stay "permanently hidden," and the screen can be split into pages so it doesn't get crowded. [Docs] — [Choice of Games: Customizing the ChoiceScript stats screen](https://www.choiceofgames.com/make-your-own-games/customizing-the-choicescript-stats-screen/). An `opposed_pair` stat renders as a two-sided bar with author-chosen labels (e.g., "Cleverness" vs "Stupidity"). [Docs/Community] — [ChoiceScript Wiki: Stat chart](https://choicescriptdev.fandom.com/wiki/Stat_chart)
- **The show-stats debate (readers and authors):** For showing them: "the more data the merrier," and "instead of trying to remember a choice you made (possibly days ago), you can just reference the stat sheet." Against: long stat lists are "overwhelming." Middle ground: scale the screen to the game's length, and hide stats that would spoil, such as loyalty in betrayal plots. Authors also reveal a stat line only once it becomes true. [Community] — [CoG forum: What do you like to see on a stat screen?](https://forum.choiceofgames.com/t/what-do-you-like-to-see-on-a-stat-screen/13510); [On visible (and invisible) stats](https://forum.choiceofgames.com/t/on-visible-and-invisible-stats/13330); [Hiding information in the stat screen](https://forum.choiceofgames.com/t/hiding-information-in-the-stat-screen/100589); [Do Choice games need stats?](https://forum.choiceofgames.com/t/do-choice-games-need-stats/1216)

### Inferences
- **Map as spine:** 80 Days and Sorcery! put the route at the centre of the interface. For a serial, a painted map with the route travelled so far is the strongest single "where are we" device. inkle's lesson from the early 80 Days version is to show **the route taken, not every possible route**.
- **Heaven's Vault's timeline** is a direct model for a reference that fills as the story reveals things: each reveal adds a dated card. Cross's criticism is a warning not to overload one view with two jobs.
- **Small chunks and the absence of timed reveals** both point to the same practice: keep scenes in short paragraphs for the phone and never gate text behind animation.
- Failbetter's art strategy (sparse, stylised, distant) supports a text-first serial. Images should frame a scene, not replace description, and they should leave room for the reader's imagination.
- From the CoG debate: a LitRPG sheet helps memory, but it should show only what has been earned and revealed, add lines when they become true, and stay short or paged on a phone.

### Gaps
- I could not open the full 80 Days postmortem, the Fallen London redesign post or the Game Developer text-UX article (blocked). The quotes come from search extracts.
- I found no inkle statement specifically about *Pendragon*'s board presentation, and no official Choice of Games blog post (as opposed to forum threads and docs) arguing for or against visible stats.
- I found no source describing Twine or Ink default web-template conventions beyond the passage-length and timed-text discussions.

## 3. Web serials and webcomics: how official sites present chapters, recaps, cast, art, glossaries and maps; official vs fan wikis

### Takeaway
The big text serials (The Wandering Inn, Worm/Ward) keep official sites **text-first and minimal**: a table of contents, chapters, and an official art page or store kept separate from the reading flow. Reference material (chapter overviews, volume "tl;dr" summaries, character pages, maps) lives mostly on **fan wikis** that carry spoiler warnings rather than true spoiler filtering. Homestuck is the outlier: music and art are part of how the story is told. Recaps ("previously on") are informal and author-made, and platform tools (Kindle) now fill that role for books.

### Cited Findings
- **The Wandering Inn, official:** An "Official Innverse Art" page says the visual lore is "commissioned and art directed by pirateaba and brought to life by the artists they work with." [Designer/Official] — [wanderinginn.com: Official Innverse Art](https://wanderinginn.com/offical-innverse-art/). Official map posters (Map of Izril, Map of Liscor) are sold through the store's "Maps of the Innverse." [Official] — [Wandering Inn store: Maps](https://store.wanderinginn.com/collections/maps). An official Fanworks page collects fan art, music and maps with the warning "Note that spoilers may be present here." [Official] — [wanderinginn.com/fanworks](https://wanderinginn.com/fanworks/). An older post mentions reader-commissioned scenes (e.g., the Inn and the Floodplains of Liscor). [Official] — [wanderinginn.com post](https://wanderinginn.com/?p=2915)
- **The Wandering Inn, fan wiki:** The welcome template warns "this wiki contains MAJOR SPOILERS and GAPS." The policies page says the wiki **hides character status** (alive or dead, etc.) at the top of pages in the infobox and puts a general spoiler warning on the landing page, but "cannot guarantee a reader will not be spoiled." A draft policy bans Patreon-only (early-access) content until chapters go public. [Community/Docs] — [TWI Wiki: Help:Policies](https://wiki.wanderinginn.com/Help:Policies); [Template:New Welcome](https://wiki.wanderinginn.com/Template:New_Welcome); [Main Draft](https://wiki.wanderinginn.com/The_Wandering_Inn_Wiki:Main_Draft). The wiki runs a [Chapter Overview](https://wiki.wanderinginn.com/Chapter_Overview), an [Arc Overview](https://wiki.wanderinginn.com/Arc_Overview) and per-volume "tl;dr" recap pages (e.g., [Volume 6/tl;dr](https://wiki.wanderinginn.com/Volume_6/tl;dr)). Fan maps on the wiki are labelled "NOT canon." [Community] — [TWI Wiki: Fanworks/Enyavar](https://wiki.wanderinginn.com/Fanworks/Enyavar)
- **Worm/Ward (Wildbow):** Ward was published at parahumans.net from Nov 2017 to May 2020. Its table of contents lists interludes alongside chapters. The fan wiki counts 269 chapters and keeps chapter guides and arc lists. [Official/Community] — [parahumans.net](https://parahumans.net/); [Worm Wiki: Ward](https://worm.fandom.com/wiki/Ward); [worm-scraper project](https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/projects/github.com%2Fdomenic%2Fworm-scraper)
- **Homestuck (webcomic, 2009–2016), music as storytelling:** Fan composers produced the music, released by Andrew Hussie's What Pumpkin label on Bandcamp. Early albums were mostly tracks already used in the comic. Later output went "far beyond" what the comic could use. In a 2019 reorganisation, unique track artwork was discarded and many albums became unavailable. Nearly all were restored on 26 Sept 2025. Totals vary by source and date (27 albums/503 tracks up to 39 albums/736 tracks). [Press/Community] — [Wikipedia: Music of Homestuck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Homestuck); [HSMusic Wiki: Official discography](https://hsmusic.wiki/group/official/)
- **Royal Road LitRPG status boxes on phones:** Royal Road's tables and styling "don't port over well" to other platforms. Images avoid that but "don't scale and line wrap" when screens change size, and text-to-speech users "won't get anything at all from an image file." Readers report that simple tables break in some e-reader multi-column modes. One writing guide says system messages should stand out (italics, brackets, boxes) "but not dominate the page." [Community] — [r/litrpg: blue box generator thread](https://redlib.hbubli.cc/r/litrpg/comments/16lqirh/blue_box_generator_free_web_app_for_litrpg); [Goodreads LitRPG: how charts are presented](https://www.goodreads.com/topic/show/18944266-how-charts-are-presented?tab=book); [LitRPG Reads: integrating game systems](https://litrpgreads.com/blog/litrpg/how-to-write-litrpg-integrating-game-systems-into-your-story-without-losing-readers). Fandom's mobile guidance: tables "always try to adapt to the available width," so size images down or take them out of table cells. [Docs] — [Fandom: Avoiding nested tables](https://community.fandom.com/wiki/Help:Avoiding_nested_tables)
- **Recaps in serials:** One author wrote summaries of every earlier book, placed at the start of a glossary-style document for readers catching up. Another series' recap assumes the reader has finished the earlier books. A Reddit serial put a "Previously on" heading above a new arc. Royal Road authors use the author's note at the top of chapters for asides such as cross-promotion. [Community, weak evidence] — [Riyria blog, Apr 2025](https://riyria.blogspot.com/2025/04/free-novels-short-stories-and-bonus.html); [r/RedditSerials](https://lr.eu.psf.lt/r/RedditSerials); [Screenwise guide on a Royal Road serial](https://screenwiseapp.com/guides/mastery-is-earned)
- **Historical precedent, Victorian serial parts:** Dickens's monthly parts generally carried **two full-page etchings** by Phiz. They were tipped in *ahead of* the episode's text and placed together, which "encouraged readers to view them as a pair." Weekly serials (*Master Humphrey's Clock*) carried two to three times more illustrations proportionally. [Scholarly/archive] — [Victorian Web: Phiz and the monthly parts](https://victorianweb.org/victorian/art/illustration/phiz/2cities/pva104.html); [King's College London: Dickens and Phiz](https://kingscollections.org/exhibitions/specialcollections/book-illustration/illustrating-literature-the-revival-of-british-book-illustration/dickens-and-phiz); [Victorian Web: Curiosity Shop](https://victorianweb.org/victorian/art/illustration/phiz/curiosityshop.html)

### Inferences
- The serial genre's norm (text-first official site, art collected separately, reference on wikis) suggests the official site can win by **owning the reference layer itself**. That is the one thing fan wikis do badly, because they cannot know where a given reader is in the story.
- The Dickens model (a pair of images at the head of each instalment, chosen to be read together) is a long-tested rhythm for illustrated serials: **a fixed, small number of images per chapter, at the chapter head, not scattered through the prose.**
- TWI's "hide character status in the infobox" practice is a cheap spoiler guard worth copying, since status fields are the most spoiler-prone part of a cast page.
- Stat boxes as images fail on phones and for screen readers. **Real HTML with a narrow, single-column layout** is better than wide tables.

### Gaps
- I found no evidence about Mother of Learning's official presentation, or about Substack or Ghost fiction serials (headers, recaps, audio). I could not research them before the search budget ran out.
- I could not verify the common belief that TWI chapters end with embedded commissioned or fan art, so it is left out.
- I found no study comparing official and fan wikis for reader trust or spoiler exposure.

## 4. Spoiler-safe reference design: "read up to X" filters, spoiler tags, X-Ray, dynamic glossaries, hover and tap previews, and changing images over time

### Takeaway
There is a clear spectrum. Most fan wikis only **warn**: AWOIAF says "read at your own risk," TWI's wiki hides status fields, and Coppermind tags pages for a set period after a release. A few tools truly **filter by position**: Coppermind's "Time Machine" shows pages as of a date, Quartermaester has a timeline slider, Kindle's "Story So Far" (June 2026) summarises only up to where you are, and Prime Video's X-Ray Recaps (Nov 2024) work "down to the exact minute." Hover and tap previews (Wikipedia, Gwern, Avowed and Pentiment glossaries) are well liked when kept small. For images that change over time, wikis use tabbed or gallery infoboxes ("pre/post-timeskip"), which work poorly on phones when there are many.

### Cited Findings
- **Coppermind (Brandon Sanderson wiki):** No content from an unreleased book may be added. Once a book is released, pages carry a `{{spoilers}}` tag for a set period. One cached version gives 6 months for Cosmere novels and 3 months for non-Cosmere novels, with shorter periods for other works. Some heavy pages get custom tags. Editors are told to leave anything uncertain out. [Docs; durations from a cached copy] — [Coppermind: Spoilers](https://coppermind.net/wiki/Coppermind:Spoilers). A **Time Machine** tool lets readers view pages as they stood before a chosen date. [Docs/Official] — [17th Shard: Coppermind Time Machine](https://www.17thshard.com/news/sitenews/you-can-now-visit-previous-versions-of-coppermind-pages-with-time-machine-r665/)
- **A Wiki of Ice and Fire:** "Spoiler Warning: This wiki contains spoilers… Read at your own risk." The most up-to-date information comes first. I found no formal per-book filter. [Docs] — [AWOIAF Main Page](https://awoiaf.westeros.org/index.php/Main_Page); [Main Page redesign draft](https://awoiaf.westeros.org/index.php/A_Wiki_of_Ice_and_Fire:Main_Page_redesign/V2)
- **Kindle X-Ray:** Opens from the reading menu with tabs for All / People / Terms / Images, sortable by relevance or alphabetically. Opened on a page, it lists that page's characters and terms first. Term cards can carry Wikipedia extracts. Authors can supply their own entries through KDP's "X-Ray for Authors," and can hide terms, which Amazon recommends only if the term is a spoiler. [Press/Docs] — [SlashGear: Kindle X-Ray explained](https://www.slashgear.com/1475659/kindle-x-ray-feature-explained/); [iDownloadBlog (2021)](https://www.idownloadblog.com/2021/02/01/x-ray-kindle-paperwhite/); [The eBook Reader: X-Ray review (2013)](https://blog.the-ebook-reader.com/2013/03/18/kindle-x-ray-feature-review-video/); [SelfPublishing.com: enabling X-Ray](https://selfpublishing.com/?p=24851)
- **Kindle Recaps (launched 2 Apr 2025, updated 2 Jul 2026):** A series "previously on" for best-selling US English titles. Readers get a warning that it "will reveal character and plot developments from this title and preceding ones." It is not spoiler-free about the book it summarises. Sources disagree on whether it is AI-only or AI plus human moderators. [Official/Press] — [About Amazon: Kindle Recaps](https://aboutamazon.com/news/books-and-authors/kindle-recaps-feature-ebook-series-refreshers); [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/this-kindle-feature-compensates-for-terrible-memory-3591046)
- **Kindle "Story So Far" (rolling out June 2026):** A summary of the book "to the point you've read without giving spoilers beyond that point." It is available from the book's menu on US Kindles and the iOS app, with Android promised later in 2026. Some recaps still carry spoiler warnings. [Press] — [9to5Mac, 11 Jun 2026](https://9to5mac.com/2026/06/11/amazons-story-so-far-feature-rolling-out-to-us-kindles-and-iphone-app/); [The eBook Reader, 9 Jun 2026](https://blog.the-ebook-reader.com/2026/06/09/new-story-so-far-feature-rolling-out-to-kindle-ereaders-and-apps/); [Engadget](https://engadget.com/2191716/amazon-story-so-far-feature-is-finally-rolling-out-to-kindles)
- **Prime Video X-Ray Recaps (Nov 2024):** Generative recaps personalised "down to the exact minute of where you are watching," with "spoiler guardrails." The launch was a US Fire TV beta covering Amazon MGM series. [Press repeating Amazon's claims; not independently tested] — [TechCrunch, 4 Nov 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/11/04/amazon-brings-generative-ai-powered-recaps-to-prime-video); [AV Club](https://www.avclub.com/prime-video-x-ray-generative-ai-recaps)
- **Wikipedia Page Previews (hovercards):** In the 2017–18 English and German tests, the feature was "welcomed" and "helping readers learn more." The disable rate was about **0.01%**. Total pageviews **fell**, but the number of **distinct pages** readers interacted with **rose**. Earlier tests (2015–16, several language editions) had mostly favourable feedback. [Data] — [MediaWiki: Page Previews](https://www.mediawiki.org/wiki/Page_Previews); [Prototypr: why it took so long to build Wikipedia's link preview](https://prototypr.io/news/why-it-took-a-long-time-to-build-that-tiny-link-preview-on-wikipedia). [Designer/Community] — [Gwern: Design](https://gwern.net/design); [Gwern newsletter, Mar 2021](https://gwern.substack.com/p/march-2021-gwernnet-newsletter); [gwern.net docs: popups.js](https://gwern-net-docs.pages.dev/frontend/popups-js); [LessWrong: Link previews](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/tmdRw8h6hm7rCsfiu/new-feature-link-previews)
- **In-dialogue glossaries in narrative games (Obsidian):** In *Avowed* (2025) you can "pause conversations anytime, see what people said, and then… look up terms that are highlighted in those conversations." Obsidian describes this as a feature also found in *Pentiment*. - **Codex entries that unlock as encountered (Mass Effect):** Entries come mainly from talking to characters, plus scanning, quests and some that are available from the start. They are split into primary and secondary. In *Andromeda*, some entries **change** as the story develops. It advises letting Back dismiss a sheet, says **stacking sheets** is one of the pattern's biggest problems, and warns against obscuring relevant content. ### Inferences
- A single-author serial site can do what fan wikis cannot: **filter by the reader's own position**. The best-supported models are Coppermind's Time Machine (show the page as of chapter N), Story So Far (summarise to here), and Heaven's Vault and Mass Effect entries that **appear and update** as they are encountered. A Codex built only from what the prose has said already sits at the strict end of this range.
- **Tap previews:** Wikipedia's data (very low disable rate, readers reaching more distinct pages) and Gwern's critique (big popups block reading) together point to **small, one-sentence-plus-thumbnail previews**. On a phone, use a short, non-stacking bottom sheet that closes with Back or a tap outside, and give no nested previews inside it.
- **Changing portraits:** The wiki gallery or tab pattern is the existing convention for per-era images, and its known phone problem is too many images to scroll. A swipeable set capped at one image per Book, with clear labels, matches the convention and avoids that problem. Only portraits for Books already reached should exist on the page.
- Mass Effect Andromeda's changing entries suggest Codex entries can be **rewritten as the story advances**. Coppermind's time-travel view suggests readers may also value seeing an earlier version.

- I found no published study of spoiler exposure on wikis or of whether position filters change reader behaviour.
- No outcome is published for Wikipedia's mobile bottom-sheet preview test, and the exact task number was not verified.

## 5. Maps, journey lines, timelines, family trees and relationship webs

### Takeaway
Maps are less universal in fantasy than assumed (about a third of novels in Ekman's sample, and more for secondary worlds). Readers of long series value **journey lines with a time control** (Quartermaester's slider, the official "Journeys" map), but the static versions spoil anyone not caught up. Placement matters: a map at the front is a promise, and a map at the end is a recap. Hover-to-locate timelines and family trees (LotR Project) are the established pattern for big casts.

### Cited Findings
- **How common maps are:** Stefan Ekman (*Here Be Dragons*, Wesleyan UP, 2013) sampled 200 fantasy novels and collections. **67 (34%)** had at least one map, and the rate was more than double for worlds that are not our own. Maps are "not the compulsory ingredients they are widely held to be" (p. 22). [Study, scholarly] — [Map Room review (2013)](https://www.maproomblog.com/2013/06/review-here-be-dragons/); [Strange Horizons review](https://strangehorizons.com/wordpress/non-fiction/reviews/here-be-dragons-exploring-fantasy-maps-and-settings-by-stefan-ekman/). A separate 2026 study of about 100,000 digitised 19th and early 20th century books found maps in only 1.7% of novels (different corpus). [Study, secondary report] — [Map Room: How often do maps appear in literature? (Jan 2026)](https://www.maproomblog.com/2026/01/how-often-do-maps-appear-in-literature/)
- **Quartermaester (fan interactive map of Westeros/Essos, 2012):** Built on the Google Maps API. A slider animates characters' movements. A "Show Character Paths" menu offers "select all" or "current locations only." Readers can follow several characters to see where paths cross. The site warns that linked wiki pages "may contain spoilers" and that the slider controls only the map. [Community/Press] — [Quartermaester](https://quartermaester.info); [Gizmodo: this spoiler-filled map tracks the travels of every major character](https://gizmodo.com/this-spoiler-filled-map-tracks-the-travels-of-every-maj-5917835); [Adweek/GalleyCat](https://www.adweek.com/galleycat/?p=85909)
- **Official journeys map:** The official atlas *The Lands of Ice and Fire* (map work by Jonathan Roberts) includes a "Journeys" map of character paths through *A Dance with Dragons*, which spoils readers not yet that far. [Press; the search summary gave this without a clear single URL, related pages: [Fantastic Maps: Lands of Ice and Fire](https://www.fantasticmaps.com/tag/lands-of-ice-and-fire/), [Library of Congress Worlds Revealed blog (2016)](https://blogs.loc.gov/maps/2016/07/imaginary-maps-in-literature-and-beyond-different-roads-sometimes-lead-to-the-same-castle/)]
- **Map placement:** A map at the front of a book "is a promise made," and one at the end "works as a recap of the journey." [Author opinion; came from a search result, either [Bobechko's Substack on maps in fantasy novels](https://blakebobechko.substack.com/p/maps-in-fantasy-and-adventure-novels/comments) or [a Goodreads author blog](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/24142640-getting-the-lay-of-the-land?tab=author), not confirmed which.] *The Spear Cuts Through Water* (2022) printed its map on the endpapers to be returned to "once finishing the book." [Designer/author] — [Tor.com: fantasy mapmaking for The Spear Cuts Through Water](https://tor.com/2022/08/25/fantasy-mapmaking-for-the-spear-cuts-through-water)
- **Progressive reveal ("fog of war") is a game and tabletop convention:** GMs reveal maps gradually with post-its or Roll20's Fog of War. Adventure designers reveal and hide areas as the party moves. Video games (e.g., *Crimson Desert*) reveal the world map through exploration. I found no printed novel doing this. [Community/Practitioner] — [Roll20 forum](https://app.roll20.net/forum/permalink/3986954); [Dungeon Fog: Adventure April](https://www.dungeonfog.com/news/adventure-april-23-01-iii/)
- **LotR Project (Emil Johansson):** Began in 2005 as a paper family tree. The 2012 site became a family tree of 800+ characters, then added a **timeline** from the Age of Trees to the Fourth Age where "you can hover over an event and see just where it took place," and later interactive maps such as Beleriand. [Press] — [Tolkien Gateway: The Lord of the Rings Project](https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings_Project); [Gizmodo: interactive LotR timeline](https://gizmodo.com/interactive-lord-of-the-rings-timeline-shows-exactly-wh-5924185); [TheOneRing.net: family tree adds timeline (2012)](https://www.theonering.net/torwp/2012/07/06/58286-lotr-family-tree-adds-historical-timeline/)
- **Heaven's Vault timeline** (see §2): events are added as discovered, and architecture places a site in history. [Press] — [GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/heavens-vault-review)

### Inferences
- A painted map that **draws the route only as far as the prose has gone** combines the 80 Days route idea, Quartermaester's time slider and the game convention of fog-of-war reveal. The best-documented reader demand is for **journey lines**, and the best-documented failure is the static journey map that spoils.
- A "route so far" with an optional slider back through Books gives the recap value of an end-of-book map without spoiling.
- LotR Project's hover link between timeline and map (tap an event, see where it happened) is a cheap and proven cross-link. On phones, use tap instead of hover.
- Because only about a third of fantasy books carry maps, a map is a differentiator, not something readers expect. It should earn its place by tracking the journey, not by showing the whole world.

### Gaps
- I found no reader study on whether maps improve comprehension of fantasy fiction.
- I found no source on serial-specific era maps (one map per Book) or on the official Wandering Inn maps' design reasoning. The store pages only list products.
- I found no design writing on relationship webs (character graphs) for fiction sites beyond family trees.

## 6. Scrollytelling and long-form digital storytelling: when interleaved images help or distract

### Takeaway
Snow Fall (NYT, 20 Dec 2012) showed that big multimedia long-form can draw an audience (3.5M page views in six days). Practitioners since then have converged on **scroll-only, mobile-first, simplify or drop elements on small screens**, and have warned that heavy interactivity is mostly ignored. Pentiment shows that typography alone can carry character, class and setting. Its designers still made the decorative fonts redundant (an easy-reading mode exists), which is a model for keeping ornament from blocking reading.

### Cited Findings
- **Snow Fall numbers:** More than **3.5 million page views and 2.9 million visitors** in about six days, nearly a third of them new to the Times site, 10,000+ Twitter shares, and 22,000 simultaneous readers at peak. [Data, NYT memo via press] — [Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/?p=626373). Editor Joe Sexton said such stories averaged **five times** the engagement of a normal article and more than double that of other long-form. [Designer statement; context unverified] — [Nieman Storyboard](https://niemanstoryboard.org/?p=23795). Sceptics questioned whether the traffic justified the effort and suggested the lasting gain was the publishing infrastructure. [Opinion] — [GigaOM via om.co](https://om.co/gigaom/how-the-new-york-times-can-fight-buzzfeed-reinvent-its-future/); [How we made Snow Fall (Source/OpenNews)](https://source.opennews.org/articles/how-we-made-snow-fall/). One source claims 21 million views. This conflicts with the six-day figure and is probably cumulative (unverified). — [Imaginary Terrain](https://imaginaryterrain.com/archive/snowfalling/)
- **The Pudding, responsive scrollytelling (Russell Goldenberg):** Keep CSS and JS breakpoints in sync. A swipe or tap mechanism "overrides default scrolling behavior and browser functionality, which I do not recommend." For elements that don't fit on mobile, the author prefers to "remove them and replace with some fixed text or annotation." "Pacing has different effects on different screens." Use CSS `position: sticky` rather than JS scroll listeners where possible. [Practitioner] — [The Pudding: Responsive scrollytelling best practices](https://pudding.cool/process/responsive-scrollytelling/); [The Pudding: sticky scrollytelling](https://pudding.cool/process/scrollytelling-sticky/)
- **Mobile-first as a discipline (Reuters Institute, 2018):** Based on interviews with eight visual team leaders (BBC, NBC, Guardian, The Times and others). Designing for phones "makes us simplify and be clearer and be quicker"; it is "good and liberating to think about the phone version first." Long-form scrollytelling is described as linear, narrative, and mostly used for features. [Qualitative study] — [Reuters Institute: Visual storytelling on mobile phones](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/visual-storytelling-mobile-phone-news-audiences)
- **Interactivity is often ignored:** A forum post cites NYT graphics editor Archie Tse's Malofiej 2016 talk (explaining why the Times was making fewer interactives) with the soundbite "readers just want to scroll." The slides are on GitHub, but I could not verify the wording. [Designer, unverified] — [Future of Coding thread](https://linen.futureofcoding.org/t/22673047/i-recall-coming-across-some-opinions-against-interactivity-i); [archietse/malofiej-2016 (slides PDF)](https://github.com/archietse/malofiej-2016). A Marfeel analysis cited by DCN found 75% of readers never get past 10% scroll depth on news articles. [Data, secondary; news context, not fiction] — [Digital Content Next](https://digitalcontentnext.org/?p=26327)
- **Pentiment (Obsidian, 2022), typography as characterisation:** About six custom fonts, made with Lettermatic, are "allocated to characters depending on their social status and level of education" (blackletter for some, more ornate hands for monastics). Lettermatic drew over 2,700 glyphs, some inked by hand with period tools. Josh Sawyer said no feature took as much time as the text rendering. In the easy-reading mode, the fonts offer **only redundant information**. [Designer] — [PC Gamer: How Pentiment's hand-crafted fonts give pen and ink a voice](https://www.pcgamer.com/how-pentiments-hand-crafted-fonts-give-pen-and-ink-a-voice); [Lettermatic: Pentiment case study](https://lettermatic.com/custom/pentiment); [GameBanshee: Sawyer on resurrecting dead fonts](https://gamebanshee.com/96xr8)

### Inferences
- For prose fiction on a phone, the scrollytelling lessons point **away** from scroll-triggered effects and toward plain scrolling. Put images at **section breaks**, give them a fixed aspect ratio so text does not jump, and never use swipe gestures that hijack the scroll inside the reading column. A swipe gallery is acceptable on a separate character page.
- Pentiment's "ornament is redundant" rule fits epigraphs, glyphs and song lines. They can be set in a display face for flavour, as long as the meaning survives in the body face, for accessibility and for reading on a small screen.
- The Snow Fall debate suggests that production-heavy chapters are not needed. The documented gains came from integrating a few strong visuals with long text.

### Gaps
- I found no controlled study isolating whether interleaved images raise comprehension or immersion in **fiction** (as opposed to news). The evidence is practitioner judgement.
- I could not open The Pudding's full article or the Nieman Storyboard analysis. The extracts above are partial.
- I did not research BBC or Guardian long-read templates specifically.

## 7. Phone reading UX: line length, typography, image placement, dark mode, tap targets, image loading, progress

### Takeaway
Use these defaults: about 45–90 characters per line (centre around 65) at 15–25 px body text. Light mode reads slightly better for most people, but offer dark mode. Tap targets should be at least 24×24 CSS px (WCAG 2.2 AA), with 44×44 as the comfortable target (AAA and Apple's guideline). Every image needs width and height (or an aspect ratio), and images near the top should not be lazy-loaded. Comprehension on phones is roughly equal to desktop in NN/g's 2016 study, but reading is slower.

### Cited Findings
- **Line length and size (Butterick):** Average line length 45–90 characters including spaces, or "2–3 lowercase alphabets." Body text 15–25 px on the web. "Shorter lines are more comfortable to read." Measure in characters, not inches, because point size changes the count. [Practitioner authority] — [Practical Typography: Line length](https://practicaltypography.com/line-length.html); [Summary of key rules](https://practicaltypography.com/summary-of-key-rules.html)
- **Comprehension on phones (NN/g, 2016):** 276 participants read articles on phone or computer. "No practical differences" in comprehension, but phone readers were slightly slower. An earlier University of Alberta study (Singh et al., c. 2010–11, cloze test) reported much lower mobile comprehension (figures of 18.93% vs 39.18% are reported secondhand). NN/g still advises brevity on mobile. [Study; primary NN/g page not opened] — [NN/g: Reading content on mobile devices](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-content/); [KU Center for Teaching Excellence summary](https://cte.ku.edu/reason-reconsider-students-mobile-reading); [UX Magazine](https://uxmag.com/articles/why-reading-on-mobile-is-uniquely-challenging)
- **Dark mode (NN/g, Raluca Budiu, 2 Feb 2020):** In a review of research, "the research evidence points in the direction of an advantage of positive polarity [light mode] for normal-vision users." Dark mode may help some low-vision users (e.g., cataracts). NN/g "strongly recommend[s] that designers allow users to switch to dark mode." This is a literature review, not NN/g's own test. [Study review] — [NN/g: Dark mode vs light mode](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/dark-mode/); [Slashdot discussion of the article](https://tech.slashdot.org/story/20/02/16/2041240/)
- **Tap targets:** WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.8 (AA) requires targets of at least **24×24 CSS px**, or spacing such that no other target is within 24 px. **Inline links in sentences are exempt.** SC 2.5.5 (AAA) asks for **44×44**. Apple's HIG recommends 44×44 **points**, and points are not CSS pixels. [Spec] — [W3C: Understanding 2.5.8 Target Size (Minimum)](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html); [Smashing Magazine (Jul 2024)](https://smashingmagazine.com/2024/07/getting-bottom-minimum-wcag-conformant-interactive-element-size/)
- **Images and layout shift (web.dev):** "We recommend adding width and height attributes to all img tags." Without them, lazy-loaded images make Cumulative Layout Shift worse. Browsers derive the aspect ratio from the attributes before rendering. Pair them with `max-width:100%; height:auto`. "Images near the top of the layout that use loading='lazy' can feel significantly slower." CSS `aspect-ratio` is a fallback when attributes can't be set. [Docs] — [web.dev: Browser-level lazy loading](https://web.dev/lazy-loading/); [web.dev: Image performance issues](https://web.dev/learn/images/performance-issues); [Unlighthouse: unsized images](https://unlighthouse.dev/learn-lighthouse/cls/unsized-images)
- **Overlays on phones:** see NN/g on bottom sheets (§4): don't stack them, let Back dismiss them, and don't hide the content they relate to. [Practitioner research] — [NN/g: Bottom sheets](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/bottom-sheet/)

### Inferences
- On a typical phone (about 360–430 CSS px wide) with 16px gutters and a 17–19 px serif, lines fall naturally in Butterick's range, so the risk is the **desktop** layout running too long. Cap the column at roughly 65ch.
- Tappable character and place names are **inline links**, so they are exempt from 2.5.8. They should still have padding or line-height generous enough to avoid mis-taps, and the controls in a preview sheet (close, "open page") should meet 44 px.
- Reserve image space with explicit dimensions so the reading position never jumps. This matters most for readers who return mid-chapter.
- Default to a light, paper-like theme with a well-made dark theme that follows the system setting, as NN/g recommends.

### Gaps
- I found no strong evidence on **reading-progress indicators** (bars, "x min left") in fiction. That topic went unresearched before the budget ran out.
- I did not re-check the 2016 NN/g article's exact text (blocked). The participant count and finding come via secondary summaries.
- I found no data on image formats (AVIF/WebP) or `srcset` savings specific to illustrated fiction.

## 8. Audio alongside text: songs, music cues and ambient sound, and immersion evidence

### Takeaway
The evidence divides clearly. **Background music with lyrics measurably (if mildly) impairs reading comprehension. Instrumental music is roughly neutral.** Sound effects and spatial cues in *audio* fiction increase listeners' mental imagery and transportation. Synced ebook soundtracks (Booktrack) report gains, but the studies were company-commissioned. VN and webcomic practice puts songs in an **opt-in music room or alongside the text**, not over the reading. Homestuck is the strongest example of music as part of how a story is remembered.

### Cited Findings
- **Background sound and reading (Vasilev, Kirkby & Angele, 2018, *Perspectives on Psychological Science*):** A Bayesian meta-analysis found a small negative effect of background sound on reading comprehension, with speech the worst. Meta-regression showed that "studies using lyrical music yielded much bigger distraction effects than studies using instrumental music." A later paper cites the music-on-reading estimate as d = −0.19 across 36 studies. [Study] — [PubMed 29958067](https://www.pubmed.gov/29958067/); [APS: Perspectives article page](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/perspectives/1745691617747398/). In a self-paced reading study, "instrumental music does not lead to distraction," while "song lyrics appear to be distracting, even if… quite mild." [Study] — [Journal of Cognition](https://journalofcognition.org/articles/273). In a college-student experiment, music with lyrics hindered reading comprehension (d ≈ −0.3) and lo-fi instrumental did not. [Study] — [PMC10162369](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10162369/)
- **Sound and imagery in audio fiction (Rodero, 2012, *Communication Research*):** Adding sound effects and "sound shots" (distance cues) to radio drama raised listeners' mental imagery, with sound shots the strongest. Imagery gains tracked attention. [Study] — [UPF repository: See it on a radio story](https://repositori.upf.edu/items/5cde832f-8c47-4ad1-8913-97a36e489f59). **Rodero & Romero (2022, *Media Psychology*):** spatial cues (reverb, footsteps, distance) produced higher imagery and **transportation** than verbal narration, and, apart from sound effects, the highest attention and recall. [Study] — [DOI 10.1080/15213269.2021.1880439](https://doi.org/10.1080/15213269.2021.1880439)
- **Synced soundtracks to text (Booktrack, c. 2013–14):** A University of Auckland study of about 260 students, **commissioned by Booktrack**, reported 30% more time reading and up to 17% higher comprehension. A follow-up with struggling readers reported up to 18% higher comprehension. The audio adjusts to reading speed. EdWeek notes the commissioning and the scepticism of others. A BYU master's thesis (2013) examined enjoyment and distraction from music or effects in fiction e-books. [Study, industry-funded; Press] — [Phys.org (2013)](https://phys.org/news/2013-11-young-readers.html); [EdWeek (Jul 2014)](https://www.edweek.org/teaching-learning/app-aims-to-boost-comprehension-by-putting-e-books-to-music/2014/07); [BYU ScholarsArchive thesis](https://scholarsarchive.byu.edu/etd/3912)
- **Music rooms in VNs:** Ren'Py's Music Room is an out-of-play gallery of tracks, parallel to the CG gallery. [Docs/Community] — [Ren'Py docs mirror](https://doc-renpy-ru.vercel.app/rooms.html); [itch.io thread](https://itch.io/t/5508392/creating-multiple-music-rooms-and-disabling-music-room-briefly-during-play)
- **Homestuck:** Music released as albums alongside the webcomic, many tracks first heard in the comic's animated pages, and a large separate catalogue. The 2019 loss and 2025 restoration of the official albums shows that the music is part of the work's heritage and that hosting it on a third-party platform carries preservation risk. [Press/Community] — [Wikipedia: Music of Homestuck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Homestuck); [HSMusic Wiki](https://hsmusic.wiki/group/official/)

### Inferences
- For songs whose lyrics appear in the prose, the reading-distraction evidence argues for **tap-to-play audio, never autoplay, never looping under the text**. Playing a sung version while the eye reads other words is the condition that harms comprehension. Placing the player **beside the sung lines** (listen, then read on) fits the evidence. An instrumental, opt-in ambient track is the only kind of under-reading audio the research does not penalise.
- The audio-drama imagery research supports the immersion value of sound *when listening is the activity*. Songs heard as their own moment (like VN music rooms) are better supported than soundtracks played under reading.
- A Codex "songs" section that lists only songs the page has sung is the Music-Room equivalent of the CG gallery: a reward and a way to remember.

### Gaps
- I could not verify browser autoplay policies (e.g., Chrome blocking autoplay with sound without a user gesture) in this session because the search budget ran out. That is a technical constraint worth checking.
- I found no designer statements on ambient audio in web IF (80 Days' or Sunless Sea's music reasoning) or on audiobook music cues. Those searches were not run.
- I found no study of reading prose with song lyrics *the same as the lyrics on the page* (as opposed to unrelated lyrics). The distraction findings use unrelated background lyrics.
