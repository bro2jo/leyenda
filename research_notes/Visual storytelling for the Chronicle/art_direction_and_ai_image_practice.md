## 1. Character consistency with image models (2025–2026): what works, what drifts, and the fixes

### Takeaway
As of November 2026 every major tool offers reference-image consistency, but none locks identity. The vendors' own advice is the same everywhere: keep one canonical reference image, attach it on every generation, restate the defining appearance details in words every time, change one thing per turn, and restart from the clean master (not from the last output) when drift starts. Fine details (freckles, logos, scars, small insignia), wardrobe and colour cast drift first. Repeated edit chains degrade the image, a failure the vendors acknowledge.

### Inferences
- **The Wardrobe workflow matches vendor advice, with two gaps.** It already does "reference picture + constructed prompt + solid background". The fixes the vendors and creators converge on are: (a) always attach the **same canonical master** (or a small reference sheet: front, three-quarter, face close-up), never the previous look's output, so errors don't compound; (b) generate each look as a **fresh conversation**, not a long ChatGPT thread (OpenAI's 2.5 claims less degradation, but forum reports and Google's own advice say to restart when drift appears); (c) keep a **fixed "identity block"** of words pasted verbatim into every prompt: build, face, hair, skin, and every scar *with its side and position* ("scar through the left eyebrow"). Naming features is the recommended fix, and mirrored or forgotten asymmetric marks are the most visible continuity break.
- **What to expect to drift, in order:** small insignia and heraldic detail (MJ docs say logos and freckles), wardrobe (creator reports), colour cast (yellow tint), face after many edits (Google, BFL), body and texture after chained edits (papers). So the pipeline should **colour-correct after keying** (neutralise the warm cast, or white-balance against the key colour, which is known), and the author should check each new look against the master at thumbnail size before filing it.
- **Keying choices:** since ChatGPT's own transparency is unreliable and gpt-image-2's is only "preview", the chroma-screen approach is sound. Re-encoding through the key-out script strips C2PA metadata (SynthID may survive), so any disclosure has to live on the page, not in the file.
- **Tool choice if the author ever leaves ChatGPT:** Gemini 3 Pro Image (5 character slots, 14 refs total) or Nano Banana 2 for multi-reference "knight + armour piece + weapon" compositions; Midjourney V8 Edit Model (4 refs) plus a fixed `--sref` code to hold the *painterly style* across portraits, places and cards; Kontext for small local edits, kept under about 5 chained edits.

### Gaps
- Official pages could not be fetched (network allowlist), so the exact current ChatGPT model (2.5 vs 2.6), the status of gpt-image-2 transparency, and the Midjourney V8 Edit Model's final release state could not be confirmed beyond search excerpts.
- No reliable source found on **mirror-flip errors** (scar or heraldry on the wrong side), **hands**, or **text-in-image** accuracy for 2026 models. The GPT Image 2.5 system card claims improved "infographic accuracy and layout" ([system card](https://deploymentsafety.openai.com/chatgpt-images-2-5)), but nothing specific to lettering on shields or banners.
- No authoritative source on AI-made **turnaround or model sheets** as a practice; creator advice only.
- No benchmark comparing tools on character consistency that is independent and recent (late 2026).

## 2. Style bibles and art direction for one coherent world (portraits, places, cards, map)

### Takeaway
Working art bibles are short, rule-based documents: shape language, a palette with *roles* (not just swatches), material logic, one lighting model, framing rules per image type, and, most valuable, paired "correct vs near-miss" examples drawn from real mistakes. The narrative games that hold mixed art together (Darkest Dungeon, The Banner Saga, Disco Elysium, Pentiment, Hades) each commit to **one historical or illustrative tradition** and let it govern every surface, from UI to type.

### Cited findings
- [Weak, practitioner] An art bible should commit "shape language, proportion rules, color and material logic, lighting mood, and rendering fidelity" before production. — [Juego Studio: Art direction in games](https://www.juegostudio.com/blog/art-direction-in-games). The highest-value page pairs "a correct asset next to a near-miss with the differences annotated", built "from real mistakes as production generates them". "The style guide is law until it's amended. Don't make exceptions quietly." — [Bugnet: how to write an art style guide](https://bugnet.io/blog/how-to-write-an-art-style-guide)
- [Weak] Silhouette test: render every character as a filled black shape, and revise any two that look interchangeable. A character should still read in greyscale, because if readability depends on colour or texture, "the underlying shape structure is not strong enough". — [Bugnet: art direct as a non-artist](https://bugnet.io/blog/how-to-art-direct-your-game-as-a-non-artist); [RocketBrush: shape language](https://rocketbrush.com/blog/shape-language-in-game-character-design-how-to-make-characters-readable-and-consistent)
- [Weak] Palette roles: keep most of the palette slightly desaturated and "Reserve full saturation for highlights, magic effects, and pickups". Show the same asset under "correct" and "wrong" lighting so artists can check themselves. — [Sprite-AI style guide](https://www.sprite-ai.art/blog/2d-pixel-art-style-guide); [Bugnet](https://bugnet.io/blog/how-to-write-an-art-style-guide)
- [Weak] Public art bibles worth studying: 0 A.D.'s Art Design Document, the Liberated Pixel Cup style guide, Battle for Wesnoth's "Creating Unit Art", Riot's League of Legends VFX style guide. — [Makko: what four public art bibles say](https://blog.makko.ai/what-public-game-art-bibles-say/)
- [Primary] **Darkest Dungeon**: Chris Bourassa names Mike Mignola, Guy Davis, Dan Panosian, Viktor Kalvachev and Duncan Fegredo as influences. He aimed for "a melancholic, mysterious, and lonely feeling" and designed the art "to reinforce and enhance the tone and design of the game". He wanted it to feel "old and worn, like the time and place in which it's set". — [GameSpot: The gothic sensibilities of Darkest Dungeon](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/the-gothic-sensibilities-of-darkest-dungeon/1100-6424880/); [Game Developer: Road to the IGF](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/road-to-the-igf-red-hook-studios-i-darkest-dungeon-i-). He also drew the UI art, so one hand set every surface. Darkest Dungeon II kept the style while moving to 3D. — [PC Gamer interview](https://pcgamer.com/interview-darkest-dungeon-2s-new-3d-look-monsters-the-narrator-and-its-surprising-aspirational-spirit)
- [Primary] **The Banner Saga**: Arnie Jorgensen built the look on Eyvind Earle's *Sleeping Beauty* backgrounds. He prototyped by splitting an Earle painting into layers and parallaxing it. The team "tried a wide range of art styles but nothing was really striking us" before that. Characters aim to "look like old frame-by-frame animated characters" without copying Disney. The chosen style "is extremely time-consuming" to render. — [Pocket Gamer: Making of The Banner Saga](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/feature/60429/the-making-of-the-banner-saga/); [GeekDad](https://geekdad.com/2012/04/interview-the-banner-saga/4/); [Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2018/09/feature_the_banner_saga_devs_discuss_trilogy_challenges_and_releasing_physical_games_on_switch). A side-by-side analysis found Jorgensen followed Earle's compositions closely but used his own palette. — [Gaming Nexus](https://gamingnexus.com/News/31863/Disney-vs-The-Banner-Saga-(and-everybody-wins!)) [Secondary]
- [Primary] **Disco Elysium**: Aleksander Rostov called hand painting "an obvious choice", saying an "untethered art style fits with the story being told". His bold brushwork unifies the style. On isometric framing: "the isometric image is wonderful in that it is flat", so "you can design the entire game as if it was a painting". Portraits change treatment to signal who a character is (truck drivers appear as their licence ID photos). — [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/disco-elysium-comes-to-life-once-the-talking-stops-zaum-details-its-approach-to-creating-a-truly-expressive-rpg); [MCV/Develop](https://mcvuk.com/business-news/we-knew-immediately-that-we-needed-to-make-a-game-with-a-striking-and-unique-look-to-accompany-the-writing-a-look-that-would-balance-the-mundane-with-the-unfamiliar-and-strange-the-art/); [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/rpg/why-does-isometric-perspective-suit-disco-elysium-you-can-design-the-entire-game-as-if-it-was-a-painting)
- [Primary/Secondary] **Pentiment**: lettering is characterisation. Middle-class writers use a refined cursive, university-educated characters a Humanist script, and older monks Gothic blackletter. A character's font can change once the protagonist learns their background (e.g. secret literacy), and spelling mistakes and erasures hint at education or state of mind. Obsidian worked with Lettermatic on six fonts, after typographic research by director Josh Sawyer and art director Hannah Kennedy. Players can switch the period fonts off for legibility. Religious characters are drawn in a more traditional manuscript style, the protagonist in a woodcut style Sawyer tied to the coming modern world. — [PC Gamer: Pentiment's hand-crafted fonts](https://www.pcgamer.com/how-pentiments-hand-crafted-fonts-give-pen-and-ink-a-voice); [Lettermatic: Pentiment](https://lettermatic.com/custom/pentiment); [Inverse](https://inverse.com/gaming/pentiment-game-art-interview)
- [Secondary/Weak] **Hades**: a bold palette sets the underworld's darkness against the vibrancy of the gods' boons. Analysts note the vibrancy comes from sparing accents that mark hazards, interactables and major events, and from "sharp" shape language of points and hard curves. — [Shapes.inc author guide](https://shapes.inc/fandom/hades/author); [Kymira style study](https://kymira.bearblog.dev/style-study-hades/). For Hades II, art director Jen Zee evolved the first game's visual language with "lunar" and "nocturnal" themes. — [Game Informer](https://www.gameinformer.com/exclusive-video/2024/07/05/supergiant-games-art-director-jen-zee-on-the-making-of-hades-ii). Supergiant: "we're first and foremost a game design-led team." — [MCV/Develop](https://mcvuk.com/business-news/behind-the-art-of-hades-we-value-artistic-integrity-and-excellence-in-artistic-craft-at-supergiant-however-were-first-and-foremost-a-game-design-led-team/)

### Inferences
- **One tradition, every surface.** Each case picks one lineage (Mignola ink, Earle's mid-century painted backgrounds, oil-painted expressionism, the late-medieval book) and runs it through portraits, places, UI and type. For the site, the bible should name one painterly lineage and state it identically in every prompt (portrait, 16:9 place, square card, map). A Midjourney `--sref` code or a fixed style paragraph is the technical carrier.
- **Rules per image type (proposal):** *Portraits*: same light direction and key-light colour across the whole cast, same crop line (e.g. mid-chest), same eye-line height; a change of Book can change the light colour, never the direction. *Places* (16:9): a horizon and time-of-day rule per region; people small or absent so no place picture shows a person the prose hasn't. *Cards* (1:1): a single silhouette-readable action on a flat or vignetted ground, so the metal frame carries rank and the art stays legible at thumbnail size (the greyscale test applies). *Full-body looks* (2:3): fixed camera height and lens feel, feet visible, the same neutral pose library.
- **Make the near-miss page from real failures** (a scar on the wrong side, a too-modern buckle, a yellow cast): it is the cheapest consistency tool for a solo author.
- **Pentiment's lesson for a text-first serial:** type and ornament are part of the art direction. Chapter headers and glyphs can "change script" as the story learns things, with a legibility toggle.

### Gaps
- No GDC talk transcript found for Jen Zee, Bourassa or Jorgensen; the material is interviews and press. Published art books (*The Art of Hades*, *The Art of Darkest Dungeon*) were not accessible.
- No source found on how a studio keeps *mixed image types* (card vs scene vs map) coherent as a named practice; the per-type rules above are inference.

## 3. Colour scripts and palette arcs across chapters and books

### Takeaway
A colour script is a thumbnail strip of the whole story in colour and light, used to plan emotional rhythm at a glance (Pixar, since *Toy Story*). The game equivalent is a palette that changes with story progress (*Gris* restores colour chapter by chapter). For a serial told over years, the same tool works at the Book level: one emotional key per Book, visible as a strip.

### Cited findings
- [Primary] Ralph Eggleston: "A Color Script is a shorthand version of the film that covers the emotions of the film in color and light." He made the first one on his own initiative during *Toy Story*, and the format let the team "move them around like a storyboard". On *Inside Out* each of Riley's emotions was assigned a colour, and the team considered letting them change colour through the film. — [AWN: Ralph Eggleston talks Inside Out](https://www.awn.com/animationworld/ralph-eggleston-talks-inside-out); [Hyperallergic: 25 years of Pixar's colour palettes](https://hyperallergic.com/610771/the-art-of-pixar-chronicle-books/)
- [Primary/Institutional] Cooper Hewitt: "The colorscript, which allows you to see the whole arc of a film's color mood at a single glance, is essential to planning and refining the visual and emotional rhythm of a film to support its story." — [Cooper Hewitt exhibition text](https://www-3.collection.cooperhewitt.org/exhibitions/102147315/text)
- [Primary] Lou Romano on his scripts: "It's really just to give, mostly the emotional tone of each scene … what they should feel like too emotionally, just with pure abstract shapes and color." His *Incredibles* scripts broke from the earlier pastel look with bold shapes. In *Up*, colour (the balloons) is reintroduced into Carl's world as an emotional beat. — [MoMA: Pixar 20 Years of Animation audio](https://www.moma.org/audio/playlist/192/2575); [/Film: Lou Romano's colour script for Up](https://www.slashfilm.com/503975/cool-stuff-lou-romanos-color-script-for-pixars-up/); [Hyperallergic](https://hyperallergic.com/610771/the-art-of-pixar-chronicle-books/)
- [Secondary] *Gris* opens "under complete desaturation", and colours are added to each later environment as chapters are completed. Creative director Conrad Roset "really wanted to start with black and white". Scenes were painted on paper and scanned in layers. Reviews disagree on the exact colour order (red first, then green, blue, yellow is the common account). — [Gamereactor: what does Gris mean](https://www.gamereactor.eu/what-does-gris-mean-we-talk-to-nomada-studio/); [The Artifice](https://the-artifice.com/?p=138955); [Gameluster review](https://gameluster.com/review-gris-a-charming-beautifully-designed-little-platformer/)
- [Secondary] Costume colour as arc: Daenerys wore blues during her liberation campaign and whites while ruling Meereen, holding back the Targaryen colours until later. — [Watchers on the Wall: Season 7 outfits decoded](https://watchersonthewall.com/game-thrones-costume-designer-breaks-season-7-queen-outfits)
- [Secondary] Tolkien wanted four colours for his *Hobbit* maps (green, blue, black, red), and the publisher judged that too costly. A deliberately small palette is a historical fantasy-map convention. — [Frumious Consortium on the Bodleian exhibition](https://thefrumiousconsortium.net/2018/09/04/tolkien-maker-of-middle-earth)

### Inferences
- **Book-level colour script:** pick, before each Book, one dominant hue plus one accent for its emotional key (e.g. a cold, desaturated Book of injury; warmer, more saturated Books as strength returns), and draw a thumbnail strip of its planned climaxes. It belongs GM-side (planning), and only finished Books' colours reach the public site.
- **Carry it on the site:** expose each Book's palette as CSS custom properties (e.g. `--book-accent`, `--book-ink`) switched by a class on the Book's pages: chapter header rule, drop-cap colour, card frame tint, map route line. Portrait galleries are swipeable per Book, so the portrait backgrounds or vignettes for a Book can share its key hue. The colour shift then shows the arc without new art.
- **Gris model fits a recovery story:** the restoration of colour as progress is earned maps onto a story where real progress drives the plot. Colour can be *withheld* in early Books and *returned* by the story, and each return is a visual reward.
- **Keep palettes in the bible as roles,** not swatches: ground, ink, light, Book accent, danger, rank metals. Then prompts and CSS name the same roles.

### Gaps
- No source on colour scripts used across *multi-volume* book series or web serials specifically; the Book-level application is inference.
- Journey (Matt Nava) colour progression did not surface in searches; not covered.

## 4. Costume design as storytelling: arcs, wear, earned gear, heraldry

### Takeaway
Costume designers treat clothes as the character's subtext and as part of the narrative ("it is the story"). Change across a story is designed as an arc: colours and silhouettes shift with the character's phase, pieces inherited from family or mentors carry meaning, and no costume is designed in isolation. Wear, aging and damage are visible history. Heraldic contrast rules (tinctures) give a ready-made legibility system for colours as identity.

### Cited findings
- [Primary] Deborah Nadoolman Landis: "Costume Design is more than storytelling, it is the story" (UCLA panel). — [Daily Bruin, 2023](https://dailybruin.com/2023/03/12/oscar-nominees-express-importance-of-costume-sketch-to-screen-costume-design-panel). In a DGA interview she describes costume as the character's subtext, communicating before the character speaks. — [DGA Quarterly, Jan 2004](https://www.dga.org/craft/dgaq/issues/0401-january-2004/bringing-characters-to-life). Her book *Dressed* says a costume must describe the individual it was designed for. — [Oscars.org: how to create great costume design](https://www.oscars.org/news/how-create-great-costume-design). She says designers work "from the inside out". — [Wikipedia: Deborah Nadoolman Landis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deborah_Nadoolman_Landis) [Secondary]
- [Primary, via press] Michele Clapton (*Game of Thrones*): costumes do character work "before any dialogue"; she wants "the clothes to speak and tell their character". Her costumes follow character evolution, and "no costume should be looked at in isolation". The reunited Starks' clothes carry "something they have taken from their parents in the shape of a dress, the cut of a cape or the quilting details". Sansa's final gown was "a celebration of almost every craft in the building, from armoury to leather to embroidery to printing". — [No Film School](https://nofilmschool.com/2017/06/michele-clapton-costume-designer-game-of-thrones-the-crown); [L'Officiel Ibiza](https://lofficielibiza.com/portraits/michele-clapton-costume-designer-interview-the-crown-game-of-thrones); [Watchers on the Wall](https://watchersonthewall.com/michele-clapton-talks-dressing-the-reunited-starks-and-indian-company-discusses-replicating-westeros); [Wiki of Thrones](https://wikiofthrones.com/game-of-thrones-costume-designer-on-why-sansa-starks-coronation-gown-is-so-special). Embroiderer Michele Carragher describes hidden narratives in Daenerys's dragon-scale dress. — [Winter is Coming](https://dev.winteriscoming.net/?p=33612)
- [Primary, via press] Marvel's *What If...?* head of visual development argues that keeping one unchanged iconic design across films gives up "storytelling tools that could show his journey". *Avatar: The Way of Water*'s 15-year gap was used to add aging detail to the leads' skin. — [SYFY: What If character design](https://www.syfy.com/index%2Ephp/syfy-wire/what-if-marvel-character-design-mcu-interview); [SYFY: Way of Water VFX](https://www.syfy.com/index%2Ephp/syfy-wire/avatar-way-water-vfx-cgi-upgrades-animator-interview)
- [Primary] *Spider-Verse* sequel: a redesign must fit the body and abilities (Miguel "isn't lithe or acrobatic", so his look isn't either). — [MPA: Across the Spider-Verse visual team](https://www.motionpictures.org/2023/06/how-the-spider-man-across-the-spider-verse-visual-team-created-a-mesmerizing-multiverse/)
- [Secondary] Rule of tincture: "metal should not be put on metal, nor colour on colour" (Humphrey Llwyd, 1568). The usual reason given is visibility (dark on dark and light on light are hard to tell apart). Wikipedia notes an alternative origin in early painting limits. Furs and charges "proper" are exceptions. — [Wikipedia: Rule of tincture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rule_of_tincture); [Heraldica: The Rule of Tinctures](https://heraldica.org/topics/tinctrul.htm). The Heraldry Society recommends using it as a contrast check: "Light on dark or dark on light." — [Heraldry Society paper](https://www.theheraldrysociety.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/Humphreys-paper.pdf)

### Inferences
- **The Wardrobe already is a costume arc**; the art direction should make it *read* as one. (1) A fixed silhouette and the identity block stay; (2) pieces change by phase, and each Book can own a dominant costume colour, matching the Book colour script; (3) earned pieces should look **used**: scuffs, repairs, a re-dyed surcoat, a borrowed or inherited piece with a visible maker's touch (the Clapton "taken from their parents" device). The Wardrobe prompt can carry a "wear" slot tied to time in the story, not just unlocks.
- **Damage and scars as history:** describe scars and repairs in the identity block, adding a new one only after the prose has shown it (the spoiler rule). Keep old portraits swipeable, so the history is visible as a sequence. That is the gallery equivalent of a costume arc.
- **Heraldry as identity system:** give the knight and each faction tinctures that obey the rule (metal on colour), so sigils read at card and favicon size. Heraldry itself has only two metals (or and argent), so a rank ladder of iron, bronze, silver and gold card frames is a game convention, not a heraldic one. The tincture logic still applies: keep the card art's ground a *colour* (dark or saturated) when the frame is a bright *metal*, so frame and art separate at thumbnail size.
- **Avoid key-colour clashes:** a blue or green costume piece (common in heraldry: azure, vert) on a chroma screen of the same family will be keyed out. The pipeline should pick the screen colour per look, opposite to the outfit's dominant hue (inference from keying practice in §6 below and [Boris FX on keying hair](https://borisfx.com/blog/hair-green-screen-tutorial-tips-for-keying-hair/), which notes clothing matching the screen complicates the key).

### Gaps
- Exact Landis wording on "costume arcs" not found; *Dressed* and *Filmcraft: Costume Design* were not accessible.
- No developer interview found on game character redesign across acts or sequels with wear and scars (searches for Ellie, Aloy and Kratos returned nothing usable).

## 5. Medieval visual sources: manuscripts, marginalia, heraldry, woodcuts, tapestry, Tolkien

### Takeaway
Medieval visual media come with ready-made, rule-bound grammars a fantasy site can borrow: the manuscript page (initials, borders, marginal commentary, pointing hands), the woodcut (flat line, limited tone, for print-like headers), the tapestry (one continuous band of scenes split by trees and buildings, with laconic captions and borders that comment), and heraldry (tinctures, simple charges). Pentiment and Inkulinati show games adopting these grammars wholesale, including their visual "rules", not just their look. Tolkien's own maps and drawings show a limited palette as a constraint and a style.

### Cited findings
- [Primary, via press] **Inkulinati**: art director Dorota Halicka brought real marginalia to a game-design class, and the team drew on "European handwritten manuscripts of 11th to 14th centuries" with medievalist consultant Łukasz Kozak (of the Discarding Images group). Marginalia "turned out to be ready-to-use concepts for units, board elements, battle types and level design", and the world obeys marginalia's own visual rules (water drawn as blue hills). Halicka: "They are medieval memes, visual gags that you just 'get'." The game won Best Indie Game and Most Original Game at gamescom 2022. — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/thrills-and-quills-the-ink-based-combat-and-magical-manuscripts-of-inkulinati); [TheSixthAxis](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2019/10/31/playing-with-history-illuminating-the-insane-game-world-of-inkulinati/); [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/the-memes-of-medieval-monks-inspired-the-artwork-for-this-comedic-turn-based-strategy); [Epic Games Store news](https://store.epicgames.com/news/inkulinati-is-a-cuter-take-on-the-medieval-strategy-game)
- [Institutional] **Pentiment's sources**: Albrecht Dürer was one model for the protagonist. The game's woodcut imagery links to the *Nuremberg Chronicle* and Erhard Reuwich's designs, and its glossary uses manicules ("helpful hands with often unnaturally long pointing fingers"). — [University of Vienna Library: Companion pieces to Pentiment](https://bibliothek.univie.ac.at/virtuelle-ausstellungen/2024/companion-pieces-to-pentiment.html)
- [Secondary] **Bayeux Tapestry** structure: a broad central frieze with narrow upper and lower borders of fables, farming and hunting. Scenes are "generally separated by highly stylised trees" and buildings rather than frames. Borders "frame but also comment, symbolically, cryptically, ironically and humorously" on the main narrative. Action sometimes breaks into the border (the fleet invades the upper border, border animals grow agitated as Hastings intensifies). The Latin *tituli* are "rather laconic". — [Smarthistory](https://smarthistory.org/the-bayeux-tapestry); [Cambridge: invisible seams and visible boundaries](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/4499AECF8A7A277E746884E249A0AB15/S0263675102000108a.pdf/bayeux_tapestry_invisible_seams_and_visible_boundaries.pdf); [The Past](https://the-past.com/feature/embroidering-history-reconciliation-and-recrimination-on-the-bayeux-tapestry/)
- [Institutional/Secondary] **Tolkien**: he drew pictures into the *Hobbit* "home manuscript" and redrew them for publication. Allen & Unwin objected mainly to his multiple colours in maps, and the 1937 first printing had eleven one-colour drawings and maps and a halftone of Mirkwood. He wanted four map colours (green, blue, black, red), judged too costly. He judged his first-edition illustrations "with the exception of the jacket, bad". The Bodleian holds the largest collection of his manuscripts and drawings; *The Art of the Hobbit* reproduces 110 illustrations from pencil to watercolour. — [Tolkien Estate: Tolkien's Art](https://www.tolkienestate.com/?p=35215); [Bodleian campaign: Mapping Middle-earth](https://www.campaign.ox.ac.uk/report2015-16/mapping-middle-earth); [Frumious Consortium](https://thefrumiousconsortium.net/2018/09/04/tolkien-maker-of-middle-earth); [Gizmodo: Art of the Hobbit](https://gizmodo.com/art-of-the-hobbit-never-before-seen-drawings-by-j-r-r-5855406)
- [Secondary] Heraldry's tincture rule (see §4) is itself a medieval legibility system for sigils. — [Wikipedia: Rule of tincture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rule_of_tincture)

### Inferences
- **Chapter headers and glyphs:** a woodcut-style vignette or historiated initial per chapter (flat line, one ink plus the Book accent) is cheap to generate consistently and survives tiny phone sizes better than painted detail. Manicules and marginal drolleries can mark asides, glossary terms or "a tale from the past" without new UI.
- **The painted map as a tapestry or manuscript map:** a small fixed palette (Tolkien's four colours is a good default), places as tiny vignettes, roads as a continuous band. The map reveals vignettes only for places the prose has visited, which honours the spoiler rule and makes the map a record of the journey. The Bayeux device of *borders that comment* fits a site where the main band is the story and marginal glyphs record deeds or seasons.
- **Borrow the rules, not just the look** (the Inkulinati lesson): write the medieval convention into the bible ("water is drawn as stacked blue hills"; "trees divide scenes"; "captions are three to seven words"). Rules are what keep an AI-assisted image set consistent.

### Gaps
- No sourced example of a web serial or web novel using a manuscript or tapestry layout; no Bayeux-in-games case found.
- No direct source for Tolkien's map conventions beyond the colour constraint (e.g. hachured mountains, north orientation); *Tolkien: Maker of Middle-earth* catalogue not accessible.

## 6. Accessibility and performance for story images

### Takeaway
Story illustrations are informative images: give them short alt text (about 15 words, a fragment) that says what is visibly there, in the story's own vocabulary, and put any longer description in a caption or details element. For a phone-first site, serve AVIF/WebP via `<picture>`/`srcset` with explicit width and height, lazy-load everything except the first visible image, and don't lazy-load or demote the hero.

### Cited findings
- [Official] The W3C WAI alt decision tree: images purely decorative or not meant for users get an empty `alt`; images that convey content get a descriptive alt ("See Informative Images"); images of text whose text is also present nearby get empty alt; for "a graph or complex piece of information" the information goes elsewhere on the page ("See Complex Images"); functional images (icons, links) describe the function. "This decision tree does not cover all cases." — [W3C WAI: An alt Decision Tree](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/images/decision-tree/); [W3C WAI Images Tutorial](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/images/)
- [Institutional] Illustrations of the subject matter "are rarely in this category of decorative images". — [Macmillan Learning: Alt Text Basics (PDF)](https://go.macmillanlearning.com/rs/122-CFG-317/images/Alt%20Text%20Basics.pdf). Maps and illustrations need a text equivalent. — [Stanford Sites User Guide](https://sitesuserguide.stanford.edu/build/media-library/alternative-text-alt-text-images-and-other-media)
- [Institutional] Cooper Hewitt Guidelines for Image Description: alt text is "a short visual description that provides a general sense of the content of an image", "structured as a sentence fragment" of "approximately fifteen words". A longer image description is separate. Skin tone "does not necessarily indicate" racial or ethnic identity, so such matters belong in a caption or long description, not the alt. Senses beyond sight can enrich a description. — [Cooper Hewitt Guidelines for Image Description](https://www.cooperhewitt.org/cooper-hewitt-guidelines-for-image-description/); summary in [WashU Sam Fox School](https://insidesamfox.wustl.edu/?p=4100) [quotes via secondary summaries]
- [Weak] Alt text for artwork "is not a place for personal interpretation" (a guide, not Cooper Hewitt's wording). — [Aesthetics for Birds: how to write alt text](https://aestheticsforbirds.com/2024/05/02/how-to-write-alt-text/). Webcomic practice: describe what happens in the panel; some comics use alt as a second joke, which serves sighted users only. — [Things in Squares](https://www.thingsinsquares.com/?p=3692); [Last Call Media: accessible comics](https://lastcallmedia.com/blog/accessible-comics)
- [Secondary, citing web.dev] Don't lazy-load the likely LCP image; lazy loading it delays the download. `fetchpriority="high"` on the hero (a Chrome team test on Google Flights cut LCP from 2.6 s to 1.9 s); carousel slides beyond the first can be low priority; lazy-load below-the-fold images; set explicit `width`/`height` to prevent layout shift; serve AVIF and WebP in `<picture>` with a JPEG fallback, with AVIF saving roughly 50% versus JPEG at comparable quality but much slower to encode. — [Unlighthouse: large images and LCP](https://unlighthouse.dev/learn-lighthouse/lcp/large-images); [Frontend Masters: optimizing images](https://frontendmasters.com/blog/optimizing-images-for-web-performance/); [MDN: Multimedia: Images](https://developer.mozilla.org/docs/Learn/Performance/Multimedia)
- [Secondary, citing HTTP Archive] The Web Almanac 2025 Page Weight chapter (July 2025 crawl) puts the median mobile homepage's image bytes at about 911 KB of a 2.56 MB total. The 2024 Media chapter found 42% of mobile pages use `srcset`, with frequent `sizes` errors. — [braindetox summary](https://braindetox.kr/ja/posts/image_cdn_optimization.html) [Weak; verify at almanac.httparchive.org]. The 2022 Media chapter recorded "accelerating adoption of AVIF" and rising native lazy-loading. — [Web Almanac 2022: Media](https://almanac.httparchive.org/en/2022/media)

### Inferences
- **Spoiler-safe alt text rule:** describe only what is visible *and* use only names the prose has already given: "the knight in a patched grey surcoat, kneeling at a well" rather than a name or place the page hasn't spoken. Treat alt text as reader-facing prose under the same wall as the site build (the build can scan alt strings exactly as it scans page text). Don't add mood or backstory the image doesn't show (Cooper Hewitt's fragment-of-15-words standard keeps this honest).
- **Per image type:** portraits: "<name as known on page>, <age or phase cue>, <one costume or scar detail>". Places: the setting's visible features. Technique cards: the action shown, *not* the mechanic. The map: short alt plus a text list of visited places (it is a complex image, so the information goes elsewhere on the page). Card frames and ornaments: empty alt (decorative). Book-swipe portrait galleries: give each slide its own alt naming the Book, so screen-reader users get the change over time too.
- **Weight budget for phone:** with the median mobile homepage already near 1 MB of images, a gallery page should load one 2:3 look at about 400–600 px wide (AVIF or WebP; measure the actual file sizes rather than assume them), lazy-load the rest, and give swipe galleries `loading="lazy"` with the first slide eager. Key-out output is PNG (alpha); the build should transcode to AVIF/WebP with alpha for display and keep the PNG only as the master.

### Gaps
- web.dev pages themselves did not surface; performance numbers are via secondary summaries citing them.
- No source addresses alt text in serialized fiction specifically, or spoiler handling in alt text; the rule above is inference.

## 7. Disclosure and reader perception of AI art in fiction (2025–2026)

### Takeaway
Reader communities in fantasy publishing are hostile to *undisclosed* AI art, and the recurring scandal pattern is discovery after the fact (stock-site labels, visible artefacts) followed by backlash. Surveys show large majorities want labels, and experiments suggest disclosure lowers ratings somewhat, with explicit human authorship and oversight softening that. Provenance metadata (C2PA) and watermarks (SynthID) now ship with ChatGPT images, but metadata is easily stripped.

### Cited findings
- [Secondary] Publishing incidents: Tor's *Fractal Noise* cover (2022) — [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/tor-book-ai-art-cover-christopher-paolini-fractalverse-1849904058); Bloomsbury's *House of Earth and Blood* edition (2023), where Bloomsbury said it was unaware the stock image was AI-generated — [inkl](https://inkl.com/news/bloomsbury-admits-using-ai-generated-artwork-for-sarah-j-maas-novel); Tor's Bramble imprint criticised over *Gothikana* (2024) — [Publishers Weekly](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/print/20240226/94388-tor-books-imprint-bramble-criticized-for-use-of-ai-generated-art-in-gothikana-cover-design.html); a *Game of Thrones* premium collector's edition drew AI-art accusations, which the publisher disputed (Nov 2025) — [AOL/Independent](https://www.aol.com/news/george-rr-martin-publisher-defends-092005996.html); two AI covers in New Zealand publishing (Nov 2025) — [The Spinoff](https://thespinoff.co.nz/books/19-11-2025/how-two-ai-book-covers-forced-the-publishing-industry-to-reckon-with-its-future); "Book Publishers Have a Major AI Problem" (July 2026) — [Plagiarism Today](https://www.plagiarismtoday.com/2026/07/09/book-publishers-have-a-major-ai-problem/). The common thread: stock assets labelled "generated with AI" were discovered by readers. — [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/as-they-supposedly-battle-ai-slop-major-publishers-reportedly-cover-their-books-in-it-2000824603)
- [Weak] Web-serial community norms: in 2023 r/ProgressionFantasy (a large reader community for web serials, much of it on Royal Road) barred promoting with AI art and required a link to the artist, with an exception for Adobe Firefly as "ethically sourced". Recent Royal Road self-promotion posts show authors labelling covers "No AI" or disclosing AI covers, which suggests disclosure is common but not uniform. No official Royal Road AI-art policy surfaced. — [r/ProgressionFantasy rules thread (mirror)](https://redlib.hbubli.cc/r/ProgressionFantasy/comments/146e9eb/discussion_rules_changes_for_promotion_and_ai); [r/royalroad (mirror)](https://r.datuan.dev/r/royalroad/rising)
- [Secondary, survey] A "Trust in the Age of Generative AI" study (nearly 10,000 consumers, seven markets): 86% of Australian respondents want brands to label AI-generated material; 29% would trust a brand less if content were AI-generated, 22% more. — [FutureFive](https://futurefive.com.au/story/australians-demand-ai-labelling-as-trust-concerns-grow). A Fractl 2026 survey reportedly found 90% want AI images labelled. — [OpenArt blog (vendor)](https://openart.ai/es-es/blog/the-ai-label-nobody-actually-trusts/) [Weak]
- [Paper, small sample] Disclosure of generative AI reduced creative ratings (Bauer et al. 2024, cited in a jewellery-advertising study, n = 127), and AI-assisted work was viewed more favourably when human oversight was explicit (Kučinskas 2025). — [Journal of Graphic Engineering and Design](https://sp.ftn.uns.ac.rs/index.php/jged/article/download/2419/1596/5589). A CSCW 2025 preprint tests how label detail and content stakes change perceptions (results not retrieved). — [arXiv 2510.19024](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.19024v1)
- [Paper] Artists surveyed (n = 459) strongly favour transparency about AI use and training data. — [Lovato et al., arXiv 2401.15497](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.15497v5)
- [Official] ChatGPT, Codex and API images carry C2PA metadata and a SynthID watermark, with a public checker in preview. — [OpenAI: Advancing content provenance](https://openai.com/index/advancing-content-provenance)

### Inferences
- **Disclose plainly, once, and say what is human-made:** e.g. a short colophon line ("Words by the author; pictures made by the author with AI image tools from his own designs and prompts"). The experiments suggest that stating the human direction (the bible, the Wardrobe's chosen pieces, curation) is what softens the penalty. A text-first serial whose art is plainly secondary carries lower stakes than a cover.
- **Don't let the pictures carry the story:** the prose is the canon and pictures only illustrate what it has said (the spoiler rule). That also limits reputational exposure: readers come for the text.
- **Metadata:** the key-out re-encode strips C2PA, so the colophon is the disclosure. Optionally keep the original ChatGPT PNG in the private archive as provenance.

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 survey specific to *web serial or web fiction readers* and AI illustrations; no official Royal Road, Wattpad or AO3 policy text on AI images retrieved.
- Several survey figures are from vendor or secondary sources and could not be verified against originals.
