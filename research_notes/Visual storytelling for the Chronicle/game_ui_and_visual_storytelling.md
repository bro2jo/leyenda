# How narrative-heavy games use visuals, UI and graphics to carry story, stakes and character growth

Research notes, compiled 2026-11-17. Organised by technique (one section per key question).

**How to read these notes**
- Source strength tag after each finding: **[D]** designer statement (primary, or a designer quoted in press), **[C]** critic or review, **[S]** study or academic work, **[W]** wiki, guide or fan analysis, **[F]** forum or player anecdote, **[M]** mirror or aggregator site (weakest; verify before quoting).
- **Method caveat (important):** this session's network policy blocked full-page fetches from pcgamer.com, gamedeveloper.com and gamespot.com (and probably other press sites). Every finding below comes from search-engine summaries of the cited pages, not from reading the pages in full. Quotes are as the summaries gave them. Check the wording against the original before quoting it in public. Where a summary did not say which page a claim came from, the citation says so and lists the likely pages.
- Release years are given for context: Hades (2020; early access 2018), Disco Elysium (2019; Final Cut 2021), Outer Wilds (2019), Return of the Obra Dinn (2018), Heaven's Vault (2019), Pentiment (2022), Wildermyth (2021), Darkest Dungeon (2016), Crusader Kings III (2020), The Banner Saga (2014), Mass Effect 2/3 (2010/2012), Baldur's Gate 3 (2023), Citizen Sleeper (2022; sequel 2025), XCOM: Enemy Unknown/XCOM 2 (2012/2016), 80 Days (2014), Hollow Knight (2017), Firewatch (2016), Journey (2012), Gris (2018), Persona 5 (2016/17), Slay the Spire (2019), Elden Ring (2022).

## 1. Journals, codices and lore screens: how story knowledge is presented visually

### Takeaway
The lore screens players remember are the ones that **record what the player has actually learned, at the moment they learn it**: Heaven's Vault's timeline, Outer Wilds' rumor graph, Obra Dinn's logbook, Pentiment's fonts that change as you learn who someone is. They also make knowledge **the** form of progress, not an add-on beside it. Codices that are only encyclopedias (Mass Effect-style) are not well documented in these sources. Hades' design ties the codex and relationship screens to story beats and to scarce gifts, so they work as a record of a relationship rather than a glossary.

### Cited Findings

**Pentiment: typography as character information (Obsidian, 2022)**
- Josh Sawyer: "Handwriting was changing a lot at this time, with different literacy levels." A peasant who may be illiterate, or who writes poorly, speaks in a peasant script. Each font signals the speaker's background, and **as Andreas learns more about someone, their speech can change fonts**. [D] — [PC Gamer, "How Pentiment's hand-crafted fonts give pen and ink a voice"](https://www.pcgamer.com/how-pentiments-hand-crafted-fonts-give-pen-and-ink-a-voice)
- Fonts also work as clues. A scribe who makes many errors could be an impostor. [D/C] — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/how-pentiments-hand-crafted-fonts-give-pen-and-ink-a-voice)
- Readability trade-off: Sawyer says Fraktur "is difficult to read today". An optional hard mode adds the long s (it looks like a cursive f) and ligatures. The fonts "are only ever offering redundant information", so the accessible easy-reading mode loses no story. [D] — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/how-pentiments-hand-crafted-fonts-give-pen-and-ink-a-voice)
- The foundry Lettermatic drew more than 2,700 glyphs, some inked by hand with period writing tools. [D, studio portfolio] — [Lettermatic: Custom fonts for Pentiment](https://lettermatic.com/custom/pentiment)
- A Rock Paper Shotgun interview (seen only on mirror sites) carries the headline that Sawyer said no feature took as much time as the text rendering. Sawyer also gave a GDC 2025 talk on the font work. [M] — [RPS mirror](https://rockpapershotgunz.pages.dev/posts/how-obsidian-entertainment-resurrected-dead-fonts-for-pentiment); [JikGuard report on the talk](https://www.jikguard.com/news/pentiment-director-explains-how-going-all-in-on-fonts-helped-elevate-the-medieval-detective-rpg)

**Heaven's Vault: a timeline that fills in as you learn (inkle, 2019)**
- Each object you find reveals something about the period it was made in and "drops a short text box onto a timeline". A passing mention of the protagonist's past in conversation brings up "an unobtrusive pop-up" saying it has been added to the timeline. [C] — [GamesRadar review](https://www.gamesradar.com/au/heavens-vault-review)
- The timeline zooms: out to the major events of the past 1,000 years, in to the key moments of Aliya's own life. [C] — [GamesRadar review](https://www.gamesradar.com/uk/heavens-vault-review)
- Reception: Kotaku said the history would be "tough to keep straight in your head without this tool". TechRaptor described "a certain joy in diving into this timeline". [C] — [Kotaku review](https://kotaku.com/heavens-vault-the-kotaku-review-1834082676); [TechRaptor review](https://techraptor.net/content/heavens-vault-review)
- The timeline sits on the pause screen next to a map and a collection of translated texts. [C] — [Gamereactor](https://www.gamereactor.eu/?p=59479)
- Senior artist Laura Dilloway drew an architectural timeline, so a player can place a site in the nebula's history by its architecture alone. The art itself encodes chronology. [D, via summary] — [Game Developer, "Knockin' on Heaven's Vault"](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/knockin-on-i-heaven-s-vault-i-how-inkle-designed-its-first-3d-game)
- Jon Ingold on the ambiguity: "you won't ever be sure if your translations are correct" (intentional). He calls the hieroglyph system "really, really hard". [D] — [Game Developer, Heaven's Vault](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/-i-heaven-s-vault-i-inkle-s-game-about-a-nebula-riding-archaeologist); [Archaeogaming interview with Jon Ingold (2019)](https://archaeogaming.com/2019/01/25/an-interview-with-jon-ingold-of-heavens-vault/)

**Outer Wilds: the ship log's rumor map, knowledge as the only progression (Mobius, 2019)**
- Alex Beachum: "Very early on we were like, OK, you're not exploring to gain upgrades or to improve your equipment." [D] — [AV Club, quoting Paste](https://www.avclub.com/outer-wilds-and-inner-depth-making-something-new-o)
- Edge reported no ship upgrades and "no collectables of any kind". The aim was for curiosity to be the only motivation. [C/D] — [Edge via PressReader (2019)](https://www.pressreader.com/australia/edge/20190620/282278141843961)
- A GDC Game Narrative Review paper (2022) puts it this way: the player is "restricted only by their knowledge and curiosity". [S, student/industry paper; the summary's claim that Beachum co-authored it is unverified] — [GDC 2022 Game Narrative Review paper](https://media.gdcvault.com/GDC+2022/Game+Narrative+Poster+Review/Micallef+Paper.pdf)
- In rumor mode the log becomes "a visual graph of partial knowledge". It records discovered locations, translated notes, signals and unresolved connections, so players remember what earlier loops revealed without the mystery turning into a quest list. [W, fan analysis] — [Birdor case study](https://birdor-blog.pages.dev/outer-wilds-knowledge-progression-product-case-study/). A fan wiki adds that the team decided against quest markers or objective logs. [W, unsourced] — [Outer Wilds fan page](https://shapes.inc/fandom/outer-wilds)
- Designer talks: "Curiosity-Driven Exploration: The Design of 'Outer Wilds'" (Alex Beachum and Loan Verneau, GDC 2020); "The 4D Level Design of 'Outer Wilds'" (GDC 2020); Kelsey Beachum, "Sparking Curiosity-Driven Exploration Through Narrative" (Independent Games Summit). [D] — [GDC news](https://www.gdconf.com/news/attend-gdc-and-learn-how-outer-wilds-nailed-curiosity-driven-game-design); [GDC Vault 1027008](https://gdcvault.com/play/1027008/Independent-Games-Summit-Sparking-Curiosity)

**Return of the Obra Dinn: the logbook as a deduction ledger (Lucas Pope, 2018)**
- The logbook is a crew roster with blanks for each person's name, cause of death and who was responsible. Faces stay blurred until there is enough evidence, and correct fates are confirmed **three at a time**. The essay calls this a design compromise. [W, interpretive essay, not Pope] — [Archania essay](https://archania.org/p/the-symbolic-world/symbolic-works/video-games/return-of-the-obra-dinn)
- The same essay says early prototypes centred on how people died, and that identifying who each person was proved the deeper puzzle. [W] — [Archania](https://archania.org/p/the-symbolic-world/symbolic-works/video-games/return-of-the-obra-dinn)
- Pope wrote that his original vertical slice "did not scale up (at all) to making a full game". [D, quoted] — [Kill Screen](https://www.killscreen.com/development-of-papers-please-creators-next-game-hits-rough-seas/)

**Disco Elysium: the Thought Cabinet and skill voices (ZA/UM, 2019)**
- The Thought Cabinet is a second build system. Thoughts come from conversations and discoveries, take time to "internalise", fill limited slots (more slots cost skill points) and can be forgotten. [W] — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disco_Elysium); [GameBanshee: Introducing the Thought Cabinet](https://gamebanshee.com/39hc9)
- Robert Kurvitz called the Thought Cabinet a "quagmire" feature that almost sank the whole project. [D] — [GameSpot](https://gamespot.com/articles/how-the-feature-that-almost-sunk-disco-elysium-was/1100-6472676/). In another interview he floated a next version in which thoughts affect their neighbours in adjacent slots. [D, via summary] — [RPG Codex: Kurvitz on GameSpot Audio Logs](https://rpgcodex.net/article.php?id=11309)
- Concept artist Anton Vill drew the thought icons. A tapestry weaves every thought's icon into one image. [W] — [Gleech](https://www.gleech.org/disco)
- Kurvitz used Twitter as the reference for the dialogue feed, because people skim a feed for its main idea rather than reading every line. [D] — [80.lv: Disco Elysium, working on UI design](https://80.lv/articles/disco-elysium-working-on-ui-design)

**Hades: codex and relationships as a record of the bond (Supergiant, 2020)**
- Greg Kasavin: the story advances with each death, and the characters openly treat the loop as learning. [D] — [Inlander](https://inlander.com/culture/hades-writer-greg-kasavin-on-how-he-made-video-game-deaths-drive-a-feel-good-story-22725237)
- Nectar is scarce, and giving it is the only way to unlock a character's story. Each gift raises the relationship one level, and the first gift returns a Keepsake. [W, guide] — [GamesRadar: Hades Nectar](https://gamesradar.com/hades-nectar); [Prima Games](https://primagames.com/tips/hades-keepsake-list)
- In Hades II, Keepsakes rank up from encounters cleared while equipped. [W] — [Pixel Twelve](https://pixeltwelve.com/articles/hades-ii-keepsakes-gifts-relationship-unlocks-guide). One guide says Hades II gifts no longer fill a meter and the relationship shows in the Codex and in ambient dialogue instead. Another guide describes a filling heart meter. **These conflict and could not be resolved** (they may describe different patches). [W] — [Broken Builds Hades II guide](https://brokenbuilds.gg/hades-ii/guides/romance); [GamesRadar Hades II Nectar](https://gamesradar.com/games/hades/hades-2-nectar)
- Critic reading: in Hades dying advances the narrative, and the House becomes "proof that the world was paying attention". [C, student critical-play blog] — [Mechanics of Magic: Critical Play, Hades (2026)](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/2026/05/11/critical-play-hades-2/)
- Kasavin was the studio's sole narrative designer but worked closely with audio director Darren Korb. [D, talk report] — [ScreenHub on Kasavin's LudoNarraCon talk](https://www.screenhub.com.au/news-article/features/digital/jini-maxwell/hades-greg-kasavin-breaks-down-supergiants-unique-approach-to-narrative-262459)

**Souls/Elden Ring: item-description lore and the gap the player fills (FromSoftware)**
- Hidetaka Miyazaki, as a child, read books too hard for him and let his imagination fill the passages he couldn't follow, **using the accompanying illustrations**. "When I was young, I used to enjoy reading books that were too hard for me, where I could only read maybe half the kanji, and using my imagination to fill in the gaps." [D, via secondary mirrors of the Guardian (2015) and PlayStation Blog; verify] — [Mirror of the coverage](https://enufyyu.pages.dev/posts/hidetaka-miyazaki-has-several-reasons-to-defend-elden-ring-against-its-greatest-criticism-hyper-fragmented-storytelling); [SCMP profile](https://www.scmp.com/lifestyle/technology/apps-gaming/article/1768480/look-inside-world-japanese-video-game-master)
- Coverage of his interviews ties this to FromSoftware's fragmented storytelling: "we want to leave some of the story to the player's imagination." [D, secondary] — [same mirror](https://enufyyu.pages.dev/posts/hidetaka-miyazaki-has-several-reasons-to-defend-elden-ring-against-its-greatest-criticism-hyper-fragmented-storytelling)

### Inferences
- The strongest lore UIs **write only what the player has witnessed, at the moment of witnessing**: the timeline pop-up, the rumor node, the font that changes once you learn someone's class. That maps directly onto a "the picture shows only what the prose has said" rule. The Codex can announce "added to the Codex" when a chapter first names a place, as Heaven's Vault does, and entries can grow or rewrite as later chapters reveal more, like Pentiment's fonts.
- Pentiment shows that typography can carry social information about a speaker (class, literacy, mood) **as long as it is redundant with the prose**. Their own rule that fonts "only ever offer redundant information" is a good accessibility rule for a phone site. A Codex or epigraph could use a distinct face per faction or culture, as a texture rather than the only signal.
- Outer Wilds and Obra Dinn show that a **graph or ledger of partial knowledge** (blanks, blurred faces, dashed links between rumours) makes the unknown visible without spoiling it. A Codex with unresolved, dashed "rumour" links between entries could carry the same feeling. It must never name what the page hasn't.
- Miyazaki's anecdote supports **sparse illustration next to dense text**: the picture is a foothold for the reader's imagination, not a full explanation.

### Gaps
- Mass Effect's codex (encyclopedic entries, some narrated aloud): no sources found this session on design intent or player recall.
- No study found that measures whether codex or journal UIs improve story comprehension or recall. Critic praise (Kotaku on Heaven's Vault) is the only evidence of comprehension.
- Lucas Pope's own devlog statement on the three-at-a-time confirmation was not found. Only an interpretive essay supports it.
- Supergiant's own statement on why relationship progress is visible (hearts) was not found. A HyperX interview asks how the expandable Codex came about, but the answer did not surface ([HyperX](https://blog.hyperx.com/article/6998/interview-with-the-hades-team)).
- Disco Elysium's skill "voices" as portraits: there is no designer source on the portrait art's intent. See section 3 for what was found.

## 2. Character growth shown visually: portraits, bodies, outfits and unlocks

### Takeaway
Growth reads most strongly when the change is **permanent, on the body or face, and caused by a story event the player remembers**: Wildermyth's transformations, Mass Effect 2's scars, Crusader Kings III's ageing and lifestyle bodies, Darkest Dungeon's afflictions. Research on avatar customisation finds that customising raises identification with the character. There is little hard evidence on whether tying cosmetics to story milestones makes the milestone feel earned. Hades ties unlocks to relationship beats, but no designer statement on that link was found.

### Cited Findings

**Wildermyth: comic panels and permanent transformations (Worldwalker, 2021)**
- Story events (visiting a town, exploring a ruin, attacking a stronghold) play out as comic strips, and the comic sections carry most of the story. [C] — [Vice review](https://www.vice.com/en/article/wildermyth-review/); [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/wildermyth/dnd-quiet-moments)
- Transformations are magical events that give a hero something permanent, such as wings, fire attacks or a familiar. Retired heroes can come back as "Mythic" versions with their transformations, traits and gear intact, and heroes can have children who join their ageing parents. [C/W] — [GameBanshee interview](https://gamebanshee.com/xykr3); [TV Tropes](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/Wildermyth)
- Why comics: the stories by lead writer Doug Austin were "far too elaborate to act as mere filler between missions". The papercraft 2D style came partly from budget. Artist Annie Austin: "We knew we were going to do 2D because I'm not a 3D artist at all". Skipping detailed animation kept the art workload manageable across so many character permutations. [D, via RPS making-of on a mirror] — [RPS "The making of Wildermyth" (mirror)](https://wwwrockpapers.pages.dev/posts/the-making-of-wildermyth-how-a-family-developer-created-one-of-the-most-innovative-rpgs-in-years)
- Nate Austin wanted procedural stories that feel personal and hand-crafted, "like a story you'd tell around a campfire". [D] — [Turn Based Lovers interview](https://turnbasedlovers.com/10-turns-interview/with-wildermyth-developer); [Goodreads repost of a dev interview](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/19162356-game-dev-interview-wildermyth)

**Crusader Kings III: portraits that age, sicken and show a lifestyle (Paradox, 2020)**
- Dev Diary #34, "It's all about appearances": ageing is seamless. In their 30s and 40s characters get rougher skin, lines and grey hair, and in old age bodies sag and posture declines. Children's adult features are present from birth but toned down, and grow more pronounced with age. [D] — [Paradox forum, CK3 Dev Diary #34](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/threads/ck3-dev-diary-34-its-all-about-appearances.1406933/page-4); [TheSixthAxis summary](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2020/07/31/crusader-kings-3-developer-diary-contracts-portraits/)
- Portraits change with lineage, age, lifestyle and illness. Bodies range from "alarmingly thin victims of starvation" to "truly impressive bulks of some high nobility gluttons". Appearance is inherited through a DNA stream with dominant and recessive genes. [D] — [DualShockers](https://www.dualshockers.com/crusader-kings-iii-new-dev-diary-explores-character-portraits-and-the-vassal-contract-system/); [GamingOnLinux](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/articles/17279/)
- No source confirmed that stress changes CK3 portraits. [Gap noted by search]

**Mass Effect 2/3: morality shown on the face (BioWare)**
- In ME2, Renegade progress worsens Shepard's facial scars and Paragon progress heals them. Distortion starts once the first Renegade bar fills, and "you really have to commit" to see the full effect. [W, guide] — [Fextralife ME2 wiki](https://masseffect2.wiki.fextralife.com/Character+Creation); players report Paragon scars fading over time. [F] — [Nexus Mods posts](https://www.nexusmods.com/masseffect2/mods/4?tab=posts)
- Paragon options are blue and Renegade red. ME2 had two separate meters. ME3 used one Reputation meter filled by both a blue and a red bar. [D/C] — [BioWare blog, "Reputation in Mass Effect 3" (2012)](https://blog.bioware.com/2012/03/01/reputation-in-mass-effect-3/); [PopMatters, "BioWare finally gets the morality meter right in Mass Effect 3"](https://www.popmatters.com/156838-2495865710.html)
- Players mod the scars to be fixed, chosen, or without the Renegade glow, which suggests many wanted control over the morality-made-visible effect. [F] — [Nexus Mods, ME Legendary Edition mod 210](https://www.nexusmods.com/masseffectlegendaryedition/mods/210)
- A disability-studies piece, "More Than Skin Deep: The Representation of Disfigurement in Modern Gaming", treats this kind of imagery critically. Only its title and topic were seen. [S/C] — [Cultures of Disability, MMU](https://culturesofdisability.mmu.ac.uk/?p=1093)

**Darkest Dungeon: stress and affliction made visible (Red Hook, 2016)**
- Heroes build up stress, shown as a meter on each character. When it fills they face a resolve check, and failure brings an affliction. The designers' goal was the human response to stress: anyone can break under pressure, and people break in different ways. [D] — [Game Developer, Darkest Dungeon's affliction system deep dive](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-i-darkest-dungeon-s-i-affliction-system)
- Tyler Sigman cited Band of Brothers as an influence and framed the game as being about the human response to stress, not about mental illness. [D] — [PC Gamer, inspiration from Band of Brothers and Aliens](https://www.pcgamer.com/how-darkest-dungeon-got-inspiration-from-band-of-brothers-and-aliens)

**The Banner Saga: hand-painted art as the story's register (Stoic, 2014)**
- Arnie Jorgensen did "virtually every piece of art" outside animation. The style is influenced by Eyvind Earle, art director on Sleeping Beauty. One wide travel scene with a tiny caravan could take "a couple weeks, easily". [D] — [GameBanshee interview](https://gamebanshee.com/3ay); [80.lv background artist interview](https://80.lv/articles/the-banner-saga-background-artist-interview)

**Customisation, cosmetics and identity: research**
- Böffel et al. (2022, *Frontiers in Psychology*, League of Legends): customising a character raised players' subjective identification with it. [S] — [PMC full text](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8765231/)
- Earlier work (Turkay and Kinzer, LOTRO) supports the claim that customisation strengthens identification with the avatar. The Proteus effect (Yee and Bailenson) finds behaviour shifting to match the avatar's appearance. [S, as cited] — [Frontiers PDF](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.770139/pdf); [DiGRA paper](https://dl.digra.org/index.php/dl/article/download/2809/2793/2852)
- A thesis comparing WoW and FFXIV transmog/glamour found that players value the freedom of appearance changes and **don't** build avatars as versions of themselves. [S, BSc thesis] — [Stockholm University, "Dress to impress but not for success"](https://gameresearch.blogs.dsv.su.se/?p=143)
- A DiGRA mixed-methods case study of Baldur's Gate 3 (149-player survey, 10 interviews) found a gap between what players expect from dynamic customisation and current design practice. [S] — [DiGRA](https://dl.digra.org/index.php/dl/article/download/2809/2793/2852)

**Hades: unlocks tied to relationship beats**
- First Nectar gift returns a Keepsake, so a relationship beat yields an object you carry. [W] — [Prima Games](https://primagames.com/tips/hades-keepsake-list). A critic reads Keepsakes as reflecting emotional bonds. [C] — [Mechanics of Magic](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/2026/05/11/critical-play-hades-2/)

### Inferences
- The most legible growth visuals are **diffs on the same figure**: CK3's same face across decades, ME2's same face scarred or healed, Wildermyth's hero now with wings. A portrait per Book will read as growth if pose, framing and palette stay constant so only the earned changes stand out (posture, scars, gear, bearing). The "compared against his former self" framing on the sheet is the same idea as CK3's or ME2's before/after.
- Wildermyth pairs each permanent change with a **comic-panel scene of the event that caused it**, so the body change has a story memory attached. On the site, an unlocked Wardrobe piece or a new portrait could link back to the chapter or scene where it was earned. That follows the "picture shows only what the prose has said" rule, and it lets the reader discover the cause instead of studying a table.
- Customisation research says identification rises when the player chooses the look. The Wardrobe's prompt-builder already does this. The WoW/FFXIV finding (players don't build themselves) suggests the knight can stay a character, not a mirror of the author.
- ME2's modding shows a risk: a visible change driven mechanically by a hidden meter can feel imposed. Changes tied to named story events land better than ones tied to thresholds.

### Gaps
- No designer statement or study found on whether **cosmetics tied to story milestones** make the milestone feel more earned (versus purchased or grind cosmetics). This needs its own search: Hades outfits/aspects, Destiny/FFXIV glamour, Monster Hunter armour as trophy.
- Darkest Dungeon's trinkets and hamlet upgrades as visual progress: not covered.
- Hades' Aspects/outfits: not covered.
- Banner Saga portrait changes across the trilogy: not covered.
- The disability-studies critique of scar-as-morality was seen by title only.

## 3. Progression screens as story: character sheets, menus, diegetic vs non-diegetic UI

### Takeaway
Stylised progression screens (Persona's red ransom-note menus, Disco Elysium's painted skill portraits, Pentiment's manuscript page, Firewatch's paper map) carry tone and identity as well as data. The designers say they are costly and must still be readable. Academic evidence that diegetic UI deepens immersion is **modest and conditional**: it helps experts in one shooter study and is preferred in VR without measurable presence gains.

### Cited Findings

**Persona 5: menus as identity (Atlus)**
- At CEDEC + Kyushu 2017, Atlus said the Persona UI must convey information **and** the series' identity. P5 used no sub-colours outside HP/MP, to protect its signature red. Lines through the centre of the screen draw the eye, and menus use a 3D character model that spins into a pose (a special tool was built for it). [D, talk report] — [Siliconera CEDEC write-up](https://www.siliconera.com/?p=668266)
- Lead UI designer Masayoshi Suto took typographic inspiration from the ransom-note covers of Sex Pistols records. [D] — [Kotaku on Metaphor/Persona menus](https://kotaku.com/-metaphor-refantazio-persona-5-menu-ui-hashino-1851667088)
- Director Katsura Hashino: early menus were "impossible to read at first" and took much tweaking to become legible. Each menu has a different layout (list, grid, dial), and the stylish menus are "actually really annoying" to design. [D] — [PC Gamer (2024)](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/rpg/persona-series-director-says-atluss-flashy-menus-are-actually-really-annoying-to-design); [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/-metaphor-refantazio-persona-5-menu-ui-hashino-1851667088)
- Critics singled out small things such as the calendar scrolling through days. [C] — [GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/persona-5s-menus-continue-to-look-stunning/1100-6439532/). Masahiro Sakurai's Famitsu column called P5's UI unusually cluttered but striking. [D, peer designer] — [Source Gaming translation, Vol. 515](https://sourcegaming.info/2016/11/24/the-aims-of-ui-sakurais-famitsu-column-vol-515/)

**Disco Elysium: a character sheet that is a cast of characters**
- The sheet lists four attributes (Intellect, Psyche, Physique, Motorics) down the left, with six skill portraits in each row, 24 skills in all. Each skill is also a voice in dialogue. [W] — [Pixel Twelve guide](https://pixeltwelve.com/guides/disco-elysium-beginner-guide-skills-checks-thought-cabinet); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disco_Elysium)
- A planned Final Cut restyle of the Psyche, Physique and Motorics portraits (to match the two-tone Intellect ones) is not in the game, reportedly after mixed reception. A fan mod restores the cancelled portraits. [W/F] — [TV Tropes trivia](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Trivia/DiscoElysium); [itch.io portraits mod](https://lambda-software.itch.io/disco-elysium-the-new-portraits-mod)
- Aleksander Rostov is credited as an artist on the game. [W] — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disco_Elysium)

**Firewatch: a diegetic paper map and compass (Campo Santo, 2016)**
- There is no HUD map: Henry navigates with a magnetic compass and a paper map, and directions are given by landmarks. Henry's map changes over the playthrough as he copies notes left by other lookouts or writes memos of his own. The tone of the notes follows how the player role-plays Henry (sarcastic or plain). [C] — [First Person Scholar, "Environment as Narratives"](https://www.firstpersonscholar.com/environment-as-narratives/); [Game Developer, "How Firewatch's UI enhances player immersion"](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-firewatch-s-ui-enhances-player-immersion)
- Chris Remo describes the player as a "narrative archaeologist" who starts with a blank map: "it's not that you're given the map of the old ranger who lived there and you're uncovering their notes". [D] — [First Person Scholar](https://www.firstpersonscholar.com/environment-as-narratives/)
- The location dot can be switched off. One reviewer found the deliberately restrictive map hurt exploration. Campo Santo later released printable maps, one clean for marking up and one textured. [C/D] — [Kill Screen on printable maps](https://www.killscreen.com/printable-firewatch-maps-add-a-new-challenge-to-the-game/); [Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/b/news/archive/2016/02/26/navigate-the-wyoming-wilderness-with-printable-firewatch-maps)

**Slay the Spire: the run map as a visible risk/reward plan (Mega Crit, 2019)**
- A design analysis quotes Anthony Giovannetti saying the core is risk versus reward. The visible branching map lets players choose safer or richer paths, with elites costing health now in exchange for a relic. [W quoting D] — [Game-mechanics analysis](https://saschb2b.github.io/game-mechanics/games/slay-the-spire/design-tensions)
- Players often default to the path with the most rest sites, and some find that this makes map choices dull. [F] — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/646570/discussions/0/3160848559779551994)

**Academic: diegetic vs non-diegetic UI and immersion**
- Iacovides et al., "Removing the HUD: The Impact of Non-Diegetic Game Elements and Expertise on Player Involvement", CHI PLAY 2015 (pp. 13–22). Removing non-diegetic elements raised cognitive involvement and sense of control, and so immersion, **in expert players**. The authors stress that expertise must be weighed when judging UI choices. [S] — [Open University repository](https://oro.open.ac.uk/46759); [UCL PDF](https://discovery-pp.ucl.ac.uk/1470398/1/chip0179-iacovidesA.pdf)
- VR study (EGVE 2021): players clearly preferred a diegetic GUI over a HUD, but no gain in presence could be shown. [S] — [Eurographics Digital Library](https://diglib7.eg.org/bitstream/handle/10.2312/egve20211333/001-002.pdf)
- Babu, RIT thesis (2012): questionnaires showed no significant difference in spatial immersion between diegetic and non-diegetic status displays. Eye-tracking showed longer fixations in the less immersive condition, which hints at a cost in information processing. [S, thesis] — [RIT repository](https://repository.rit.edu/theses/5755/)

### Inferences
- Persona shows that a **strict, small palette and one typographic voice** make dense menus feel like part of the fiction. The cost is legibility, which Atlus fixed by iteration. For a phone-read site, metal-coloured rank crests and metal-framed Art cards are the equivalent identity system. Keep the metal colours as the only accent family, like P5's single red, and test small-screen legibility first.
- Disco Elysium's portraits turn each stat into a **character with a face and a voice**. Arts shown as square card pictures already do this. A one-line voice or epigraph on each card would extend the idea without adding numbers.
- Firewatch's annotations in the protagonist's hand are the in-world way to put a map and journal on a page. Notes in the knight's voice on the map or Codex would be diegetic and spoiler-safe, since they record only what he has seen.
- The academic evidence doesn't justify hiding all data for immersion's sake. It does support making the **default view diegetic and stylised, with numbers one tap away** (expert readers gain from less chrome; new readers need the data). That matches the author's wish to discover mechanics rather than study them.

### Gaps
- Fagerholt and Lorentzon's 2009 Chalmers thesis "Beyond the HUD" (the diegetic / non-diegetic / spatial / meta taxonomy) is the standard reference but did not surface in these searches. Verify it before citing.
- Darkest Dungeon's hamlet as a progression screen, FTL's sector map, and Persona's social-link rank cards and calendar: no design sources found. Persona 5's confidant rank-up card was not documented.
- No study found on stylised (non-diegetic but in-world-styled) progression screens and story comprehension.
- The Game UI Database (gameuidatabase.com) was not reachable for screenshots.

## 4. Stakes made legible: odds, dice and relationship numbers

### Takeaway
Showing odds **before** a roll makes stakes clear but invites players to treat the numbers as promises. Players read a high percentage as "success" and feel cheated by misses, so XCOM and Civilization quietly tilt the odds. Disco Elysium and Baldur's Gate 3 make the roll itself a dramatic beat. Disco Elysium separates retryable white checks from one-shot red checks; BG3's long d20 animation is praised for tension and criticised for pace. On relationships, visible meters (Mass Effect, Hades) give clarity, while BG3's hidden approval caused tuning and legibility problems. **No controlled study was found** showing that hiding numbers keeps players more focused on story.

### Cited Findings

**Disco Elysium: displayed odds, white vs red checks**
- Before you commit, the interface shows the skill and a success chance, and hovering breaks the chance down by modifier. Checks are 2d6, so any check sits between 3% and 97%. Passive checks show no odds. [W] — [Pixel Twelve guide](https://pixeltwelve.com/guides/disco-elysium-beginner-guide-skills-checks-thought-cabinet); [Disco Elysium wiki (Skills)](https://breezewiki.discard.no/discoelysium/wiki/Skills); [Steam thread on passive checks](https://steamcommunity.com/app/632470/discussions/0/1609400247624182352)
- White checks can be retried, but only after raising the skill or finding new information. Red checks are one attempt only, and you live with the result. [D/W] — [RPG Codex preview](https://rpgcodex.net/article.php?id=11127); [GameAnalytics Rezzed 2018 interview](https://www.gameanalytics.com/blog/disco-elysium-rezzed-2018-interview)
- Design intent: make failure interesting rather than punishing, with failure states anticipated and designed. [D, preview report] — [RPG Codex preview](https://rpgcodex.net/article.php?id=11127)
- Some players believe the displayed odds are inaccurate. This is anecdotal and unconfirmed. [F] — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/632470/discussions/0/1644304412665644101)

**Baldur's Gate 3: the visible d20 (Larian, 2023)**
- Larian shows an actual d20 being rolled for significant actions, which lends tension to each decision. [C, preview] — [USgamer preview](https://www.usgamer.net/articles/baldurs-gate-3-preview)
- The visible dice also onboard new players, since for many BG3 was their first D&D: one way to onboard them was to simplify the rules and show the rolls. [C; the search summary did not say which press piece carries it, so verify] — [GameBanshee, BG3 early access and RNG interview](https://www.gamebanshee.com/3h6hu). Swen Vincke counts blending many choices and consequences with intuitive cinematic presentation among the game's key achievements. [D] — [Game World Observer](https://gameworldobserver.com/2023/09/06/swen-vincke-dnd-interview-badlurs-gate-3-player-empathy). An RPS interview (seen on a mirror) carries the headline that BG3 "has created a new audience for RPGs thanks to how it expresses rules". [M] — [RPS mirror](https://wwwrockpapershotgos.pages.dev/posts/baldurs-gate-3-has-created-a-new-audience-for-rpgs-thanks-to-how-it-expresses-rules-says-larian-ceo)
- Criticism: Kotaku called the roll "fuzzy, meaningless", not using physics, and said it "sucks the magic out" compared with a real table. Players complain about long, floaty, unskippable roll transitions. [C/F] — [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/baldurs-gate-3-larian-rpg-pc-steam-dice-rolls-d20-1850729004); [lemm.ee comment](https://lemm.ee/comment/2018610)

**Citizen Sleeper: dice and clocks as story (Gareth Damian Martin, 2022)**
- The game runs on dice, clocks and skills, ideas Martin took from tabletop RPGs. [D] — [Game Developer, "How Citizen Sleeper was inspired by tabletop RPGs and gig work"](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/how-citizen-sleeper-was-inspired-by-tabletop-rpgs-and-gig-work)
- Abstract tools carry a lot: dice can stand for luck or energy, clocks for progress or damage. [D] — [TEETH newsletter interview](https://buttondown.com/TeethRPG/archive/a-magical-little-ritual-an-interview-with-gareth/)
- Events and jobs are tracked on clocks that tick each day, so the player sees how much time remains. [C] — [Superjump preview/interview](https://superjumpmagazine.com/citizen-sleeper-2-hands-on-preview-interview-with-gareth-damian-martin)
- In Citizen Sleeper 2, dice break under stress and need costly repair, so the dice themselves model trauma. [D/C] — [NPR (2025)](https://www.npr.org/2025/01/30/g-s1-45425/citizen-sleeper-2-gareth-damian-martin); [AV Club](https://www.avclub.com/bodies-and-long-term-harm-gareth-damian-martin-discusses-citizen-sleeper-2)

**XCOM and Sid Meier: displayed odds and perceived fairness**
- Jake Solomon: hit chances are emotional, not mathematical. Players read an 85% as a success rate, not a 15% chance to miss. The team didn't want players missing several 85% shots in a row, because the game would feel punitive. [D] — [Game Developer, "Jake Solomon explains the careful use of randomness in XCOM 2"](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/jake-solomon-explains-the-careful-use-of-randomness-in-xcom-2-i-)
- Hidden adjustments: XCOM: Enemy Within has a hidden modifier that can override the displayed chance, stronger on easier difficulties. One article says that on easier settings a displayed 85% behaves closer to 95%, "according to an interview with Jake Solomon" (that article was not pinned down, so verify). [C/D] — [PCGamesN, secret dice rolls in Enemy Within](https://www.pcgamesn.com/secret-dice-rolls-xcom-enemy-within); [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/firaxis-director-shared-that-he-was-frustrated-when-he-missed-shots-in-xcom)
- Asked about fixed hit-chance results in Enemy Unknown, Solomon reportedly said "I probably should have done that, actually." [D, reported] — [Softpedia](https://games.softpedia.com/blog/Lead-XCOM-Designer-Regrets-Fixed-Hit-Chance-Results-319573.shtml)
- Sid Meier, GDC 2010, "The Psychology of Game Design (Everything You Know Is Wrong)": Civilization Revolution showed combat odds up front. Players who lost at 3-to-1 felt cheated. Players accepted a loss at 2-to-1 but not at 20-to-10, though the odds are identical. The game was then tilted in the player's favour. [D] — [GDC Vault 1012186](https://gdcvault.com/play/1012186/The-Psychology-of-Game-Design); [Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/b/news/archive/2010/03/12/sid-meier-gamers-arent-logical); [Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/62807/sid-meier-and-rob-pardo); [Game Developer report](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/pc/gdc-sid-meier-s-lessons-on-gamer-psychology)
- A recent descendant: Dispatch's developers said at GDC that anything shown above about 76% succeeded automatically, after XCOM's example. [C, headline] — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/strategy/just-like-xcom-superhero-comedy-dispatch-cheats-random-percentages-of-success-in-the-players-favor-anything-that-had-over-a-76-percent-success-chance-would-automatically-succeed)

**Relationship numbers: visible vs hidden**
- Mass Effect: approval is shown as Paragon/Renegade bars. ME3 merged the two into one Reputation meter, which a critic called getting the meter right. [D/C] — [BioWare blog (2012)](https://blog.bioware.com/2012/03/01/reputation-in-mass-effect-3/); [PopMatters](https://www.popmatters.com/156838-2495865710.html)
- BG3 hides approval values. Larian said approval thresholds had been set too low, so small gains made companions fall for the player too quickly, and the fix was fewer romance overtures. [D, reported] — [GosuGamers](https://gosugamers.net/news/69288-baldurs-gate-3s-thirsty-companions-were-a-bug-not-a-feature); [Sportskeeda](https://sportskeeda.com/esports/larian-studios-tone-baldur-s-gate-3-s-pushy-companion-romances-that-s-a-good-thing)
- Players use mods to reveal hidden approval values. [M] — [aggregator on Alfira's unused approval](https://gleetron.pages.dev/posts/baldur-s-gate-3-players-find-unused-approval-meter-for-alfira)
- A critic argues that quest turning points can be talked through on the spot even with weak approval, which shrinks the weight of earlier conversations. Players ask for a way to explain their actions to companions. [C] — [Exmove Substack](https://exmove.substack.com/p/all-my-beefs-with-baldurs-gate-iii); [OpenCritic news](https://opencritic.com/news/18881/-wish-i-could-tell-my-companions-baldurs-gate-3-players-think-it-would-benefit-from-one-addition-to-dialogue-options)
- Hidden vs visible stats in choice-based fiction: authors on the Choice of Games forum are split on whether fewer visible stats make a story feel more narrative. Another writer notes that players often work out hidden values anyway, through wikis. [F/C] — [Choice of Games forum](https://forum.choiceofgames.com/t/on-visible-and-invisible-stats/13330); [DEV Community, "Re: Games & Numbers"](https://dev.to/onwritinggames/re-games-numbers-39ba)
- Jon Ingold (inkle): players assume a game isn't responding to their choices, so a game must prove quickly that it is paying attention. [D] — [Game Developer, "T.M.I.: Preserving the suspension of disbelief through ambiguity" (2015)](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/-t-m-i-preserving-the-suspension-of-disbelief-through-ambiguity)

### Inferences
- For d20 roll glyphs: Disco Elysium's **white vs red** distinction is a clean way to mark stakes without numbers. A visual cue for a once-only roll versus an ordinary one lets readers feel the weight without studying odds. Showing odds before a roll invites the "85% means success" fallacy (Solomon, Meier). Since the author wants discovery, show the **result** (glyph, maybe the face rolled) and the stakes framing, and keep odds behind a tap or on the sheet.
- BG3's criticism is about **duration and fake physics**. On a phone a roll reveal should be short or skippable. A static glyph that "lands" once (a single frame or short CSS transition) gets the tension without the drag.
- Citizen Sleeper's **clocks** (segmented circles that fill as time or pressure builds) are a proven, tabletop-derived way to show a deadline or rising danger with no numbers. A rope that frays or knots as a Book nears its end is a natural, in-world variant of a clock.
- On companion approval: visible bars (Mass Effect) make morality gamey. Hidden approval (BG3) leaves players unsure whether anyone noticed, and Ingold's point is that the game must show quickly that it noticed. A middle path fits a discovery-minded reader: show **qualitative states** (a word, an icon, a portrait expression) that change **only at named story beats**, and keep raw numbers off the page.

### Gaps
- No controlled study found on whether hiding relationship or approval numbers increases attention to story. The evidence is designer opinion and forum debate.
- Disco Elysium designers' own reasons for showing percentages (versus only red/white colour) were not found.
- Hades hearts: no designer source on why relationship progress is shown as hearts.
- Larian's own statements on why approval is hidden were not found. Only the threshold-tuning admission surfaced.

## 5. Maps as narrative: routes, fog of war, annotation, maps that change

### Takeaway
Narrative maps work when the map **is the record of the journey**: routes that light up as you travel them (80 Days), notes added in the protagonist's hand (Firewatch), maps you must buy and that update only when you rest (Hollow Knight), a caravan crossing painted land under a closing darkness (Banner Saga). Each makes the map a memory of choices and a statement of what is still unknown. Map usability on small screens is a known pain point.

### Cited Findings

**80 Days: the globe as branching story (inkle, 2014)**
- inkle could make "a map-based branching adventure where every choice was loaded with ready-made context": "Delhi or Moscow? Brisbane or Yokohama?" [D] — [inkle blog, "So what is 80 Days?" (2014)](https://www.inklestudios.com/2014/04/15/so-what-is-eighty-days.html)
- Meg Jayanth plotted the route Fogg and Passepartout take in the novel. The adventures spin out in new directions, but the timing was kept roughly the same. [D] — [inkle blog, "Approaching journey's end" (2014)](https://www.inklestudios.com/2014/07/14/approaching-journeys-end.html)
- Players "literally uncover a map that lights up as you explore it". Progression **is** your growing knowledge of the world, shown as uncovered routes. [C] — [Cubed3 review](https://www.cubed3.com/games/reviews/pc/80-days)
- The map uses a 1920s art-deco style. [C] — [thinkspace analysis](https://thinkspace.csu.edu.au/awood/?p=81)
- Usability complaint: city names are unreadable unless you zoom right in. [F] — [Quarter to Three forum](https://forum.quartertothree.com/t/80-days-thread/76165)

**The Banner Saga: the caravan line (Stoic, 2014)**
- Travel stood in for Dragon Age-style exploration the team couldn't afford. Stoic described it as King of Dragon Pass "on the road", crossed with Oregon Trail. Players balance travel speed against the caravan's endurance and morale while a wall of darkness closes in, and camping lets it draw nearer. [D] — [Game Informer, "Chatting The Banner Saga design" (2013)](https://gameinformer.com/games/the_banner_saga/b/pc/archive/2013/12/30/chatting-the-banner-saga-design-with-ex-bioware-developer); [GameBanshee interview](https://gamebanshee.com/3ay)
- Jorgensen recalls people admiring travel scenes posted as wallpaper and assuming they were concept art, when they were real gameplay screens. [D] — [GameBanshee interview](https://gamebanshee.com/3ay)

**Hollow Knight: maps bought and drawn (Team Cherry, 2017)**
- An area's incomplete map must be bought from the cartographer Cornifer before it can be filled in. If you miss him, the shopkeeper Iselda sells it in town at a higher price. The Quill (120 Geo) lets your maps update, and they update **only when you rest at a bench**. [W] — [Hollow Knight wiki: Map and Quill](https://hollowknight.wiki/w/Map); [Fextralife](https://hollowknight.wiki.fextralife.com/Map+and+Quill)

**Firewatch: annotation as characterisation** (detail in section 3)
- Henry's map collects copied notes and his own memos, and their tone follows the player's role-play. [C/D] — [First Person Scholar](https://www.firstpersonscholar.com/environment-as-narratives/)

**Outer Wilds and Heaven's Vault: knowledge maps** (detail in section 1)
- The rumor graph shows partial knowledge, and Heaven's Vault's pause screen pairs the map with the timeline and translated texts. [W/C] — [Birdor case study](https://birdor-blog.pages.dev/outer-wilds-knowledge-progression-product-case-study/); [Gamereactor](https://www.gamereactor.eu/?p=59479)

**Slay the Spire: the route ahead is visible** (detail in section 3)
- The map exposes upcoming node types so the route becomes a risk/reward plan. [W] — [Game-mechanics analysis](https://saschb2b.github.io/game-mechanics/games/slay-the-spire/design-tensions)

### Inferences
- A painted map with the route travelled is closest to 80 Days and Banner Saga. Draw the line **only as far as the prose has gone**. Places named but not visited can appear faint or unlabeled, the way 80 Days lights a route only once it is explored. That gives "fog of war" for free under the no-spoiler rule.
- Hollow Knight's "map updates only when you rest" maps neatly onto a serial: **update the map at chapter close**, not mid-chapter, so the map becomes part of the weekly ritual.
- Firewatch suggests small **annotations in the knight's voice** at places on the route (a line from the scene that happened there, linking to the chapter). This makes the map a memory, not a diagram.
- 80 Days' usability complaint is a warning for phones: labels must stay legible without pinch-zoom. Use tap-to-reveal place cards rather than dense text on the painting.

### Gaps
- The Oregon Trail trail map, FTL's sector map, and fog-of-war research: no sources found this session.
- No designer source on 80 Days' route-line rendering or on how players used the globe to recall the story.
- Team Cherry's stated intent for the buy-and-draw map system was not found. Only wiki mechanics.

## 6. Pacing and moments: palette shifts, rare illustrations and visual restraint

### Takeaway
Games with strong arcs plan colour across the whole story: Journey's palette follows an emotional curve, and Gris restores colours stage by stage as its heroine recovers. Labour-intensive art (Banner Saga's painted vistas, Wildermyth's comics) is deliberately scarce and saved for story beats. Restraint is partly budget, but designers present it as a choice that makes each image count.

### Cited Findings

**Journey (thatgamecompany, 2012): a colour curve that follows the narrative**
- The palette shifts across the game along "a curve established by the artists that is reflective of the narrative". Matt Nava aimed to make "the colors guide the players' emotions and reactions". [D, trade press] — [Computer Graphics World, "Mysterious Journey" (2012)](https://www.cgw.com/Publications/CGW/2012/Volume-35-Issue-4-June-July-2012/Mysterious-Journey.aspx)
- Nava: spaces were designed to emphasise the emotional arc through "lighting, murkiness and mood". [D] — [Vice interview](https://www.vice.com/en/article/scuba-diving-game-developers-make-surreal-underwater-game)
- The gradually shifting palette created a sense of discovery that players wanted to share. [C] — [InformIT](https://www.informit.com/articles/article.aspx?p=2697997&seqNum=2)
- Talk: Matt Nava, "The Art of Journey", GDC 2013. [D] — [GDC Vault 1017799](https://gdcvault.com/play/1017799/The-Art-of); [Game Developer write-up](https://gamedeveloper.com/art/video-deconstructing-the-art-design-of-i-journey-i-)
- Reported but unverified in English: the sky began green and was changed to bright blue late in the game as a reward. [C, Spanish-language] — [Vida Extra](https://www.vidaextra.com/aventura-plataformas/se-cumplen-10-anos-inolvidable-viaje-journey-su-director-artistico-comparte-artes-conceptuales-sus-paisajes)

**Gris (Nomada Studio, 2018): colour returns as the heroine recovers**
- Nomada first planned to add colours, then built the levels around the five stages of grief, with each colour representing a stage. One interview gives denial–grey, anger–red, bargaining–green, depression–blue, acceptance–yellow. [D] — [PLITCH interview with Nomada Studio](https://blogs.plitch.com/en/blog/interview-with-nomada-studio). **Conflicting accounts:** other analyses give a different order (red, green, blue, yellow, then the full spectrum). [C] — [A Garden of Stories](https://agardenofstories.substack.com/p/gris)
- Adrián Cuevas: "The most important thing in the game are the colours." [D] — [Gamereactor](https://www.gamereactor.eu/what-does-gris-mean-we-talk-to-nomada-studio/)
- Game Informer called it the best modern game about overcoming trauma. [C] — [Game Informer (2019)](https://www.gameinformer.com/index.php/the-virtual-life/2019/01/16/gris-is-the-best-modern-game-about-overcoming-trauma)

**Restraint and cost of art**
- Banner Saga: one wide travel vista with a tiny caravan "can take a couple weeks, easily". [D] — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/the-banner-saga-background-artist-interview)
- Wildermyth: skipping detailed animation made the art workload manageable, and the comic format carries the story. [D, mirror] — [RPS making-of (mirror)](https://wwwrockpapers.pages.dev/posts/the-making-of-wildermyth-how-a-family-developer-created-one-of-the-most-innovative-rpgs-in-years)
- Darkest Dungeon's narrator was added late. Red Hook realised it needed a narrator to get some things across while making the "Terror and Madness" trailer. [D, reported; the search summary did not say which of these pages carries it] — [Game Developer, "Designing for despair"](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/-i-darkest-dungeon-i-designing-for-despair-and-kicking-you-when-you-re-down); [80.lv, Red Hook on making Darkest Dungeon](https://80.lv/articles/red-hook-studios-talks-about-the-creation-of-darkest-dungeon/?amp=1)
- Pentiment uses its typography as **redundant** emphasis, so readability modes lose nothing (section 1). [D] — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/how-pentiments-hand-crafted-fonts-give-pen-and-ink-a-voice)

### Inferences
- A **colour script per Book** (an accent or wash that shifts as the Books advance, like Journey's curve or Gris's restored colours) is a low-cost, spoiler-safe way to make a Book transition felt across the whole site. The Knot rope could take the new Book's colour when it ties.
- Rare full illustrations work best as **punctuation at turning points** (a Book's climax, a Knot tied), not on every chapter. Banner Saga and Wildermyth show that scarcity is affordable and reads as weight.
- Darkest Dungeon's narrator story suggests that a single recurring device (one voice, one epigraph style) can carry tone across a large UI more cheaply than art. Epigraphs at chapter heads already do this.

### Gaps
- No study found that measures the effect of palette shifts or rare illustrations on story comprehension or emotional response in games.
- Act-transition art changes in narrative RPGs (title cards, Persona's calendar transitions, Disco Elysium's day changes): no design sources found.
- The Gris colour-to-stage order could not be resolved between sources.

## 7. Designer talks, postmortems and academic work: the source base

### Takeaway
The best primary sources are GDC talks (Meier 2010, Nava 2013, Outer Wilds 2020, Sawyer 2025), long interviews (Kasavin, Kurvitz, Solomon, Martin, Ingold, Jayanth), and two or three CHI PLAY / EGVE / thesis studies on diegetic UI. Academic work on **UI and narrative comprehension** specifically is thin. Most evidence is designer intent plus critic reception, not measured player understanding.

### Cited Findings
- GDC 2010, Sid Meier, "The Psychology of Game Design (Everything You Know Is Wrong)": displayed odds and perceived fairness. [D] — [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1012186/The-Psychology-of-Game-Design)

### Inferences
- Evidence comes in three layers: (1) designer intent, which is plentiful; (2) critic and player reception, plentiful but anecdotal; (3) measured effects on comprehension and immersion, scarce and mostly about HUDs in action games. Recommendations for a text-first serial should rest mainly on (1) and (2), and say so.
- One thread runs through the strongest examples: **the visual records what the player already knows and changes when the story changes it** (Heaven's Vault timeline, 80 Days routes, Pentiment fonts, Hollow Knight maps, Wildermyth transformations, CK3 portraits). That is the same constraint the site already imposes ("a picture may only show what the prose has put on the page"), so the games here are close models, not loose analogies.

### Gaps
- No academic work found that tests codex, journal or progression-screen design against **story comprehension or recall**. That would need a targeted search of DiGRA, Game Studies and CHI PLAY ("narrative comprehension", "quest log", "journal").
- Fagerholt and Lorentzon (2009), the diegetic UI taxonomy, is not verified this session.
- Game Maker's Toolkit transcripts and Game UI Database entries were not reached.
- Full texts of the Game Developer, PC Gamer and GameSpot articles could not be opened (network policy). Exact quotes need checking against originals before public use.
